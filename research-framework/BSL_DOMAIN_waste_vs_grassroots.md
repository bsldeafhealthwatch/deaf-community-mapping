# Domain 12: Waste vs. Grassroots Delivery

**Last verified:** 4 September 2026
**Re-check cadence:** Reviewed alongside whichever domain generated the underlying figure (this domain is a cross-cutting lens, not a standalone data source — per the framework's own note in Section 2 of `BSL_DEAF_RESEARCH_FRAMEWORK.md`)
**Confidence level:** Low — this is explicitly the most evidence-thin domain in the project; the framework document itself notes this is a lens applied within other domains rather than a standalone pot of facts, and this pass confirms there is currently no dedicated primary source to anchor it

---

## Summary

Domain 12 asks where taxpayer money intended for Deaf accessibility is lost to overhead/agency margin versus reaching frontline, community-rooted delivery. This pass found **no dedicated NAO or PAC inquiry specifically examining BSL interpreting agency margins or national Deaf-specific programmes** — the closest primary source is the NAO/PAC examination of Access to Work (Domain 5), which is a finding about administrative capacity and processing delays absorbing effective access, not a finding of money being misappropriated or wasted in the conventional sense. The one concrete "cost of inaction" figure circulating in advocacy material — SignHealth's claim that inaccessible health services cost HM Treasury at least £80-100 million a year — is the same contested figure already flagged as needing source-tracing elsewhere in this project's verification work, and should not be treated as independently confirmed here either.

## Known Gaps & Contested Figures

- **No dedicated primary source exists for this domain specifically.** This is itself the finding: despite substantial public spending flowing through interpreting frameworks (Domain 11: at least £473m in framework capacity BSL sits within) and substantial charity-sector activity (Domain 10), nobody — not NAO, not PAC, not a select committee — appears to have conducted an independent, BSL-specific value-for-money or waste audit. This absence should be stated plainly as a finding, and is itself a legitimate campaigning point ("nobody has ever independently audited where this money goes").
- **The £80-100m/year SignHealth figure is contested and already flagged elsewhere in this project** (see Domain 1's Verification Status entry) — it should never appear in this domain's public-facing material without the same "SignHealth/campaigners estimate" attribution required everywhere else it's used. Do not treat its repetition in *Locked Out* as independent corroboration — *Locked Out* is repeating SignHealth's own figure, not verifying it against a separate source.
- Domain 11's finding that no BSL-only spend figure exists anywhere in public procurement data is the direct precondition blocking this domain from making any quantified waste-vs-grassroots claim — without a BSL-only spend baseline, there is no way to calculate what proportion is "waste" versus "reaching the frontline."

---

## Money Trail

This domain **is** the money-trail question applied across the whole project, per the framework's own design. Its honest current state: the underlying data needed to answer "how much of Deaf-accessibility spending reaches frontline delivery versus overhead/agency margin" does not appear to exist in any single published source. The path to answering it runs through Domain 11 (isolating BSL-specific spend from bundled frameworks) and Domain 5 (tracking Access to Work's support-worker spend line down to actual interpreter pay versus agency margin) — this domain cannot be filled independently of those two.

---

## FOI Targets Arising

1. **NAO** — whether any current or planned work programme includes a BSL-interpreting-specific or Deaf-accessibility-specific value-for-money study, distinct from the existing Access to Work report
2. **NHS SBS / NHS PiP** (building on Domain 11's targets) — call-off data that would allow tracing what proportion of framework spend reaches individual interpreters' pay versus agency overhead/margin
3. **DWP** (building on Domain 5's targets) — the same interpreter-pay-vs-agency-margin breakdown within the £227m Access to Work support-worker spend line
4. **HM Treasury** — whether the SignHealth £80-100m/year "cost of inaction" figure has ever been independently reviewed or costed by Treasury, or whether it has only ever been cited, never verified, in government-adjacent publications
