## Summary

This domain covers Deaf and DeafBlind people **living out** later life — state pension and Pension Credit access, Care Act social-care assessment and residential placement, loneliness and isolation, and transport concessions — as distinct from Domain 16, which covers **dying**. There is no official later-life BSL loneliness rate, no CQC tag for BSL-specialist care homes, and no SWL count of Deaf-elder placements; this thinness is the batch's actual finding, not a search failure. The Pension Service does publish a working inbound BSL video-relay button (InterpretersLive!) on its GOV.UK contact pages, but VRS is inbound-only — DWP cannot call a Deaf claimant back through it, so award letters and uprating notices still go out as print by default. Travel concessions turn on a specific audiometric threshold (70 dBHL both ears) or state pension age, not on Deafness generally, and there is no London companion Freedom Pass. Four overseas delivery models exist as a menu (campus residential, mobile signing workforce, an over-65s interpreting fund, and rights-inside-generic-homes), none of which this project recommends adopting wholesale — a campus ask without a UK placement count is theatre, per the source review.

## Boundary with Domain 16 (End-of-Life & Palliative Care)

Domain 16 covers dying — hospice and last-days care, DWP Special Rules for End of Life, family-as-interpreter at the point of death. This domain covers **living out** later life while alive — pensions, ordinary (non-terminal) social care, isolation, transport. Named individuals whose testimony belongs to Domain 16 (Alan Graham, Pearl Clinton, Francesca Bussey, Mrs W) **stay in Domain 16** and must not be imported here as Domain 20 case studies — pointer only, no duplication of testimony across the boundary.

This project treats a documented absence as a finding, not a gap to fill with a plausible-sounding statistic. The following are explicitly **ABSENT**, confirmed by search, not merely "not yet found":

- **No official BSL/Deaf-specific later-life loneliness rate.** Do not substitute the Community Life Survey's 14% (disabled adults) or 4–5% (65+ general population) figures, and do not substitute RNID's 36% figure — that is a BSL-user *stigma* subset, not an age-banded loneliness rate, and must never be presented as "over a third of older Deaf people are lonely."

- **No named DeafBlind later-life PHSO case** was found in this batch (contrast with Domain 16, which does carry named PHSO/health-access cases — this absence is specific to the later-life/non-dying context).
- **No UK DeafBlind or Deaf-elder employment/loneliness cross-tabulation** exists linking Domain 20's population to Domain 6 (Neurodivergence) or Domain 5 (Access to Work) data.

## Current Policy / Legal Position

- **Care Act 2014 statutory guidance** splits by need type: paragraph 6.80 covers the general Deaf-interpreter duty; paragraphs 6.91–6.96 create a **specialist assessor** duty specifically for deafblind people. These are not the same duty and must not be collapsed into one another — a BSL-Deaf adult who is not deafblind does not get the 6.91–6.96 specialist-assessor route.
- **AIS does not apply to the Pension Service or DWP.** The applicable framework is the Equality Act 2010 reasonable-adjustment duty, plus BSL Act 2022 reporting obligations on DWP as a listed ministerial department — not AIS, which is an NHS/adult-social-care instrument (DAPB1605/4019).
- **Travel concessions are eligibility-gated, not Deafness-gated.** Disabled Person's Freedom Pass requires 70 dBHL+ in both ears, or the Older Person's Pass is available at State Pension Age regardless of hearing status. Taxicard eligibility is mobility/severe-sight-based, not a Deaf product. London does not issue a companion Freedom Pass (Croydon's "Companion Badge" is a parking anti-theft product, not a companion travel concession).
- **No Care Act, CQC, or DWP instrument currently requires disaggregating any of the above by BSL/Deaf/DeafBlind status** — the absence of that data field is itself part of this domain's finding, consistent with the ABSENT convention above.

## Known Gaps & Contested Figures

- **RNID's 36% figure is a BSL-user stigma/attitude subset, not an age-banded loneliness rate** — must never be quoted as "over a third of older Deaf people are lonely."
- **Community Life Survey's 14%/4–5% figures are general-population, not Deaf-specific** — do not substitute for a missing Deaf-elder rate.
- **Sense's ~250,000 "deafblind over 70" figure is a 2017-rebased model, not a measured loneliness or placement statistic.**
- ***Locked Out*'s "only 2 residential homes that cater specifically to BSL users" claim carries a named-list contest** in the source review (prompt 67) — do not repeat this figure without also carrying the contest; the named list itself was not independently re-verified in this pass as complete or current.
- **Finland's 36% is a separate, international figure** and must not be presented as a UK statistic despite the coincidental match to RNID's 36%.
- **No published fulfilment/refusal rate exists for Pension Service BSL VRS contact** — only that the button exists and is inbound-only.

## Commissioning Models (from prompt 70 — "choose / cost," not "we demand all four")

This project does **not** campaign for any one model in the near term. The source review is explicit: "A campus ask without a placement count is theatre." The four models below are logged as an international menu for a future costed decision, not a current recommendation.

| Model | Overseas proof | UK hook | Honest limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Campus** (dedicated Deaf residential/nursing) | De Gelderhorst (Netherlands, 117 apartments + nursing); Bob Rumball (Canada, 64 beds); NEHD Danvers (US) | Care Act placement + specialist residential procurement; Longley Road is the local existence proof | Capital + staffing cost; a single national home also imposes an uprooting/relocation cost on residents (per prompt 69's transport/mobility findings) |
| **Mobile signing workforce** (Deaf-led home-care staff) | Swedish Deaf assistant-nurse team (*BMC Geriatrics*, Dec 2025, n=4 — a very small evidence base) | Local-authority home care, direct payments, or VCSE contract; possible ICB prevention-spend angle | The overseas evidence base is four people — do not present as a proven model; needs a Deaf-led provider, not a generic "sensory support" relabelling |
| **65+ interpreting fund** (dedicated post-work-age BSL interpreting budget) | Australia's National Sign Language Programme (NSLP) — not tied to NDIS, covers aged care, Medicare, banking, funerals | A DHSC or DWP-administered line for people with no social-care package | Different welfare-state design; the closest current UK gap is "too old for Access to Work, too language-specific for generic older-people charities" |
| **Rights inside generic (non-Deaf-specific) homes** | US Fair Housing Act enforcement; the "*A Home for Henry*" case | Equality Act 2010 + AIS (health-adjacent) + CQC inspection | This is already the current English default in theory — the gap is in practice, not in the legal framework |
