# Domain 14: Devices & Technology, Including AI

**Last verified:** 4 September 2026
**Re-check cadence:** Quarterly — this is a fast-moving domain, especially the AI/avatar sub-topic
**Confidence level:** Medium (strong on the AI-avatar debate and Access to Work equipment scope; weak on UK-specific NHS hardware waiting-time data; **one clear geographic mismatch flagged below**)

---

## Summary

This domain splits into two distinct strands that should not be conflated. **Assistive hardware** (NHS hearing aids, cochlear implants, alerting/vibrating devices, loop systems) is a supply-and-waiting-time story similar in shape to Domain 1. **AI sign-language technology** (avatars, machine translation) is a live, actively contested strand with its own politics — the central fault line is not "does the tech work," but **who controls it and whether Deaf people have the choice to refuse it**. The UK currently has one major, publicly funded AI BSL project (SignGPT, £8.4m) explicitly built on Deaf co-investigator involvement, alongside commercial AI avatar products that have drawn direct criticism from Deaf linguists and the World Federation of the Deaf for treating interpretation as a solved translation problem rather than a live cultural act.

## Key Organisations & Actors

- **RNID** — leading a Sciencewise-funded project (announced Dec 2025) specifically to bring grassroots Deaf community dialogue into how AI BSL translation is developed, framed explicitly as filling a gap where only academic voices have so far been heard
- **UCL DCAL (Deafness, Cognition and Language Research Centre)**, University of Surrey, University of Oxford — the academic/technical partners behind SignGPT; Professor Kearsy Cormier (DCAL director) is cited defending the project's "real co-creation" between Deaf and hearing researchers, which is itself the contested claim worth tracking
- **Dr Maartje De Meulder** (Deaf researcher, HU University of Applied Sciences / Heriot-Watt University) — the clearest public advocate for a Deaf-led governance standard: her BDA conference remarks (2025) specifically call for funding to be *tied to* Deaf principal investigators, Deaf co-investigators, and Deaf board representation, not just Deaf consultation
- **World Federation of the Deaf (WFD)** and **World Association of Sign Language Interpreters (WASLI)** — the 2018 joint statement remains the standing international reference position against treating avatars as interpreter-equivalent
- **Commercial AI avatar vendors** (e.g. Robotica/"Cassie", GoSignAI) — positioned as solving interpreter scarcity; not academically peer-reviewed in the sources found, and drawing direct scepticism from Deaf commentators and researchers
- **Access to Work (DWP)** — the primary UK funding mechanism for individual assistive technology (visual/vibrating alert systems, amplified phones, loop systems, video relay services) — direct overlap with Domain 5, not yet researched in full; this domain should not duplicate that work, only flag the technology-specific angle

## Current Policy / Legal Position

- No UK-specific statute or regulation found this session governing the use of AI sign-language translation in public services (health, justice, benefits) — this appears to be an active policy gap, not merely an unresearched one; worth testing directly via FOI (see below) rather than assuming either way
- **Access to Work** funds assistive technology for employed/self-employed Deaf people as a matter of standing DWP scheme design, not new legislation — equipment covered explicitly includes video relay services, alerting devices, amplified telephones, and closed-loop systems, per RNID's and NDCS's own guidance
- **NHS audiology** operates under the general 18-week Referral to Treatment standard that applies across NHS elective care — there does not appear to be a Deaf/hearing-loss-specific waiting time standard distinct from this general target

---

## ✅ Resolved: NHS Audiology Six-Week Wait Figure

The 38% figure previously flagged as needing primary verification is confirmed: NHS England's Accredited Official Statistics (Diagnostic Waiting Times and Activity Statistical Report, March 2025) show **38.2%** of patients waiting 6+ weeks for an audiology assessment at end-March 2025, up 0.3 percentage points from 37.8% in March 2024 — verified directly against Table 2 of the primary report.

**Still unresolved:** there is no equivalent official series for the *fitting* stage (after assessment, before a hearing aid is actually fitted) — NHS England's diagnostics collection (DM01) covers assessments only. Any "referral to fitting" timeframe circulating commercially is not backed by a national published statistic; individual trust FOI responses exist (e.g. roughly 8 weeks reported by some trusts) but do not form a national series.

## ⚠️ Other Known Gaps & Contested Figures

**Geographic mismatch flagged:** search results returned information on the **National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program** — this is a **US Federal Communications Commission programme**, not a UK scheme. There does not appear to be a direct UK equivalent found this session for centrally funded DeafBlind-specific equipment distribution (braille displays, tactile alerting devices) outside of what Access to Work or individual local authority social care budgets might cover on a discretionary basis. **Do not cite the US programme as applicable to UK claimants** — this is exactly the kind of cross-contamination the source hierarchy in the framework document is designed to catch. This gap should be tested directly against Domain 3 (DeafBlind-specific provision) when that domain is researched.

**Cochlear implants — reported by a SuperGrok cross-check, not independently re-verified this session:** no official NHS England waiting-time series for adult cochlear-implant referral-to-surgery appears to exist (DHSC written parliamentary answer UIN 91346, 24 Nov 2025, reportedly confirms this). Activity-count figures were reported — Hospital Episode Statistics adult (18+) cochlear implant procedures: 767 (2022/23), 923 (2023/24), 889 (2024/25); separately, the British Cochlear Implant Group reportedly recorded 1,085 new adult implants UK-wide in the year to 31 March 2024. A cited academic study (Swords et al., PLOS Medicine, April 2024, the INTEGRATE study, n=6,171) reportedly found 35.7% of adults meeting audiometric eligibility criteria were told they were eligible, but only 9.7% were actually referred for implant assessment — an under-referral finding, not a post-assessment funding-refusal figure. **None of these specific figures or their source URLs (the PQ, the PLOS Medicine paper, the BCIG page) have been independently opened and checked this session** — treat as a strong lead for the team to verify directly before citing externally, following the same discipline that caught the RADF and audiology figures.

**Other gaps:**
- Whether any NHS Trust or UK public body has piloted or procured AI BSL avatar technology for patient-facing use has not been established — flagged as an FOI target, not assumed either way
- No data found on interpreter workforce impact — i.e., whether AI translation tools are measurably displacing paid human interpreter bookings anywhere in the UK, or whether this remains a future risk rather than a present one

## Money Trail

- **SignGPT's £8.4m is public research funding** (UKRI-adjacent, per the reporting) — worth an FOI/direct enquiry to establish the exact funding body, the governance structure (does it actually meet De Meulder's "Deaf PI, Deaf co-investigators, Deaf board" bar, or fall short of it), and whether any output from the project has been costed for eventual public-service deployment
- **Commercial AI avatar vendors represent a different money question** from SignGPT: if any public body (NHS, DWP, courts) were to procure a commercial AI avatar product as a cheaper substitute for human interpreters, that would be a direct instance of the Domain 12 "waste vs. grassroots" pattern — cheaper technology procurement potentially undermining the human interpreter workforce this whole project's other domains are trying to strengthen and fund properly. No evidence such procurement has happened yet in the UK — but it is the exact risk Dr De Meulder is publicly naming, and worth monitoring rather than dismissing
- **Access to Work equipment spend breakdown** (technology vs. human support, and specifically for Deaf/DeafBlind claimants) was not found as a published disaggregated figure — a clean FOI target

## FOI Targets Arising

1. **DWP / Access to Work** — annual spend breakdown between equipment/technology and human communication support (interpreters, notetakers, STTR) specifically for Deaf and DeafBlind claimants, most recent 3 years
2. **UKRI or the specific funding council behind SignGPT** — full grant governance structure, confirming (or not) Deaf-led principal investigator and board representation as called for by Dr De Meulder
3. **NHS Trusts (starting SWL ICB footprint)** — whether any AI sign-language translation or avatar technology has been piloted, trialled, or procured for patient-facing use, and if so, whether Deaf-led evaluation was part of the procurement process
4. **NHS England** — primary-source audiology waiting time statistics (assessment and fitting), to replace the commercial secondary-source figures currently in this briefing
5. **NHS England / relevant commissioning bodies** — current cochlear implant referral-to-surgery waiting times and annual number of adult referrals not funded, if such data is held centrally
