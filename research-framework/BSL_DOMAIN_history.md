## Summary

This domain gives the historical throughline the rest of the project's advocacy framing depends on: BSL and Deaf community life in Britain moved from tolerated informal signing in early Deaf schools, through nearly a century of institutionalised oralism following the 1880 Milan Congress, to a slow rebuilding of linguistic recognition culminating in non-statutory government recognition in 2003 and statutory recognition in 2022. The throughline advocacy groups draw — that oralism-era education policy caused generational language deprivation that still shapes today's health, justice, and welfare outcomes for Deaf people — is the current official-advisory framing (most recently restated in *Locked Out*, Nov 2025), not an independently audited causal study. The dates and events themselves are standard, well-corroborated Deaf-history facts; the causal claim connecting them to 2026 outcomes is the community's own historiography and should be presented as such.

## Key Organisations & Actors

- **British Deaf Association (BDA)** — founded 1890 directly in response to Milan-era oralist policy; the oldest and most historically continuous Deaf-led organisation in this project's stakeholder set
- **International Congress on Education of the Deaf (ICED)** — the recurring international conference whose 1880 Milan resolutions caused the century-long oralist turn, and whose 1985 Manchester and 2010 Vancouver meetings mark the community's pushback and eventual formal reversal
- **BSL Advisory Board** — the current body most actively connecting this historical framing to present-day policy recommendations via *Locked Out*

---

## Current Policy / Legal Position

The historical arc directly explains the layered, incomplete legal picture documented in Domain 7: 2003's non-statutory recognition and 2022's statutory-but-narrow recognition are best understood as two successive, partial responses to more than a century of active suppression rather than as isolated modern policy choices. This framing matters for how the project presents legislative gaps — the BSL Act 2022's narrowness (Domain 7) is not simply an oversight but the latest point in a slow, contested recognition process that took over 140 years from the Milan Congress to reach even non-enforceable statutory recognition.

## Known Gaps & Contested Figures

- **The causal claim that oralism-era education policy directly explains today's health/justice/welfare outcome gaps is community historiography and current official-advisory framing, not an independently audited causal study.** This is an important distinction for public-facing material: the historical facts (Milan 1880, the suppression of signing, the slow recognition timeline) are solid; the specific causal mechanism linking 1880s education policy to a 2026 health-access statistic has not been isolated and tested by an independent study in anything found this session. Present it as the Deaf community's and BSL Advisory Board's own framing, attributed as such, not as a proven causal chain.
- No figures on how many Deaf children were affected by oralist education policy across the relevant decades (1880s-1970s) were sought or found in this pass — this domain is a timeline/framing piece, not a quantified history, and that's an appropriate scope limit rather than a gap to fill aggressively.
- The 1893 Act's precise provisions and the mechanism by which it "is associated with" oralist provision were not read directly from the Act's text this session — worth confirming exact wording if this fact is used prominently.
