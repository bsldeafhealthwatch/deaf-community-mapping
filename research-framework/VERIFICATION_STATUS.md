## 1. Purpose

Every domain briefing already flags its own contested/stale figures. This document pulls every load-bearing figure across all five domain files into one place and rates it against a single bar: **would this survive being challenged by someone who checked the source themselves?**

This is what "3x accuracy" means in practice here — not repeating a claim three times, but requiring three things before a figure is presented as settled fact on the public site: (1) it traces to a named primary or tier 1–4 source, (2) that source has actually been opened and read by someone on the team (not just cited by an AI cross-check), and (3) it isn't stale (12+ months old and unconfirmed as still current).

## 2. Rating System

| Rating | Meaning | Safe to publish as... |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Confirmed** | The actual primary/named source was opened and read directly this session or a prior verified session | A stated fact, full stop |
| 🟡 **Lead-only** | Came from a secondary report or an AI cross-check (e.g. SuperGrok) citing a real document, but nobody has opened that document to confirm the claim is actually in it | A "worth checking" note internally — **never** published as fact until upgraded |
| 🟠 **Advocacy-estimate** | Traces to a charity/campaign's own modelling or inflation-adjusted figure, not an independent or government costing | Publishable only with attribution in the same sentence ("campaigners estimate...") — never as a bare number |
| ⚪ **Stale** | Was accurate when sourced but is old enough (12+ months, often much more) that presenting it as current would mislead | Historical context only, explicitly dated |

## 22. Standing Methodological Finding: The Grassroots-Voice Gap

This project has now run two rounds of research that included live social-media search (Batch 2, Section D2, and the ordinary course of prior passes), and in both, the same pattern held: **charity, campaign, and institutional voices are well-represented on X/social media; individual Deaf BSL users' own first-hand accounts of NHS access, especially anything SWL-specific, are essentially absent.** This is now logged as a standing finding in its own right, not a search failure to be quietly retried until it goes away — flagged explicitly by the project owner as something requiring deliberate action at a later stage, not just more searching.

**What is already known that bears on this:**
- Healthwatch SWL's own written English-language patient survey (part of the BSL GP-access report, Domain 1) received **zero responses**, despite a BSL video version being offered alongside it — a striking, already-confirmed data point suggesting format, not apathy, may be the barrier.
- RNID's own material states there is "no consensus" on even the basic size of the BSL-using population (Domain 1) — a community this hard to count through conventional means is also a community likely hard to reach through conventional (English-text) social platforms.
- BSL is a distinct visual-spatial language with its own grammar, not signed English — for a BSL-dominant person, composing a fluent, persuasive complaint in written English is a genuine second-language task, not a natural extension of how they think or communicate. This is a plausible, non-speculative structural explanation for low visibility on text-first platforms like X, and should be tested directly (Batch 3, Section E2, asks Grok to search for existing research/commentary on exactly this) rather than assumed.

**Why this matters beyond just "more data would be nice":** every piece of lived-experience evidence in this project so far has arrived pre-filtered through an organisation — a charity's survey, a Healthwatch focus group, a tribunal case. That is valuable, but it is mediated evidence, not the grassroats voice itself. A campaign or evidence base built entirely on institutional mediation is vulnerable to the criticism that it represents what charities say Deaf people experience, not what Deaf people say themselves — a distinction that matters both for the strength of the eventual public-facing case and for staying true to this project's own stated purpose (empowering Deaf people to make their own access needs known).

**Planned direction, updated 5 September 2026 following Batch 3's results (see Section 23 below for full detail):** Batch 3 tested this directly and the finding has sharpened considerably. The linguistic explanation is now documented, not speculative: BSL is a distinct language from English, not signed English, and multiple independent UK sources (the BSL Advisory Board's own *Locked Out* report, a GOV.UK accessibility co-design study, a financial-services regulator's own guidance) all independently make the same structural point — text-first platforms and forms are a genuine barrier, not a preference. The grassroots voice **does exist on video platforms** (YouTube, Facebook) but is overwhelmingly **charity-produced** rather than independently authored, and **no genuinely independent, SWL-local BSL vlogger or grassroots account was found anywhere** discussing NHS access. This means the earlier open question — "does the grassroots voice exist somewhere we haven't looked, or does it genuinely not exist yet" — now has a provisional answer: it does not yet exist in SWL-local, independent form. The practical implication has shifted from "search harder" to "the project may need to actively create this evidence base" — for example via an organised BSL-language community event with recorded video output, following the model of a Healthwatch Lancashire event that produced exactly this kind of record and led to an ongoing local ICB relationship. This remains a future decision point requiring its own ethical/consent framework before being built, not a current task, but it is now a considerably better-evidenced one.
