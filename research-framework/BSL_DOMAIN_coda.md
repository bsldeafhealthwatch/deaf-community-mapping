# Domain 19: CODA (Children of Deaf Adults) & Deaf-of-Deaf Children

**Last verified:** 15 September 2026
**Re-check cadence:** Annually, or when CODA UK & Ireland's camp cycle/dates change, or AIS implementation guidance is revised
**Confidence level:** Low-Medium — the structural/legal finding (no UK headcount exists for either population) is solid and independently reviewed; population figures are explicitly unresolved by design, not by oversight

---

## Summary

This domain covers two distinct, non-interchangeable populations: **hearing children of Deaf parents** (CODA, the common usage) and **Deaf children of Deaf parents** (Deaf-of-Deaf). No UK body counts either population. The two widely-quoted "90%" statistics — most children of Deaf parents are hearing, and most Deaf children have hearing parents — are both directionally accepted but neither is a UK-measured figure; the commonly-cited NDCS figure traces to a 2004 **US** paper (Mitchell & Karchmer) whose own authors argue the "mythical ten percent" is overstated. This domain is a cross-cut of AIS (family-as-interpreter), safeguarding, SEND, and family-support material already covered elsewhere in this framework — it is not a standalone service gap, and a proposed Domain 18-numbered standalone page was explicitly rejected on review (see Domain-numbering note below). The single sharpest policy question this domain raises is the family-as-interpreter one: what happens when a hearing child is used to interpret for a Deaf parent, where the honest answer is a stack of guidance levels (AIS 2025's "generally not," DHSC's "inappropriate," NHS Scotland's absolute "never under 16") rather than one national ban.

## Key Organisations & Actors

- **CODA UK & Ireland CIC** — the one small dedicated door for hearing children of Deaf parents (camp, events); **not** to be confused with Co-Dependents Anonymous UK (`codauk.org`), a different organisation with a similarly-abbreviated name
- **NDCS (National Deaf Children's Society)** — serves Deaf children of any parental hearing status; this is a Deaf-**child** door, not a CODA-specific product
- **BDA** — *BSL in Our Hands* campaign, aimed at hearing parents of newly-identified Deaf children, again not a CODA product
- **DHSC** — publishes migrant-health guidance describing use of a child as an interpreter for a parent as "inappropriate"
- **NHS Scotland / Public Health Scotland** — the one hard UK "never under 16" interpreting rule, Scotland-specific
- **Local child-protection procedures** (multiple UK local authorities) — state a child should "never" be used as an interpreter in child-protection work specifically

## Current Policy / Legal Position

There is no single statute banning the use of a child to interpret for a Deaf parent in England. What exists is a stack of guidance at different strengths: AIS 2025 implementation guidance states family/carers should *generally not* be relied on and professional support *must be offered*, but a recorded explicit preference can still stand (softer than an outright ban); DHSC migrant-health guidance calls the practice "inappropriate"; local child-protection procedures say a child should "never" be used in CP work specifically; the Equality Act 2010 s.20 duty sits on the *service*, requiring it to provide a professional; NHS Scotland's own policy is the one UK jurisdiction with an absolute "never under 16" rule. The US Americans with Disabilities Act (28 CFR § 35.160(c)) is a binding comparator in that jurisdiction but is not UK law and must not be imported as if it were.

Children and Families Act 2014 s.96's young-carer definition could plausibly stretch to cover language-brokering by a hearing child for a Deaf parent, but no national data series names the role, and one UK study (Napier & Ackroyd 2022) records a Deaf parent explicitly refusing a school's young-carer referral for their child on these grounds.

## Known Gaps & Contested Figures

- **No UK "number of CODAs" exists** — do not derive one from the Census 2021 BSL-main-language figure (22,000); that measures something different (people who report BSL as their main language, not parental hearing status of hearing children).
- **The two "90%" statistics are both international-derived or rule-of-thumb, not UK-measured** — NDCS's figure traces to Mitchell & Karchmer 2004 (a US paper whose own authors argue the figure is overstated); the "90% of CODAs are hearing" figure traces to Schein 1989, also not a Census result.
- **"Tens of thousands" (BBC coverage)** is journalistic colour, not a citable statistic.
- **No SEND/EHCP/Teacher-of-the-Deaf system treats Deaf-of-Deaf children as a separate identified pathway** — CRIDE does not collect parental hearing status, so this cannot currently be measured even if a pathway existed.
- **Whether hearing CODA children are treated as young carers under the Children and Families Act 2014 s.96 is unresolved** — no national data series names language-brokering as a recognised young-carer category, and at least one Deaf parent has explicitly refused such a referral for their child.

## Don't list (binding — reprinted from the source review for this file's own use)

- Don't print a UK "number of CODAs." None exists — don't derive one from the Census 2021 BSL-main-language figure.
- Don't print "tens of thousands" (BBC) as a statistic.
- Don't print NDCS's "more than 90%" figure as a measured UK percentage.
- Don't treat the Schein 1989 "90% hearing" rule of thumb as a Census result.
- Don't use CODA when the sentence is actually about a Deaf-of-Deaf child, or when it is about hearing parents of a Deaf child — none of these are interchangeable.
- Don't cite Co-Dependents Anonymous UK as if it were the CODA organisation.
- Don't write that using a child to interpret for a Deaf parent is "illegal" or "against safeguarding law" in England — the actual stack is AIS "generally not" → DHSC "inappropriate" → local CP "never" → Scotland NHS "never under 16" (the one hard UK rule) → US ADA (comparator only, not UK law).
- Don't write "AIS bans family interpreters" — softer than that; professional support must be offered but a recorded explicit preference can still stand.
- Don't write that hearing CODA children are automatically young carers under the Children and Families Act 2014 s.96 — plausible, unmeasured, and at least one Deaf parent has explicitly refused such a referral.
- Don't write that SEND/EHCP/Teacher-of-the-Deaf practice treats Deaf-of-Deaf children as a separate pathway — CRIDE doesn't collect this data.
- Don't present US KODA camps, Paul Preston's 1995 research, or the 2021 film *CODA* as descriptions of the NHS or English SEND system.
- Don't hold any page built from this domain out as a children's service, a safeguarding referral route, or legal advice.
