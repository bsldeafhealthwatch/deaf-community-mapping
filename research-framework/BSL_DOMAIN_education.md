# Domain 15: Education, Early Years to Higher Education

**Last verified:** 4 September 2026 (BSL GCSE timeline updated 6 September 2026 — see below; a materially worse development than anything previously recorded)
**Re-check cadence:** Quarterly — the BSL GCSE timeline and Teacher of the Deaf apprenticeship funding are both actively moving
**Confidence level:** High on workforce decline and DSA mechanics (primary-sourced); Medium on BSL GCSE timeline (genuinely contested/moving, flagged as such rather than resolved)

---

## Summary

This domain was missing from the original framework entirely — a genuine gap, not a deprioritisation. It matters because education access sits upstream of nearly everything else in this project: the language deprivation cited as a root cause of poor health literacy in Domain 1's own statistics traces directly back to early-years and school-age failures to give Deaf children full language access. Three storylines dominate current evidence: a collapsing Teacher of the Deaf workforce now hit by a fresh 2026 funding policy change; a BSL GCSE promised in 2018 whose delivery timeline has repeatedly slipped; and a Disabled Students' Allowance system in higher education that, per the Deaf children's own charity's case study, can fail BSL users through basic access oversights (offering only remote needs assessments to a BSL user who needed one face-to-face).

## Key Organisations & Actors

- **National Deaf Children's Society (NDCS)** — the primary campaigning voice on Teacher of the Deaf workforce numbers and the BSL GCSE; publishes both the workforce statistics and direct case studies of DSA system failures
- **Consortium for Research into Deaf Education (CRIDE)** — the research body producing the underlying workforce-decline data NDCS and others cite; worth treating as the primary data source to verify directly rather than relying on secondary reporting of its findings
- **British Deaf Association (BDA)** — the most vocal critic of the BSL GCSE's delivery timeline, explicitly framing the delay as a Deaf-led-workforce issue ("who will teach our language to the highest quality if not Deaf people who are fluent in it?")
- **Signature** — the UK's leading awarding body for BSL qualifications, involved in BSL GCSE subject-content development since the original 2018 commitment
- **Ofqual** and the **Department for Education** — the regulatory and policy bodies responsible for the BSL GCSE's approval and rollout; per BDA's account, engagement with the Deaf community on this has been limited ("almost everything has happened behind closed doors")
- **Student Finance England / Wales / the devolved equivalents** — administer DSA; note this is a **devolved** system, so England, Wales, Scotland and NI figures and rules differ and should never be conflated
- **Individual local authorities** — bear the funding burden for Teacher of the Deaf training where Apprenticeship Levy funding has been withdrawn, on top of already "overstretched budgets" per NDCS's direct account

## Current Policy / Legal Position

- **SEND and Alternative Provision Improvement Plan (2023)** — the last government's plan explicitly acknowledged the importance of Teachers of the Deaf and promised to increase numbers; the funding mechanism built to deliver on that promise (a new Level 7 apprenticeship route) has since been undermined by a separate 2026 Apprenticeship Levy policy change — a clear case of one government policy commitment being quietly reversed by an unrelated funding rule change
- **BSL GCSE** — committed to by the Department for Education in 2018, following a proposal from Signature and sustained campaigning by NDCS and Deaf young campaigners (notably Daniel Jillings). As of the most recent material found, Ofqual has finalised subject content and rules (per Dec 2025 sources), but the actual first-teaching and first-certification dates remain unresolved — see gaps
- **Disabled Students' Allowance** — funded and administered on a devolved basis; needs-assessed (not means-tested), can fund BSL interpreters, notetakers, and specialist equipment in higher education. Under the Equality Act 2010, universities may be required to top up support where DSA's maximum is insufficient — an enforcement mechanism that depends on individual students and disability advisers knowing to invoke it

## ⚠️ Known Gaps & Contested Figures

**BSL GCSE timeline is genuinely unresolved, not just under-researched — and, per a 6 September 2026 follow-up pass, has moved considerably further and worse than the sequence below on its own would suggest.** The evidence found in the original session shows a moving target: 2018 commitment → 2025 original "first teaching" target → BDA's 2025 prediction of a slip to 2027 → a reported Ofqual acknowledgement that certification might not happen before 2029 → then, by December 2025, sources describing Ofqual's "final rules" (confirmed 14 Nov 2025) as a "historic step forward." **The follow-up pass found that within a month of those rules being finalised, all four exam boards that could have delivered the qualification declined to do so:** AQA and Pearson (10 Dec 2025), Eduqas (11 Dec 2025, open to revisiting), and Cambridge OCR (12 Dec 2025, "not feasible for us to develop a specification"). Finalising rules turned out not to be a precondition satisfied on the way to teaching — it was followed immediately by the collapse of the delivery route entirely. Signature, previously a subject-content contributor rather than an exam board, is now (as of a board decision in January 2026) attempting to become an Ofqual-recognised awarding organisation itself, essentially restarting the qualification's development from an earlier stage than "final rules" implied. Its executive director's most recent statement (16 March 2026) gives an aim of **first teaching by 2030** — later than every previous figure in this domain's sequence, including the 2029 certification date once treated as the worst case. **Do not state a specific BSL GCSE start date as settled fact.** If a single figure must be cited, **2030 (Signature's stated aim for first teaching, contingent on Ofqual recognition not yet achieved)** is the current best-available figure — but this domain's own re-check discipline (quarterly, given how fast this has moved even within a single quarter) should be followed literally here, not treated as a formality.

**Quarterly re-check (7 Sept 2026): no public status change since the 16 March 2026 statement — a genuine negative result, not a gap in this domain's research.** Checked Signature's own news feed directly (which lists items through 1 September 2026, including unrelated items as recent as that date), Signature's "GCSE in BSL" qualification page (last substantively updated per its own content 18 February 2026, itself already stale relative to March), and independent press (Liam O'Dell, the most consistent tracker of this story) for anything after March 2026. The most recent Signature/BSL-GCSE-adjacent item found is a 15 May 2026 piece quoting BSL Advisory Board co-chair Dr Joanna Atkinson, which restates the March position ("Signature confirmed... it would work to achieve recognition") without reporting any progress, application submission, or Ofqual response since. **No evidence was found that Signature has formally submitted an Ofqual recognition application, that Ofqual has responded, or that the 2030 first-teaching aim has moved in either direction.** Ofqual's own Register of Regulated Qualifications could not be checked directly this pass (robots.txt blocks automated fetching) — a genuine tooling gap, not a finding; a manual check or FOI to Ofqual would close this properly. Given the six-month silence from Signature's own channels on a topic they were actively publicising as recently as March, the most likely explanation is that the promised feasibility study is still underway with nothing yet to report — consistent with Foster's own December 2025 characterisation of the process as "incredibly complicated, expensive and challenging" — rather than an active but unreported update. Re-check again next quarter (~Dec 2026).

**CRIDE report citation needs firming up.** The "one in five posts lost" figure is well-attested via secondary reporting (CYP Now) but the CRIDE report itself has not been opened directly this session — the exact publication year and full dataset should be verified against CRIDE's own publication before this becomes a headline citation in external-facing material.

**DSA figures are devolved and inconsistently reported.** Sources found this session gave different DSA maximums (£34,671 for Wales 2026–27; an older, likely outdated £25,575 figure from a 2023-dated commercial source; a truncated £27,78[3] figure from Disability Rights UK with no clear nation/year attached). **England's current DSA maximum for the current award year has not been confirmed from a primary source this session** — this is a clean, fast FOI/direct-check target given how directly it affects Deaf higher-education students in the project's own SWL footprint.

**No data found this session on:**

- EHCP (Education, Health and Care Plan) processing times or dispute/appeal rates specifically for Deaf children
- Newborn hearing screening programme coverage and early-intervention pathway timeliness
- Further education (post-16, pre-university) support arrangements — DSA does not apply to FE colleges, which per NDCS's own guidance are separately responsible for organising and funding support, and no figures on how well colleges do this were found

## Money Trail

- The Teacher of the Deaf funding story is a clean, well-evidenced example of the project's "waste vs. real services" lens: a funded, planned training pipeline (the Level 7 apprenticeship, fundable via the Apprenticeship Levy) was undermined by an unrelated Treasury/skills-policy change restricting Levy use for over-21 Level 7 apprentices — precisely the population Teacher of the Deaf trainees come from, since the qualification requires already being a fully qualified teacher. This is not a case of money being wasted through overhead or agency margin; it's a case of one policy lever cancelling out another government's own stated commitment.
- **DSA tuition/support money is real and substantial** (£34,671/year maximum in Wales for 2026–27) but the NDCS case study shows the money being undermined by process failures (no face-to-face assessment slots available for a BSL user who needed one) rather than by underfunding — a distinct failure mode from the funding-cut stories elsewhere in this project, worth keeping analytically separate
