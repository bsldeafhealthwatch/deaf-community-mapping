# Domain 4: DWP — PIP & DLA (Assessment, Award Rates, Appeals, Deaf-Specific Issues)

**Last verified:** 4 September 2026
**Re-check cadence:** Quarterly, or on any Timms Review milestone (interim report Jul 2026; final review due autumn 2026)
**Confidence level:** Low-Medium — the central, most policy-relevant questions (under-award rates, appeal success rates for Deaf/BSL claimants) have **no published official figure at all**; what exists is mostly process description

---

## Summary

Personal Independence Payment (PIP) and Disability Living Allowance (DLA, for children) are the core disability benefits most directly affecting Deaf and DeafBlind claimants' income. Unlike Access to Work (Domain 5), PIP eligibility criteria are statutory and points-based rather than discretionary — but there is **no statutory guarantee of a BSL interpreter at assessment**; provision runs through Equality Act 2010 reasonable-adjustment requests and assessment-provider processes (video relay services via Capita HAAS, DWP's own VRS/Relay UK). Most strikingly for this project's evidence base: there is no official DWP data breaking out PIP award rates, under-award patterns, or appeal/tribunal outcomes by deafness or BSL use at all — this absence is itself the headline finding of this domain pass. A live government review (the Timms Review) is examining how sensory impairments are captured in the PIP assessment, with a final report due autumn 2026 and no PIP design changes promised before then.

## Current Policy / Legal Position

PIP eligibility itself is set out in statute (Welfare Reform Act 2012 and associated regulations) via a points-based functional assessment — this is structurally different from Access to Work's discretionary grant model (Domain 5). Communication-support needs are assessed under Activity 9 ("communicating verbally") in the standard PIP descriptors, which advocacy sources argue is poorly suited to capturing BSL-first communication needs — but this is a practitioner critique, not a documented statistical failure rate. There is no PIP-specific statutory right to a BSL interpreter at assessment; access runs through the general Equality Act 2010 reasonable-adjustments duty, mirroring the pattern already found in Domain 13 (justice) and Domain 1 (NHS) — a recurring structural theme across this whole research framework where BSL Act 2022 recognition (Domain 7) does not itself create enforceable service-level rights, and each domain instead falls back on the same general-purpose Equality Act duty applied inconsistently.

The "4-point rule" — a proposed additional eligibility threshold requiring at least one 4-point score on a single PIP activity — was removed from the UC and PIP Bill on 1 July 2025 before becoming law, so it never took effect. The Timms Review, with its interim report in July 2026 and final report due autumn 2026, is the live process most likely to change how sensory/communication needs are assessed — DWP has stated no PIP design changes will happen before the final review lands.

## Known Gaps & Contested Figures

- **The single biggest gap in this domain: no official DWP data exists breaking out PIP award rates, under-award patterns, or appeal/tribunal outcomes by deafness or BSL use.** This should be treated as a headline finding for the project's own site, not just a research note — it directly parallels Domain 13's finding that no modern national headcount of Deaf prisoners exists, and Domain 5's finding of no BSL-only AtW spend figure. A pattern is forming across this project: statutory/administrative systems that affect Deaf people do not disaggregate their outcomes data by deafness or BSL use, making it structurally impossible to prove or disprove discrimination through official statistics alone.
- **Independently reconfirmed 8 September 2026** via a dedicated national-DWP research pass (prompts 20-26 in `SuperGrok_Research_Prompt_Pack.md`): PIP official statistics publish hearing and visual descriptors/points but not "BSL user" or "DeafBlind" as claimant categories — same conclusion as above, reached via a separate search. Full write-up of that pass's interpreter-provision-at-assessment findings (contractor, VRS routing, tribunal/Ombudsman record) now lives in Domain 5 (`BSL_DOMAIN_access_to_work.md`, "National DWP Accessibility" section) rather than being duplicated here, since PIP/ESA/UC assessment booking is a shared DWP-wide process, not PIP-specific. The equivalent Jobcentre Plus in-person picture (distinct from assessment-centre booking) is Domain 18 (`BSL_DOMAIN_jobcentre_plus.md`).
- Whether Capita HAAS's SignVideo VRS route is genuinely reliable in practice (uptake rates, wait times, complaint rates) was not found in this pass — worth a direct FOI/request to Capita HAAS or DWP.
- The Timms Review interim report's exact findings on sensory impairment assessment have not been read in full this session — only referenced via secondary reporting.

## Money Trail

- No AtW-style spend figures apply to PIP/DLA in the same way — PIP is a statutory cash benefit paid to claimants, not a scheme funding third-party support services, so the "money trail" question here is less about interpreter budgets and more about whether Deaf claimants are being correctly assessed and awarded the benefit they are entitled to. The absence of disaggregated award-rate data (above) means it is currently impossible to quantify whether Deaf/BSL claimants are systemically under-awarded in cash terms.
