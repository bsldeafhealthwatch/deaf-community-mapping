## Summary

This domain maps the professional infrastructure BSL interpretation depends on: the regulators (NRCPD, SRLPDC in Scotland, RBSLI as an independent voluntary register), and the severe supply shortage relative to Deaf BSL user population. The headline finding — a ratio of roughly 1 registered interpreter to every 60 Deaf BSL users — recurs across multiple domains in this project (health, justice, benefits, education) as the underlying resource constraint behind almost every access failure documented elsewhere. Deafblind interpreter supply is a specific, acute sub-crisis: NRCPD's own register lists only 19 nationally (1 in London), while a June 2026 government funding announcement cited a starting figure of just 8, with a funded target of 68 — the two official-adjacent figures for the same population do not agree, and both are far below evident need.

## Key Organisations & Actors

- **NRCPD (National Registers of Communication Professionals working with Deaf and Deafblind people)** — the primary UK regulator/register for England, Wales and Northern Ireland; the register most frequently specified as the required standard in procurement and advocacy recommendations
- **SRLPDC (Scottish Register of Language Professionals working with Deaf and Deafblind People)** — Scotland's equivalent register, publishing its own annual accounts with registrant figures
- **RBSLI** — an independent voluntary register, accepted on some (not all) public-sector frameworks alongside NRCPD/SRLPDC; notably the register with no published headcount
- **BSL Advisory Board** — via *Locked Out*, the source recommending statutory NRCPD-only procurement guidelines and an end to family/child interpreting
- **RNID** — source of the 1,500-interpreter/87,000-BSL-user prevalence estimate

---

## Current Policy / Legal Position

There is no single statutory requirement mandating which register(s) a public body must use when commissioning a BSL interpreter — practice varies by framework and is currently a mixed-register model in most public procurement found in this pass (NRCPD and/or RBSLI and/or SRLPDC). The *Locked Out* report's call for statutory guidelines requiring NRCPD-only qualified interpreters, and an explicit ban on using children or family members as interpreters, is a live policy recommendation rather than current law — this is a concrete, trackable "ask" for advocacy purposes, distinct from the many domains in this project where the honest finding is simply an absence of data.

## Known Gaps & Contested Figures

- **Deafblind interpreter count: 19 (NRCPD register) vs. 8 (government announcement)** — both are described as current, official-adjacent figures for the same population, and they do not agree. This exact contested figure also appears in Domain 3 (DeafBlind-specific provision) and Domain 11 (DeafBlind population figures) — needs resolving against NRCPD's own methodology (are the 19 all currently active/available, while 8 was a narrower "currently taking work" figure?) before either number is used as a headline claim.
- **No published RBSLI headcount** — a genuine open question given RBSLI's role in public procurement; without knowing its size, it's impossible to assess whether it meaningfully expands the interpreter pool or how it compares in scale to NRCPD.
- **No published evidence, positive or negative, on "qualification-shopping"** — the concern that agencies might lean on RBSLI-registered (rather than NRCPD-registered) interpreters to fill gaps more cheaply or easily is plausible given the supply shortage, but nothing in this pass proves or disproves it happening systematically.
- **Population denominators are contested across sources**: 87,000 deaf BSL users (RNID/BDA) vs. 151,000 total BSL users (BDA) vs. 22,000 people in England & Wales reporting BSL as main language (2021 Census) — these measure different things (fluent BSL users vs. all BSL users vs. main-language BSL users) and should never be treated as interchangeable in public-facing material.

## Money Trail

- £15,000 funding for Deafblind interpreter competency-assessment weekends (delivered via Signature and NRCPD), tied to the 23 Jun 2026 announcement targeting growth from 8 to 68 Deafblind interpreters — this is a very small sum relative to the scale of the stated ambition (a more than 8-fold increase in registered Deafblind interpreters), worth flagging as a value-for-money question in its own right
- No broader interpreter-workforce funding figures (e.g. training bursaries, NRCPD registration subsidy schemes) were found in this pass
