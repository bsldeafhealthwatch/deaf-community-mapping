## Summary

Deaf and DeafBlind people's access to end-of-life and palliative care in the UK sits inside the Accessible Information Standard (AIS) — which already requires BSL and Deafblind interpreters "promptly and without unreasonable delay" in every NHS and NHS-funded setting, including hospices — but AIS has no separate terminally-ill protocol, and enforcement is complaint-led. Two Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman (PHSO) findings name Deaf patients specifically at the end of life (Alan Graham, published 11 Feb 2026; Mrs W, 20 May 2015), plus one CQC penalty (University Hospitals Birmingham, £8,000, Oct 2022) for a Deaf patient who later died. No published CQC hospice inspection, and no published DWP or PHSO finding, names a Deaf or DeafBlind patient specifically in a hospice or Special Rules for End of Life (SREL) context — the DWP gap is structural, since 99% of SREL claims are awarded without a face-to-face assessment, meaning "interpreter at assessment" is rarely the live issue.

The DeafBlind-specific evidence base is thinner still, to the point of near-absence: no UK hospice or NHS palliative protocol exists for tactile BSL, Deafblind Manual, or social-haptic communication with a dying patient, and the national workforce (RIDB — Registered Interpreter for Deafblind People) sits at just 19 nationally (1 in London; zero in South West, East Midlands, and Yorkshire & Humber), against a government funding target of 68. International practice offers small, local models (Minnesota, Ottawa, Jacksonville, France, Germany) rather than a measured evidence base — no country in scope runs a documented tactile/RIDB last-days service.

This domain was compiled from a four-prompt Grok research batch (8 September 2026), converted into a working brief, and spot-verified against primary sources before being folded into the dossier. See the companion FOI log (`foi-tracking/EOL-Palliative-FOI-companion-log.xlsx`) for the local-SWL discovery questions this domain's national findings still need answered.

## Key Organisations & Actors

- **NHS England / CQC** — AIS is the governing standard; CQC enforces only indirectly via fundamental standards (dignity, consent, safety), not a direct AIS audit function
- **PHSO (Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman)** — the only body that has published named end-of-life findings for Deaf patients (Alan Graham 2026; Mrs W 2015)
- **DWP** — administers SREL/PIP; holds little case-level interpreter-failure data by structural design (accepted SR1 claims skip assessment)
- **Royal Trinity Hospice** and **St Helena Hospice** — the two English hospices with published Deaf-access practice (Hearing Loss Working Group / Hospice UK award; BSL Live/24-7 VRI respectively) — neither is a tactile/DeafBlind solution
- **BSL Advisory Board / Dr Joanna Atkinson** — authors of *Locked Out* (27 Nov 2025), the main UK advocacy document naming the DeafBlind end-of-life gap and recommending in-person interpretation for bedridden/EOL patients (Recommendation 8)
- **RAD/ASLI** — professional bodies whose pandemic-era guidance (still circulating) states remote interpreting is "unlikely to be suitable for end of life care communication"

## Current Policy / Legal Position

AIS (issued under s.250, Health and Social Care Act 2012) applies to every NHS provider/commissioner and most NHS-funded independent hospices, requiring registered (NRCPD/RBSLI) BSL and Deafblind interpreters "promptly." The Equality Act 2010 anticipatory reasonable-adjustment duty sits underneath, and PHSO treated both as the governing standard in the Alan Graham finding. DWP's SREL test (from 3 April 2023: death reasonably expected within 12 months, widened from 6) is evidenced by an SR1 form and bypasses assessment entirely for 99% of claimants — meaning DWP-side interpreter access is a non-issue for the accepted majority, but leaves the earlier (prognosis conversation) and later (register office, coroner) stages as the actual points of failure, per *Locked Out*'s anonymised MND case study.

**Independent hospices that take no NHS money sit outside AIS and outside the Freedom of Information Act** (they are charities, not public authorities) unless a commissioning contract pulls them in — this is a live gap for any FOI-based follow-up work.

## Known Gaps & Contested Figures

- No published national AIS compliance rate for patients on an end-of-life pathway; no Trust-level BSL fulfilment series for palliative wards; no CQC "never event" for rapid-decline interpreting failure
- No published hospice-sector BSL fulfilment rate for unplanned/rapid-decline (out-of-hours) episodes; no national one-hour emergency face-to-face standard
- No published DWP case, FOI series, or PHSO finding names a Deaf or DeafBlind SREL claimant with an interpreter failure at DWP assessment — structural absence, not evidence of no problem
- **DeafBlind evidence base is thin to the point of absence**: no named UK PHSO, CQC, coroner, or inquest case is DeafBlind + dying + tactile-communication-failure specifically. Locked Out's MND case study is a Deaf BSL husband, not DeafBlind — do not relabel it
- No UK published last-days pain-assessment tool, consent protocol, or "residual code" (a minimal touch-based yes/no system) for a dying tactile-communication user whose hands can no longer sign
- *Locked Out*'s specific quotes ("hospice care is not a peaceful option...", Recommendation 8 text) have not yet been independently re-confirmed word-for-word against the full report — flagged as Medium confidence pending that check
- No large international quantitative series measures hospice outcomes for Deaf or DeafBlind patients specifically; comparative studies (Cerilli 2023, Maddalena 2012, Carpenter 2020, Bright 2023) are small, mostly US/Canada/Germany, and rarely DeafBlind-specific

## Money Trail

- RIDB competency-assessment grant: **£15,000** (23 Jun 2026), delivered via Signature and NRCPD — funds assessment weekends adding successful candidates to the existing register, not new training places
- No independent NHS or hospice-sector costing of end-of-life BSL/Deafblind access gaps was found (distinct from the general £80-100m NHS accessibility-gap estimate covered in Domain 1, which is a charity estimate, not a measured EOL-specific cost)
- PHSO compensation in the Alan Graham finding: £900 to each of two grandchildren, £750 to his daughter — redress for distress caused, not a systemic funding fix

## FOI Targets Arising

Full templates and a live tracker are in `foi-tracking/EOL-Palliative-FOI-companion-log.xlsx` (Templates T7 and T8). Summary asks:

- SWL acute/community trusts and named hospices (Royal Trinity, Princess Alice, St Raphael's, Shooting Star, plus any ICB-funded hospice-at-home) — RIDB / communicator-guide / Deafblind Manual / hands-on BSL bookings, last 24 months, by setting and urgency (planned/same-day/out-of-hours), fulfilment and cancellation
- Same trusts/hospices — whether a written last-days tactile/haptics protocol exists; whether any English hospice has ever booked an RIDB for an inpatient death, and for how many hours
- SWL trusts/hospices — whether communication support is recorded as being in place during the prognosis conversation preceding an SR1 form
- DWP — whether SR1/SREL records flag BSL/Deafblind communication needs at all; BSL VRS call volumes for SREL claims; recorded complaints about interpreter refusal at the register office or coroner stage

**Note:** independent hospices receiving no NHS funding are not public authorities under FOIA — Template T7 sends these as direct requests instead, without expecting the statutory 20-working-day clock.
