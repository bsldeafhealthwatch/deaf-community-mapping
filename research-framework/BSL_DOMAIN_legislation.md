## Summary

This domain is the legal backbone the rest of the project keeps referring back to. Its single most important finding — already anticipated in Domain 13 (Victims & Prisoners) — is that the **British Sign Language Act 2022 recognises BSL as a language but does not itself create any enforceable individual right to BSL provision in any specific service.** Individual, tribunal-enforceable rights to reasonable adjustments and non-discrimination continue to come from the Equality Act 2010, not the BSL Act. The BSL Act's substantive duties are narrow and administrative: a Secretary of State reporting duty on *government departments'* own public-facing communications (not communications with individual members of the public), and a duty to issue non-binding guidance. This distinction — recognition versus enforceable right — is the single most important legal fact this project needs to get right, because it is repeatedly the honest answer to "why doesn't the BSL Act just fix this."

---

## Key Provisions (BSL Act 2022 — direct from statute text)

- **Section 1(1):** "British Sign Language is recognised as a language of England, Wales and Scotland."
- **Section 1(2):** "Subsection (1) does not affect the operation of any enactment or rule of law." — the explicit statutory statement that recognition does not rewrite the Equality Act 2010 or create a new justiciable service right.
- **Section 2:** duty on the Secretary of State to publish a report on what *listed UK ministerial departments* have done to promote or facilitate BSL in **public** communications (announcements, plans, consultations, press/social/web) — explicitly excludes communications with individual members of the public. Reporting period after the first report may be up to three years. The fourth report (May 2025–Apr 2026) was published 15 Jul 2026.
- **Section 3:** Secretary of State must issue (or arrange) **guidance** on promoting and facilitating BSL. Guidance "may for example" include best-practice advice and case studies — it does not impose duties on NHS trusts, local authorities, courts, or other public bodies.
- **Section 4:** the Act extends to **England and Wales and Scotland only** — not Northern Ireland.

## Current Policy / Legal Position

The layered legal picture for BSL users in the UK, from broadest to narrowest:

1. **Equality Act 2010** — the actual enforceable floor: reasonable adjustments duty and protection from discrimination, applying across employment, services, health, justice, education. This is the statute doing the real legal work in every other domain of this project (health, justice, benefits, employment).
2. **BSL Act 2022** (England, Wales, Scotland) — symbolic/administrative recognition plus a narrow government-department reporting and guidance duty. Explicitly does not override or add to the Equality Act.
3. **BSL (Scotland) Act 2015** — a genuinely more substantive Scotland-only statute requiring national and listed-authority BSL plans, which is a materially stronger duty than anything in the 2022 UK-wide Act.
4. **BSL (Wales) Bill**, passed by the Senedd 11 Mar 2026 — a new, Wales-specific instrument; its substantive content relative to the Scotland Act and the 2022 UK Act has not been reviewed in this pass and is a clear next step.
5. **Human Rights Act 1998 and Care Act 2014** — named in this domain's original scope but not substantively researched in this pass; flagged as an open gap below.
