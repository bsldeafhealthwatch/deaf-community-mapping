## Summary

Four organisations anchor the Deaf-sector third sector landscape this project already engages with via the stakeholder matrix: RNID (broad hearing-loss remit, largest income, not exclusively BSL-user led), SignHealth (Deaf healthcare access and mental health, Deaf-led, heavily NHS-contract-funded and running a deficit), BDA (the historic Deaf-led national campaigning organisation, smallest of the four by income), and Action Deafness (Deaf-led regional service delivery, now absorbing deafPLUS via an April 2026 merger). Each charity's spending mix (raising funds vs. charitable activities) and government-contract dependence differ sharply — SignHealth is by far the most government-contract-dependent (£6.875m across 94 contracts, 94.6% of spend going to charitable activities), while RNID has the lowest charitable-activities share of the four (68.6%) alongside the largest raising-funds cost base. These figures are useful for coordination and funding-bid framing (Domain 8 of the framework's Audience Map already flags this as a served-but-worth-strengthening audience need) but should be read as a neutral financial comparison, not an efficiency ranking, absent further context on each charity's actual service model. A second pass adds the wider funding-landscape context: Disability Rights UK's Funding Justice campaign shows government contract money in the disability sector overwhelmingly favours large, non-DPO-led charities (£460m vs. £12.5m to DPOs in 2022-23), and sizing the four named Deaf charities' combined government income (≈£10.8m) against a large comparator like the National Autistic Society (£80.70m) is a legitimate, sourced argument for funding bids — provided it is never presented as an official cross-condition budget allocation, since no such table exists anywhere in government data.

## Funding Landscape & Comparator Charities

A separate question from any one charity's own accounts (above) is how Deaf-sector funding compares to other impairment groups, and whether government/NHS money is reaching Deaf-led organisations specifically. **Bottom line: there is no published UK government or NHS budget line that totals "Deaf-specific third-sector funding" and compares it, per head of need, with other impairment groups.** What exists instead is (a) Disability Rights UK's *Funding Justice* analysis, which splits government contract spend between large non-DPO disability charities and DPOs/DDPOs using 2022-23 data; (b) Charity Commission government-contract/grant figures for named Deaf and comparator charities through 2024-25; and (c) proxy public spend such as Access to Work (Domain 5). These can support a funding bid, but only if not presented as an official "Deaf vs. autism vs. sight-loss allocation" — no such table exists.

### What a funding bid can and cannot say

**Can say, with sources:**
1. Funding Justice shows government *contract* money in the disability sector is highly concentrated in a small number of large, non-DPO-led charities (£460m vs. £12.5m in 2022-23). Deaf-led organisations that meet the DDPO test sit on the thin side of that split.
2. Among named national Deaf charities, SignHealth is the outlier — most of its income is government contracts for care, therapy and domestic-abuse work. RNID and BDA receive comparatively little government contract income and depend on donations/legacies/Lottery funding — a structural risk for campaigning and BSL-community infrastructure specifically.
3. A single large autism charity (NAS) received about £80.7m in government contracts/grants in 2024-25 — roughly 7-8 times the combined government income of RNID + SignHealth + BDA + Action Deafness in their latest filed years. This is a scale comparison of named charities, not proof of a DHSC "autism vs. Deaf" budget allocation.
4. There are documented local withdrawals of Deaf-led specialist provision (Edinburgh, 2025-26).
5. Access to Work shows government does spend heavily on deaf/HoH workplace support (£90m/28% of AtW spend, per Domain 5), but that money does not substitute for core grants to Deaf charities — it funds individual workplace support, not organisational capacity.

**Cannot say from current sources:**
- "Government/NHS allocated £X to Deaf charities and £Y to other disability groups in 2025-26." That table does not exist anywhere found.
- "Deaf services are under-funded by Z% relative to population share." The population denominators conflict (22k/87k/151k/9m depending on definition) and there is no official need-weighted allocation formula to compare against.
- That Funding Justice's £460m/£12.5m split is a 2025-26 outturn or a Deaf-specific finding — it is 2022-23, all-disability, DPO vs. non-DPO, and does not isolate Deaf organisations at all.

**Practical bid framing:** ask funders to resource Deaf-led/DDPO capacity (BDA, Action Deafness, local Deaf societies) precisely because public contracts flow disproportionately to large mixed-disability or health providers, while BSL-first advice, advocacy and community infrastructure remain grant-dependent and locally fragile.

## ✅ New (5 Sept 2026): Live Case Study — Deaf Action Edinburgh Service Withdrawal

Directly retrieved from Deaf Action's own campaign page, "Protect Deaf Services Edinburgh" (Scotland, not England, but a directly relevant, current, concrete example of the "local authority funding withdrawal" risk this project has flagged in the abstract elsewhere — see Domain 12). City of Edinburgh Council has, over an 18-month period, withdrawn three specialist deaf-led services: a **Deaf Social Worker** (cut Sept 2024), a **Specialist Equipment Service** for deaf-specific alerting devices (cut March 2025), and a **Community Care Assistant Service** providing preventative deaf-led support (cut November 2025). Deaf Action's own framing is directly relevant to this project's cost-of-inaction argument (see Domain 5's Business Case section): it explicitly argues that removing deaf-led preventative support risks **shifting costs onto NHS and statutory services** rather than genuinely saving money, since deaf residents are now referred to generic services without BSL access or Deaf-cultural competence. The campaign's asks are reinstatement, deaf-led alternatives, a transparent equality impact assessment, and co-design with the deaf community. **This is a real-time, ongoing example (not resolved as of this research pass)** and should be watched for outcome — a useful concrete case study for advocacy material illustrating the "waste vs. grassroots" and "cost of inaction" arguments with a live, named example rather than only modelled figures.

## Known Gaps & Contested Figures

- **No post-merger combined accounts exist yet for Action Deafness + deafPLUS.** The merger took effect 16 Apr 2026; Action Deafness's own YE 30 Sep 2024 figures above pre-date it entirely, and deafPLUS's own last accounts (YE 31 Mar 2024, £835k income) are now over two years stale relative to today's date. The first genuinely informative combined financial picture will not exist until Action Deafness's next accounts filing (expected after 30 Sep 2026).
- **RNID's "raising funds" cost (31.4% of spend) is notably higher than the other three charities** (5.4%, 6.8%, near-zero for Action Deafness) — this is presented as a neutral fact here, not an efficiency judgement, since RNID's income base and fundraising model differ structurally from the other three (much larger, broader-remit organisation with different fundraising channels). Any public-facing comparison should avoid implying inefficiency without further context on what each charity's fundraising spend actually buys.
- These figures were not independently re-opened against the Charity Commission register this session — see Verification Status for the upgrade path (each is a short, direct Charity Commission lookup).
- **No published UK government or NHS total exists for "Deaf-specific third-sector funding," and none of the available proxies (Funding Justice, individual charity accounts, Access to Work) can be combined into one. Funding Justice's £460m/£12.5m split is 2022-23, all-disability, and does not isolate Deaf organisations at all — using it as if it were a Deaf-specific or current-year figure would misrepresent the source.**
- Population/need denominators for any per-head funding comparison are contested (22,000 Census main-language BSL users vs. 87,000 BDA-estimated deaf BSL users vs. 151,000 total BSL users vs. 9 million RNID working-age hearing-loss figure) — there is no official need-weighted allocation formula across impairment groups, so no "under-funded relative to population share" percentage can currently be stated from a primary source.

## Money Trail

This domain **is** the money trail for the third-sector actors in this project. Key observations:

- SignHealth is running at a deficit (£9.736m income vs £10.397m expenditure) while being the most government-contract-dependent of the four (94 contracts, £6.875m) — worth watching for financial sustainability risk given its central role in Deaf mental health and justice-adjacent services (InterpreterNow) documented elsewhere in this project.
- BDA, the historically oldest and most explicitly Deaf-led campaigning organisation, has by far the smallest income (£2.478m) and lowest government-contract value (£29k) of the four — meaning the organisation most focused on rights-based advocacy is also the most resource-constrained, a relevant fact when weighing which organisations to partner with or cite for campaigning credibility versus service-delivery capacity.
- Action Deafness's near-zero "raising funds" cost (£3k) alongside £1.537m in government contracts suggests a service-delivery-first funding model with minimal independent fundraising infrastructure — worth confirming this reading is accurate rather than an artifact of accounts categorisation.
- Sized against comparator charities: National Autistic Society's £80.70m in government contracts/grants (2024-25) is roughly 7-8 times the combined government income of RNID + SignHealth + BDA + Action Deafness in their latest filed years (≈£10.8m) — a genuine scale difference worth using in funding bids, but only as a named-charity comparison, never as evidence of an official cross-condition budget allocation, since no such allocation table exists.
- RNIB's £6.70m in government contracts/grants (7.0% of its £95.88m income) shows a legacy-heavy funding model closer to RNID's and BDA's pattern than to SignHealth's or NAS's contract-dependent model — useful context when arguing that Deaf-led organisations are not uniquely reliant on donations relative to comparable disability charities.
