## 1. Recruitment & Hiring-Stage Access (Grok prompts 103–108)

**Independently reviewed and spot-checked 14 September 2026.**

**Headline:** An employer or recruitment agency must adjust the recruitment process for a disabled applicant — including a BSL or DeafBlind interpreter at interview — unless not reasonable on the facts (Equality Act ss.20, 39, 55). Access to Work's Communication Support at Interview (CSI) is a separate, candidate-applied, 100%-funded fast-track; it does not move or discharge the employer's duty. General pre-offer health questions are banned except for named exceptions (s.60); asking whether someone needs an adjustment for *this interview* is one of those exceptions.

**Spot-checked live, both holding:**
- GOV.UK CSI page (last updated 8 Oct 2025) — candidate applies before interview, 100% of approved costs, no cost-share, paid after the interview, 2-working-day decision aim.
- ***Murphy v Sheffield Hallam University*** (EAT/6/99, [1999] UKEAT 0006_19_1101, 11 Jan 2000) — independently verified against the primary BAILII transcript (not just a secondary case note), 14 September 2026. Disability Discrimination Act 1995 (not Equality Act 2010): university unlawfully discriminated against a profoundly deaf applicant by failing to arrange an interpreter for his 16 December 1997 interview; **£2,500** awarded. Interview was adjourned and completed 18 December 1997 with an interpreter ("a highly competent amateur signer") present. A separate claim that he was not offered the job because of his disability **failed** at tribunal and on appeal — his disability was found not to be an effective cause of the selection decision. Now fully citable with this citation attached.

**Don't list (binding):**
- Do not print CSI as a transfer of the s.20 duty, and do not print a CSI-hours cap (2 vs 3 hours) in public copy — still contested after two research passes.
- Do not describe Murphy's "amateur signer" detail as a complaint about interpreter quality — the transcript is explicit no such point was taken.
- Do not present Murphy's appointment-discrimination claim as having succeeded — it failed at both tribunal and EAT.
- No documented UK standard or duty-holder split exists for accessibility of applicant-tracking systems/recruitment platforms — who carries the duty when a candidate applies via a third-party ATS (agency, platform provider, or end employer, or all three) is unsettled by any published guidance or case found.

**What actually closes the gap:** FOI folded into the existing companion log (booker/payer identity, booking volumes, whether the process-adjustment question is held separately from health/monitoring questions); link GOV.UK CSI, ACAS recruitment/interviewing pages, EHRC s.60 guides; a dated stub sentence only.

## 2. Occupational Health & Working Patterns (Grok prompts 92–96)

**Independently reviewed and spot-checked 13 September 2026.**

**Headline:** Occupational Health advises; the Equality Act s.20 duty to make reasonable adjustments sits with the employer, not OH. The official UK lone-working leaflet (HSE INDG73) does not mention BSL or Deaf staff — it already permits non-phone check-in methods ("email etc," "other devices designed to raise the alarm") without ever naming who they're for. Flashing alarms and vibrating pagers are named kit in fire-safety and RNID employer guidance; whether they're issued to someone on a night or lone shift is an individual PEEP/adjustment question, not something any published national protocol answers.

**Spot-checked live, both holding:**
- ACAS *Using occupational health to help someone at work* (updated 17 Dec 2025) — verbatim: "It's up to the employer to decide whether to put in place the recommendations from the occupational health assessment." No BSL/Deaf content on the page.
- HSE INDG73 — confirmed absence of any Deaf/BSL/hearing-impairment mention; keep-in-touch wording verbatim: "pre-agreed intervals of regular contact... using phones, radios, email etc, bearing in mind the worker's understanding of English," plus "other devices designed to raise the alarm."

**Don't list (binding):**
- Do not print that OH itself carries the Equality Act s.20 duty — the duty-holder is the employer.
- Do not print "OH must assess night/lone/home working" — no FOM, SOM, or ACAS product creates that requirement.
- Do not print "duty of care breach," "non-compliant OH report," or "unlawful lone-working practice" — no statute, regulation, or judgment supports those phrasings on this batch's findings.
- Do not print INDG73 as forbidding text/app check-in — it already permits non-phone methods; the finding is that it doesn't name Deaf/BSL, not that HSE bans accessible methods.
- Do not present *Iqbal v Home Office* (2200324/2016) as a win, a night-shift case, or a home-working case — it is an office-hours interpreter-hours dispute; the claimant lost.
- Do not cite PAS 79:2007 as the live fire-risk product — the housing part (PAS 79-2) was withdrawn 6 August 2021.

**What actually closes the gap:** FOI folded into the existing companion log (PEEP template lone/out-of-hours question; pagers/visual-alarm devices issued to staff in the last 3 years; whether the lone-working SOP names a non-phone check-in option; whether a BSL interpreter is routinely offered for staff OH appointments, distinct from patient AIS bookings); link ACAS, RNID employer H&S page, Home Office PEEP guide, NHS Employers 2025 article.

## 3. Internal HR/IT SLAs (Grok prompts 97–102)

**Independently reviewed and spot-checked 14 September 2026.**

**Headline:** There is no UK statutory clock for how fast an employer must fulfil an agreed reasonable adjustment. The Equality Act duty applies to agency and contract workers as well as employees (s.41(4), Sch. 8). Whether HR actually serves those tickets in the same queue regardless of contract type is unpublished — not documented as either true or false.

**Spot-checked live, one correction required:**
- ACAS *Reasonable adjustments at work* (updated 30 Jan 2025) — confirmed verbatim: no days-clock; duty covers "employees and workers, contractors and self-employed people hired to personally do the work, job applicants."
- **Khan v Reed Talent Solutions Ltd (ET 1801643/2024) — corrected.** The judgment PDF was read directly. There is **no "22 June 2023" date anywhere in it** (a figure that had propagated across three prior findings files). The tribunal's delivery evidence sits in **August–October 2023** (chasing 28–30 Aug 2023; estimated chair delivery 9 Oct 2023). The employment chain is multi-entity, not a simple agency-to-council step: PayStream (umbrella, from Nov 2022) → Reed itself as PAYE temp (Jan–Apr 2023) → SWES (umbrella, Apr–Nov 2023) → direct Council employment from 3 November 2023. s.20/21/41(4) upheld on 7 of 9 items. **Any future citation of this case must use the corrected chain and "August–October 2023 (exact date not specified; estimated chair delivery 9 Oct 2023)," never "22 June 2023."** Not a Deaf/BSL case — cited only as an illustration of a documented internal-ticket delay reaching tribunal.

**Don't list (binding):**
- Do not print a statutory days-clock for fulfilling an agreed adjustment — there isn't one.
- Do not print the WPC two-week figure, the Lords PSC four-week figure, or Costigan's PMB as law — all are asks or drafts, not enacted requirements.
- Do not print "NHS Employers two weeks" as a national NHS SLA — it is a self-assessment checklist question, not a target.
- Do not print "HR serves in-house clients first" as a documented rule — no published contract, FOI, CIPD/REC report, or judgment found.
- Do not print *Toth v HR Go/Mayr-Melnhof* as national practice — the claim failed (party name is Toth, not "Torth" as one source filename has it).
- Do not print a Workplace Adjustment Passport as a legal guarantee binding the next manager, contractor, or employer — Royal Mail's own passport expressly excludes agency, contractors, and the self-employed.

**What actually closes the gap:** FOI folded into the existing companion log (same ticket queue for agency/bank/contractor vs. substantive staff; published SLA per queue if different; median calendar days to fulfilment split by contract type; passport issuance counts by contract type); link ACAS RA page, NHS Employers/TUC/Civil Service passport templates.

## 4. Outsourced/Offshored Internal Service Channels (Grok prompts 121–126)

**Independently reviewed and spot-checked 14 September 2026. Strongest "curate first" verdict of the seven — almost entirely absence-of-evidence held together by one solid statutory gate and one solid funding-gap argument.**

**Headline:** Outsourcing the helpdesk does not switch off the employer's Equality Act duty. Access to Work can fund a Deaf employee's own video-relay minutes; it is not a grant that retrofits or varies a managed-service contractor's phone-only desk. Whether any named contract is in breach is a tribunal question on the facts — unpublished here.

**Spot-checked live, both holding:**
- Equality Act 2010 **s.31(5)** — confirmed verbatim: where an employer arranges for another person to provide a service only to its own employees, the employer is not the service-provider, and the employees are treated as a section of the public. This is the load-bearing gate for the whole batch and it is solid — a contractor **can** separately owe a Part 3 services duty on these facts.
- AtW staff guide civil-servant exclusion — confirmed as an edit to the guide's own introduction wording, logged 13 November 2025; the underlying policy transfer of responsibility to individual departments took effect **1 April 2022**. Do not present 13 November 2025 as the date civil servants lost AtW eligibility — it is only when the guide's wording was updated to state the pre-existing 2022 change plainly.

**Don't list (binding):**
- Do not print that a named employer, outsourcer, or procurement contract is unlawful or in breach without a named statute, regulation, or judgment on these exact facts — none exists in this batch.
- Do not print "the duty is discharged because we outsourced IT" — neither s.20 nor s.39 says that.
- Do not stretch Equality Act Sch. 3 para 20 (the pension/insurance carve-out) to cover occupational-scheme administration — the statute names personal pension schemes only.
- Do not treat PCR 2015 reg. 42(8) as governing every live 2026 public contract — new covered procurements since 24 Feb 2025 sit under the Procurement Act 2023, which did not copy the sentence across.
- Do not print "most UK staff helpdesks are voice-only" as a measured rate — no census exists, only absence of a published one.
- Do not print AtW as a fund capable of varying a managed-service contract or funding a contractor-side VRS seat — no official text describes it that way.
- Do not assume VRS is a neutral channel for a whistleblowing disclosure — a named interpreter becomes a witness to the disclosure, and no UK source reconciles that tension; do not print "Protect offers BSL VRS" (Protect's own site shows phone plus a web form; the BSL sentence is a third-party directory note, not a Protect product).

**What actually closes the gap:** FOI folded into the existing companion log (does the staff IT-support/payroll/facilities/whistleblowing/pension-helpline contract specify any non-voice channel; who pays for a BSL interpreter or VRS minute to use it); link EA s.31(5) and Sch. 3 para 20, the 2026 Services Code, the AtW staff guide VRS definition.

## 5. Reasonable Adjustments Inside Financial-Services Chinese Walls (Grok prompts 127–133)

**Independently reviewed and spot-checked 14 September 2026. Genuinely novel-and-unsettled territory — the findings confirm that framing rather than overreaching past it.**

**Headline:** No published FCA rule, EHRC Code paragraph, or UK Finance product tells a firm how to put a BSL interpreter, live captioner, or AI note-taking tool on the informed side of a Chinese Wall. The Equality Act duty is not switched off because the employer is FCA-regulated. Whether excluding the employee is unlawful has not been decided. **Do not treat silence as a ban or as permission.**

**Spot-checked live, both holding:**
- FCA Handbook **SYSC 10.2.2 R** (last substantive update 3 July 2016) — confirmed silent on disability anywhere in the rule text.
- UK **MAR Art. 18(1)–(2)** — confirmed verbatim: "contract of employment, or otherwise performing tasks through which they have access," naming only advisers, accountants, and credit rating agencies as examples. No mention of interpreters anywhere in the article. The "otherwise performing tasks" wording is *capable of reaching* an interpreter as a reading — this is independently confirmed as a reading, not an FCA conclusion.

**Don't list (binding):**
- Do not print that wall-crossing an interpreter is permitted or prohibited under UK MAR — Art. 18 does not name interpreters.
- Do not print that a named bank, Control Room, or deal team is unlawful, non-compliant, or in breach — no named judgment or Final Notice exists on these facts.
- Do not print exclusion from a wall-crossed meeting as automatically discrimination — ss.15/19 carry an untested objective-justification defence.
- Do not print "MAR yields to the Equality Act" or the reverse — no published hierarchy exists.
- Do not print NRCPD registration as legally sufficient for MAR purposes — its confidentiality clause has a harm/lawful-disclosure carve-out, a risk sentence in a live deal room, not a safe harbour.
- Do not print that an AI notetaker in a wall-crossed call is or is not market abuse — no FCA rule names the collision; the FCA AI-approach page moves without a changelog and must be re-checked at the point of any future citation, not treated as settled after this review.
- Do not import SEC SECR 6-80, ADA undue hardship, ESMA Q&As, MiFID II Arts. 16/23, or any non-UK regime as UK duty.

**What actually closes the gap:** FOI to the FCA as regulator, and separately as employer, on whether any published document addresses this collision; link SYSC 10.2, UK MAR Art. 18, EA ss.15/19/20/39 — no wall-crossing SOP, no AI-tool whitelist, no City-specific charter to be drafted.

## 6. TUPE — The Legal-Employer-Change Event (Grok prompts 158–163)

**Independently reviewed 13 September 2026; browser-confirmed refresh 14 September 2026.**

**Headline:** TUPE transfers the employment contract automatically, and the new employer owes the Equality Act s.20 duty from day one — but there is no published rule that a specific interpreter booking, workplace adjustment passport, or Access to Work award transfers with the employee automatically. DWP's own public guidance to employers and its internal guidance to case managers give **two different pictures**, addressed to two different audiences, and this is a documented administrative mismatch — not a discovered legal breach, and not "the two texts disagree."

**Browser-confirmed live 14 September 2026, both still present (not withdrawn):**
- AtW **employer factsheet**: "they may be able to transfer equipment to their new employer, but they cannot automatically transfer awards for support workers or travel" — contact the AtW team. Does not mention TUPE.
- AtW **staff guide**, "New employer (new owner of business), same job" block, paragraphs 39–44: "Under the TUPE regulations, the new owner of a business is obliged to take on the contractual obligations of their predecessor. This includes the cost sharing agreements with AtW." A same-job takeover "should be treated as a repeat application." A second, distinct box (paras 45–49, "New employer, different business, same job") covers the more common private-sector service-provision-change pattern; which box case managers actually apply to an SPC is unpublished. **Do not cite 13 November 2025 as this page's current update date** — its own timestamps read 18 June 2026 (visible/JSON-LD) and 4 September 2026 (`govuk:updated-at` meta). The embedded TUPE hyperlink on the page points at a dead Directgov URL — do not treat it as a live GOV.UK TUPE explainer.

**Don't list (binding):**
- Do not print "TUPE requires the adjustment to transfer" — reg. 4 does not name disability, reasonable adjustments, passports, or Access to Work.
- Do not print "employee liability information must include disability status/adjustments/a passport" — reg. 11 is a closed list and none of its heads names it.
- Do not print any "grace period" for the incoming employer's Equality Act duty — none exists; the duty bites from the moment the contract has effect, subject only to the Sch. 8 para. 20 knowledge defence.
- Do not print "Access to Work continues automatically across TUPE" **or** "Access to Work always requires a brand-new application after TUPE" — both are false on the texts as found.
- Do not print that the staff-guide TUPE block was withdrawn, moved, or removed — it was not; only the previously-cited landing date was stale.
- Do not cite *Gunn*, *Stinton v Cambria Automobiles*, or *Anne v GOSH* as authority that an informal adjustment or passport transfers under TUPE — none of the three decides that question.
- Do not invent or generalise a first-person "the interpreter vanished on TUPE day" account — none was found anywhere searched; that absence is itself the finding for this batch's lived-experience leg.

**What actually closes the gap:** re-fetch the AtW staff guide before any public stub goes up (it has shown three different timestamps across two review passes in one day without a changelog entry against this heading); FOI to DWP on how often the staff-guide's carry-forward script is actually used by case managers versus customers being told to submit a fresh application; link the two DWP pages side by side rather than drafting new summary copy.

## 7. Grievance and Escalation (Grok prompts 109–114)

**Independently reviewed and spot-checked 14 September 2026. This is what happens after 3 (HR/IT SLA) has already missed the ticket, or after 1 (Recruitment) has already failed at hire — it is not the SLA itself, not recruitment, not Jobcentre complaints, and not AtW.**

**Headline:** A grievance does not pause the Equality Act tribunal clock. Notify Acas first. Employment-tribunal representation is not ordinary legal aid. Acas and the tribunal publish BSL *routes*; how a declared need is actually fulfilled inside the conciliator conversation itself is unpublished. A missed internal SLA is not, by itself, a finding of discrimination.

**Spot-checked live, both holding:**
- `acas.org.uk/employment-tribunal-time-limits` — current clock is 3 months minus 1 day; from **1 October 2026** most claims move to 6 months minus 1 day, but **only for claims whose time limit starts on or after 1 October 2026** — not a blanket calendar-date switch. "Going through grievance, disciplinary or appeal procedures does not change your time limit."
- `acas.org.uk/notify` — the Accessibility-during-early-conciliation declaration list names "Relay UK text relay," "an interpreter," "a British Sign Language interpreter," and "email only contact" as options a claimant can declare — confirmed live, literal wording.

**Don't list (binding):**
- Do not print that a missed internal SLA, a refused adjustment, or Acas's process is automatically unlawful or discrimination — reasonableness is fact-specific.
- Do not print that a grievance pauses the EA clock — only Acas notification pauses it (s.140B/ERA s.207B).
- Do not print the pre-1-October-2026 window as governing a claim whose time *starts* on or after that date, or vice versa — the change is conditional on the start date, not the calendar date.
- Do not print Northern Ireland as Acas territory — NI uses the Labour Relations Agency (1 month + 14 days).
- Do not print "Acas has no BSL route" — the declaration list, including the literal "a British Sign Language interpreter" option, is confirmed live. The gap is the fulfilment method inside the conciliator conversation itself, and DeafBlind is unnamed on Acas's pages.
- Do not print HMCTS BSL provision as automatic — it is request-based.
- Do not print legal aid as ET representation as of right — LASPO left Legal Help/CLA for discrimination *advice*; representation at the ET generally needs Exceptional Case Funding, generally not granted.
- Do not print an ET issue fee as currently live — *Unison* [2017] UKSC 51 quashed the 2013 fee; no live fee exists as of this pass.
- Do not conflate the two Bentley cases — *M Bentley v Ealing* (tribunal admin miss) and *C Bentley v Oaks Park* (school workplace + interpreter reprimand) are different people, different respondents.
- Do not print a published-case win-rate — most claims settle or withdraw; the named cases in this batch are a slice, not a census.

**What actually closes the gap:** page-watch first (does a claimant who ticks "BSL interpreter" on the EC notify form get VRS, a booked interpreter, or email-only?), then one FOI in the existing companion log if the page stays silent; put 23 September 2026 (Acas Code consultation close) and 1 October 2026 (tribunal window change) on the watches calendar.
