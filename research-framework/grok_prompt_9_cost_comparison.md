# Grok Prompt 9 — VRS cost vs. in-person/face-to-face BSL interpreting: the missing comparison

This is prompt 9 in an ongoing research series on UK Video Relay Service (VRS) access for Deaf BSL users (a "BSL Access Register" evidence dossier, part of a Deaf Network Health Group project). Prompts 1–8 covered GDPR barriers, third-party call refusal, enabling legislation, workplace/travel access, corporate structure of VRS providers, Contact Scotland BSL's scope, and 999 BSL call volumes — all independently verified and merged into the dossier. This prompt fills a gap none of the previous eight addressed: **what does booking a live, in-person, face-to-face BSL interpreter actually cost in the UK, and how does that compare to VRS costs already in the dossier?**

Please research this with the same rigour as previous rounds: open primary sources directly, quote them, and grade every finding **(a)** = you opened the source and can quote it, **(b)** = secondary description of a source you have not opened, **(c)** = searched and found no evidence. Do not present (b) or (c) material as if it were (a).

## What's already confirmed in the dossier (for context/comparison — do not re-research these, just use them as anchors)

- DVLA's VRS contract: **£30,000 for 12 months** (24 Apr 2024–23 Apr 2025), awarded to D A Languages Ltd, covering all DVLA-related VRS calls for that period.
- SignHealth's BSL Health Access (2020–21): launched with **£800,000** of the charity's own reserves, handled 25,000+ conversations in roughly a year before NHS funding was withdrawn.
- Crown Commercial Service's RM6302 framework (2025–2028): BSL VRS sits inside a general "Visual Interpreting Services" lot alongside face-to-face interpreting, with **no published breakdown** separating VRS spend from face-to-face spend.
- Ofcom's 2011 estimate for a *universal* (any-number) VRS service: **£41.6m/year** (medium demand) to **£113.4m/year** (high demand).
- 999 BSL (emergency-only VRS) handled approximately 17,000–23,000 calls per service year across 2022–2025 (varies by year; falling in the most recent year).

## What I need researched

### 1. UK face-to-face BSL interpreter booking costs — the primary question
Find actual published rates, contracts, or framework pricing for booking a live, in-person, professional BSL interpreter in the UK. Specifically:
- Typical agency day rate, half-day rate, and hourly rate for a NRCPD-registered (or equivalent) BSL/English interpreter.
- Minimum booking fees/minimum charge periods (many UK interpreting agencies charge a 2-hour or half-day minimum even for a 20-minute appointment — confirm if this is standard, and find actual published figures).
- Travel time and mileage/expenses typically charged on top of the booking fee, and how agencies structure this (e.g., portal-to-portal charging).
- Any NHS-specific framework rates (e.g., the same Crown Commercial Service RM6302 framework's Lot 4 "Visual Interpreting Services" — does its published framework agreement include actual day-rate or hourly-rate figures for face-to-face interpreting specifically, as opposed to VRS?).
- Any published rate card from a major UK interpreting agency (e.g., Sign Solutions, D A Languages, Language Empire, Action Deafness Communications, or similar) that separates VRS/remote pricing from face-to-face pricing.
- NUBSLI (National Union of British Sign Language Interpreters) published recommended minimum rates for freelance interpreters, if any exist publicly.

### 2. Direct cost-per-contact comparison
Using whatever real figures you find in (1), construct an actual cost-per-hour or cost-per-booking comparison against VRS costs. For example: if a face-to-face interpreter costs £X for a 2-hour minimum booking (including travel), and a VRS call typically lasts Y minutes, what is the per-minute or per-contact cost difference? Show your working and be explicit about what assumptions you're making (e.g., "assuming X minutes average call length, sourced from Z").

### 3. Public-sector spend comparison, if findable
Does any UK public body publish a cost comparison of its own between face-to-face interpreting spend and VRS spend for the same population/service area? (NHS trusts, ICBs, or local authorities sometimes discuss this in board papers, FOI responses, or procurement business cases when justifying a shift toward remote interpreting.) If you find a real UK public-sector document making this comparison with actual figures, quote it directly.

### 4. The "scalability" claim — is it evidenced or assumed?
The dossier currently implies (without a direct citation) that VRS is more cost-scalable than face-to-face interpreting because of the travel-time/minimum-booking cost structure of the latter. Find out whether this claim has ever been made explicitly, with figures, by Ofcom, a UK government body, an NHS commissioning document, or a Deaf-sector charity (BDA, RNID, SignHealth, or the BSL Advisory Board's *Locked Out* report) — as opposed to being an assumption this dossier or I have made without a primary source behind it.

### 5. Counter-considerations — where face-to-face might have advantages not captured by cost alone
Briefly note (with sources if you find any) any published UK material on situations where face-to-face interpreting is specifically preferred or required over VRS despite higher cost — e.g., legal/court settings, mental health assessments, safeguarding situations, or where a Deaf person's language needs (deafblind, additional needs, non-standard BSL dialect) make video relay impractical. This is important for the dossier to avoid overclaiming that VRS should simply replace face-to-face interpreting on cost grounds alone.

## Output format

For each numbered section, give me the actual figures/quotes you find, the source (with URL if available), the date of the source, and your (a)/(b)/(c) confidence grade. End with a short "bottom line" table comparing the clearest VRS cost figure(s) against the clearest face-to-face cost figure(s) you found, on a like-for-like basis where possible (e.g., cost per hour, cost per typical contact/call).
