Cash, not booking

Extra-cost benefits vs interpreter provision. The money and the appointment are two different systems.

Companion page to How the system is built (duty/payer/booker/VRS) — this page covers who pays a person extra-cost cash, not who books an interpreter.

PIP, ADP, Access to Work, Care Act, DLA, Attendance Allowance — six UK pots, eight countries

Every row on this page answers one question: does this scheme give a Deaf or DeafBlind person money to spend as they choose, or does it arrange an interpreter for a specific appointment? Confusing the two is the most common misreading in press coverage of Deaf benefits — a PIP award is not a booked BSL slot at the GP.

What each UK pot actually is

The recurring error in press coverage: reading a benefit's diagnosis list as if it were the legal test, or assuming any of these pots pays for a specific interpreter booking.

PIP (England / Wales / NI)

What it actually is
Extra-cost cash. Activity 9 ("communicating verbally" — see BSL_DOMAIN_pip_dla.md, this project's own domain file uses Activity 9, not Activity 7 as this Grok draft states check) is the usual BSL hinge.
What it is not
Not an interpreter booking. Diagnosis lists in the press are not the legal test.

ADP (Scotland)

What it actually is
Same activity skeleton as PIP; claim process can start via Contact Scotland BSL.
What it is not
Still cash, not a clinic interpreter.

Access to Work

What it actually is
Work-only grant. Cap £69,260, frozen through 2026/27. Pays for BSL / VRS at work.
What it is not
Does not fund the GP or hospital appointment.

Care Act Deafblind assessment

What it actually is
Council must use a specialist assessor check reg. 6. Can open communicator-guide support.
What it is not
Does not create RIDB (registered interpreter) capacity.

DLA / Child Disability Payment

What it actually is
Extra-cost cash for under-16s.
What it is not
Not the school BSL budget — that runs through CRIDE / local-authority education, a separate pot.

Attendance Allowance / PADP

What it actually is
Pension-age care-cost cash — where most modelled DeafBlind people over state pension age sit.
What it is not
No mobility component; no tactile-interpreting hours built into the award.

UK pot → nearest international cousin

Same caution as the systems-comparison page: a statute or a bigger cap abroad is not proof anyone is better served at the appointment. These are structural cousins, not a league table.

PIP / ADP — extra-cost cash, does not book the room

Norway grunnstønad + hjelpestønad; Sweden merkostnadsersättning; US SSDI/SSI (earnings/poverty floor, not extra-cost cash); Australia DSP + NDIS plan; Netherlands — issues hours, not PIP-style cash.

Access to Work — work interpreting, cap frozen at £69,260

Netherlands UWV funds 15% of working time (more available on request); Sweden Arbetsförmedlingen grant cut to 120,000 SEK/year from Jun 2025 check; Norway NAV books work support after a grant; US — ADA duty sits on the employer, plus Vocational Rehabilitation; Australia JobAccess Employment Assistance Fund (EAF).

Care Act Deafblind assessment

Netherlands: 168 private hours if DeafBlind vs 30 if Deaf in a parallel scheme check — see BSL_DOMAIN_deafblind.md, which cites a comparable 168-vs-30 split without full sourcing; Norway names a deaf-blind escort/interpreter in the same law as Deaf interpreting; US SSPs (Support Service Providers) are not an ADA-guaranteed aid — state-by-state patchwork; Australia NDIS / NABS text, health still a separate pot.

What to steal — and what not to

  1. The IS / IS NOT framing itself. Every SWL-facing benefits explainer should state the negative as clearly as the positive — this is the single most useful structural idea in the Grok draft.
  2. Netherlands' hours-based model as a named contrast to AtW. Do not import Dutch hour-caps into a UK benefits case — the schemes are not equivalent.
  3. Naming the DLA→CRIDE/education handoff explicitly. Parents conflating DLA with a school's BSL budget is a real, recurring confusion worth a standing line in the dossier.
  4. Flagging Attendance Allowance as where most modelled DeafBlind pension-age people sit. Useful framing — but "most modelled" needs its modelling assumptions stated before this goes public.
  5. Do not publish the international cousin table as-is. Three lines are marked "check" above (PIP Activity number, Sweden's 2025 grant cut, the Netherlands 168-vs-30 split) — this project's own verification discipline (VERIFICATION_STATUS.md) requires a primary source before anything with a specific figure goes on the public dossier.

Where this needs verification before it goes further

Three things to check against primary sources before any of this reaches dossier/bsl_dossier.html: (1) whether the PIP BSL hinge is Activity 9 or Activity 7 — BSL_DOMAIN_pip_dla.md in this project already says Activity 9, sourced to the DWP PIP Assessment Guide, so this draft's "Activity 7" needs reconciling or correcting; (2) the Care Act "regulation 6" citation for specialist Deafblind assessors, which has not been checked against the actual regulations; (3) the Sweden Arbetsförmedlingen grant-cut figure and date, and the Netherlands 168-vs-30-hour DeafBlind/Deaf split, neither of which has a primary source attached in this draft. None of these are safe to state as fact in the public dossier until sourced and dated the way every other figure in this project's evidence base is.
