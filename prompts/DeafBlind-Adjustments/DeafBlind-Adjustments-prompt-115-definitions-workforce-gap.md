## Headline

The live NRCPD public table still has a **separate category**. It is printed as **“Deafblind Interpreter”** on the figures page and **“Registered Interpreters for Deafblind People”** on the categories page (RIDB in government and NRCPD prose). On this pass (11 September 2026) the national RIDB total is still **19**. That matches prompt 81’s 10 September 2026 snapshot. It has not moved in 24 hours. Dual registrations mean 19 is a category total, not necessarily 19 unique people who only do this work.

Regional zeros on the same table are unchanged: **East Midlands 0, South West 0, Yorkshire and The Humber 0.** London 1, South East 3, West Midlands 4, North West 3, Scotland 3 (NRCPD, not SRLPDC), East of England 2, North East 1, Wales 1, Northern Ireland 1. Combined London + South East = 4. Homepage live feed: **2,089** professionals in total (2,087 on 10 September).

**“8 RIDB” is not the live table.** It is the figure NRCPD gave Liam O’Dell on 12 February 2025 and the “before” baseline in the GOV.UK competency-assessment grant story of 23 June 2026. That grant is **£15,000**, expected to take the bench to **68**. Sixty-eight is an aspiration, not a delivered headcount. The 19 now on the table is consistent with assessment weekends starting to add people; it is not eightfold.

**Category split, which is the actual gate finding.**

- Deafblind Manual Alphabet, Block Alphabet, Hands-On Signing and Visual Frame Signing are **methods inside the one RIDB category**, not four NRCPD registers. A standard Registered Sign Language Interpreter (RSLI) is a different category (live total **1,664**). Holding RSLI does not put someone on RIDB.
- **Communicator-guide / guide-communicator is not an NRCPD category.** NRCPD said so in substance on 12 February 2025: an extra registration category “will take careful consideration and planning.” The BSL Advisory Board Deafblind working group (May 2025 minutes) recorded work “to resolve issues on regulating communicator guides.” That work has not produced an NRCPD category on this pass.
- Sense employs **communicator guides** as a community-support job (acquired deafblindness) and **intervenors** (congenital). Those are provider roles, not a public register. Signature Level 2/3 awards name communicator-guides as a *target group*, not a licence.
- Scotland’s SRLPDC **does** print separate categories. Accounts to **31 March 2025**: **2 Deafblind Manual Interpreters** and **1 Guide Communicator**, plus 61 Registered BSL/English Interpreters. Do not add the SRLPDC 2 + 1 onto the NRCPD 19 — some people may be dual-registered; the figures are different bodies.
- RBSLI is BSL/English interpreter and translator only. No DeafBlind category and no published current headcount found on this pass (last sourced academic count remains 2019: 52).
- Deafblind Enablement launched a **Professional Register for Deafblind Specialists** in Deafblind Awareness Week 2026 (Facebook/LinkedIn 29 June 2026; domain `deafblind-specialist-register.co.uk`) for communicator-guides, Deafblind interpreter-guides and specialist assessors. **No published headcount** found. Treat as a page that exists, not a counted bench.

NRCPD’s approved RIDB course on this pass is Signature’s **Level 6 Certificate in Working Effectively with Deafblind Manual and Visual Frame and Hands On Signers**, plus a recognised Level 6 BSL (or other sign language) qualification. There is **no Regulated Trainee RIDB** column on the figures table (unlike TSLI / trainee relay / trainee SLT).

The EHRC Employment Statutory Code and the Equality Act s.20 duty can cover a DeafBlind-specific auxiliary aid as a fact-specific reasonable adjustment. Neither instrument names RIDB, deafblind manual, or guide-communicator as a required qualification. Absence of a named Code paragraph is not a finding that the duty does not apply.

**Hard-don’t for anything public.** Do not print “unregistered guide-communicator = illegal.” Do not print “8 RIDB” as current. Do not print 68 as delivered. Do not add SRLPDC and NRCPD totals. Do not call DBE’s register a national regulator.

## 115.1 Methods inside RIDB are not extra registers

NRCPD’s own sentence is the clean split. An RIDB uses one or more of: Deafblind Manual Alphabet, Block Alphabet, Hands-On Signing, Visual Frame Signing, and also “relays auditory and visual information such as reactions to what has been said and the movement of other people.” That last clause is environmental information — closer to what a guide-communicator also does — but NRCPD still files the person as an *interpreter*, not as a guide.

A hearing-sighted RSLI working in a standard signing space is not, on the register, an RIDB. Booking “a BSL interpreter” for a DeafBlind employee can therefore be the wrong product even when the booking is fulfilled.

Signature’s ladder matches the methods: separate L3/L6 certificates for Manual and for Visual Frame / Hands On, plus the combined L6 certificate that NRCPD lists as the approved RIDB course. Guiding appears at Level 2 (unit T202 *Deafblind Manual Communication and Guiding*). That is an awarding-body unit, not an NRCPD category.

## 115.2 Communicator-guide sits outside NRCPD

Three UK facts, not one:

1. **NRCPD does not register the role.** Confirmed by the live category list and by NRCPD’s February 2025 statement that a new category would need planning.
2. **SRLPDC does, in Scotland, and the published stock is one person** at 31 March 2025.
3. **Providers use the job title anyway.** Sense communicator guides (acquired) and intervenors (congenital); Deafblind Scotland Guide Communicators (including an NHS-funded booking route in some boards — health, not employment); DBE lists communicator-guide among the roles it wants on its new private register.

Do not add those provider staff onto the RIDB 19. Do not treat DBE’s June 2026 register as a substitute regulator. Do not print a UK guide-communicator headcount; none is published outside the SRLPDC “1”.

## 115.3 What “shortage” is allowed to mean here

The live fact is the bench: **19 RIDB, three English regions at zero, London 1.** That is smaller than the general SLI shortage already logged in prompts 81–83 by two orders of magnitude (1,664 SLI). The June 2026 grant treats that scarcity as a government-recognised gap and funds assessment weekends aimed at 68. Whether 68 is reachable, how many of the 19 take employment work, and how long a workplace booking waits, are **unpublished**.

Sarah Reed’s February 2025 petition and the Board’s “lack of Deafblind interpreters / upskilling communicator guides” language are the advocacy layer. They do not create a statutory quota.

## 115.4 What this does to 116–117

A funding cap or an HR ticket delay (116, and the already-compiled 97–102) lands on a workforce that is regionally empty in the South West, East Midlands and Yorkshire and The Humber. Physical navigation support (117) is exactly the work that sits in the communicator-guide job description and *outside* the NRCPD category list. The gate finding for the rest of the batch is therefore: the communication-interpreter product has a public two-digit register; the guide-communicator product does not, except for one person on a Scottish year-end account.
