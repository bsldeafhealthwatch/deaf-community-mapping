## Verdict for any public page

**Curate first. Draft almost nothing.**

Agree with Grok's own headline sentence; it survives the spot-check:

> A DeafBlind employee's workplace adjustment is not the same product as a BSL interpreter for a hearing-sighted Deaf colleague. The live NRCPD category is Registered Interpreter for Deafblind People (RIDB) — a two-digit national bench. Communicator-guide is a job, not an NRCPD category. Access to Work can fund a support worker or travel buddy and has no separate DeafBlind tariff; the cap is one number. There is no official count of DeafBlind people in work.

What already exists and should be linked, not rewritten:

| Need | Point at |
|---|---|
| Who is qualified to interpret for a DeafBlind person | NRCPD *Registration Categories* + live *Registration figures* — filter to Deafblind Interpreter / RIDB. Date-stamp whatever number is quoted. |
| Ordinary BSL interpreter is a different card | Same NRCPD table, RSLI column (1,664). Holding RSLI does not put someone on RIDB. |
| Employer duty (fact-specific) | Equality Act s.20; EHRC Employment Code; ACAS *What reasonable adjustments are*. Neither names RIDB or CG as the required step. |
| Who can pay | AtW staff guide + employer factsheet — Support Worker / Travel Buddy mapping; no CG tariff; cap £69,260 frozen through FY2026/27; cost share does not apply to support workers. |
| Sight-loss workplace etiquette (not dual-sensory — don't conflate) | GOV.UK *Employing disabled people*; Cabinet Office VI colleague guide; RNIB *Working with blind and partially sighted colleagues*; BDF sight-loss guidance. Label each as VI, not DeafBlind. |

## Don't list (print near any in-work / adjustments page)

- Do not print current AtW funding levels, employer provision, or the absence of DeafBlind-specific guidance as unlawful discrimination or a breach of duty without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment.
- Do not print "unregistered guide-communicator = illegal." CG is not an NRCPD category; absence of a category is not a criminal offence.
- Do not print **8 RIDB** as current — that is the Feb 2025 / Jun 2026 government baseline. The live table (re-checked 14 Sep 2026) is **19**.
- Do not print **68 RIDB** as delivered — that is the £15k competency-grant aspiration, not a headcount reached.
- Do not add Scotland's SRLPDC count (2 Deafblind Manual + 1 Guide Communicator) onto the NRCPD 19 — different registers, possible dual registration.
- Do not call Deafblind Enablement's Professional Register a national regulator — the page exists; no headcount is published.
- Do not print a separate AtW "RIDB hourly band" — none is published; the BSL band (National £26–£47 / London £30–£52) is the closest card, and NUBSLI's fee table has no RIDB column.
- Do not print the cap as any figure other than **£69,260**, frozen through FY2026/27 — do not repeat RNID's own printed £69,620 typo.
- Do not print Sense's 2014 written evidence (ATW0075) — manual-vs-BSL re-justification, 14-week renewal gap, 15→10 hours, "fifth of going rate," £30k salaried cap — as 2026 staff-guide practice.
- Do not print cost share as applying to support workers — the live employer factsheet puts support workers on the "consider up to 100%" list; only special aids/equipment and premises take the size-based cost share.
- Do not print "HSE requires a workplace guide-communicator" — HSWA/MHSWR are general; HSE's disability pages do not mention deafblind, guiding, or workplace orientation.
- Do not print RNIB's spoken hazard call-out guidance as a DeafBlind protocol — wrong channel if the colleague is also Deaf.
- Do not treat prompt 94 (PEEP, lone working, visual/vibrating alarms — emergency evacuation) as if it answered day-to-day Tuesday-afternoon navigation (117).
- Do not print Norway's uncapped NAV interpreter-guide hours, US SSP monthly caps, Australian EAF dollar caps, or NAATI RPDBI as UK rules — international only (118).
- Do not print "the US has workplace SSPs so England is non-compliant" — US SSP is a state-by-state community patchwork; ADA Title I still sits on the employer.
- Do not print a national "employers treat DeafBlind staff as Deaf" rate — the entire UK evidence base for that claim is two sources twelve years apart (Fletcher 2019; Sense 2014).
- Do not reuse Francesca Bussey, Pearl Clinton, Alan Graham, or Mrs W (health/EOL testimony); do not reuse Rimmer or Clarke (JCP); do not reuse Tomasz Borys (deaf BSL AtW cap — not DeafBlind).
- Do not stretch *Locked Out* (health and social care, 27 Nov 2025) into an employer HR cell.
- Do not print the RNID/DeafATW 2026 survey (n=358) as a DeafBlind finding — the title includes "deafblind" but published splits and the named case are BSL-user.
- Do not print Sense's **451,211** or **125,452 aged 20–69** as a census or Care Act register figure — it is a 2017-rebased model on 2010 prevalence rates.
- Do not print the June 2026 "**12,000** Deafblind people with communication needs" as an ONS figure — it is a briefing denominator for the RIDB competency grant.
- Do not use the 125,000 modelled working-age stock as the denominator for the 19-person RIDB register — they describe different populations (mostly later-life acquired dual sensory loss vs. congenital/Usher/BSL-then-sight-loss).
- Do not print a UK DeafBlind employment rate — none exists; Irish 23% / US 29% figures stay international.
- Do not print Census BSL-main (~22,000–25,000 E&W) as a DeafBlind count, or AtW's 3,210 BSL-interpreter customers as a DeafBlind customer count.
- Do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. Follow money and fulfilment — who is on which register, what the award letter actually names, who walks the person to the meeting.
