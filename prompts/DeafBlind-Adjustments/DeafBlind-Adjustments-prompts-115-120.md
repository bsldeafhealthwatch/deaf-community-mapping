Covers the in-post workplace adjustment itself for a DeafBlind employee, as distinct from a Deaf/BSL-only employee — tactile BSL and the deafblind manual alphabet, guide-communicator support, assistive technology, and the funding/cost structure for provision that a BSL-interpreter-only adjustment does not require.

Checked first: the project index and existing batches. DeafBlind appears throughout the project — prompts 20–26 and 27–33 (DWP/Jobcentre Plus, benefits and job-seeking), 66–71 (older Deaf/DeafBlind people), 81–83 (interpreter regulation: prompt 81 already published a live NRCPD **RIDB** headcount and Scotland SRLPDC Deafblind-manual / guide-communicator figures — this batch must live-recheck those numbers, not treat them as unpublished), 92–96 (Occupational Health, which names DeafBlind lone-working/emergency-evacuation risk but does not isolate DeafBlind provision from Deaf provision), 97–102 (SLA/HR processing speed, contract-type-neutral), 103–108 (recruitment, which raises tactile BSL/braille/large-print as a DeafBlind-specific sub-question inside a broader recruitment prompt rather than as its own subject), and 109–114 (grievance/tribunal, same fold-in pattern). In every one of these, DeafBlind is folded in alongside Deaf/BSL as "Deaf and DeafBlind" or raised as a single sub-question. None isolates the DeafBlind *in-post* adjustment itself — tactile/manual interpretation, guide-communicator support, and DeafBlind-specific assistive technology are materially different provision from a BSL interpreter for a hearing-sighted-signing Deaf employee, with a different cost structure, a different (much smaller) qualified-workforce pool, and different funding precedent. This batch is not a duplicate: it gives DeafBlind its own dedicated pass for the first time.

Anchor: a DeafBlind employee (BSL-user with acquired or congenital sight loss, potentially Usher syndrome or another dual-sensory-loss cause) whose workplace adjustment cannot be met by a standard BSL interpreter alone — they may need a trained deafblind manual interpreter or guide-communicator (a distinct, much rarer qualification from a hearing-sighted-signing BSL interpreter), assistive technology that a Deaf-only employee would not need (braille displays, screen-magnification alongside communication support), and support with physical navigation of the workplace itself (guiding to meeting rooms, alerting to physical hazards) that sits outside what any "reasonable adjustment" checklist built around Deaf/BSL alone anticipates.

Proposed domain: stub under the existing Welfare & Employment cluster, alongside 92–96 / 97–102 / 103–108 / 109–114 — this is the DeafBlind-specific slice of the same in-post-adjustment pipeline stage, not a new numbered Domain.

Numbers 121–149 are already reserved in the master index (outsourced service channels, Chinese Walls, workplace devices/AI, EAP). Do not reuse 121. Next free number remains **150**.

### Notes for this batch

- **Gate prompt: 115.** If the live NRCPD table still publishes RIDB as a separate category, that *is* a public headcount — do not print “no public headcount” as the headline. The more likely gate finding is the *category split*: RIDB is not the same thing as a guide-communicator, visual-frame signing may not be a register category, and communicator-guides may sit outside NRCPD entirely. A tiny, regionally zero-heavy qualified bench (prompt 81 already recorded EM/SW/YH at zero on 10 September 2026) should shape how 116–117 are read: a funding cap or SLA delay matters differently against a workforce this scarce. Live-recheck the table; do not copy “8 RIDB” or the 19 figure forward without opening the page.
- **Prompt 120 is the denominator check and should be treated with active scepticism.** DeafBlind prevalence estimates are almost always Sense’s own (a reputable but advocacy-interested source, not independently verified). This prompt must say so plainly rather than presenting a Sense figure as an official statistic. The SWL map’s 8,000–11,000 deafblind figure is a modelled Sense-prevalence estimate, not a measured register — do not launder it into a UK employment denominator.
- **Still-alive scepticism applies to any named guide-communicator scheme or service found in 116–119.** Small, grant-funded specialist provision in this space is exactly the kind of initiative previous batches (Deaf-older, CODA) found listed but dormant; check last public activity and latest accounts, not just whether a page exists. DBE’s June 2026 Professional Register is the live example of a page that can exist without a published headcount.
- **Hard-don't for this batch:** do not print any claim that current AtW funding levels, employer provision, or the absence of DeafBlind-specific guidance constitutes unlawful discrimination or a breach of duty without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment. The likely finding across most of this batch is an absence of provision or data, not a breach of an existing standard. Do not print “unregistered guide-communicator = illegal.”
- **Held-back legs:** none — six prompts, because the topic genuinely splits into workforce/definitions, funding/cost structure, physical-safety adjustment (a category the Deaf-only batches never isolated), international comparison, lived experience, and the underlying population data the whole batch’s proportionality depends on.
- **Relationship to 81–83:** prompt 81 already answered “is NRCPD statutory?” and already printed a RIDB count. This batch asks what the *DeafBlind-specific categories* are and whether the bench has moved. Do not reopen statutory-regulation.
- **Relationship to 92–96, 97–102, 103–108, 109–114:** this batch does not repeat the OH legal-basis question (92), the lone-working/evacuation question (94), the SLA-speed question (97), the recruitment-stage question (103), or the grievance/tribunal question (109). It assumes all of those frameworks apply equally in principle to a DeafBlind employee and asks what is different about the DeafBlind-specific *content* of the adjustment itself once inside any of those frameworks.
- **Relationship to 20–26 / 52–54:** AtW cap, processing times, and the 3,210 BSL-interpreter customer count stay in those cells. Prompt 116 only asks whether the cap or the staff-guide bands treat DeafBlind-qualified support differently.
- **Do not conflate with Graeme’s personal OH/HR thread** — this batch produces general, citable evidence for the dossier, not advice on an individual adjustment request.
- **England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland** are separate systems. SRLPDC figures are Scotland. Do not paste a Scottish register count into an England NRCPD cell.
