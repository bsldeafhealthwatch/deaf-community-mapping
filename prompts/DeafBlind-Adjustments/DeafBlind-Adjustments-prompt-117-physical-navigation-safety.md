# Prompt 117 — Is day-to-day workplace navigation a distinct adjustment, or only communication plus a fire alarm?

**Master index prompt 117.** Third file in the DeafBlind-specific in-post workplace adjustments batch (prompts 115–120). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Isolate *routine* physical navigation and hazard awareness for a DeafBlind employee — guiding to a meeting room, flagging a moved cable or wet floor, workplace orientation after a layout change — as a category separate from a BSL interpreter and separate from emergency evacuation. Ask whether any published UK instrument treats that as an employer Equality Act s.20 duty, an Access to Work-fundable item, an HSE requirement, or none of these. Cross-reference prompt 94 (lone working / night-shift / PEEP / visual-vibrating alarm) and do not rerun it. Do not print current practice as unlawful without a named statute, regulation or judgment.

## Headline

No published UK workplace-safety or reasonable-adjustment product treats **day-to-day DeafBlind navigation** as its own category.

What exists is three general duties and a pile of *single-sense* etiquette:

- **Equality Act 2010 s.20** is fact-specific. A guide, a workplace tour, or a kept-clear walkway *can* be a reasonable adjustment (auxiliary aid/service, or a change to a provision, criterion or practice). The EHRC Employment Code and the EHRC “examples in practice” page give colour-contrast and furniture moves for a visually impaired person’s *safe mobility*, and a combination-of-steps example for a blind worker moving to an unfamiliar part of the building (assistance-dog facilities). They do **not** name a DeafBlind employee, a guide-communicator, or tactile/BSL-on-the-move hazard alerting.
- **HSWA 1974 s.2** and **MHSWR 1999** require a suitable and sufficient risk assessment. HSE’s disability pages (index last updated 16 March 2023; easy-read June 2025; “accessible communication” principle page live 2026) do **not** mention deafblind, dual sensory, guiding, or workplace orientation. HSE INDG73 was already checked in prompt 94 and does not name Deaf/BSL; it also does not name DeafBlind navigation.
- **Access to Work** will consider funding a support worker or travel buddy (prompt 116). That person *could* guide. The staff guide does not label the product “workplace orientation” or “guide-communicator.” Cost share does not apply to the person; special aids (tactile maps, contrasting nosings, vibrating kit) sit on the equipment rule.

Single-sense guidance is thicker and still not this cell:

- GOV.UK *Employing disabled people* (updated 20 November 2025): sight-loss adjustments include “arranging a tour of the workplace” and a risk assessment. Hearing-loss adjustments are seating, adapted phones, formats. Dual sensory is not a heading.
- Cabinet Office *Guidance on supporting colleagues with Visual Impairments* (14 February 2023): colleagues “should respect the VI colleague’s personal space and ask how they would like to be guided” between meeting rooms. No deafblind heading.
- RNIB *Working with blind and partially sighted colleagues*: spoken hazard call-outs and sighted-guide technique. Spoken call-outs are the wrong channel for a DeafBlind colleague.
- BDF sight-loss employer guidance (28 June 2024): clear walkways, arrival guiding, NaviLens tags. Sight loss, not dual sensory.
- ACAS *What reasonable adjustments are* (30 January 2025): layout, lighting, sign-language interpreter. No guiding example.
- Employers’ Forum on Disability *Disability communication guide* (2019, still circulating): a “Meeting people: who are deafblind” page tells the reader many DeafBlind people “need to be guided,” to offer the elbow, and that the person “may be supported by a communicator-guide or interpreter.” That is meeting etiquette, not an HSE or EHRC workplace-navigation standard.

**Named UK case, LGSCO, PHSO or employment-tribunal finding** describing a DeafBlind employee’s *in-post day-to-day navigation or workplace-hazard* experience: **no reliable figure found** on this pass. Sense ATW0075 (2014, prompt 116) mentions a support worker “to assist with travel and inaccessible paperwork” — travel-to-meetings, not floor-plate orientation. Do not file prompt 94’s PEEP / lone-working material here.

The institutional split from 115–116 is the load-bearing fact. The work this prompt describes — “eyes and ears,” guiding, environmental information — is exactly the communicator-guide job description. That job is not an NRCPD category and has no AtW tariff. Published employer checklists stop at “BSL interpreter + visual fire alarm + keep the walkway clear.”

**Hard-don’t for anything public.** Do not print “employers are in breach for not providing a workplace guide-communicator.” s.20 is fact-specific; HSWA is general. Absence of a named product is not a decided contravention.

## 117.1 The category does not exist as a published product

The prompt asked whether navigation support is documented as an employer duty, an AtW-fundable item, an HSE requirement, or none of these.

The accurate answer is **all three general hooks, none of the specific product**:

- Employer duty: s.20 *can* cover it, case by case. HSWA *can* require the risk assessment to notice it. Neither text tells an employer “book a guide-communicator for layout changes.”
- AtW-fundable: a person *can* be funded under Support Worker / Travel Buddy. The label on the form will not say “DeafBlind workplace navigation.”
- HSE requirement: no.

That is a different finding from “there is no legal duty.” Do not collapse the two.

## 117.2 Why single-sense guidance does not transfer

Sight-loss guidance assumes a spoken commentary (“step down,” “cable on the left”). Hearing-loss guidance assumes the person can see the flashing beacon, the wet-floor cone, the rearranged furniture. A DeafBlind employee can lose both channels at once. None of the live UK employer pages on this pass writes that sentence. RNIB’s spoken hazard protocol is the cleanest illustration of the miss: it is good VI practice and the wrong medium.

## 117.3 Cases are unpublished, not “hidden in prompt 94”

Prompt 94 is evacuation and lone working. This cell is Tuesday-afternoon “the meeting moved to the third floor and the chair is in the aisle.” No named UK judgment, ombudsman decision or first-person workplace-navigation account was found for that fact pattern. Charity CG job descriptions describe the *work*; they describe social-care and community settings, not an employer’s floor plate.
