## Headline

There is **no official UK statistic** for “how many working-age DeafBlind people are in employment.”

What exists is a **modelled prevalence stack** and a set of **single-sense official series** that never cross-tab.

**Sense (advocacy model, say so every time).** Live Sense page: **451,211** people in the UK estimated deafblind in 2022, of whom **125,452 aged 20–69** and **302,380 aged 70+**. Method footnote: Operational Research Society 2017 applied Centre for Disability Research (Robertson & Emerson, CeDR 2010:1) age-sex prevalence rates to later ONS population estimates. Projected **over 610,000 by 2035**. Deafblind UK’s 2024–25 impact report repeats “over 450,000.” That is the same model, not a second count. The project’s SWL 8,000–11,000 is the same model applied to six boroughs — **not** a measured register.

CeDR 2010 itself distinguished two thresholds: ~**356,000** any co-occurring vision and hearing impairment; ~**132,000** more severe; nearly three-quarters of the severe group aged 70+. Working-age is the minority of the modelled stock. Usher: Sense 2010 research, **around 10,000** in the UK — still the figure Sense prints.

**Official statistics that do *not* give a DeafBlind employment number.**

- **Census 2021** (England and Wales): main language includes British Sign Language. Project / Locked Out working figures: about **22,000–25,000** people with BSL as main language; Locked Out also prints ~151,000 BSL users of whom 87,000 are deaf. There is **no** dual-sensory or “DeafBlind” language or disability category on the census form, so there is no Census employment cross-tab for DeafBlind people.
- **DWP *The employment of disabled people 2025*** (updated 24 Mar 2026): Q2 2025 disability employment rate **52.8%**, **5.5 million** disabled people in work, gap **29.7 pp**. Impairment breakdowns exist for hearing and for seeing as separate “main health condition” groups in the APS/FRS family. Resolution Foundation (2025) excluded hearing from one chart because the sample was too small. **No published “both hearing and seeing” employment rate.**
- **Access to Work FYE Mar 2025:** “Deaf or hard of hearing” **4,980** customers paid, **£90.3 million** (28% of element spend); “Difficulty in seeing” **£38.2 million** (12%). BSL Interpreter element: **3,210** customers, average payment about **£18,200** (UIN 120203 / prompt 116). **No DeafBlind, RIDB or guide-communicator row.**
- **Registered Blind and Partially Sighted People, England 2022–23** (NHS England / SSDA 902): a sight register. Not dual sensory. Not employment.
- **Care Act 2014 / Assessment Regulations 2014 reg. 6:** local authorities must identify, make contact with and keep a record of deafblind people, and arrange a specialist assessment. **No national published return** of those local registers with a working-age or employment split was found. Locked Out (27 Nov 2025) treats that gap as an action: implement the identification duty; add a dual-sensory category to public disability data.
- **June 2026 government RIDB press:** “approximately **12,000** Deafblind people with communication needs.” That is a briefing denominator for the £15k competency grant, not an ONS or Care Act register total. Treat as **contested / unofficial**.

**Prompt 23 is not superseded.** There is still no official DeafBlind employment rate. The 2025 DWP employment publication and the FYE 2025 AtW tables refine the *single-sense* official picture. They do not create a dual-sensory cell.

Proportionality for 115–119 therefore cannot be calculated from an official in-work count. The working-age modelled stock (~125,000 aged 20–69, Sense 2022) is mostly older-working-age acquired dual sensory loss, not the congenital / Usher / BSL-tactile group that RIDB and communicator-guides serve. Mixing those populations is how a 125,000 denominator gets used to describe a 19-person register.

## 120.1 Two populations inside one modelled number

Sense’s 125,452 aged 20–69 is mostly **acquired dual sensory loss in later working age**. The RIDB / tactile / communicator-guide workforce in prompts 115–116 serves a much smaller group: congenital or early-acquired deafblindness, Usher, BSL-then-sight-loss. Using 125,000 as the denominator for a 19-person RIDB register produces a false scarcity ratio. If a public line is needed, print both the model *and* the 12,000 “communication needs” briefing figure *and* the absence of an official in-work count — and say which is which.

## 120.2 Why official series will not yield the cell without a new question

Census language, FRS/APS main-condition, AtW primary medical condition, and the sight register are all built as single-impairment or single-language fields. A person who is DeafBlind is coded once. Locked Out’s ask — a dual-sensory category in public disability data, plus Care Act identification actually returned nationally — is the only published route to an official denominator. It has not been built.

## 120.3 What this does to 115–119

Proportionality claims (“19 RIDB for X workers”) cannot be closed from official data. The honest sentence is: **the in-work DeafBlind population is unpublished; the modelled working-age stock is Sense’s; AtW and DWP count hearing and seeing separately; the specialist communication workforce is 19 RIDB and no CG register category.**
