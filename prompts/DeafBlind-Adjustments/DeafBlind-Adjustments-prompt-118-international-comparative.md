## Headline

**International — context only.**

No asked jurisdiction gives a private employer a statutory days-clock or a single national “book a workplace SSP” product that drops into a UK AtW cell. What exists are three different architectures:

1. **Nordic social-insurance entitlement (closest to a standing funded model that explicitly includes work).** Norway has provided interpreter-guide / Deafblind interpreting since 1980. WFDB’s Norway case: the service is free, the old hours cap has been removed, the remaining limit is availability, and work is in scope (NAV / National Insurance Act). Finland’s Kela interpreter service covers people with hearing impairment *or* combined visual and hearing impairment. These are public-body entitlements, not employer grants. They do not transfer onto AtW’s cap-and-claim design.

2. **United States SSP / CoNavigator — state-by-state community programs, not a federal workplace scheme.** Helen Keller National Center catalogs active SSP/CN programs. Funding is a patchwork: state legislature + governor budget, vocational rehabilitation, Medicaid waiver, county funds, foundation grants, even golf tournaments. Many programs cap monthly hours and serve shopping, appointments and community access. Workplace use appears where VR is the payer or a consumer takes hours to a job site. That is not ADA Title I (the employer still holds the reasonable-accommodation duty, including interpreters). RID publishes a Standard Practice Paper on interpreting for Deaf-Blind individuals; RID’s live national credentials remain NIC and CDI. There is **no** current RID “Certified DeafBlind Interpreter” exam. Federal 34 CFR Part 396 defines “interpreter for individuals who are deaf-blind” for *training-grant* purposes.

3. **Australia — workplace funding via EAF plus a distinct NAATI credential.** The Employment Assistance Fund names Auslan *and* deafblind interpreting among fundable workplace services. Able Australia’s employment page lists vibrating pagers, braille tech and Auslan for DeafBlind employees. NAATI offers **Recognised Practising Deafblind Interpreter (RPDBI)**: already hold a NAATI Auslan or Deaf-interpreting credential **and** complete the Monash Deafblind Interpreting Course. Skills are recognised, not tested by NAATI. Communication-guide / community support remains a separate Able Australia role, not the RPDBI.

Canada recognises **Deafblind Intervenor** as an occupation (Job Bank outlook; George Brown Polytechnic C158; Ontario agencies such as Sensity). BC funds community intervener hours (CNIB Deafblind Community Services / Wavefront, extra $654,000 from April 2025) and a 2026 CNIB “Come to Work” training cohort for people who are blind, deafblind or have low vision. That is labour-market training and community intervener supply, not a standing in-post workplace CG grant analogous to AtW.

Ireland’s RISLI / ISL Act regime notes Deaf interpreters for tactile signing; no separate DeafBlind workplace funding scheme was found.

WFDB (2nd Global Report 2023; statement January 2024) asks every government for competencies, a training and certification programme, a registry and booking system, and fair pay. That is an advocacy specification, not a delivered product.

**What does not transfer to the UK.** US VR/Medicaid-waiver architecture and state legislatures; Norwegian National Insurance entitlement with no hours cap; Australian EAF dollar caps and NAATI’s monopoly-adjacent credential; Canadian college-to-agency intervenor pipeline. Do not print any of those as a missing UK statute.

## 118.1 Standing workplace model vs community SSP

The cleanest “standing funded model that includes work” in the asked set is **Norway’s NAV interpreter-guide service**, with Finland’s Kela dual-sensory interpreter service as a sibling. Both are social-insurance / national-agency entitlements the *person* draws on, including at work. They are the opposite institutional design from AtW (employer/employee claim against a cap).

The US SSP catalogue is the most-cited “model” in English-language advocacy. Treat it carefully. It is many programs, many payers, many hour caps, and it is mostly community access. Using it as “the Americans have workplace guide-communicators, the UK does not” is a category error. ADA still sits on the employer; SSP hours are a parallel, state-built service.

Australia is the closest *grant-to-workplace* cousin of AtW, and it does name deafblind interpreting on the EAF/Able pages. That is still Auslan-infrastructure plus a recognised-practising add-on, not a CG scheme.

## 118.2 Distinct registration / training

Distinct from ordinary SLI:

- **Australia NAATI RPDBI** — the only asked jurisdiction with a live, named national credential that is *not* just “Auslan interpreter who also does tactile.”
- **Canada** — college diploma pathway into a named occupation (intervenor), used mainly by specialist agencies and school boards.
- **United States** — RID practice paper + federal training-grant definition; **no** live RID DeafBlind exam.
- **Nordics** — the distinctiveness is in the *service product* (interpreter-guide) more than in a separate professional register visible from this pass.
- **Ireland** — tactile flagged as a Deaf-interpreter use-case inside RISLI, not a separate register.

WFDB wants all of those pieces in one national system. No asked jurisdiction has shipped the whole list.

## What is unpublished, contested, or non-transferable

- A single US federal SSP statute for workplace support — **does not exist**; the catalogue is state-level and self-reported.
- How often US SSP hours are actually used on an employer’s floor plate rather than for shopping and appointments — **unpublished** at national level.
- Whether Norway’s “no hours cap” survives workforce shortage in every county — availability is the stated limit; utilisation series not pulled on this pass.
- EU-wide interpreter-guide regulation — **not found**. Directive 2000/78 is general reasonable-accommodation. Member-state products vary.
- A transferable “best” model for the UK — **not a finding this prompt can make**. The designs are too different.
