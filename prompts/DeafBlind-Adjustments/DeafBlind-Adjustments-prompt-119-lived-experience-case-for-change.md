## Headline

The UK first-person file for **in-post DeafBlind workplace adjustments** is thin.

The one named, published, working-age employee who writes as DeafBlind *and* about the office is **Ben Fletcher** (Usher; IBM then Financial Times principal engineer). His 2019 FT Medium essay is the cleanest “treated as Deaf / hearing-normed even when colleagues know I am DeafBlind” account: baseball-cap glare, wobbly gait misread as drink, “communicative labour,” interpreter as co-author of workplace English, “the only Deafie in the village.” His 2016 SignVideo profile and 2019 Monki Gras talk add booking friction (“ideally two interpreters”) and the repeated ask to bring his own interpreter. His NHS / childbirth testimony is **out of this cell** — health access, not in-post RA.

Everything else in the live stack is one of four weaker types:

- **2014 Sense ATW0075** — two unnamed Sense employees: deafblind-manual vs cheaper-BSL challenge, 14-week renewal gap, 15→10 hours, “fifth of going rate,” £30k salaried cap. Documented. Historic. Already the named cost/rarity cluster in prompt 116. Do not reprint as current practice.
- **Survey titles that say “deafblind” and then publish BSL splits.** RNID / DeafATW Dec 2025–Jan 2026 n=358 is “deaf, deafblind and hard of hearing.” Case AB and the published tables are BSL-interpreter customers. No published DeafBlind-only row.
- **Entry-to-work programmes, not in-post testimony.** Deafblind UK Employability (announced 25 Nov 2025; live page 18–60, not in full-time work). Action Deafness Connect to Work Hertfordshire. These are jobseeking products.
- **Policy sentences, not first-person.** ASLI Pathways to Work response (30 Jun 2025): AtW “gives deaf jobseekers confidence… The same applies for deaf and deafblind people who work with deafblind interpreters, communicator guides.” Sense WPC evidence: promote and simplify AtW for complex needs. DeafATW AWS0121 (24 Mar 2026): Darren Townsend-Handscomb is an interpreter who specialises with deafblind professionals; the submission’s operational asks are BSL-system asks.

**Locked Out (27 Nov 2025)** is health and social care. Dual-sensory systems “unequipped” is an NHS finding. Do not stretch it into an employer HR cell.

**International qualitative (do not fill a UK cell):** Ehn, Lundin, Möller, Wahlqvist, Anderzen-Carlsson 2020, *International Journal of Qualitative Studies on Health and Well-being* — Swedish adults with Usher type 2 on work, lighting, afternoon meetings, tactile interpreting at staff meetings. PLOS One scoping review 18 Mar 2026 on acquired deafblindness and labour-market barriers (environmental / policy-heavy). Useful context; not UK testimony.

There is **no** published first-person UK account found on this pass of a DeafBlind employee describing *workplace navigation* (the 117 cell) or of an OH provider collapsing DeafBlind into Deaf. The “treated as Deaf” sentence rests on Fletcher plus the 2014 Sense “why not a BSL interpreter” challenge. That is two sources, twelve years apart, different employers. Do not generalise them into a national rate.

**Hard-don’t.** Do not print a case for change that treats absence of testimony as proof of widespread employer failure. The gap is the finding.

## 119.1 What the thin file actually supports

Two UK sources, twelve years apart, describe the same *mechanism*: an institution reaching for a BSL interpreter (or a hearing-office norm) when the person has asked for something else — deafblind manual, tactile, a cap against glare, two interpreters, a support worker who can travel. That is consistent with prompts 115–116 (RIDB is tiny; CG is not a register category; AtW has no DeafBlind tariff). It is **not** a prevalence figure and it is **not** a 2026 case series.

Fletcher is also a reminder that the adjustment that worked in his office was cultural and cheap (a talking-stick / ball so people stop interrupting; colleagues learning some BSL) as well as paid interpreting. Do not flatten his account into “AtW failed.”

## 119.2 The live policy ask is AtW operations plus RIDB supply

Nobody in the 2025–26 consultation stack published a “statutory workplace guide-communicator” ask. The live asks that mention DeafBlind employees are:

- keep AtW paying for deafblind interpreters / communicator guides as well as RSLIs (ASLI, one sentence);
- stop phone-only authentication and unexplained cuts (DeafATW, scheme-wide, with deafblind named as locked-out by phone auth);
- grow the RIDB list (£15k competency grant);
- run into-work programmes (Deafblind UK, Action Deafness).

That is smaller than a “case for a new legal product.” Record it at that size.

## 119.3 What is unpublished

- Any 2025–26 named DeafBlind employee describing a refused workplace guide, a failed orientation, or an OH assessment that listed “BSL interpreter” and stopped.
- A DeafBlind-only cut of the RNID/DeafATW 358.
- Published outcomes from Deafblind UK’s Employability Programme (participants’ in-work adjustment experience).
- A first-person UK account of tactile / hands-on interpreting at an employer’s staff meeting (the Swedish paper has that sentence; the UK file does not).

## What not to print

- A national rate of “employers treat DeafBlind staff as Deaf.”
- ATW0075 hours, rates or the £30k cap as if they were 2026 staff-guide rules.
- Locked Out health findings as workplace RA evidence.
- Fletcher’s labour-ward story as an employment-adjustment case.
- Rimmer, Clarke, Borys, Bussey, Clinton, Graham, Mrs W.
- Swedish or PLOS percentages in a UK cell.
- “The absence of testimony proves the adjustment is never made” — absence of testimony proves the published file is thin.
