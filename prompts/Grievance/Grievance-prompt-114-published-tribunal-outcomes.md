# Prompt 114 — Published tribunal / court outcomes involving deafness, BSL or DeafBlind status

**What this prompt is for.** A sample of published Employment Tribunal, EAT and court decisions in which deafness, BSL use or DeafBlind status is material. The question is what those published texts actually decide — workplace adjustment, disciplinary-without-interpreter, tribunal-administration miss, or something else. Prompt 114 is **not** a win-rate, a typical-outcome series, or a substitute for 113 (lived experience).

## Headline

The published slice is **mixed**, **small**, and **not typical**.

What the named texts actually decide:

- **Workplace process without an interpreter can succeed as a s.20 / s.15 claim.** *Thatcher v Premiere Care (Southern) Ltd* 2302883/2019: investigation meeting on 4 February 2019 ran without a sign interpreter; suspension notified without an interpreter or an adjusted letter. Unfair dismissal, direct disability discrimination and failure to make reasonable adjustments all succeeded. Two interpreters were present *at the tribunal hearing*.
- **How an employer treats the claimant’s usual interpreter can itself be unfavourable treatment.** *Miss C Bentley v Governing Body of Oaks Park High School* 3322426/2021 (reasons sent January 2025): profoundly deaf claimant; SLT perceived her as “unhelpful and difficult” in large part because managers did not sign and did not treat communication as a deafness issue. Claim succeeded in part, including s.15 on reprimanding her BSL interpreter on 24 September 2021. Remaining claims, including indirect discrimination, were dismissed.
- **Claimants also lose.** *Iqbal v Home Office HR Directorate* 2200324/2016: profoundly deaf Home Office worker; BSL booking-hours dispute plus unfair dismissal. Direct discrimination, failure to make reasonable adjustments and unfair dismissal **all dismissed**. Do not file as a tribunal-access failure; the hearing ran with representation.
- **Time still kills claims.** *Galloway v Lanarkshire Deaf Club* 8000548/2024 (Scotland, judgment sent 10 November 2025): profoundly deaf claimant, two BSL interpreters at the preliminary hearing; unfair-dismissal claim **dismissed as time-barred**. *Stinton v Cambria Automobiles (South East) Ltd* 3200016/2018: RA complaint out of time; just-and-equitable extension refused; other discrimination complaints dismissed. BSL interpreters and an intermediary *were* arranged for the hearing.
- **One documented tribunal-administration miss.** *Mr M Bentley v London Borough of Ealing* 2203096/2019: claimant had emailed a request for a sign-language interpreter; the tribunal did not action it; EJ Nicolle read the file on the morning of the 17 January 2020 CPH, could not proceed (claimant profoundly deaf and unable to lip-read; communication by handwritten notes only), vacated and relisted with an interpreter and a longer slot. Later costs judgment (6 August 2020) is about particularisation of a notice-pay claim, not about that miss. The miss is an *administration* fact. It is not a finding that “HMCTS has no BSL route” (prompt 110 already closed that door).
- **Rimmer is a service case, not an employer-grievance hearing failure.** *Rimmer v SSWP* 1802885/2022 and 1800113/2023: JSA/Jobcentre Plus at Leeds Park Place. Partial success on s.15 and RA for specified 2021–22 failures; several earlier BSL-auxiliary complaints dismissed on limitation or on the facts; total award £49,880 including aggravated and exemplary damages plus a training recommendation. Advised by Kirklees Citizens Advice and Law Centre; counsel John Horan. Do not recycle as evidence that Acas or the tribunal hearing itself failed.
- **Deaf organisations appear as respondents.** *Hedger v British Deaf Association* 3318925/2019 (Watford, 2022): hearing ran with two BSL interpreters. *Galloway v Lanarkshire Deaf Club* as above. *Vickers v Sense* 2600169/2022 and *Prime v Sense* 6017398/2024 are claims *against* the national deafblind charity; Vickers’s published preliminary judgment turns on the claimant’s eczema/allergies, not on the claimant being DeafBlind.

**No published ET/EAT judgment was found this pass in which the claimant’s own DeafBlind / tactile status is the material impairment.** Sense as respondent is not a DeafBlind-claimant series.

No published judgment was found that turns on “the employer missed its published internal SLA number, therefore discrimination.” Delay appears as part of a facts-package (*Abertawe* / s.123), not as an SLA-breach cause of action.

**Hard-don't.** Do not print a win-rate. Do not treat this list as typical. Do not file Rimmer or Iqbal as tribunal-hearing failures. Do not print that a missed SLA is automatically unlawful.

## 114.1 What the sample is allowed to support

It supports four narrow statements, each tied to a named text:

1. A workplace investigatory or disciplinary process run without a sign interpreter can be a successful s.20 (and, on the facts, s.13 / unfair-dismissal) claim — *Thatcher*.
2. Treatment of the claimant’s usual interpreter can be unfavourable treatment arising from disability — *C Bentley*.
3. A requested tribunal interpreter that is not booked can vacate a hearing — *M Bentley v Ealing*. The same file then shows the tribunal relisting with an interpreter. Absence of a published fulfilment protocol (110) and one documented miss can sit together without either being inflated into “HMCTS never provides BSL.”
4. Limitation still ends claims brought by deaf claimants — *Galloway*; part of *Rimmer*; the RA head in *Stinton*. Prompt 109’s clock is not theoretical.

It does **not** support: a national win-rate; “tribunals are inaccessible”; “Acas has no BSL”; “a missed SLA is unlawful”; “DeafBlind claimants lose because of tactile gaps.”

## 114.2 Rimmer and Iqbal — keep the cell clean

Rimmer’s facts are Jobcentre interpreter supply, LMS fields, a DEA email, and a training recommendation. The hearing itself had three named interpreters and a BSL-translated statement. Putting Rimmer in a “tribunal accessibility” column repeats the error 110 already boxed.

Iqbal lost on the workplace booking regime. The tribunal heard her. That is a merits loss, not a process-access holding.

## 114.3 DeafBlind remains an empty published-judgment cell

Sense appears as a respondent. No opened judgment this pass turns on the claimant being DeafBlind or needing a tactile / communicator-guide adjustment at work or at the hearing. Treat that as a publication gap, not as proof that no such claim has ever been brought.
