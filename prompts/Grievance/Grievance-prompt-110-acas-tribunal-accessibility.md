# Prompt 110 — Is Acas early conciliation and the grievance / tribunal hearing accessible to a BSL-user or a DeafBlind claimant?

**Master index prompt 110.** Load-bearing / most-sceptical file in the Grievance and escalation batch (prompts 109–114). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Establish what Acas, the employer (at the grievance meeting), and HMCTS / the employment tribunal actually publish about accessible communication for a Deaf BSL-using or DeafBlind claimant. Separate three rooms: the employer’s grievance hearing, Acas’s conciliation conversations, and the tribunal hearing. Do not assert that the telephone model is a confirmed barrier without a source. If a published route exists, log it. If DeafBlind is unnamed, log that absence.

## Headline

The opening hypothesis — “Acas has no published accessibility policy” — is **wrong for the helpline and only half-right for conciliation**. Acas publishes BSL and relay routes. They are split across pages and they are **request-based**, not an automatic video-relay conciliation product.

- **Helpline (0300 123 1100):** InterpretersLive! video relay; Relay UK text relay `18001 0300 123 1100`; an accessibility form that can produce a BSL or lip-reading video call, usually within two working days (`acas.org.uk/request-advice-if-you-cannot-call`).
- **Early-conciliation notification (0300 123 1122):** online form is the default; Relay UK `18001 0300 123 1122`; spoken-language interpreter on request. A dedicated block on `acas.org.uk/notify` headed “Accessibility during early conciliation” tells the claimant to declare Relay UK, “an interpreter”, “a British Sign Language interpreter”, or “email only contact” at notification or when the conciliator is allocated.
- **How conciliation itself runs:** Acas’s process page (30 January 2026) is still voice-shaped. If the claimant has said they cannot use a telephone, Acas will email. The conciliator “will speak with you or your representative.” There is **no published page that says the conciliator will join an InterpretersLive! call, book an in-person BSL interpreter, or run a three-way video session as standard.** Email-only and “tell your conciliator you need a BSL interpreter” are the documented routes. Whether that request is fulfilled by VRS, by a booked interpreter, or by writing only is **not published**.

**DeafBlind is unnamed** on every Acas page checked this pass. No tactile BSL, deafblind manual, hands-on signing, or braille route is listed. The closest published fallback is email-only contact plus the generic accessibility form.

The **employer’s grievance meeting** is a different duty-holder. Acas’s live how-to (step 4 of the formal grievance procedure, page still live 10 September 2026) tells the employer to “arrange for an interpreter if the employee has difficulty speaking English” and to “consider whether reasonable adjustments are needed for a disabled employee.” That is guidance, not a booking SLA. The statutory companion (ERA 1996 s.10 / Acas Code 1 paras 35–36) is a colleague or union official — **not** the BSL interpreter. The interpreter, if provided, sits under Equality Act s.20 as an auxiliary aid. No named published judgment was found of an employer refusing a BSL interpreter *at the grievance hearing itself* (as distinct from refusing the underlying workplace adjustment that caused the grievance).

At the **employment tribunal**, BSL is provided on request, funded by HMCTS, through the Ministry of Justice language-services contracts: spoken languages via thebigword; non-spoken / BSL via Clarion UK Ltd (thebigword acquired Clarion in March 2024; Lords Public Services Committee, 16 January 2026). It is **not** automatic “as of course.” The claimant must tell the tribunal. Practitioner evidence (Shoosmiths / Lexology, December 2023) is that bookings are often confirmed only in the week before the hearing and the interpreter may appear on CVP. One named ET administrative miss: *Bentley v London Borough of Ealing* 2203096/2019 — the claimant had asked for a sign-language interpreter, the tribunal did not action it, the 17 January 2020 preliminary hearing could not proceed and was vacated. That is one case, not a rate.

**Hard-don't.** Do not print that Acas “has no BSL route.” It does, for the helpline, and it invites a BSL-interpreter declaration for conciliation. Do not print that the telephone model is therefore accessible in practice — fulfilment of the conciliation-stage request is unpublished. Do not print that a tribunal interpreter is provided automatically. Do not file *Rimmer* or *Iqbal* as tribunal-hearing interpreter failures; they are Jobcentre / workplace cases.

## 110.1 Three rooms, three duty-holders

Do not collapse these.

1. **The employer’s grievance meeting.** Duty-holder: the employer, Equality Act s.20 (auxiliary aid / PCP). Acas tells the employer to consider adjustments and to arrange “an interpreter if the employee has difficulty speaking English.” That sentence is spoken-language-shaped; BSL arrives, if at all, through the disabled-employee adjustment bullet, not through a named BSL booking standard. The s.10 companion is a different person from the interpreter. Failure to provide a reasonably required interpreter at the meeting can be pleaded as a further s.20 failure. No named published judgment of that specific failure was found this pass.

2. **Acas early conciliation.** Duty-holder: Acas as a public body running a statutory pre-action process. Published routes exist (notify-page declaration list; helpline InterpretersLive!; Relay UK; email-only). The *conversation* Acas describes is still “we’ll call you / we’ll email you / the conciliator will speak with you.” That is not the same as a published VRS conciliation product. Logging the absence of a fulfilment specification is the honest finding; inventing a barrier beyond that is not.

3. **The employment tribunal hearing.** Duty-holder: HMCTS / the tribunal, via the Equal Treatment Bench Book and the MoJ language-services contracts, plus the tribunal’s own common-law duty to make adjustments so the hearing is fair (*Rackham*; *J v K* — the ET adjustment duty is not s.20 itself). BSL is arranged on request through Clarion / thebigword special services, at no cost to the party. It is not auto-listed when the claim mentions deafness.

Northern Ireland is a fourth room (LRA). This pass found no LRA BSL product to cite.

## 110.2 What Acas actually publishes — and where it stops

The sceptical instruction on this prompt was: if there is no policy, log the absence. There *is* policy text. It is fragmented.

- Helpline BSL = InterpretersLive! plus a pre-booked BSL/lip-reading video call via the accessibility form (2-working-day aim).
- Notification BSL = declare it on the form or tell the conciliator. Relay UK is specified with a number. Email-only is specified as a contact mode.
- Conciliation conversation = call unless the person has said they cannot use a telephone, in which case email. “Speak with you or your representative” remains the process sentence.

What is not on the public pages: a sentence that the conciliator will join the same InterpretersLive! service used for the helpline; a DeafBlind method; a service-standard for how many days after allocation a declared BSL request is fulfilled; any volume figure for how many EC cases declare BSL.

A claimant who can use InterpretersLive! for the *helpline* still has to get from “I declared BSL” to an actual interpreted conversation with the conciliator. That middle step is unpublished. Prompt 109’s clock still runs while it is being sorted out. A representative (union, friend, lawyer) can take the conciliation calls — Acas expressly allows that — which is a practical workaround, not a published BSL product.

## 110.3 Tribunal hearings — request, contract, one named miss

The public message from the judiciary is consistent: tell us early, we will arrange, you do not pay. The operational message from practitioners is that confirmation comes late and the interpreter may be remote. The contract message from the Lords committee (January 2026) is Clarion for BSL, now inside thebigword’s group, with off-contract fallback from noon the day before.

*Bentley v Ealing* is the one published employment-tribunal file this pass found in which staff failed to action a requested sign-language interpreter and a hearing had to be vacated. The judge recorded that the only communication possible on the day was handwritten notes, which was not good enough for a profoundly deaf claimant who could not lip-read. That is a documented miss. It is also six years old, one case, and the claim later ran into non-attendance on the relisted date. Do not turn it into “tribunals typically fail to book BSL.”

*Rimmer* stays in the Jobcentre column. *Iqbal* stays in the workplace-OH column.

## 110.4 DeafBlind

Across Acas contact / notify / request-advice, HMCTS T424 landing page, the Scotland ET BSL video, and the Equal Treatment Bench Book extracts read this pass, **DeafBlind, tactile BSL, and deafblind manual are not named** as a conciliation or hearing product. Email-only, Relay UK, large print / braille in the Bench Book’s general disability chapter, and “tell us your needs” are the available public sentences. That absence is the finding. Do not fill it with a Sense or Deafblind UK campaign ask — that is prompt 113.
