# Prompt 111 — What free or low-cost advice and representation exists at grievance / tribunal stage, and is BSL built in?

**Master index prompt 111.** Cost and advocacy file in the Grievance and escalation batch (prompts 109–114). Compiled 10 September 2026. **What this prompt is for.** Map the live funding and advocacy stack a Deaf BSL-using or DeafBlind claimant can actually use once informal resolution has failed: legal aid (what is in scope after LASPO), Civil Legal Advice, EASS, FRU, Advocate, Disability Law Service, Citizens Advice / law centres, unions, and named Deaf charities. ## Headline

Employment-tribunal **representation is not in the ordinary legal-aid scheme**. LASPO 2012 took most employment work out of scope. What remains for a discrimination claim in England and Wales is:

- **Legal Help / Civil Legal Advice** for initial advice and case-preparation help on a discrimination matter, via the CLA gateway (0345 345 4345), means-tested. CLA’s own GOV.UK page says you can use its free interpreting service if you are deaf or hard of hearing and need a BSL interpreter.
- **A legal-aid certificate for representation in the county court** if the claim is brought there (goods/services discrimination), not the usual route for a workplace reasonable-adjustment claim.
- **Exceptional Case Funding** under LASPO s.10 if refusing representation at the employment tribunal would risk a Convention-rights breach. ECF is a separate determination on top of means and merits. It is not the default product. LAG’s discrimination briefing is blunt: “Generally, legal aid for representation will not be granted for the ET.”

There is **no issue fee** to start an employment tribunal claim. Fees introduced in 2013 were quashed in *R (Unison) v Lord Chancellor* [2017] UKSC 51. A 2024 MoJ consultation proposed a £55 issue fee; it is **not** a live fee on this pass.

The free-and-low-cost stack that *does* exist is advice-heavy and representation-thin:

| Route | What it does | BSL built in as standard? |
|---|---|---|
| EASS | Informal advice and template letters. Explicitly **not** legal advice or representation. | **Yes for contact:** Convo video relay, Mon–Fri 09:00–19:00 and Sat 10:00–14:00; textphone 0808 800 0084. Partnership with RAD Deaf Law Centre is published on the contact page. |
| Civil Legal Advice | Means-tested Legal Help on discrimination. | **Yes for the gateway call:** GOV.UK CLA page lists a free BSL interpreter for deaf/hard-of-hearing callers; Relay UK; textphone. Whether the *referred solicitor* then runs the file in BSL is unpublished. |
| Disability Law Service | Discrimination Legal Help contract (plus community care / housing / public law). Advice, casework if eligible and if capacity. | Contact is phone/email. **No published “BSL as standard on every file” line.** DLS says at least 75% of its board and half its staff are deaf or disabled (LAG, “Why we must be heard”). | Citizens Advice / law centres | Local advice and some casework. Rimmer was advised by Kirklees Citizens Advice and Law Centre. | Local and variable. Law Society find-a-solicitor filter can list firms that “provide British Sign Language interpreters.” Not a national CAB BSL product. |

| Advocate (Bar pro bono) | Volunteer barristers. Listed on the judiciary’s March 2026 ET advice sheet. | No published BSL-as-standard protocol found. |
| Unions | Representation is a membership product, discretionary, merits-tested. | Built-in BSL is union-specific and unpublished as a national rule. One anonymous JustAnswer post (Sep 2025) alleges Unite provided an interpreter only after a complaint and that an earlier tribunal was run without one. **Anecdote, not a named judgment.** |
| BDA / RNID / SignHealth / Sense | Signposting, policy, AtW evidence. No public “we will represent you at the ET” product found for these brands this pass. (Do not confuse the British *Dietetic* Association’s union-legal page with the British Deaf Association.) | Helplines exist; BSL access to those helplines is a different cell from ET representation. |
| EHRC | Occasional strategic funding. Precedent / public-interest cases, not a volume service. | Not a claimant hotline for ET representation. |

No survey or official series was found that counts how many Deaf/BSL or DeafBlind claimants abandoned a grievance or ET claim because of cost or communication-access. The closest published first-person on *access to justice* cost is still AtW-shaped (RNID 2026 survey; AWS0095) — that is prompt 52–54 / 87 territory, not this cell. Counsel magazine (Feb 2025) on the Deaf Legal Network records Deaf people being asked to bring family members to interpret at solicitors’ offices; that is legal-*services* access, not an ET statistic.

**Hard-don't.** Do not print that legal aid covers employment-tribunal representation as of right. Do not print that EASS will take the case to the tribunal. Do not treat a helpline VRS product as a representation product. Do not generalise the Unite forum post.

## 111.1 What LASPO actually leaves standing

Three products get talked about as if they were one.

1. **Legal Help (CLA).** In scope for discrimination, including workplace discrimination advice. Means-tested. The gateway is a phone (or BSL-interpreted, or Relay UK) conversation that can refer to a contracted provider. It funds advice and help preparing documents. It does not, by itself, put a barrister in the tribunal.

2. **A representation certificate.** Available in the county court for in-scope discrimination. A workplace RA claim is almost always an employment tribunal claim, where a certificate is not the ordinary product.

3. **Exceptional Case Funding.** The residual safety net. Two determinations: exceptional-case (Convention-rights necessity or risk) and ordinary qualification (means + merits). *EB* [2026] EWHC 402 (Admin) is a 2026 restatement of that structure in another context; it does not create an ET-discrimination entitlement. LAG’s working description remains the honest one: representation at the ET is generally not granted.

A claimant who is told “there is legal aid for discrimination” and hears “someone will take my tribunal case” has been sold the wrong product. Write the distinction in any public sentence.

There is still no fee to issue the ET1. *Unison* stands. Cost barriers that remain are advocates’ time, expert reports, and the unpaid hours of being a litigant in person — not a counter fee.

## 111.2 Routes that exist, graded by what they actually do

**Advice only, BSL contact published:** EASS (Convo + RAD partnership) and the CLA gateway. EASS will not represent. CLA may open a Legal Help file.

**Advice and some casework, BSL not published as standard:** DLS (discrimination contract, deaf/disabled-staffed), local CAB and law centres. Rimmer’s law-centre support is a named example of this layer working; it is one case and it was a Jobcentre *service* claim, not an employer-grievance claim.

**Representation, geography- and volunteer-limited, BSL protocol unpublished:** FRU (London/SE), Advocate, occasional union legal officers, occasional law-centre advocates. FRU is explicit that a volunteer is not guaranteed and that hearings listed for more than three days are hard to staff.

**Not found as ET advocates this pass:** SignHealth, RNID, Sense, British Deaf Association. They remain signpost and policy organisations for this cell. The Deaf Legal Network is an emerging referral conversation, not a fulfilment desk.

A BSL interpreter at the *hearing* (prompt 110, Clarion / HMCTS) is not counsel. A claimant can have a first-class tribunal interpreter and still be a litigant in person.

## 111.3 Barriers that are documented — and those that are not

Documented:

- Structural: ET representation out of ordinary scope (LASPO).
- Structural: FRU geography.
- Structural: CLA provider capacity described as thin.
- Qualitative: Deaf Legal Network / Counsel 2025 — solicitors asking Deaf clients to bring family interpreters.
- Anecdotal: one anonymous 2025 forum post about a union interpreter arriving late and a previous LiP tribunal without an interpreter.

Not documented as a number:

- How many Deaf or DeafBlind people did not start or did not finish an ET claim because they could not get an advocate or could not get BSL at the advice stage.
- ECF grant/refusal counts for this claim type.
- A charity survey specifically on grievance/ET access cost (RNID 2026 is AtW).

Do not fill those cells with the TUC 82% adjustment-wait figure (prompt 98) or the AtW 109-day figure (prompts 52–54). Wrong institutional actor.

## 111.4 Interaction with 109 and 110

Finding a volunteer at FRU, an ECF determination, or a union decision to support all take calendar time. Prompt 109: the grievance does not pause the clock; only Acas notification does. A claimant who waits for an advocate before notifying Acas can lose the window. Prompt 110: Acas and HMCTS will (on request) provide a communication channel; they will not provide a representative.
