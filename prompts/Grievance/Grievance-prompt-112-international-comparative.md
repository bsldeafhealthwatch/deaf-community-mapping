## Headline

**International — does not fill a UK cell.**

No jurisdiction in the asked set publishes a “sign-language interpreter will be in the room as standard, without a request” product at the pre-litigation conciliation / mediation stage. The dominant published model is the same shape as Acas and HMCTS (prompts 110–111): **declare the need, the agency pays, fulfilment is request-based.**

What is thicker than the UK public pages, in places:

- **United States (EEOC / ADA).** Two 2005 EEOC Q&As (still live on eeoc.gov, pages touched 10 September 2026) tell parties to give advance notice for a sign-language interpreter at mediation, and tell mediation providers they must pay for it absent undue hardship. The provider’s overall resources, not the fee for that one session, set the hardship test. CART must be provided if an interpreter would not be equally effective. DOJ’s ADA Mediation Program Q&A says the Key Bridge Foundation “ensures that all mediations are accessible” and is “skilled at handling requests” including sign-language interpreters, at no cost. That is a named delivery contractor plus a published payment rule. It is still request-plus-notice, not an automatic interpreter at every session. EEOC mediation itself is **voluntary** (unlike Acas notification, which is mandatory even if the parties then decline to talk).
- **Canada (CHRC).** Published Accommodation Policy for members of the public lists “providing a sign language interpreter” and “video relay service (VRS), closed captioning … or other relay services” as example accommodations. Request-based; if the specific ask cannot be met, the Commission explains in writing. A CHRC mediation video (January 2025) tells parties to “talk to your mediator to make arrangements.” CRTC 2025-95 (14 May 2025) is a telecoms-regulator proceeding guide, not an employment-conciliation rule, but it does name **tactile interpretation** as a requestable accommodation at appearing hearings — the only official document in this pass that puts DeafBlind tactile on a public conciliation-adjacent page. Charter s.14 is a *court* right to an interpreter if the party is deaf; it does not automatically rewrite CHRC mediation.
- **Australia (Fair Work Commission).** Practice note *Fair hearings*: parties can request an interpreter for a conference or hearing at no cost; a Member who thinks one is needed may adjourn until one can be provided. A separate bullet covers a room with audio equipment for a “hearing impaired” party. Fair Work Ombudsman publishes Auslan NES videos and points deaf callers to the National Relay Service, not to a standing Auslan conciliation desk. Auslan in courts has recommended tandem-interpreter standards (JCDI); that is court standards, not FWC conciliation-as-standard.
- **EU.** Directive 2000/78 Art. 5 is the reasonable-accommodation duty. It does not create a Union-wide pre-litigation conciliation product, and it does not set a days-clock. Member-state labour inspectorates and equality bodies run their own mediation. No member-state statute found this pass that puts a national sign-language interpreter in every labour-mediation session as of right.

On **delay as evidence**: the closest official sentence is EEOC *Enforcement Guidance on Reasonable Accommodation and Undue Hardship* — “the employer should act promptly to provide the reasonable accommodation. Unnecessary delays can result in a violation of the ADA.” That is a promptness rule with examples (a parking-space request sat with a supervisor). It is **not** a holding that breach of an employer’s self-set SLA number is, by itself, discrimination. No named case in the asked jurisdictions was found that turns on “the employer missed its published 10-working-day ticket SLA.” Prompt 100’s finding stands: timely / prompt language, no private-employer days-clock.

What does **not** transfer to the UK: EEOC mediation is optional and sits inside an agency-investigation model; Acas early conciliation is a jurisdictional precondition to issuing most claims (prompt 109). US Title I undue-hardship is a federal statutory defence with a published cost-example for interpreters at mediation; the UK test is s.20 “reasonable to have to take.” Canadian Charter s.14 is a constitutional court-interpreter right with no UK counterpart of that form. Australian FWC “Member may adjourn” is a commission power, not an Acas power.

**Hard-don't.** Do not write “the US provides ASL as standard at EEOC mediation” without the request-and-notice qualifier. Do not import EEOC delay language into a UK s.20 cell as if it were authority.

## 112.1 Accessible conciliation abroad is request-based too

The load-bearing comparison for 110 is not “other countries do this automatically.” It is: the US, Canada and Australia have **written down the payment rule and the request route** more tightly than Acas has written down fulfilment of a declared BSL need.

- EEOC: provider pays; hardship is institutional resources; CART if SLI is not equally effective; advance notice expected.
- CHRC: example list includes SLI and VRS; written refusal reasons.
- FWC: no-cost interpreter on request; Member can stop the conference until one arrives.

Acas (prompt 110) publishes the declaration list and the helpline InterpretersLive! product, and does **not** publish how a declared BSL interpreter is delivered inside the conciliator conversation. That gap is a UK publication gap, not proof that the US runs a different legal model.

DeafBlind tactile appears once, in a Canadian telecoms-regulator bulletin. Do not promote that to “Canada provides tactile interpretation at employment mediation.”

## 112.2 Delay is relevant; an SLA number is not the rule

EEOC’s “unnecessary delays can result in a violation” is the cleanest official sentence in the set. It matches the *shape* of UK s.123(4)(b) and *Abertawe* (prompt 109): time and reasonableness are fact questions. It does not match “the employer missed the 10-day HR SLA, therefore discrimination.” No such holding was found. Workplace-ASL refusal cases (Walmart, Didlake, the Amazon pleading) are about not providing the aid at work, not about missing an internal ticket target.

## 112.3 Do not paste these into a UK cell

Acas remains a mandatory notification step with a 12-week outer period and a Day-A/Day-B clock (109). EEOC mediation is a voluntary diversion from investigation. Mixing those two produces a false “the UK should copy the EEOC 84-day median” sentence. The 84-day figure is EEOC’s own mediation-processing average, not a reasonable-adjustment SLA.
