## Prompts

**109. The escalation route itself — internal grievance, ACAS early conciliation, and employment tribunal, and where a missed SLA or refused adjustment fits legally**
> As a UK employment law researcher, using the ACAS Code of Practice on disciplinary and grievance procedures, the Employment Rights Act 1996, the Employment Tribunals Act 1996 (early conciliation requirement), and the Equality Act 2010 time limits for bringing a disability-discrimination claim, establish (1) the correct, current sequence and time limits for escalating a missed adjustment or refused reasonable adjustment — internal grievance, then mandatory ACAS early conciliation, then an employment tribunal claim, including the standard three-month-less-one-day tribunal time limit for an Equality Act claim and how ACAS early conciliation affects that clock, (2) whether a missed internal SLA (as distinct from an outright refusal) is, on its own, evidence a tribunal has accepted as contributing to a finding of failure to make reasonable adjustments, citing any named case, (3) whether an agency, contract, or outsourced worker (per prompts 97–102's territory) faces any different or additional procedural step in identifying who the correct respondent is (the agency, the end-user employer, or both), and (4) what is unpublished or contested. Flag thin, unpublished, contested, or anecdotal evidence rather than presenting it as settled. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL. Do not print any claim that a missed SLA is automatically unlawful or automatically evidence of discrimination — a tribunal weighs facts case-by-case, and this prompt must not manufacture a fixed rule where none exists.

**110. Accessibility of the grievance and ACAS conciliation process itself — is a BSL interpreter provided, and what happens for a DeafBlind claimant**
> As a UK workplace-accessibility researcher, using ACAS's own published guidance and accessibility policy, published employment tribunal guidance on reasonable adjustments for hearings (HMCTS/Ministry of Justice guidance on tribunal accessibility), and any documented case, charity report (SignHealth, RNID, BDA, Sense), or news report describing a Deaf, BSL-using, or DeafBlind claimant's experience of a grievance hearing, ACAS conciliation call, or tribunal hearing, establish (1) whether ACAS's early-conciliation process (which is often conducted by telephone) has any published accessible-communication route for a Deaf or BSL-using claimant (e.g. video relay, textphone, in writing only), and what a DeafBlind claimant's route would be given ACAS's process is not designed around video communication at all, (2) whether HMCTS/employment tribunal guidance provides for a BSL interpreter at a tribunal hearing as a matter of course or only on specific request, and any documented case of an interpreter failing to attend or being refused at a tribunal or grievance hearing, and (3) what is unpublished, contested, or a single documented case that should not be generalised. Flag thin, unpublished, contested, or anecdotal evidence — this is likely to surface little to no ACAS-specific published accessibility policy, and that absence should be logged as the finding rather than papered over. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL.

**111. Legal aid, cost, and advocacy support at the grievance/tribunal stage**

Flag thin, unpublished, contested, or anecdotal evidence. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL.

### Notes for this batch

- **Gate prompt: 109.** The time-limit and respondent-identification findings govern how 110–111 are read — if the three-month tribunal time limit is confirmed as tight relative to any documented SLA delay (from 97–102), that interaction is itself worth flagging as a headline finding: a slow internal process could functionally run out a claimant's tribunal window.
- **Prompt 110 is the load-bearing/most-sceptical prompt in this batch.** ACAS's telephone-based conciliation model is a plausible structural gap for Deaf/BSL-using claimants, but this must not be asserted without a source — if no published ACAS accessibility policy is found, log the absence as the finding, not a confirmed barrier.
- **Prompt 114 needs care around sample size.** Published tribunal decisions are a small, non-representative slice of all claims (most settle or are withdrawn) — any finding here must state that limitation rather than implying the published cases represent typical outcomes.
- **Hard-don't for this batch:** do not print any claim that a missed SLA, a specific grievance outcome, or ACAS's process is unlawful, non-compliant, or automatically evidence of discrimination without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment. A tribunal weighs "reasonableness" on the facts; this batch must not manufacture a bright-line rule.
- **Held-back legs:** none — six prompts, because the topic splits cleanly into the escalation-route mechanics, ACAS/hearing accessibility specifically, cost/advocacy-access barriers, international comparison, lived experience, and the published-outcomes data question, each governed by a different institutional actor (ACAS, HMCTS, EHRC/legal aid bodies).
- **Relationship to 97–102 and 103–108:** this batch assumes an SLA has already been missed or an adjustment already refused (97–102) or a recruitment-stage request already failed (103–108) and picks up at the point the employee/candidate seeks formal redress. Do not re-litigate whether an SLA exists here — that is settled ground from 97–102.
- **Relationship to 20–33:** DWP/Jobcentre complaints (prompt 33) are a different ladder. Do not paste a DWP complaints finding into an employer-grievance or ACAS cell.
- **Do not conflate with Graeme's personal OH/HR thread** — this batch produces general, citable evidence for the dossier, not advice on an individual grievance or claim.
- **England, Wales and Scotland** share the ACAS early-conciliation requirement; Northern Ireland uses the Labour Relations Agency. Do not treat an England finding as covering NI, or a Scotland grievance practice as an England rule.
