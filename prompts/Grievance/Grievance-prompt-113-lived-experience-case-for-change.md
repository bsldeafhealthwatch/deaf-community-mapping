## Headline

Testimony on **this** ladder is thin.

What can be named without inflating it:

- **A published employer-grievance / disciplinary-BSL case with a first-person frame inside the judgment.** *Mr M Strutt v Royal Mail Group Ltd* 3313411/2020 (reserved judgment published 7 January 2026). The claimant, in his own expression “profoundly Deaf,” raised a bullying and harassment grievance against a colleague in early 2020. The claims before the tribunal were about Royal Mail’s *handling of that grievance* and the later dismissal for gross misconduct — including an alleged failure to make reasonable adjustments, “in particular in relation to the use of BSL interpreters,” during the grievance and the disciplinary procedure. Unfair dismissal **succeeded**. The disability-discrimination complaint **was dismissed**. That split matters: a Deaf employee can win on fairness of dismissal and lose on the BSL-adjustment head in the same file. Do not print the case as “the tribunal found the grievance was inaccessible.”
- **A published grievance that the deaf claimant experienced as being spoken over.** *Mrs N MacLennan v Royal Mail Group Ltd* 4102115/2022 (Aberdeen, 23 August 2022). Born deaf; BSL and lip-reading; two hearing aids; tinnitus. Raised a grievance in late 2020; it progressed to stage two and was not upheld apart from one minor point. The written reasons record that she “was very upset … felt he had spoken over her and been aggressive and loud. She had felt intimidated.” She raised an appeal and did not pursue it. Lipspeakers and a BSL interpreter assisted *at the tribunal hearing*. This is a hearing-record of how a grievance *felt*, not a holding that every Royal Mail grievance is run without BSL.
- **A published line that treats the workplace interpreter as the claimant’s voice.** *Miss C Bentley v Oaks Park High School* 3322426/2021 (114). “The Claimant described her BSL interpreter as her ears and her voice. We accept that description.” The successful s.15 head was the reprimand of that interpreter on 24 September 2021. First-person inside a judgment, not a magazine interview.
- **Acas-provided BSL, union-stage BSL contested — forum-sourced, named.** Posts on JustAnswer (March and July 2025) in the name of **Nesarajah Navaratnarajah**, later heading a London Central ET claim against Unite the Union, state: he requested a BSL interpreter to go through Unite’s legal advice; the officer “advised he was unsure how to facilitate this”; Unite later booked an interpreter for a May 2024 meeting which was cancelled after a Sainsbury’s settlement; “I contacted Acas … They did provide BSL interpreters, however they cannot offer legal advice, only consultation.” Treat as **named, unverified forum testimony**, consistent with 110 (Acas publishes a BSL route) and 111 (EASS/CLA/unions are not a single representation product). Do not promote the posts to a published judgment.
- **Rimmer, in his own words, is still a Jobcentre sentence.** “I still feel a bit angry about the wasted years but I’m pleased that the truth has finally come out. I have been telling the DWP about the difficulties I have faced for years and finally someone has believed me.” (*The Guardian*, 28 May 2024.) That is first-person. It is not an employer-grievance / Acas-conciliation sentence. Leave it in the JCP column.

**DeafBlind first-person on this ladder: none found.** No named DeafBlind employee or candidate describing a grievance meeting, an Acas conversation, or an ET hearing in their own words.

**Policy ask that is actually sourced.** Deaf Unity’s BSL employment-rights guide (21 October 2024) and Deaf Umbrella’s *How to ask for support* pack (PDF in circulation August 2025) tell people to raise a grievance and then go to Acas / the tribunal. That is a how-to, not a campaign demand for a new statutory product. Locked Out’s adult-pathway ask is a different domain. No sourced “give every Deaf claimant a publicly funded ET advocate” demand was found in a first-person employment-grievance piece this pass.

**Hard-don't.** Do not generalise Strutt + MacLennan + C Bentley into “Deaf grievances are inaccessible.” Do not file Rimmer here as an employer case. Do not treat JustAnswer as a judgment. Do not invent a DeafBlind voice.

## 113.1 What “thin” is allowed to mean

Thin does not mean “nothing happened.” It means the public first-person archive for *this pipeline stage* is a handful of judgment extracts and one forum thread. That is the same shape as prompt 91 (neurodivergence lived experience) and prompt 101 (HR-SLA lived experience): working-age Deaf testimony exists, it is not a survey, and older-age / DeafBlind voices on this specific ladder were not found.

A case for change that is honest on this evidence is:

1. Grievance and disciplinary meetings are the meetings where an interpreter is most load-bearing (*Strutt* pleaded it; *Thatcher* in 114 won on an investigation without one; *C Bentley* won on the interpreter being reprimanded).
2. Winning the unfair-dismissal head does not automatically win the BSL-adjustment head (*Strutt*).
3. Acas can be experienced as having BSL and still not being a lawyer (Navaratnarajah forum; 110–111).
4. Time still ends the claim even when the hearing is fully interpreted (*Galloway* in 114).

That is not a statute. It is the pattern the named files will bear.

## 113.2 Do not pad with the wrong first-person

Rimmer’s anger at wasted years is real and published. Putting it in an employer-grievance paragraph implies the DWP was his employer in the s.39 sense. It was not. Bussey and the Little Mix mothers are access-to-services first-person. They do not describe an Acas certificate or an ET1.

## 113.3 Case for change — sourced, not invented

If a later briefing needs an ask, the live hooks already on the dossier are: Acas publishing *how* a declared BSL need is fulfilled inside the conciliator conversation (110); CLA/FRU geography and ECF rarity (111); the 3-month clock that does not wait for an advocate (109). None of those asks is created by 113. 113 only shows that the people who have spoken publicly about this ladder are few, mixed in outcome, and so far not DeafBlind.
