## Headline

There is **no official UK prevalence or co-occurrence figure** for autism, ADHD or dyslexia among Deaf BSL-users. The live UK numbers that do exist measure something else: CRIDE 2025 reports **24%** of deaf children as having an “additional need” (not condition-split; not BSL-split). NDCS restates that as “1 in 4”. The BSL Advisory Board’s *Locked Out* (27 Nov 2025) uses a wider **30–40%** “additional disabilities” band for people deaf from an early age, and states that **3.5% of autistic children are deaf** — that 3.5% is a Board-report claim, not an ONS/NHS statistical release, and the underlying study was not independently opened on this pass. The frequently recycled **1–2% of deaf children have ASD** figure in UK academic papers is **US literature**, not a UK census or NHS rate. Record the UK autism-in-BSL-users cell as **no reliable figure found**.

“**DeafDisabled**” and “**neurodivergent Deaf**” are **not UK policy or NICE terms**. GOV.UK’s inclusive-language guide treats Deaf/BSL community identity and “neurodivergent” (autism, ADHD, dyslexia) as separate vocabularies. *Locked Out* uses “additional disabilities”, “neurodevelopmental conditions” and “intersectional”. Do not print DeafDisabled as if it were a statutory category.

On the pathway: **NICE CG128** (under-19s autism) tells local autism teams to have the skills *or access to professionals* for children with “severe hearing impairment”. That is not a mandate for a BSL-fluent assessor, a BSL-validated instrument, or a combined Deaf+autism clinic. **NICE NG87** (ADHD) likewise lists hearing problems as something the information offer should take into account; it does not create a BSL diagnostic product.

What *does* exist, for **children only**, is a UK research stack built around **National Deaf CAMHS (NDCAMHS / Deaf CAMHS)**:

- **ADOS-2 Deaf adaptation** — first validation, Wright et al., *Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders*, 2021 (online 24 Mar 2021); n=122 deaf children aged 2–18, 63 with ASD; sensitivity 73% / specificity 71% against a blinded NICE-standard NDCAMHS assessment. The authors’ own recommendation: for a child whose first language is BSL the trained assessor should be **fluent in that sign language**; BSL Level 3 is **not sufficient**; do not run the play-based assessment through an interpreter as the primary interactant.
- **ADI-R Deaf adaptation** and **SRS-2 Deaf adaptation** (the latter translated into BSL and validated in England, n=198) sit in the same MRC/NDCAMHS programme.
- Deaf CAMHS pages (e.g. MFT Northern arm, updated 6 Nov 2025) state that the service **offers assessment and support for autism and ADHD** for severely to profoundly deaf children up to 18.

That is a **specialist child mental-health service**, nationally commissioned, not a universal local autism-team default. A Deaf BSL-using child on an ordinary ICB autism waiting list is not automatically inside NDCAMHS.

For **adults**, *Locked Out* is explicit: “there is a rapidly-increasing demand for adult ADHD and autism assessments in the general population, but **no specialist NHS or private pathways for deaf BSL users**.” Adapted tools exist for deaf *children*; the same report says there are **no clinical norms for deaf adults** and **no BSL questionnaires for ADHD**. The Board’s action list (same day, 27 Nov 2025) therefore *asks* government to “**Establish national adult deaf clinical assessment and support pathways for autism and ADHD**.” That is an ask, not a live commissioned product. Do not print it as a right or as a service that already exists.

General NHS autism waits are long and have **no Deaf/BSL split**: NHS England *Autism Statistics, April 2025 to March 2026* (published 14 May 2026) recorded **270,701** open suspected-autism referrals in March 2026, of which **89.7%** had been open at least 13 weeks. Those numbers describe the hearing-majority pathway. They do not measure how a BSL-user moves through it.

**Hard-don't for anything public.** Absence of a combined or BSL-specific pathway is not a finding that current pathways are unlawful. NICE did not create a duty that ICBs must staff a BSL-fluent ADOS assessor. Equality Act reasonable adjustment and AIS contact-method rules are different legal objects and live in other batches.

## 85.1 Definitions — policy versus advocacy

UK official language does not run the two needs together. The GOV.UK inclusive-language page tells writers to use “d/Deaf, user of British Sign Language (BSL)” for the language community, and separately to use “neurodivergent” for a person who is not neurotypical, “for example, for people with conditions such as autism, ADHD or dyslexia.” It also records that many BSL-using Deaf people “may not consider themselves to be disabled.” That is the opposite of a fused “DeafDisabled” administrative category.

The document that comes closest to naming the intersection in government-adjacent prose is *Locked Out* (BSL Advisory Board, 27 November 2025, drafted with Dr Joanna Atkinson). It talks about “additional disabilities”, “neurodevelopmental conditions including autism, ADHD, learning disability, dyslexia, dyspraxia and language disorders”, and “intersectional” disability-plus-language-minority status. It does not adopt “DeafDisabled” as a term of art. On this pass, BDA, SignHealth and NDCS public pages were not found using “DeafDisabled” as a standing UK product name. Treat the compound label as **advocacy or academic usage at most**, not as something a clinician or ICB is required to code.

## 85.2 Prevalence — what can and cannot be printed

Print CRIDE’s **24% additional need** and NDCS’s **1 in 4** only with the qualifier that the category is undifferentiated. It includes learning difficulty, SLCN, physical disability, MSI and autism; it is not an autism rate and it is not a BSL-user rate. Only **2%** of deaf children in education are recorded as using British or Irish Sign Language as their language of education (NDCS / CRIDE). Multiplying 24% by 2% to invent a “Deaf BSL + neurodivergent” headcount would be a made-up cell.

The US **1–2%** figure must stay in the international column. Wright’s own 2023 UK comparison paper says precise UK epidemiological data are **not available** and then cites US estimates. That is the correct posture.

*Locked Out*’s **3.5% of autistic children are deaf** runs the other way (deafness among autistic children). Even if the footnote study holds, it does not answer “how many Deaf BSL-users are autistic.” Flag it contested until the footnote is opened.

Scotland’s 2018 Census paper (14.1% of autistic adults 25+ reported deafness/partial hearing loss) is the same wrong-direction problem, and “deafness or partial hearing loss” is not “Deaf BSL-user.”

## 85.3 Pathway and tools — children have a research product; adults have an ask

The honest UK picture is split by age.

**Children.** A research-validated ADOS-2 / ADI-R / SRS-2 Deaf-adaptation stack exists because NDCAMHS clinicians and the York/MRC team built it. The operational claim that should be printed is narrower than “the NHS pathway is BSL-accessible”: a nationally commissioned Deaf CAMHS service will assess autism and ADHD in severely to profoundly deaf children, and the research team around that service has published Deaf adaptations of the main instruments, with an explicit warning that interpreter-mediated ADOS is not the recommended model for a BSL-first child. Whether a family on a local ICB waiting list is referred into that service is a fulfilment question for prompt 86, not a finding that NICE has nationalised the adaptation.

**Adults.** *Locked Out* is the strongest published UK statement: no specialist NHS or private pathway; no adult clinical norms; no BSL ADHD questionnaire. The same Board, the same day, asked for a national adult deaf autism/ADHD assessment pathway that does not require a mental-health diagnosis. Until a commissioner publishes a specification, that remains an ask.

**Waits.** Use the NHS Digital autism series for the scale of the ordinary pathway (270,701 open referrals, March 2026; 89.7% beyond 13 weeks). Do not attach those percentages to Deaf BSL-users.

## 85.4 What is unpublished or contested

- Any UK **rate** of autism, ADHD or dyslexia among Deaf BSL-users.
- How often a local autism team actually “accesses” hearing-impairment expertise, and whether that access is a BSL-fluent ADOS-trained clinician or an interpreter booking.
- National **operational rollout** of the ADOS-2 Deaf adaptation beyond the research/NDCAMHS network (UKRI GTR flagged the infrastructure output as not provided to others).
- Current NDCAMHS **team count** (research papers: ten centres; *Locked Out*: four regional teams plus one inpatient unit; MFT page: three Northern sites). Re-open before mapping.
- Whether any **private** adult clinic advertises a BSL-fluent combined assessment. *Locked Out* says no specialist private pathway; re-check in prompt 90 rather than treat that sentence as a market survey.
- The footnote behind *Locked Out*’s 3.5% and 30–40% bands.
- Adult BSL-normed diagnostic tools — none found; absence is the finding.

Do not let prompts 86–88 invent a prevalence or a mandated pathway to fill these cells. The gate finding is the absence.
