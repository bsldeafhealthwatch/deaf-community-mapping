# Prompt 90 — Who actually serves Deaf BSL-users with neurodivergence in the UK, and are they still alive?

**Master index prompt 90.** Sixth file in the Neurodivergence Overlap batch (prompts 85–91). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps stay gaps.

**What this prompt is for.** Named UK organisations, projects and partnerships at the *intersection* — not a directory of every Deaf charity plus every autism charity. A page that loads is not proof the service is alive. Charity Commission / Companies House / OSCR status, last accounts year, last dated public activity.

**Gate from 85–89.** No official UK co-occurrence rate. No mandated combined pathway. Childhood assessment product exists as research (ADOS-2 Deaf adaptation) and as NDCAMHS practice. Adult pathway is a Board ask. International comparators are clinics and one Dutch national provider, not statutes. This file asks who in the UK looks like those clinics.

**Hard-don't.** Do not invent a “national Deaf-autism service.” Do not describe a dormant body as current. Do not list Gallaudet or Kentalis as UK services.

---

## Headline

There is **no UK charity whose registered objects and current public offer are “Deaf BSL-users who are autistic / ADHD / dyslexic.”** What exists is:

1. **NHS specialist assessment (children)** — National Deaf CAMHS / NDCAMHS, already logged in 85. Not a charity. Live service that will assess autism and ADHD in severely/profoundly deaf CYP.
2. **Two specialist providers that name autism as part of a Deaf-plus caseload**, not as a national diagnostic brand:
   - **Hayfield Limited** (Scotland) — OSCR **SC000436**, company **SC097262**. Accounts to **31 Mar 2024** signed 28 Nov 2024, PDF on the charity’s site April 2025. Income **£2.30m**. 43 people supported that year via Glasgow HSCP framework. Homepage: “Deaf adults with additional, multiple and complex disabilities including … autistic spectrum disorder.” Care homes, housing support, day support. **Alive.** Local/regional, not UK-wide.
   - **Royal Deaf Education Trust / the Deaf Academy** (Exmouth) — Charity Commission **1124523**, reporting up to date. Strategic plan 2023–28 (published Jan 2024): **65 students**, all Deaf with additional needs; internally reported mix includes **autism 25%**, SEMH 47%, learning difficulties 36%, MSI 45%. Sensory-play fundraising appeal March 2025. **Alive** as a non-maintained special school/college, not a walk-in diagnostic clinic.
3. **Advice pages, not services.** NDCS *Deafness and autism* page last reviewed **May 2025** (charity 1016532 / SC040779). It signposts families to **Deaf CAMHS** for diagnosis and to **NAS** general autism pages. That is a hyperlink, not a joint commissioned pathway.
4. **Local community space, not a national pathway.** **KATAKATA C.I.C.**, company **09701731**, Active. Accounts to 31 Jul 2024; confirmation statement 21 May 2025. Brixton café/community hub with a “BSL & Neurodiverse Community” page and a Freedom Festival 13 Sep 2025. Founder-led, Lambeth-scale. Do not scale it into a national service.

**No formal Deaf-sector × neurodivergence-sector partnership** (NDCS×NAS, SignHealth×ADHD UK, BDA×BDA-of-autism) was found as a signed project, shared contract or joint referral pathway. NDCS→NAS is signposting.

**Dormant-risk flag.** **British Society for Mental Health and Deafness (BSMHD)**, charity **1057135**, still appears in charity directories and is linked from Hayfield’s site. Public X activity on the handle associated with it last clearly dated around a **May 2021 AGM**. Do not treat BSMHD as a current intersection service until a 2024/25 filing or dated 2025–26 activity is opened. It was always a mental-health-and-Deaf society, not an autism society.

SignHealth (BSL psychological therapy; Talking Therapies contracts already in the SWL/SEL trackers) and NAS (269425 / SC039427) are both live national charities. Neither markets a dedicated Deaf+neurodivergence diagnostic or support product on the pages opened this pass.

## 90.1 What “specifically serving” actually looks like

Hayfield and the Deaf Academy are the two VCSE bodies that *name autism on a Deaf caseload* and still file. Hayfield is adult social care in and around Glasgow. The Deaf Academy is a South-West special school whose own plan says a quarter of students are autistic. Neither is a UK-wide diagnostic pathway. Neither replaces NDCAMHS for children or invents the adult pathway Locked Out asked for.

Katakata is real and recently active. It is a Brixton CIC café and community corner. Putting it in the same sentence as NDCAMHS would be a scale error.

## 90.2 Signposting is not a partnership

NDCS telling parents to read NAS pages is good advice-page hygiene. It is not a memorandum of understanding, a shared helpline, a joint referral form or a co-branded clinic. Until one of those documents appears, the dossier should say **no formal Deaf × neurodivergence partnership found**.

## 90.3 Still-alive scepticism

BSMHD is the body this batch was most likely to mis-describe as current. Directory listing + a website URL + a Hayfield footer link is exactly the pattern the skill warns about. Last dated public activity found: 2021. Re-open Charity Commission 1057135 accounts before printing BSMHD as a live route in public copy.

## 90.4 What is unpublished or contested

- Charity Commission 1057135 latest accounts and activity status.
- Whether SignHealth’s BSL therapy service will accept a primary presenting problem of autism or ADHD rather than depression/anxiety.
- Whether NAS has any BSL advice product that this pass missed (easy-read and minicom exist historically; a BSL diagnostic pathway page was not found).
- ADHD UK / British Dyslexia Association Deaf-specific offer — not found, not the same as proven absent from every sub-page.
- Any grant-funded “Deaf autism project” that ran, reported once, and left a PDF online.
- How many of Hayfield’s 43 people in 2023/24 had a recorded autism diagnosis — the accounts do not split the caseload.

Prompt 91 may use NDCS’s named parent quote on the autism page (“It’s because he’s deaf”) and the Deaf Academy / Hayfield descriptions as organisational context. It should not turn two providers and one advice page into a national support landscape.
