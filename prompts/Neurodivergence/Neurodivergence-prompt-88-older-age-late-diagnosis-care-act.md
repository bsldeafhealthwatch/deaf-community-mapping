## Headline

Care Act assessment has **two separate specialist-assessment duties**, and neither is a combined Deaf+neurodivergence product.

1. **Deafblind** — Care and Support (Assessment) Regulations 2014 reg. 6: the assessment *must* be carried out by a person with specific training and expertise relating to deafblind people (statutory guidance: at least QCF/OCN Level 3; higher if needs are complex). This is the only condition for which the regulations name a specialist assessor.
2. **Autism** — statutory guidance para. 6.90: the Care Act “strengthens” earlier autism-assessment guidance; assessors must have the skills for the assessment in question; if they lack experience of autism they *must consult* someone who has it. That is a consultation duty, not a named specialist-assessor grade. Oliver McGowan mandatory training (Health and Care Act 2022; Code of Practice from 6 Sep 2025) is CQC-registered staff training on learning disability and autism. It is not a Deaf+autism product and it is not a Care Act specialist-assessor grade.

A Deaf BSL-user who is **not** deafblind is covered by the ordinary assessment rules plus the duty to involve the person and provide an interpreter where communication is affected (guidance 6.38 / 6.96). There is **no** regulation that requires the assessor to be BSL-fluent or autism-trained *and* deaf-aware as a single competency.

NICE CG142 (adult autism, last amended June 2021) tells autism teams to take account of communication needs “including those arising from … sight or hearing problems” and to consider referring into a specialist autism team when the person has a “complex coexisting condition, such as … a severe … hearing … problem.” That is a complexity flag inside the general adult-autism pathway. It is not a commissioned BSL-fluent adult autism clinic.

The specialist clinic that **does** exist for older Deaf BSL-users is the **Queen Square / NHNN cognitive clinic** (established 2011, NHS England national funding from 2017). It assesses *acquired* cognitive decline and suspected dementia in Deaf BSL-users. Published series: median age at referral **71.5**. Referral rules exclude developmental (rather than acquired) cognitive difficulties. It is not an adult autism or ADHD service.

Locked Out (27 Nov 2025) asks for commissioned BSL-led pathways covering “learning disability and neurodevelopmental conditions (such as ADHD, autism)” and for diagnostic and post-diagnostic support in BSL. That remains an ask. Prompt 85 already recorded that adult Deaf autism/ADHD pathways are a Board request, not a service.

**Late diagnosis in the general older population is documented; the Deaf slice is not.** A 2025 British Psychological Society-linked estimate, reported in the Guardian (26 Jun 2026), is that **89–97% of autistic people over 60 are undiagnosed** — “neglected generation.” That figure is not Deaf-split. No official or charity count of undiagnosed autism or ADHD among older Deaf BSL-users was found.

**Named cases.**

- *Oldham MBC v KZ* [2024] EWCOP 72 (T3), Theis J, 4 Dec 2024. Deaf BSL-preferring young man (20 at judgment). Non-specialist capacity assessments, one using a BSL Level 1 service manager as “interpreter,” had labelled him as lacking capacity in all domains and as having a borderline learning disability. Specialist Deaf psychologist: **extreme language deprivation**, *not* learning disability; no evidence of ADHD, psychosis or OCD; **any autism assessment should be put on hold** until language could develop in a proper placement. Vice-President guidance: capacity assessment of a deaf BSL-fluent person should ideally be by an assessor qualified at the relevant BSL level; a non-specialist expert is “not an appropriate substitute.” This is the strongest published UK judgment on **mis-label versus language deprivation** in a Deaf BSL-user. KZ is a young adult, not an older person. The mechanism (wrong tool → wrong label → wrong Care Act / MCA plan) is the one this prompt is watching for in later life. Do not print it as an older-age case.
- LGSCO **24 017 923**: older profoundly deaf BSL-user, Care Act plan recorded BSL as first language, council could not find BSL-skilled carers, family used to coach agency staff. Interpreter issues in assessment and respite. **Not** a neurodivergence case.

No LGSCO, PHSO or Court of Protection judgment was found whose facts are an **older** Deaf BSL-user whose autism or ADHD was missed, or whose dementia was mistaken for lifelong neurodivergence (or the reverse).

**Hard-don't.** Do not print a right to a combined Care Act assessment or to a BSL-fluent adult autism diagnosis. The duties that exist are: assess apparent needs (Care Act s.9); skilled assessor + consult on autism; specialist assessor if deafblind; interpreter where needed; MCA capacity assessment that is actually valid for a BSL-user (*KZ*). Queen Square is a dementia clinic, not an autism clinic.

## 88.1 Two specialist duties, no third product

The Care Act assessment regulations picked **one** condition for a mandatory specialist assessor: deafblindness. Autism sits in statutory guidance as “consult someone who knows the condition.” Deaf-without-sight-loss sits in the ordinary skilled-assessor + interpreter rules. A Deaf BSL-using autistic older adult therefore meets the Act as *two parallel duties*, not as a designed intersection. Oliver McGowan adds a staff-training floor on autism and learning disability for CQC-registered providers; it does not create a BSL-fluent autism assessor.

## 88.2 What exists for older Deaf BSL-users is a dementia clinic

Queen Square is the honest “there is a service” sentence for this life stage. It is commissioned for acquired cognitive decline in BSL-users. It has a validated BSL cognitive screen. It is the place a GP should know about when an older Deaf BSL-user’s family says “memory.” It will not diagnose adult autism, and its published referral rule turns developmental presentations away.

NICE NG98 (refer people with dementia or MCI to audiology) runs in the other direction: if you already think dementia, check hearing. It does not tell adult-autism services to check BSL.

## 88.3 Late diagnosis — general figure, empty Deaf cell

The “neglected generation” estimate (89–97% of autistic people over 60 undiagnosed) is the best published late-diagnosis number this pass found. It is not a Deaf figure. Applying it to older Deaf BSL-users would be filling a UK cell with an unsplit statistic. Do not.

*KZ* is the document that shows *how* a wrong cognitive label gets attached to a Deaf BSL-user when the assessor and the language do not match. Language deprivation was read as learning disability; an autism assessment was parked. That is the mechanism to watch when an older Deaf adult is described as “always been difficult” or “has a learning disability from childhood” without a specialist Deaf assessment. It is not itself an older-age judgment.

## 88.4 What is unpublished or contested

- Any count of older Deaf BSL-users with diagnosed, suspected, or later-identified autism or ADHD.
- How often a Care Act assessor who is neither deafblind-trained nor autism-experienced consults *both* a ToD/sensory specialist *and* an autism specialist for the same person.
- Whether Queen Square, NDCAMHS (children) or any adult Deaf mental-health service will accept an older BSL-user for *neurodevelopmental* rather than neurodegenerative assessment.
- Primary BPS paper behind the 89–97% figure — open it before quoting the percentage as a dossier number.
- Wales / Scotland / NI social-care equivalents at this intersection (Scotland has the ALLIANCE dementia×sensory work; that is not autism).
- A named older-age Ombudsman or CoP case on the compound facts.

Prompt 89 should not import a Care Act “combined older-age pathway.” There isn’t one. There is a deafblind specialist-assessor duty, an autism consultation duty, a dementia clinic for BSL-users, and a Court of Protection warning about using the wrong assessor.
