## Spot-check note (this review's independent verification)

Two of the most load-bearing claims in prompt 85 were checked directly against primary sources rather than trusting Grok's quotation:

1. **NHS England autism waiting-list statistics** (270,701 open suspected-autism referrals, March 2026; 89.7% / 242,708 open ≥13 weeks; published 14 May 2026) — **confirmed verbatim** against the NHS Digital "Autism Statistics, April 2025 to March 2026" publication's own Key Facts section. This figure is safe to cite as-is, with the caveat Grok already applied: it is the general-population pathway, with **no Deaf/BSL split**.
2. **BSL Advisory Board *Locked Out* report** (published 27 Nov 2025, GOV.UK) — the report's existence and publication date are **confirmed**. Its general framing (gaps in specialist mental health provision, lack of specialist ADHD/autism pathways for BSL users) is corroborated by secondary coverage. However, the two specific statistics Grok pulled from it — **30–40% additional disabilities among people deaf from an early age**, and **3.5% of autistic children are deaf** — could **not** be independently located in the fetched excerpt or in secondary summaries checked on this pass. This is not evidence the figures are wrong; a long GOV.UK PDF is often truncated by automated fetch tools, and Grok already flagged both as contested/footnote-not-opened in its own writeup. The practical effect: **Grok's own hedging on these two figures was correctly calibrated, not overconfident** — treat them as still-open, exactly as prompt 85 already says, and re-open the source PDF directly (not a fetch tool) before either figure goes anywhere public.

No other claim in 85 or 90 was independently re-verified this pass; the per-prompt one-liners below rely on Grok's sourcing as given, consistent with the project's general practice of full re-verification only for the highest-stakes cells.

## Don't list (print near any public page)

- Do not print an official UK autism/ADHD/dyslexia **rate among Deaf BSL-users** — no such figure exists. CRIDE's 24% additional-need rate is not condition-split or BSL-split; NDCS's "1 in 4" restates CRIDE, not an autism rate.
- Do not print *Locked Out*'s **30–40% additional disabilities** or **3.5% of autistic children are deaf** as settled — both remain contested pending the underlying footnote/study, and this review's own spot-check could not independently locate either figure in the accessible excerpt of the source report. Confirm against the actual PDF before any public use.
- Do not print US **1–2% ASD-in-deaf-children**, IDEA "Deaf Plus," NDIS lists, or Kentalis as UK facts — all are international, explicitly labelled as such by the source papers themselves.
- Do not print that a Deaf neurodivergent person has an **enforceable right** to a combined or BSL-specific assessment pathway, or that current pathways are **unlawful**. No statute, regulation, NICE "must," or judgment creates one.
- Do not print **NICE CG128 / NG87** as a BSL-fluent-assessor mandate. CG128 requires teams to have skills *or access to* professionals for severe hearing impairment; NG87 lists hearing problems as a factor to account for. Neither names BSL or mandates a specific assessor.
- Do not print the **ADOS-2 / ADI-R / SRS-2 Deaf adaptations** as a commissioned national service — they are validated research instruments from one MRC-funded programme (UKRI MR/K015435/1), whose own infrastructure record flags "provided to others? No."
- Do not print an adult NHS or private BSL autism/ADHD pathway as existing. *Locked Out* records the absence explicitly; Psychiatry UK/RTC is an English-language product, not an NDCAMHS-for-adults equivalent.
- Do not print "DeafDisabled" as a NICE, NHS England, or GOV.UK term — the GOV.UK inclusive-language guide keeps "Deaf/BSL" and "neurodivergent" as separate vocabularies.
- Do not print AtW as two grants or two caps — it is one grant, separate tariffs, one cap (£69,260, frozen to 31 Mar 2027), with no published overlap count inside the 3,210 BSL-interpreter customers.
- Do not print a combined PIP product — "neurodivergent and Deaf claimants" language in any source is evidence of the compound need being raised, not a description of an integrated assessment pathway.
- Do not print Oliver McGowan training or the Queen Square clinic as Deaf+autism services. McGowan is LD/autism staff training; Queen Square is an acquired-dementia clinic for older BSL-users, median age 71.5 — a different clinical population entirely.
- Do not print *Oldham MBC v KZ* as an older-age precedent — KZ was 20; the legal mechanism may be usable, the age-band framing is not.
- Do not print LGSCO Brent 22 000 781 or FTS/HEC/23/0162 as a national or England-wide "autism missed because Deaf" ratio — two individual cases in different jurisdictions.
- Do not print Hayfield or the Deaf Academy as a national Deaf-autism service — both are real and active, but regional/school-scale caseloads that happen to name autism, not a UK diagnostic pathway.
- Do not print BSMHD (charity 1057135) as a current route until a 2024/25 filing or dated 2025–26 activity is found — last clearly dated public activity found was a May 2021 AGM.
- Do not print Katakata CIC as a national pathway — an active, real Brixton café/community hub, Lambeth-scale.
- Do not print the NDCS→NAS outbound hyperlinks as evidence of a formal partnership or joint clinic — it is signposting, not an MOU.
- Do not generalise the two NDCS family stories, one Scottish judgment, and one video into "families say the system always…" — three or four individual accounts, not a survey.
- Do not reuse Elliott (the HMRC dyslexia/ADHD PHSO matter), Francesca Bussey, Amanda Casson-Webb, Thomas (MSI), or PHSO Alan Graham as overlap testimony — none are Deaf-neurodivergence cases.
- Do not paste Scotland's tighter NHS interpreting policy, or an FTS/HEC Scottish tribunal reference, into an England-facing claim.
