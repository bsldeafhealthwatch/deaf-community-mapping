# Prompt 87 — Do AtW, PIP and workplace adjustments assess Deaf/BSL and neurodivergence together, or as separate boxes?

**Master index prompt 87.** Third file in the Neurodivergence Overlap batch (prompts 85–91). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps stay gaps.

**What this prompt is for.** The *compound* working-age question only. A Deaf BSL-using autistic or ADHD adult who needs an interpreter *and* processing-time or sensory adjustments. Not a rerun of Deaf-only welfare (20–26, 52–54) and not Jobcentre desk practice (27–33).

**Already sourced — do not re-litigate.** AtW cap **£69,260** frozen 8 Apr 2024 through 31 Mar 2027 (prompt 52). BSL Interpreter AtW customers **3,210** in 2024/25, average payment **£18,200** (UIN 120203, 20 Mar 2026; prompt 52). NAO average processing **109 working days** (Nov 2025). DWP is not under AIS; Equality Act s.20 is the duty family. Elliott is dyslexia/ADHD, not Deaf × HMRC — leave him in the HMRC file.

**Gate from 85–86.** No official UK co-occurrence rate. No NICE-mandated BSL diagnostic pathway. No combined EHCP protocol. Adult diagnostic pathway is a Board *ask*, not a service. A working-age claimant may therefore arrive at AtW or PIP **without** a formal autism/ADHD diagnosis.

---

## Headline

Access to Work is **one grant**. A customer can list more than one impairment and more than one support type on the same claim — a BSL interpreter *and* a job coach or “strategy training” for a neurodivergent condition. The staff guide prices those products on **separate rate cards**. The published customer tools are also separate: a **BSL Adjustments Planner** (27 Nov 2024) and a general **Health Adjustment Passport**. The BSL planner asks, as a side question, whether the person has “another disability or health condition.” That is not a dual-assessment protocol. Official statistics split AtW by *support-worker type* (BSL Interpreter = 3,210) or by broad condition group (mental health / learning conditions driving application growth). They do **not** publish how many of those 3,210 BSL-interpreter customers also have autism or ADHD.

The compound money fact that *does* follow from prompt 52: the **£69,260 cap is one ceiling**. Interpreter hours and neurodivergence coaching compete inside it. There is no published extra envelope for a second impairment.

PIP is also **one claim**. Descriptors score functional impact, not diagnostic labels. A claimant with deafness and autism fills in one form. There is no published DWP product called a “combined Deaf + neurodivergent PIP assessment.” The Timms Review interim report (9 Jul 2026) groups “neurodivergent and Deaf claimants” as people for whom “communication barriers, sensory overload and inaccessible formats add further layers of distress.” That is steering-group evidence language, not a new assessment route.

Workplace reasonable adjustments sit under Equality Act s.20. ACAS publishes one page on neurodiversity adjustments and employers separately publish deaf-aware practice. No ACAS page, EHRC code chapter, or named occupational-health standard was found that treats “BSL interpreter plus sensory/processing adjustments” as a single assessed package. An employer can agree both. The law does not require a combined assessment product first.

**Named compound cases: none found.** Searches of Upper Tribunal (AAC), Employment Tribunal / EAT reports, LGSCO and PHSO on this pass produced Deaf-only cases (Rimmer; UT AAC 2018/95 on BSL and PIP reading descriptors — “it has not been asserted that she has any other disabling conditions”) and neurodivergence-only cases (KW [2025] UKUT 267 PIP, autism + hyperacusis; *Stedman v Haven Leisure* [2025] EAT 82, autism + ADHD applicant; *Halstead v JD Wetherspoons*, autistic employee). None is a Deaf BSL-user *and* autistic or ADHD claimant or employee as the issue in dispute. Do not recruit Elliott.

**DWP assessment-provider / OH capability at the intersection: no reliable figure found.** AtW staff guide lists BSL support-worker rates and, separately, strategy training “to help individuals with specific learning difficulties, neurodiverse conditions, mental health issues.” Connect to Work has a “specialist pathway for neurodivergent and learning-disabled participants” (ministerial answer, 2026). Neither document names a Deaf+neurodivergence assessor competency.

**Hard-don't.** One AtW form and one PIP form are not an enforceable right to a combined diagnostic or combined workplace assessment. Failure to fund both an interpreter and a coach, if both are needed, would be argued as an AtW discretionary decision or an s.20 adjustment failure — case by case — not as breach of a pathway that does not exist.

## 87.1 One form is not a combined pathway

The public AtW page already names hearing loss, autism and ADHD as example conditions and names a BSL interpreter and a job coach as example support. A claimant does not file two AtW applications because they are Deaf *and* autistic. They file one. That is the limit of the “together” finding.

What they then get is priced and processed as separate lines. The staff guide’s BSL hourly band is not the same line as strategy training for neurodiverse conditions. The customer-facing planners are twins, not a single dual tool. Official tables count BSL-interpreter customers in one column and mental-health/learning-condition customers in another. Nobody publishes the overlap.

PIP works the same way at claim level (one form, all conditions) and has no published combined-assessment chapter. Timms grouping “neurodivergent and Deaf claimants” is evidence language from a review, useful for prompt 91, not a DWP specification.

## 87.2 The cap is where the two needs actually collide

Prompt 52’s freeze (£69,260 through 31 Mar 2027) becomes a compound-need fact here without changing the number. A full-time BSL-interpreter package already averages £18,200 and can run far higher. Adding ADHD coaching or a job coach eats the same ceiling. DWP has not published an uplift for a second impairment. That is the money finding. It is not a legal conclusion that the cap is unlawful.

## 87.3 Cases — do not invent a compound precedent

The reported authorities split cleanly: Deaf/BSL cases on one pile, autism/ADHD cases on another. UT AAC 2018/95 even records the absence of a second condition as a fact. Until a judgment names both, the dossier should say **no named UK working-age compound case found**, not “the law treats them separately” as if a court had so held.

## 87.4 Assessors and occupational health

No published DWP assessment-provider specification, Capita/IAS/Maximus training module, or Faculty of Occupational Medicine note on this intersection was found. Connect to Work’s neurodivergence pathway and AtW’s BSL rate card are parallel offers. An OH report *can* list both sets of adjustments. That is ordinary s.20 practice, not evidence of a trained intersection capability.

## 87.5 What is unpublished or contested

- How many of the 3,210 BSL-interpreter AtW customers also receive neurodivergence-related support.
- How often an AtW adviser reduces interpreter hours to fit coaching under the cap, or the reverse.
- Whether PIP assessment providers have any BSL-fluent assessors who also hold autism/ADHD competency — or whether the model is always interpreter + English-language questionnaire.
- Named ET/UT/PHSO/LGSCO compound cases after this pass.
- Occupational-health sector training at the intersection.
- The ministerial Connect to Work answer: open the primary written answer before quoting the InYourArea paraphrase in public copy.

Prompt 88 should not import a working-age “combined AtW pathway.” There isn’t one. There is one grant, two product lines, one cap, and no named compound case.
