# Prompt 89 — International comparative practice (context only — does not fill a UK cell)

**Master index prompt 89.** Fifth file in the Neurodivergence Overlap batch (prompts 85–91). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps stay gaps.

**What this prompt is for.** Context only. A named US clinic or an Australian plan category does **not** fill a UK prevalence cell (85), an EHCP cell (86), an AtW cell (87) or a Care Act cell (88). Do not write that a foreign protocol “should” be the UK model.

**Gate from 85.** The ADOS-2 / ADI-R / SRS-2 Deaf adaptations already logged as UK research products were built by an international Delphi team and are designed to be usable in BSL, ASL or Auslan. This file places those tools in the comparative landscape and asks whether any jurisdiction went further — a *named national pathway* or a *compound-need benefit rule*.

**Hard-don't.** Every row below is international unless it is explicitly restating a 85 tool for orientation. Legal architecture that does not transfer: ADA, IDEA FAPE and eligibility categories, NDIS “reasonable and necessary,” provincial human-rights codes.

## Headline

No jurisdiction opened on this pass has a **named national diagnostic pathway** or a **named statutory benefit** whose purpose is compounded Deaf-sign-language-user × neurodivergence. What exists is:

1. **Research-adapted tools**, led from the UK DIADS programme and used/discussed internationally: ADOS-2 Deaf adaptation (Phillips et al., 2021–22), ADI-R Deaf adaptation (Wright et al., 2022), SRS-2 Deaf adaptation (Wright et al., 2020). Authors recommend a deaf signing clinician lead the assessment if sign is the child’s language — not an interpreter-mediated standard ADOS. An Austrian/German team separately adapted ADOS-2 modules 1–3 for **deaf adults with intellectual disability** who communicate visually (Hofer et al., 2021, Innsbruck).
2. **Named specialist clinics**, not national pathways: Gallaudet Psychology Clinic (ASL-fluent team; autism, ADHD, learning-disability referrals); UNC Chapel Hill Hearing and Development Clinic (interdisciplinary D/HH autism clinic, described as 20+ years’ practice); Boston Children’s Deaf and Hard of Hearing Program (neuropsychology for D/HH children, including those who also have autism). Royal Kentalis in the Netherlands is the closest *system-scale* comparator: a national specialist organisation whose CMB (“communicative multiple disability”) stream explicitly takes deaf/hard-of-hearing people with additional autism, intellectual disability or socio-emotional problems. That is a commissioned specialist provider, not a statute titled “Deaf + autism pathway.”
3. **Education and benefit systems that keep the categories separate.** US IDEA lists Deafness, Autism, Hearing impairment, Deaf-blindness and Multiple disabilities as distinct eligibility categories. Practitioner language “Deaf Plus” is not a statutory category. Australia’s NDIS publishes **separate** primary-disability evidence lists for Autism and for Hearing impairment; Auslan interpreting can go into a plan as a support, which is not a dual-assessment product. No Canada-wide or EU-wide compound-need benefit rule was found.

Mood and Shield (2014, US) is the cautionary pre-adaptation paper: standard ADOS-2 with eight native-ASL autistic deaf children produced **discrepant module classification in 5 of 8 cases**. That is why the later adaptations exist. It is not itself a protocol.

Ireland (Educational Psychology in Practice, 2026) describes an educational psychologist helping to *establish* an autism assessment pathway inside a D/HH MDT service — evidence that the pathway was not already sitting on the shelf.

**What does not transfer.** IDEA’s FAPE/IEP machinery, ADA auxiliary-aids rules, NDIS planning, and Dutch “zintuiglijk gehandicaptenzorg” eligibility thresholds are domestic legal objects. Citing them as if they created a UK duty would be a category error.

## 89.1 Tools travelled; national pathways did not

The honest comparative sentence is: the UK produced the best-published child-assessment adaptations, an Austrian team produced an adult-ID adaptation, and a handful of US university/hospital clinics and one Dutch national specialist organisation actually *see* these children. Nobody opened on this pass has legislated a combined diagnostic right or a combined benefit category.

That is why 85’s UK finding (research product, not a mandated pathway) is not an outlier. It is the international pattern.

## 89.2 Clinics are not statutes

Gallaudet, UNC HDC, Boston Children’s and Kentalis are real services. They can be named as comparators of *practice*. They cannot be cited as evidence that the UK is in breach of a standard those countries imposed on themselves by law. IDEA still splits eligibility. NDIS still splits evidence lists. Kentalis is commissioned specialist care under Dutch sensory-disability rules, which do not bind an English ICB.

## 89.3 What is unpublished, contested, or non-transferable

- Operational rollout of the ADOS-2 / ADI-R Deaf adaptations outside the original research sites (already unpublished in the UK — 85).
- Whether Gallaudet or UNC use the Phillips adaptation or a local clinical modification (the 2023 UNC paper still describes some ADOS modifications as “clinically informed, not evidence-based”).
- Autism CRC guideline line-by-line on Deaf/Auslan — not fully mined this pass.
- Canadian provincial programmes (Ontario, BC) — not opened.
- Any jurisdiction’s *benefit* rule that uplifts payment because the person is both Deaf and autistic, as opposed to stacking two ordinary supports.
- ADA / IDEA / NDIS / Dutch ZG-care legal tests — do not transfer.

Prompt 90 should look for UK organisations that resemble Gallaudet-clinic or Kentalis-CMB practice. It should not treat those foreign names as UK services.
