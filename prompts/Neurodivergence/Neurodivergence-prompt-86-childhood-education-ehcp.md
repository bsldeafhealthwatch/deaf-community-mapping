# Prompt 86 — Does the EHCP (and four-nation equivalents) hold both Deaf/BSL and neurodivergence, or does one need get missed?

**Master index prompt 86.** Second file in the Neurodivergence Overlap batch (prompts 85–91). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps stay gaps.

**What this prompt is for.** Map the *education-law* layer for a Deaf BSL-using child who is also autistic, ADHD or otherwise neurodivergent. Different statute from working-age DWP (87) and older-age Care Act (88). Do not invent a combined diagnostic pathway because 85 did not find one.

**Index check.** Prompt 85 already established: no official UK co-occurrence rate; NICE CG128 is “skills or access” for severe hearing impairment, not a BSL-fluent assessor mandate; ADOS-2 / ADI-R / SRS-2 Deaf adaptations exist as research products around NDCAMHS; Deaf CAMHS will assess autism and ADHD in severely/profoundly deaf CYP; adults have an ask, not a service. This file does not re-litigate those cells. It asks what the *school and plan* system does with the two needs.

**Gate reminder.** Absence of a UK prevalence figure and of a NICE-mandated BSL diagnostic tool is the starting fact. This prompt must not paper over it with an EHCP “pathway” that does not exist.

## Headline

England does **not** have a documented combined Deaf+neurodivergence EHCP pathway. The Children and Families Act 2014 / SEND Code of Practice system is **needs-based**: every identified special educational need must go into Section B, and the local authority **must seek advice from a qualified Teacher of the Deaf** when the child is deaf. That is a consultation duty, not a dual-diagnosis protocol and not a requirement that the autism assessor is BSL-fluent. Primary-need coding on the School Census (Hearing Impairment *or* Autistic Spectrum Disorder) can hide the other need as “secondary.” Most deaf children do **not** have an EHCP at all.

The live numbers, from CRIDE 2025 England / School Census 2024/25:

- **8,079** children with an EHCP where deafness is a primary *or* secondary need (5,585 primary, 2,512 secondary).
- Against CRIDE’s **45,902** deaf children in England that is about **18%** with an EHCP.
- School Census records **28,958** children with deafness as primary or secondary SEN — only 63% of the CRIDE caseload. The other 37% of deaf children known to sensory services do not appear on the published SEN register as HI.

NDCS’s own parent-facing line is the operational fact: “Most deaf children do not have an EHCP. Most children need an EHCP to go to a deaf school.” There is no published national figure for how many of those 8,079 plans also name autism or ADHD.

**Overshadowing is documented as a research finding, not as a current official statistic.** University of Manchester / NDCS *Complex Needs, Complex Challenges* (2011) found that when deaf children have additional complex needs, **deafness is often overshadowed** by the other difficulties (late diagnosis, wrong amplification, missing specialist support). NDCS’s current *Deafness and autism* advice page still says the signs overlap and that autism in a deaf child “will often be diagnosed later.” That is charity guidance, not a measured miss-rate.

**Four nations are different legal objects.**

**Deaf schools / resource provisions / ToD services.** No published *national* ToD or Deaf-school protocol for identifying neurodivergent Deaf pupils was found. What exists is school-level acknowledgement that many pupils have additional needs (Elmfield Ofsted May 2023; Oak Lodge Wandsworth; Deaf Academy handbook) and local threshold documents that mention autism as an add-on to hearing-impairment banding (e.g. Newcastle). The QToD mandatory qualification is about deaf education, not a mandated autism module. DfE’s Feb 2026 SEND-reform consultation funds a national SEND training programme and quotes a Neurodivergence Task and Finish Group — that programme is not a Deaf+autism product.

**Named cases.** Two stand up as documents, not as a series.

1. **LGSCO London Borough of Brent 22 000 781**, decision 19 Oct 2022. Young person **autistic and with hearing loss**; EHCP specified weekly SALT and that BSL interpreters “must be provided” in education settings. Council failed to pay the interpreter; provision in the plan was not secured. Fault upheld. This is a **fulfilment** failure on a plan that already named both needs — not a finding that autism was missed because the child was Deaf.
2. **FTS/HEC/23/0162** (Scotland), published 12 Feb 2024. Deaf autistic BSL-using child. Tribunal held the current setting had not employed suitably qualified BSL teachers and that the specified school could meet both the ASD need and the BSL language/culture need. Strongest named **Deaf + autism + BSL** education judgment found on this pass. It is a Scottish placing-request decision. It does not bind an English EHCP.

No England First-tier or Upper Tribunal judgment was found whose *ratio* is “the autism (or ADHD) was missed because the child is a Deaf BSL-user,” or the reverse. Do not generalise two cases into a national miss-rate.

**Hard-don't.** There is no enforceable right to a combined or BSL-specific EHCP assessment pathway. The statutory duties that do exist are: assess all identified SEN (CFA 2014), seek ToD advice for a deaf child, secure the provision written into Section F (s.42 CFA 2014), and make Equality Act reasonable adjustments whether or not there is a plan. Failure to write autism into Section B when it has been identified would be a contents defect appealable to the Tribunal — that is not the same as a duty to run a BSL-adapted ADOS.

## 86.1 What the England plan actually does

An EHCP is not a diagnosis certificate. Section B must list every special educational need that has been identified. Section F must specify the provision. Section I names the setting. Health (C/G) and social care (D/H) can be recommended by the Tribunal but are not the main appealable sections.

For a deaf child the distinctive statutory hook is the **ToD advice**. NDCS tells parents the local authority must consult a qualified Teacher of the Deaf as part of the assessment. That puts a deaf-education specialist in the room. It does not put a BSL-fluent autism assessor in the room, and it does not require the ToD to have an autism qualification. If autism has already been diagnosed (for example by NDCAMHS — prompt 85), it should appear in Section B. If it has not been diagnosed, the EHCP process has no separate engine for finding it.

Primary-need coding is the administrative risk. A child can be counted as Hearing Impairment with autism as secondary, or as Autistic Spectrum Disorder with hearing impairment as secondary. Published national tables this pass give the EHCP *deafness* count (8,079) and the CRIDE additional-need count (24%), not the intersection. Do not infer the intersection from either number.

## 86.2 Overshadowing — research, not a 2026 rate

*Complex Needs, Complex Challenges* (Manchester / NDCS, 2011) is still the document NDCS points special-school staff at. It found bidirectional masking: other disabilities hide the hearing loss, and hearing loss makes other diagnoses harder. One in five children in that sample had no ToD. Treat it as the best published UK qualitative/quantitative study of the *mechanism*, dated 15 years. It is not a current official miss-rate and it is not BSL-specific (the sample is “deaf children with additional complex needs,” not “BSL-using autistic children”).

NDCS’s live *Deafness and autism* page translates that mechanism into parent advice: expect later autism diagnosis; ask for a setting that can do both. That is the honest public sentence. It is not a finding that local authorities are in breach.

## 86.3 Schools and ToDs — practice exists, a national protocol does not

Deaf schools and resource provisions obviously educate children who have more than deafness. Elmfield, Oak Lodge and the Deaf Academy say so in inspection and handbook text. Newcastle’s threshold document even writes “autism” into the higher-band multi-agency offer. None of that adds up to a published national identification protocol, a BATOD competency, or a QToD module that this pass could open. The Feb 2026 DfE SEND-reform training envelope is general staff SEND/neurodivergence training, not a Deaf+autism product.

Whether a given Deaf school can actually run a BSL-fluent autism assessment, or must refer out to NDCAMHS, is a fulfilment question that would need school-by-school checking. Do not assume the existence of the Wright adaptations (prompt 85) means every Deaf school uses them.

## 86.4 Named cases — two documents, not a series

Brent 22 000 781 is the England Ombudsman example: both needs were already in the plan; the council did not pay the interpreter the plan required. That is s.42 fulfilment, the same legal object as any other missed EHCP provision.

FTS/HEC/23/0162 is the Scotland example: the tribunal treated BSL as a language-minority right *and* treated ASD as a need the specified school was set up to meet. It is the closest published judgment to the batch’s childhood anchor. It is not English law.

An England tribunal whose facts were “Deaf BSL-using autistic child, autism missed / BSL missed / wrong primary need” was not found. Until one is, do not write that the Tribunal has established a pattern.

## 86.5 What is unpublished or contested

- Current official **HI × ASD** (and HI × SEMH/ADHD) School Census cross-tab.
- How often ToD advice on an EHC assessment raises possible autism/ADHD rather than only audiology and language.
- How often a local autism team, faced with a Deaf BSL-using child, refers into NDCAMHS rather than running an English-language ADOS.
- National operational use of the ADOS-2 Deaf adaptation in education (prompt 85 already flagged rollout as unpublished).
- Wales IDP and NI statement practice at this intersection.
- A measured “one need recognised, the other missed” rate. Overshadowing is a 2011 mechanism plus NDCS advice, not a 2026 percentage.
- QToD course content on neurodivergence — not opened this pass.

Prompt 87 should not import an EHCP “combined pathway” that this file did not find. Prompt 91 may use Brent and FTS/HEC/23/0162 as named documents; it should not turn them into a national lived-experience rate.
