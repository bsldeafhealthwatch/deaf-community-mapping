# Prompts 168–169 — Devices & Technology addendum: self-directed AI literacy/writing tools (proofreading, plain-English, translation) at work

Added: 14 September 2026. Addendum to the existing Devices-Technology batch (134–140), not a new numbered Domain or a new leg of Domain 14 beyond what 134–140 already opened.

Checked first: `Devices-Technology-prompts-134-140-independent-review-by-claude.md` and all seven 134–140 findings files, plus `claude/grok-findings-devices-technology-workplace.md`. This is not a duplicate. 136 asks whether an AI signing avatar is offered *instead of* a human interpreter — a communication-channel substitution the employer proposes. 137 asks about an AI notetaker used *in a meeting* — recording/transcription and colleague consent. Neither asks about an employee independently using a general-purpose AI tool (Claude, Copilot, ChatGPT, Grok, Gemini, or a local/self-hosted model) on their own initiative for a *different* job: proofreading their own written English, converting a long document into bullet points, or translating dense text into plain/easy-read language, as a personal accommodation for BSL being their first language rather than English. This is closer in kind to the AIS "written English is not automatically accessible to a BSL-first person" finding already logged in `claude/grok-findings-templates-section.md` (prompt 51) than to 136/137's meeting-technology object, and nothing in the project has asked it in an employment (as opposed to NHS-patient-letter) context.

Distinct from 139 (lived experience on AI *offered instead of* human support) because this is the employee's own choice of tool for their own output, not something offered or imposed. Distinct from 129 (Chinese Walls AI note-taking) because that is deal-team record-handling, not personal writing assistance. Distinct from prompt 51's NHS-letter finding because the duty-holder and legal framework are different (EA 2010 Part 5 employment vs AIS/NHS service provision), though the underlying "BSL is not written English" fact is the same and should not be re-derived.

Anchor: a Deaf BSL-first employee whose written English is a second/weaker language, who starts using an AI writing tool unprompted — to proofread emails, summarise a long report into bullets, or turn a dense policy document into plain English — for their own comprehension and output, without asking for or receiving any interpreter or notetaker. Test (a) whether this is recognised anywhere as a reasonable adjustment or is treated as a personal productivity choice with no employer obligation attached, and (b) what happens when the text being proofread, summarised, or translated is the employer's confidential material and the tool is a public cloud AI service rather than a local/on-device model.

Proposed domain: addendum to existing Domain 14 employment strand (134–140). Not a new Domain, not a new numbered batch beyond these two prompts.
