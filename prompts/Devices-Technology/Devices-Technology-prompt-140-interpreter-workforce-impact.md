# Prompt 140 — Interpreter workforce impact: is workplace AI displacing paid human BSL bookings?

**Master index prompt 140.** Last file in the Devices & Technology workplace batch (prompts 134–140). Compiled 11 September 2026. This prompt closes the Domain 14 gap that said “no data found on interpreter workforce impact — whether AI translation tools are measurably displacing paid human interpreter bookings,” scoped here to **workplace / employer-booked** hours, not public-service interpreting generally. Do not repeat prompts 81–83. Do not paste Domain 14’s SignGPT £8.4m figure or WFD/WASLI 2018 as if they measured UK workplace booking hours. Do not treat a vendor “solves scarcity” claim as workforce-impact evidence.

---

## Headline

**Not measured.** The Domain 14 gap closes as a **confirmed absence of a workplace-hours series**, not as a measured decline.

What *is* published points the other way, or talks about risk rather than hours:

- **Access to Work “BSL Interpreter” customers rose**, not fell: 2,810 (2020/21) → 2,890 → 2,940 → 3,170 → **3,210 (2024/25)**. Average payment £12,700 → **£18,200**. Official DWP tables PAY02 / AVG02, given in UIN 120203 (20 March 2026).
- NAO February 2026, real-terms spend on the BSL-interpreter support-worker type: **£42m → £63m** from 2020/21 to 2024/25. Same direction.
- NRCPD Sign Language Interpreter count on the live regional table this pass: **1,664** (totals include dual registrations). Project memory 5 September 2026: 1,657 SLI. Live homepage feed: **2,089 professionals in total** across all categories. The register is not publishing a collapse.
- NUBSLI April 2026 fees guidance still frames a **“national shortage of interpreters.”** The fees page now carries an **AI disclaimer** (recordings must not be used to train AI). That is copyright / training-data protection, not a measured hours-lost series.
- ASLI June 2025 and the Cambridge procurement paper describe **workforce displacement as a risk** if buyers specify AI as a substitute. ASLI also records **under-employment and inefficient booking** as current labour-market facts. Risk + booking friction ≠ a published employer-hours displacement trend.

No UK agency, NRCPD statistical release, ASLI/NUBSLI/VLP dataset, or academic paper was found that counts employer-booked human BSL hours replaced by AI captions, transcripts or avatars. RNID 2026 “18% relied more on auto/AI subtitles because of an award shortfall” is user coping under a cap, already logged in 134/139. It is not a booking-platform time series.

## 140.1 How to read the official numbers without inventing a trend

A rising AtW BSL-interpreter customer count and a rising real-terms spend line are **not** proof that AI is *not* substituting anywhere. They are proof that the only official workplace-adjacent series we have has not turned down through 2024/25. AtW is one payer. Employer-only bookings (no AtW) have no public census. Agency diaries are private.

A rising NRCPD stock is not hours worked. Dual registrations inflate the total. Trainees are listed separately.

NUBSLI’s “shortage” sentence and ASLI’s “under-employment + inefficient booking” sentence can both be true in the same market: demand exists, allocation is poor, rates in some public frameworks are below the fees guide. That labour-market mess is the prompts-81–83 object. It is not an AI-hours series.

---

## 140.2 What the sector *has* put on paper

- **Risk language.** ASLI and the Cambridge procurement paper: if buyers write AI as a substitute, the workforce that public services and employers depend on is destabilised. That is a mechanism claim. It is not a measured 2024/25 hours delta.
- **Training-data refusal.** NUBSLI fees-page AI disclaimer: do not use recordings of the interpreter’s work to train models. IP / consent, not volumes.
- **User coping.** RNID 18%. Cap / backlog, not a platform dashboard.

None of those three is a substitute for the series Domain 14 asked for.

## 140.3 Gap status after this prompt

Domain 14 flagged: “no data found on interpreter workforce impact.”

**Status after 140:** confirmed absence of a UK workplace (employer-booked) human-hours-displaced-by-AI series. Official AtW BSL-interpreter customers and real-terms spend rose to 2024/25. NRCPD SLI stock is not falling on the live table. Sector bodies describe shortage, under-employment, booking friction and *future* displacement risk. Next empirical step, if anyone funds it, is an agency or AtW FOI that asks for **hours booked**, last three years, split human F2F / VRS / other — not “list the AI tools.”

Do not print that AI has, or has not, replaced workplace interpreters as a national fact.
