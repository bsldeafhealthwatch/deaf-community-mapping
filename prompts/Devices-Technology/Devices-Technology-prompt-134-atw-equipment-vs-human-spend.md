# Prompt 134 — Does DWP publish Access to Work spend split between equipment and human communication support for Deaf and DeafBlind claimants?

**Master index prompt 134.** First file in the Devices & Technology workplace batch (prompts 134–140). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Close, or confirm still open, Domain 14’s own named FOI target: a published breakdown of Access to Work spend between assistive technology / equipment and human communication support (interpreters, notetakers, STTR) *specifically for Deaf and DeafBlind claimants*. Ask whether any documented shift toward captioning software or AI transcription and away from human interpreter bookings is official, FOI-disclosed, or only advocacy/anecdote. Do not restate the cap, the 3,210 BSL-interpreter customer count, or NAO processing times unless a source newly splits those numbers by kit versus human support.

## Headline

**Domain 14’s FOI target remains open.** This prompt found new *all-customer* official splits and an official refusal of item-level equipment data. It did **not** find the Deaf / DeafBlind kit-versus-human cross-tab Domain 14 asked for.

What DWP *does* publish, as Official Statistics in development (release 14 October 2025; approvals revision 29 January 2026; expenditure tables not revised):

- Spend by **element**, all conditions together: Support Worker **£226.9 million** (71% of 2024/25 provision spend); Special Aids and Equipment **£18.9 million** (6%); Travel to Work £53.3 million (17%).
- Spend by **primary medical condition**, all elements together: “Deaf or hard of hearing” **£90.3 million** (28% of element spend); 4,980 customers; average any-element payment £16,900.
- Support-worker **type**, all conditions together: “BSL Interpreter” **3,210** customers paid in 2024/25, average **£18,200**. NAO restates BSL-interpreter spend at **£63 million** in 2024–25 prices.

What DWP does **not** publish, and what no FOI found on this pass disclosed:

- Element × primary-condition expenditure (SAE versus Support Worker inside the Deaf / hard-of-hearing £90.3 million).
- A DeafBlind, dual-sensory, RIDB, or guide-communicator row.
- An item list of SAE products (captioning licences, alerting kit, loops). Baroness Sherlock, UIN **HL3809**, 16 January 2025: SAE items are stored as **descriptive free text**; analysing them would exceed the cost limit.

There is **no official series** showing AtW funding shifting from human interpreters toward captioning software or AI transcription. The staff guide still prices human BSL, remote VRS/VRI *human* bundles, STTR, and SAE as separate products. Cost share applies to SAE and premises adaptations, not to support workers — so the published contribution rule, if it pulls in any direction, is toward human support rather than kit.

The only 2025–26 *Deaf-sample* split found is advocacy, not official: RNID / DeafATW survey (Dec 2025–Jan 2026, n=358) — **90%** of respondents have communication-support awards, **17%** specialist equipment; **98%** of BSL-user respondents have communication support. Separately, **18%** of BSL-user respondents said they “must rely more on automatic or AI subtitles” because of an award shortfall. RNID’s own gloss is that this is **not** an adequate substitute. That is claimant-reported coping under a shortfall, not a DWP product switch.

**Hard-don’t.** Do not print the missing cross-tab as a finding that AtW “prefers technology” or “prefers interpreters.” Do not treat vendor “approved for Access to Work” pages (Caption.Ed, Speechify) as spend evidence. Do not treat HL3809’s 2023/24 SAE total of £21 million as the Deaf equipment figure — it is all-customer.

## 134.1 What 20 / 24 / 52–54 / 116 already closed — and what they did not

Already closed, and not re-argued here:

- Cap £69,260, frozen through 31 March 2027 (prompt 52).
- Official BSL-interpreter customer series and average payment (UIN 120203; prompts 20 / 52 / 116).
- Deaf/HoH £90.3 million / 4,980 customers (prompts 20 / 116).
- Staff-guide BSL, VRS-bundle, STTR and Communication Support bands; no RIDB tariff (prompt 116).
- Cost share does not apply to support workers (prompt 116).

Still open after those batches, and still open after this one: **pounds of SAE inside the Deaf/HoH £90.3 million**, and any official count of captioning-software awards to BSL-using or DeafBlind customers.

A tempting but invalid subtraction — £90.3 million Deaf/HoH minus £63 million NAO BSL-interpreter — does **not** yield a Deaf equipment figure. The £63 million is an all-condition support-worker subtype in 2024–25 prices; the £90.3 million is a condition bucket covering every element (interpreter, CSW, VRS minutes, travel, SAE, miscellaneous). The remainder is a mix. Do not print it as kit spend.

## 134.2 The official grain stops one join short of the question

DWP’s published tables are built as parallel slices:

1. Element (Support Worker / SAE / Travel / MHSS / adaptations).
2. Support-worker type (BSL Interpreter / Communication / Job Aide / Job Coach / Driver / Other).
3. Primary medical condition (Deaf or hard of hearing / difficulty in seeing / mental health / learning disability / other).

The join of (1) or (2) with (3) is unpublished. Dual-sensory loss is not a condition label at all.

HL3809 is the clean official sentence on why an equipment *catalogue* will not appear from a routine extract: SAE awards are free-text. A future FOI that asks only for “SAE spend where primary condition = Deaf or hard of hearing, last three years” is a smaller ask than “list the items,” and is the remaining live target. It is not guaranteed to clear s.12. It is the right next letter, not a second tracker.

## 134.3 Shift toward technology: no official trend; one advocacy coping cell

Scheme-level money 2018/19–2024/25 moved **into people**, not into kit. NAO: Support Worker spend +125% real terms to £227 million; the steep subtype is **job aides** (£6 million → £64 million), a different product from BSL interpreting. BSL-interpreter spend in the same NAO real-terms series rose £42 million to £63 million. SAE spend in the 2024/25 official release *fell* 15% in real terms to £18.9 million.

That all-customer picture cannot be read as “Deaf awards are moving to AI captioning.” It also cannot be read as proof they are not: the Deaf slice is unpublished.

What exists instead:

- **Staff guide** still funds human interpreters, human VRS/VRI, human STTR, and SAE as parallel options. Advisers are told to seek a cost-effective solution that meets the employment need. That is a general value-for-money instruction, not a published “use auto-captions instead of RSLI” rule.
- **RNID 2026 survey** records some BSL users leaning on automatic / AI subtitles because the award no longer covers interpreter hours. RNID states that is not an equivalent. Treat as lived-experience input for prompt 139, not as a DWP statistics cell.
- **Vendor pages** claiming AtW approval for Caption.Ed or Speechify show that assessors *can* put a captioning licence on an SAE or miscellaneous line. They do not show volume, Deaf/BSL share, or displacement of booked interpreter hours.

Cost share is the one structural tilt in the published rules: an employer may have to contribute to kit and premises, and not to the interpreter. That is not evidence of practice. It is the live contribution rule.

## 134.4 What stays unpublished or contested

- Deaf / DeafBlind SAE spend, 3-year series.
- Captioning-software / AI-transcription award counts, by condition.
- Any official statement that AtW is substituting technology for human BSL in workplace awards.
- Whether VRS/VRI minute bundles (human interpreters on a video platform) are being recoded toward AI products — the staff-guide bundles are still human VRS/VRI.

International equipment-distribution schemes, including the US FCC National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program, stay out of this cell.
