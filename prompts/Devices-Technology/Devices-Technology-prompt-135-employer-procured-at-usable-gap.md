# Prompt 135 — What workplace assistive technology is now built-in, and where “provided” is not usable?

**Master index prompt 135.** Second file in the Devices & Technology workplace batch (prompts 134–140). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Split workplace kit into (a) features already inside the software licence most UK employers already pay for, and (b) items that still need a request, a procurement, or Access to Work. Then test the “provided but not enabled” gap: captions off by default, an admin policy that hides the button, auto-captioning treated as a finished adjustment, a visual alarm that exists on paper. Do not treat a vendor marketing page as evidence that the feature is on in UK workplaces. Do not import Rowley v Cabinet Office (public-service briefings) as an employment judgment.

**Index check.** This file is the research *answer* to prompt 135. Prompt 134 closed only the AtW *spend* grain. Prompt 93 mentioned captioning and home VRS in passing. Prompt 102 covers passports, not which button is on. Prompt 129 is finance-wall AI notes. Human interpreters stay a separate product (136 / 140).

## Headline

Built-in is now real. **Usable by default is not.**

On the dominant UK enterprise stack:

- **Microsoft Teams** live captions are a licence feature. Microsoft’s own admin docs (updated 9 July 2026) state the default meeting policy is **“Not enabled, but users can enable”** (`DisabledUserOverride`). Captions are **off until a user turns them on**. An admin can set the policy to **Off**, which removes the button. That is the cleanest documented “provided ≠ enabled” mechanism found.
- **Google Meet** automated captions can be turned on by a participant from the meeting bar. Transcripts and some translation features sit on paid Workspace SKUs and can be disabled by a Workspace admin. RNID’s own Meet guide (2022, still the charity’s how-to) tells users that if a feature is missing they should “contact your organisation’s admin.”
- **Zoom** automated captions, on AbilityNet’s own how-to, “will have to be enabled by the meeting organiser.”
- **Windows 11** ships Magnifier, Narrator, Live Captions (Win+Ctrl+L), Voice Access, contrast themes. AbilityNet’s *My Computer My Way* exists precisely because those toggles are unused unless someone shows the employee they are there.

What still needs a request, a purchase, or AtW (prompt 134 / 116):

- A human BSL interpreter, RIDB, lipspeaker, or NRCPD STTR.
- Specialist AT that is not in the OS / M365 / Workspace bundle (JAWS, commercial Dragon, refreshable braille, dedicated looping, vibrating pagers).
- Physical kit: visual / vibrating fire alarm, fixed desk, induction loop.
- AtW-funded VRS *minutes* and captioning *licences* that sit outside the enterprise suite.

No UK official series measures how many employers have left Teams captions on the default, how many have set them to Off, or how often auto-captions are written down as the completed adjustment. The documented *shape* of the gap is:

1. **Policy default** — feature present, off until clicked, or hidden by admin (Microsoft Learn).
2. **Host / organiser gate** — Zoom; some Meet transcript controls (AbilityNet; RNID Meet page).
3. **Unfamiliarity** — AbilityNet workplace brochure: people are “often unaware of the accessibility and productivity tools built into mainstream packages such as Office 365 and Google Suite.” BDF 2023: workplaces “took a relatively long amount of time to become familiar with accessibility features of online meeting platforms.”
4. **Quality / substitute** — RNID 2026 survey: auto-captions unreliable with accents, multi-speaker, jargon; some respondents offered AI captioning as a cheaper stand-in for STTR / notetaker; BSL users still need English literacy the captions assume.
5. **Residual barriers after “an adjustment”** — BDF Great Big Workplace Adjustments Survey 2023: **24%** of those with adjustments used assistive IT; **56%** said disability-related barriers remained after adjustments; managers reported internal IT **incompatible** with some AT and did not know how to fix it.

No named UK employment tribunal was found that turns on “the licence included live captions but the admin policy was Off” or “auto-captions were treated as discharging the interpreter request.” Absence of that case is the legal finding, not a conclusion that the practice is lawful or unlawful.

**Hard-don’t.** Do not print that built-in captions satisfy Equality Act s.20. Do not print that an admin who leaves the Microsoft default in place is in breach. Do not file Rowley v Cabinet Office [2021] EWHC 2108 (Admin) (COVID briefings, public sector PSED / s.29) as a workplace Part 5 authority.

## 135.1 Built-in versus requested

Three layers, not two.

**Layer A — already in the licence / OS, user or admin must still switch it on.** Teams live captions, Meet automated captions, Windows 11 Live Captions / Magnifier / Narrator / Voice Access, Office Immersive Reader and Dictate, Chrome Live Caption. AbilityNet treats this layer as the first reasonable-adjustment conversation precisely because it is cheap and already paid for.

**Layer B — in the suite but SKU- or admin-gated.** Meet transcripts and some translation on paid Workspace; Teams Premium / Copilot extras; an admin policy that sets captions to Off; a Zoom host who has not enabled CC.

**Layer C — not in the suite.** Human communication professionals, specialist AT, physical alerting and looping. This is the AtW / procurement list from prompts 116 and 134. Built-in English captions do not become BSL.

Jisc’s public-sector captioning guide is useful only as a quality warning, not as employment law: automatic captions “do not meet user needs or accessibility requirements, unless they are confirmed to be fully accurate. Usually they need significant editing.” That is WCAG / PSBAR language for published video, not a Part 5 rule.

## 135.2 The documented “provided ≠ usable” mechanisms

1. **Default off.** Microsoft’s sentence is the hardest primary source in this file: live captions are off by default; the user can enable them unless the admin has set Off.
2. **Admin hide.** Same page. No UK count of how often `Disabled` is used.
3. **Organiser gate.** AbilityNet on Zoom. Some Meet transcript controls.
4. **Nobody showed the employee the toggle.** AbilityNet “often unaware”; BDF “took a relatively long amount of time to become familiar.”
5. **IT stack rejects the specialist AT.** BDF managers, 2023, still cited on the March 2026 toolkit page.
6. **Auto-captions written down as the adjustment.** RNID 2026 is the Deaf-specific testimony. It is a survey, not a judgment.

Civil Service and NHS Employers pages reviewed this pass tell staff to *ask* about meeting adjustments and name interpreters as an example. They do not publish a “captions on by default in our tenant” rule.
