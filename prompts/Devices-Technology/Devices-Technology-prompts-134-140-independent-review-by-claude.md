## Don't list (print near any workplace-AI / AT page)

- Do not print that offering AI technology instead of a human interpreter is lawful or unlawful, or that a named employer's practice is discriminatory, without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment.
- Do not print Domain 14's NHS/patient-facing findings (SignGPT, Trust avatar pilots, AIS) as an employment claim. Different duty-holder, different legal framework (EA Part 5 employment vs NHS AIS/service-provision).
- Do not print the all-customer Support Worker/SAE split as the Deaf/HoH split — the join is unpublished (134); HL3809 confirms an item-level FOI hits s.12 (free-text SAE records).
- Do not print an official AtW shift from interpreters to AI captioning. **No official series says that — and the one series that exists moved the other way**: BSL-interpreter customers and real-terms spend both rose every year to 2024/25 (re-verified). RNID's 18% is award-shortfall coping, not a DWP policy change.
- Do not print RNID's £69,620 as the cap. The official cap is £69,260 (carried from prompts 52–54; not re-opened here, flagged for consistency).
- Do not treat built-in English captions, unsaved live captions, or a meeting recap as discharging a requested BSL interpreter.
- Do not print "Teams captions are on by default" or "the recording-consent gate is on by default." Both are **Off by default** (re-verified live for the captions policy).
- Do not print a named UK employer as having piloted or procured an AI signing avatar as a substitute for staff-interpreter hours. Prompt 136: not found. TfL/Signapse/SWR/InSignLanguage are passenger-information deployments, not workplace-meeting substitutes.
- Do not print WFD/WASLI 2018, De Meulder, or CAN-ASC-6.2 as UK employment-law conclusions. Public-authority, research-governance, and Canadian-standard texts respectively — none transfers.
- Do not print that a colleague objecting to an AI notetaker is acting unlawfully, or that the disabled employee may insist on the bot as of right. ICO Art. 21 and EA s.20 both apply; no official source ranks them (137).
- Do not print "consent at the start of the call" as the ICO-preferred basis — ICO says consent is rarely valid in employment.
- Do not print that every voice recording is Article 9 biometric data — it is personal data; special-category only if used to uniquely identify.
- Do not file the Philip Maguire/Microsoft "game changer" quote as UK civil-service testimony — it is an EMEA feature, most likely Irish Department of Finance.
- Do not reuse Bussey, Clinton, Graham, Rimmer, Clarke, Borys, or Murphy as workplace-AI testimony.
- Do not generalise the two RNID survey quotes (n=358) into a national employer-substitution rate.
- Do not print that AI has, or has not, replaced workplace interpreters as a national fact. No hours series exists either way (140); the customer/spend series that does exist rose.
- Do not treat a vendor "solves interpreter scarcity" line as workforce-impact evidence.
- Do not repeat prompt 129 (MAR/SYSC walls) inside a general-meeting GDPR paragraph, and do not collapse 135 (captions off) into 93 (OH) or 137 (saved transcript/recap) — persistence is a distinct processing fact from availability.
- Do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. Follow money and fulfilment.
