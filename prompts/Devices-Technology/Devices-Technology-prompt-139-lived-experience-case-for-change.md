# Prompt 139 — Lived experience and the case for change (workplace AT / AI)

**Master index prompt 139.** Sixth file in the Devices & Technology workplace batch (prompts 134–140). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier.

**What this prompt is for.** UK first-person and named policy asks about *workplace* assistive technology and AI tools — captions that do not work, a bot offered instead of a human, a feature that exists on the licence but is off, an avatar floated as interpreter-substitute. Do not reuse Bussey, Clinton, Graham, Rimmer, Clarke, Borys or Murphy. Do not file RNID / DeafATW 2026 *AtW-delay* quotes as employer-AT evidence unless the source itself names a workplace tool. International testimony stays labelled international.

---

## Headline

UK first-person on this object is **thin and survey-weighted**. The load-bearing published cluster is the RNID / DeafATW 2026 Access to Work survey. It names workplace tools. It is still a survey, not a census, and most quotes sit next to an AtW shortfall rather than a named employer policy.

What it documents:

- Auto-captions and an **Otter** notetaker used as coping when human support is missing or late. The captions miss voices; the employee “zone[s] out.”
- A BSL user given **voice-to-text on a laptop** instead of a booked interpreter at training; she passed, her hearing colleague scored 100% and was promoted over her.
- Some respondents **offered AI captioning as a cheaper substitute** for STTR or an electronic notetaker.
- **18% of BSL-user respondents** (20% of all respondents) said they had to rely more on automatic or AI subtitles because of an award shortfall. That sentence is AtW-shortfall coping. It is not, by itself, evidence that a named employer switched policy from interpreters to AI.

What it does **not** document:

- A named UK employee offered an **AI signing avatar** instead of a requested human interpreter at work. (Prompt 136 found no employer case; this pass found no first-person of that offer either.)
- A named DeafBlind in-post account of workplace navigation kit or AI. (Prompt 119 already logged that gap.)
- A named neurodivergent UK employee’s first-person on an employer-imposed AI notetaker, or on a colleague blocking one. NAS’s 2026 employment toolkit *recommends* AI notes for 1:1s; that is guidance, not testimony.

Policy asks that *are* on this object: ASLI June 2025 (do not introduce workplace AI before Deaf-user testing; do not slow human-support investment); BDA *AI and BSL* paper (AtW / government buyers at risk of being mis-sold cheap AI); RNID Sciencewise dialogue 2026 (community co-design of AI BSL translation). Liam O’Dell 1 January 2026 flags DfE exploring “AI solutions” for BSL without starting with BDA — education-product governance, adjacent, not an employer RA case.

Do not generalise two named survey quotes into a national rate of employer substitution.

## 139.1 What the quotes will and will not carry

The Otter quote and the training quote are the only UK first-person this pass that *name a workplace tool* and describe failure or substitution. Both come from one advocacy survey (n=358, Dec 2025–Jan 2026). They support a case-for-change sentence about **quality and substitution pressure**. They do not support a national rate, a named employer, or a tribunal fact-pattern.

The 18% BSL-user AI-subtitle figure is real inside that survey. The survey itself attributes it to an **award shortfall**. Keep that cause. Do not slide it into “employers have switched to AI.”

No first-person was found of the prompt-136 object (avatar offered at work). Absence of testimony matches absence of a named employer case.

Neurodivergent first-person on *this* object (employer AI notetaker as adjustment, or colleague blocking it) was not found. NAS telling employers the tool can help is not a lived-experience cell.

## 139.2 Policy asks that belong here

Three UK texts ask for governance of workplace / employment-adjacent AI BSL or AI-as-support:

1. ASLI June 2025 — capability and testing before introduction; keep funding humans.
2. BDA discussion paper — buyer-side mis-selling risk at AtW and government.
3. RNID Sciencewise dialogue — co-design with BSL users.

Those are asks. They are not findings that a named employer has already substituted.

Liam O’Dell’s DfE piece stays in the education-governance column. Do not stretch it into an HR cell.
