## Headline

The published UK list for a Deaf or hard-of-hearing office employee is stable and short. RNID, NHS Employers (article dated 9 June 2025), NHS England’s 2017 *What Works* employment guide, and commercial BSL workplace-assessment firms all recycle the same bundle: BSL interpreter or VRI for meetings; flashing-light fire alarm; amplified or captioned telephony / video relay; seating and lighting for lip-reading or sightlines; hearing loop or personal listener where residual hearing is used; plain-English minutes or captions.

Two institutional layers share that list and must not be collapsed:

1. **Employer reasonable adjustments** (Equality Act s.20) — the employer decides, with or without an OH report (prompt 92).
2. **Access to Work** — a DWP grant that can fund communication support and equipment *beyond* what the employer is expected to provide. RNID’s AtW page (updated 15 April 2026) tells claimants a workplace assessment “can include your home if you work from there,” and the BSL Adjustments Planner / Health Adjustment Passport prompts include meetings, equipment and “emergency procedures: whether you need visual alerts or vibrating alarms.” AtW is a payer, not the duty-holder.

OH’s own published templates do **not** contain a Deaf/BSL specimen report. ACAS’s OH pages (17 December 2025) use a chair-adjustment example. SOM 2026 is general. The document that actually lists the Deaf bundle is almost always a **workplace assessment** (AtW-commissioned or specialist) or an employer/charity checklist, not an FOM pro-forma.

Home / hybrid adaptation is **thin in official OH guidance and present in charity / AtW claimant tools**. RNID states the workplace “can include your home.” NHS Employers’ 2025 NHS-staff article stays on-site (flashing fire alarms, loops, desk position). No named UK tribunal or Ombudsman finding was located on this pass where a standard office-hours OH recommendation was made and then failed to transfer to a home-working arrangement. Record that cell as no reliable named case found — not as proof that the transfer always works.

**Hard-don't.** Do not print that an employer “must” fund a full-time interpreter because an OH report said so. Reasonableness is case-specific. Do not print AtW as the legal adjustment.

## 93.1 The office list is a charity / employer checklist, not an OH template

When a manager asks “what does OH usually write?”, the honest answer on this pass is: there is no published national OH specimen. The list everyone quotes lives on RNID, NHS Employers and AtW/planner pages. A competent workplace assessor will put the same items into a report the employer then treats as OH-adjacent advice. Do not invent an official FOM template behind that practice.

## 93.2 AtW is the usual payer for the expensive items

Interpreters, VRS bundles and much of the alerting kit are the items AtW is designed to fund. That is why the published lists blur employer adjustment and AtW award. The legal objects stay separate (prompt 92; prompts 52–54). RNID’s 2026 AtW experience survey is evidence that the *funding* layer is under strain; it is not a finding about what OH wrote.

## 93.3 Home / hybrid is acknowledged, not specified

RNID’s one sentence — the workplace can be the home — is the strongest official-adjacent acknowledgement located. It does not specify video-relay reliability at home, a home fire-alarm standard, or a welfare-check method when the employee is unsupervised. Those remain individual PEEP / s.20 questions. NHS Employers 2025 has not rewritten its list for hybrid NHS roles.

## 93.4 What is unpublished or contested

- No published “typical OH report” corpus for Deaf employees.
- No named UK case of office recommendations failing to transfer to home working.
- Commercial assessor vignettes (Omar in the warehouse; Priya in training) are marketing case studies, not tribunal findings. Flag as anecdotal if reused.
- Iqbal is the named office-hours BSL + OH judgment; the claimant lost. Do not present it as a victory case or as a home-working precedent.
