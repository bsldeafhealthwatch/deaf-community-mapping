# Prompt 94 — Night shift, late / “graveyard” shift and lone working: is there a documented Deaf-specific emergency or welfare-check provision?

**Master index prompt 94.** Load-bearing file in the Occupational Health / working-patterns batch (prompts 92–96). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Test whether UK lone-working and night-work guidance has ever been adapted for a Deaf or DeafBlind worker whose standard check-in or emergency alert is a phone call or a shout. The expected result, stated in the batch notes, is an evidence gap. Log the absence as the finding. Do not paper over it with a paraphrase of generic HSE lone-working advice. Do not print current practice as unlawful without a named statute, regulation or judgment.

**Index check.** `prompts/SuperGrok_Research_Prompt_Pack.md` already lists 92–96. Read through the prompt-92 gate: HSE and the Fire Safety Order bind the employer / responsible person; OH is an adviser.

## Headline

**The headline finding is an absence.** HSE’s live lone-working product — INDG73 *Protecting lone workers* (rev4, March 2020; PDF still the cited text) — requires employers to assess lone-working risk and to have a system “to keep in touch… and respond to any incident.” Suggested methods include phones, radios, email, visiting supervisors, manually or automatically operated alarm devices, and a check that the worker has returned to base or home. The leaflet tells the employer to bear in mind “the worker’s understanding of English.” It does **not** name Deaf, BSL, DeafBlind, text check-in, or vibrating alert.

That silence is the cell. It is not a finding that HSE has banned those methods, and it is not a finding that using a phone-only check-in is automatically unlawful. MHSWR 1999 requires a suitable and sufficient risk assessment; INDG73 is guidance on how to think about lone work. The Equality Act s.20 duty (prompt 92) can still require a different check-in method in an individual case. Those are different claims.

What *does* exist, one layer over:

- RNID’s employer health-and-safety page (updated 26 May 2026) tells employers to fit flashing alarms in toilets, locker rooms and prayer rooms, and says **critical-alert paging systems are “especially useful for lone workers or in large workplaces.”** It also says travel and lone-working policies should “take into account the safety of people who are deaf or have hearing loss.” That is charity guidance to employers, not an HSE protocol and not a published OH-provider SOP.
- The Home Office *means of escape for disabled people* guide (updated 12 September 2023) puts two questions into the PEEP interview: **“Do you work alone in the building? Do you work out of hours?”** Preferred alerts remain flashing beacons and vibrating pagers. A buddy system “should not be done on an informal basis.” That is the closest official UK text to the anchor scenario. It is an individual fire plan, not a night-shift staffing protocol.
- No UK employer, NHS Employers page, care-sector regulator, or named OH provider was found on this pass with a published Deaf-specific night-shift or lone-working protocol (vibrating pager + text buddy + fire-warden rule written down as a sector product).
- No UK tribunal, PHSO or LGSCO finding was found that turns on a Deaf or DeafBlind employee working a night, late or lone shift where the emergency or welfare check was a spoken or telephone mechanism with no accessible alternative. Nearby cases exist and must be labelled correctly: *Iqbal* is office-hours BSL hours; *Sheikh v CIS Security* is night shifts and OH, but the impairment is not Deafness; the BBC EAT shift case (2026 commentary) is late shifts and OH misreading, not Deafness.

**Hard-don't.** Do not print “HSE lone-working practice is unlawful for Deaf staff.” Do not invent a fulfilment rate for vibrating pagers on night shifts.

## 94.1 Why the HSE silence matters, and why it is not a breach finding

INDG73 already lists “email” and “other devices designed to raise the alarm” next to phones. A text or app check-in, or a vibrating man-down device, sits inside that list without HSE having to name Deaf staff. The gap is therefore not “HSE forbids accessible check-in.” The gap is “HSE has not written the Deaf/BSL version, and no UK sector body appears to have written it either.”

Printing the gap as a breach would require a named duty that current generic practice fails as a class. That duty was not found. Individual s.20 claims remain possible; they are not this file.

## 94.2 What the PEEP guide actually gives the anchor scenario

The 2007/2023 disabled-escape guide is the one official UK document that asks the right questions (alone? out of hours?) and names the right kit (beacons, vibrating pagers, non-informal buddy). It applies to the **premises emergency plan**, usually via HR or the building manager, not via an OH clinic. A standard daytime-office OH line that stops at “visual fire alarm” has not, on the published evidence, been rewritten into that PEEP interview for night or lone work.

## 94.3 Sector split — held, not expanded

The batch notes said a genuine NHS/care vs security/facilities split would justify a follow-up prompt rather than expanding 94 after the fact. This pass did not find a published Deaf protocol in either sector. NHS Employers 2025 stays on the office bundle. Security night-shift case law located is not Deaf. Do not spend prompt 97 on that split unless a later source actually names one.

## 94.4 What is unpublished, contested, or has never been asked

- No official count of Deaf BSL employees on night or lone rotas in England, Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland.
- No fulfilment series for vibrating pagers or text check-ins.
- No published OH-provider method for asking “what happens at 03:00 if the fire panel sounds and you are the only person on site.”
- Charity pages (RNID) tell employers to think about it; they do not evidence that employers have.
- Irish HSE and North American vendor pages are not UK cells.
