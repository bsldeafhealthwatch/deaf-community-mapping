Added: 10 September 2026. Covers Occupational Health (OH) — the employer-commissioned service that assesses fitness for work and recommends workplace adjustments — for BSL-using Deaf and DeafBlind employees, across the different conditions work actually happens in: standard in-office hours, home/hybrid working, night and late/"graveyard" shifts, and lone working (on-site with no colleague present, or off-site/mobile roles).

Checked first: the project index and existing batches. Prompts 20–26 (DWP/national) and 27–33 (Jobcentre Plus) cover *DWP's own* claims and job-seeking processes; 52–54 cover *DWP's* Access to Work funding cap and claimant scripts. None of these touch an *employer's* Occupational Health service, which sits under the Equality Act 2010 reasonable-adjustment duty and is a separate institutional actor (typically an external OH provider contracted by the employer, e.g. HR-referred). This is not a duplicate of the neurodivergence batch (85–91) either — that covers diagnostic pathways and DWP/AtW processes for a different (neurodivergent) population; this batch is about the OH referral and adjustment-recommendation process itself, for Deaf/BSL/DeafBlind staff specifically, and about what happens once someone is working, not applying for benefits. It is also distinct from Graeme's own personal OH/workplace-adjustment project thread (a live, ongoing HR/OH process) — this batch is deliberately the general, sourced, citable evidence base, not case-specific advice.

Anchor: a Deaf BSL-using employee whose OH referral produces standard daytime-office recommendations (interpreter for meetings, visual fire alarm) that say nothing about what happens when that same employee works from home, is rostered onto a night or "graveyard" shift with a skeleton staff, or is the only person on site or on a mobile/lone-working assignment — situations where a colleague's spoken alert, a phone call, or a shouted evacuation instruction is the actual safety mechanism, and where standard OH paperwork is silent on it.

Proposed domain: this is a stub under the existing Welfare & Employment cluster (alongside 20–26/27–33/52–54), not a new numbered Domain — it is about the employer/OH institutional layer that sits between those DWP-facing prompts and the workplace itself.

## Prompts

**92. Legal basis, definitions, and what Occupational Health is actually obliged to assess**
> As a UK employment law and occupational health researcher, using the Equality Act 2010 and its statutory Code of Practice, HSE (Health and Safety Executive) guidance, ACAS guidance on Occupational Health referrals, and the Faculty of Occupational Medicine's published standards, establish (1) the legal basis and scope of an employer-commissioned OH referral for a Deaf or DeafBlind employee — is OH itself under any statutory duty, or does the Equality Act reasonable-adjustment duty sit with the employer regardless of what OH recommends, (2) whether any published OH assessment standard, template, or Faculty of Occupational Medicine guidance specifically addresses BSL/Deaf communication needs (as distinct from generic "hearing impairment" checkbox language), (3) whether HSE fire safety and emergency-evacuation guidance (e.g. PAS 79 fire risk assessment guidance, or sector-specific HSE guidance) requires or recommends any specific provision for a Deaf or DeafBlind worker beyond a visual/vibrating alarm, and (4) what is unpublished or contested. Flag thin, unpublished, contested, or anecdotal evidence rather than presenting it as settled. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL. Do not print any claim that OH or an employer is in breach of a legal duty without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment.

**93. Standard in-office and hybrid/remote provision — what a typical OH report actually recommends**
> As a UK workplace-accessibility researcher, using published OH report templates or example recommendations (ACAS, CIPD, Business Disability Forum, Access to Work's own published guidance on what it will fund), employer accessibility policy documents where publicly available, and any tribunal case or Ombudsman finding describing an actual OH recommendation for a Deaf employee, establish (1) what a standard OH recommendation typically covers for a Deaf/BSL-using employee working standard office hours (interpreter provision for meetings, visual fire/alarm systems, textphone or video relay for calls, minute-taking or captioning) and how far these are treated as OH's own remit versus referred on to Access to Work, (2) whether OH assessment practice or published guidance has adapted these recommendations for home/hybrid working (e.g. video relay reliability at home, captioning software, emergency contact/welfare-check arrangements when the employee is unsupervised at home), (3) any documented case or complaint where standard office-hours provision was recommended but did not transfer to a home-working arrangement, and (4) what is unpublished or contested. Flag thin, unpublished, contested, or anecdotal evidence. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL.

**94. Night shift, late/"graveyard" shift, and lone-working provision — the emergency and welfare-check gap**
> As a UK health-and-safety and workplace-accessibility researcher, using HSE guidance on lone working and night work, sector-specific guidance (e.g. NHS Employers, care sector, security/facilities management — sectors known to run night and lone-shift patterns), Equality Act reasonable-adjustment case law or tribunal findings, and charity reports (SignHealth, RNID, BDA) on Deaf employment, establish (1) whether HSE's own lone-working guidance (which already requires a "check-in" or welfare-monitoring system for any lone worker) has ever been adapted or documented for a Deaf or DeafBlind lone worker where the standard check-in method is a phone call, (2) any documented case, tribunal finding, or charity report describing a Deaf or DeafBlind employee working a night, late, or "graveyard" shift, or a lone-working role, where the emergency alert, evacuation instruction, or welfare check relied on a spoken or telephone mechanism with no accessible alternative, (3) whether any UK employer, sector body, or OH provider has a published protocol for night-shift/lone-working Deaf-specific provision (e.g. a vibrating pager-style alert, paired-buddy check-in via text, a specific fire-warden protocol), and (4) what is unpublished, contested, or has simply never been asked. Flag thin, unpublished, contested, or anecdotal evidence rather than presenting it as settled — this prompt is likely to surface an evidence gap rather than a documented solution, and that absence is itself the finding. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL. Do not print any claim that current lone-working or night-shift practice is unlawful without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment.

### Notes for this batch

- **Gate prompt: 92.** The legal-basis/scope question governs how 93–94 are read — if OH itself carries no independent statutory duty (likely; the Equality Act duty sits with the employer), 93 and 94 should be read as "what employers/OH typically do" not "what they are legally required to do," and that distinction must not collapse in any write-up.
- **Prompt 94 is the load-bearing prompt for this batch.** It is very likely to return thin or no UK-specific findings — HSE lone-working guidance is generic and unlikely to name Deaf/BSL provision at all. Log that absence as the headline finding, not a failed search; do not let Grok paper over it with a plausible-sounding generalisation from general lone-working guidance.
- **Hard-don't for this batch:** do not print any claim that current OH, employer, or lone-working practice is unlawful, non-compliant, or a breach of duty without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment. The likely finding is an absence of specific provision, not a breach of an existing requirement — those are different claims.
- **Held-back legs:** none — the batch is short (five prompts) because the topic is narrow; if 94 surfaces a genuine sub-split (e.g. NHS/care-sector shift work vs. security/facilities lone-working, which have different regulatory bodies), that would justify a follow-up prompt rather than expanding this one after the fact.
- **Do not conflate with Graeme's own OH referral project** — this batch produces general, citable evidence for the dossier, not advice on his individual case.
