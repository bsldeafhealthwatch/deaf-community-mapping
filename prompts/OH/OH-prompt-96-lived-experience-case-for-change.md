## Headline

Published UK first-person testimony that names **Occupational Health as the institutional actor** for a Deaf or DeafBlind employee is **thin**. What exists in volume is testimony about **workplace communication support and Access to Work**, which is a different door (prompts 20–26 and 52–54). RNID’s 2026 AtW survey of deaf, deafblind and hearing-loss claimants is the largest recent first-person set; it is about DWP funding of interpreters and equipment, not about an employer’s OH clinic or a night-shift pager.

On non-standard hours and emergency alerting, the UK published trail is also thin and mostly **employer-facing advice rather than worker testimony**:

- RNID tells employers that pagers are “especially useful for lone workers” (26 May 2026) — that is RNID’s voice, not a named employee’s.
- NHS England’s 2017 *What Works* employment guide quotes “Asif,” a BSL-using social-care assessor whose AtW-funded interpreter attends community visits with him. That is lone-ish mobile work with an interpreter present, not a night-shift spoken-alarm gap.
- Commercial assessor vignettes (Omar in a warehouse given strobes and a vibrating pager) are marketing case studies.

No named UK campaign or consultation response was found that is *specifically* about Deaf night-shift or lone-working emergency provision. Closest live policy asks sit next door: Locked Out (27 November 2025) is health and social *care* access, not employer OH; RNID and Deaf-sector AtW evidence to the Work and Pensions Committee (2026) is about the grant cap and process; SOM’s 2026 select-committee evidence asks for better hybrid-working guidance for disabled staff generally.

Named formal findings that touch OH + Deafness remain the tribunal file already logged in prompt 93 (*Iqbal v Home Office*, 2017 — office-hours interpreter hours; claim dismissed). Do not recycle Rimmer, Clarke, Bussey, Elliott or Thomas into this batch; those names belong to JCP, PHSO healthcare and other domains.

**Hard-don't.** Do not generalise two vignettes into a national rate. Do not print a US or Canadian damages case as UK lived experience.

## 96.1 Two different experiences, only one of which is documented

The prompt asked for testimony of an OH referral *and separately* of non-standard hours without adapted alerts. The UK published record answers the second only through employer-advice pages (RNID pagers-for-lone-workers) and one 2017 named vignette of a community assessor who *has* an interpreter with him. It barely answers the first at all. That split is the finding: people write about AtW and about meetings; they do not, in sources located this pass, write about what the OH clinician asked at 11:00 on a Tuesday about a 03:00 fire panel.

## 96.2 Do not borrow names from other batches

Rimmer and Clarke are Jobcentre in-person cases (prompts 27–33). Bussey and Pearl Clinton are healthcare / end-of-life. Elliott is HMRC / PHSO-adjacent in another domain. Thomas was excluded from the neurodivergence lived-experience file for the same reason. Leave them there.

## 96.3 The case for change that *can* be printed

Print the gap, not a campaign that does not exist yet:

- Prompt 92: duty is the employer’s; OH has no BSL-named standard.
- Prompt 93: the published list is daytime-office kit; home/hybrid is a sentence, not a protocol.
- Prompt 94: HSE lone-working guidance does not name Deaf staff; no UK sector protocol and no named judgment were found.
- Prompt 96: almost no first-person UK account of the OH appointment or of a night/lone failure.

A public page that turns that stack into “employers are breaching the Act on night shifts” would be a hard-don’t. A public page that says “the official lone-working leaflet and the official OH standards do not mention BSL, and we could not find a worker account of the night-shift version of the PEEP” would be accurate.

## 96.4 What would close the lived-experience cell

A named, dated first-person account (Limping Chicken, DNS, a charity report with consent) of (i) what the OH report actually listed and omitted, and/or (ii) a night, late or lone shift where the alert or check-in was spoken-only. Until that exists, the cell stays empty. FOI to large public employers for “PEEPs that record lone or out-of-hours working for staff who are deaf or use BSL” is a tracker question, not a new platform.
