# Prompt 37 — Lived experience and the case for change

**Master index prompt 37.** Compiled 8 September 2026 from the AIS working brief (prompts 34–37). Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner.

**Anchor case.** A BSL Deaf patient attends a planned, non-emergency hospital follow-up. The trust system does not hold (or does not act on) a BSL flag. The patient is telephoned instead of texted, emailed or relayed. No in-person BSL interpreter is booked. A video relay link is offered instead.

This prompt surfaces what has gone wrong and been felt by real people — formal complaints, advocacy asks, survey texture, and named services that have fixed part of the problem. Where testimony is anonymised or aggregated, that is said in the same sentence.

---

## Headline

The published named cases cluster on **no interpreter in the room** (and on using family or children as fallback). The **outbound-phone** half of the anchor case is repeatedly described in Healthwatch and local Deaf-community feedback, but is almost never a standalone PHSO finding. *Still Ignored* (2025) is the current quantified successor to SignHealth’s *Sick of It* (2014): 70% never asked about needs; only 7% always get a communication professional; 80% of sign-language users had a booked professional fail to attend. Royal Trinity Hospice is the named good-practice example on the contact-method half.

## 37.1 Formal complaints, CQC findings, PHSO investigations

Use the name only where the family or the Ombudsman has put it in the public domain.

### Named cases

**Alan Graham — Queen Elizabeth Hospital Birmingham / University Hospitals Birmingham NHS FT.** Events 2021; PHSO published 11 February 2026. Born Deaf, BSL first language, heart failure. Professional interpreters on **3 of 11 inpatient weeks**. Sixteen-year-old grandson asked to tell his Deaf mother that CPR would not be attempted and that Alan might not survive the night; 12-year-old granddaughter also used. Not the outpatient-phone scenario, but the same root: need known, system did not produce a registered interpreter, family used as fallback. Trust apologised; payments £900 / £900 / £750. PHSO raised the pattern with NHS England and RNID.

**UHB “Mr X” — CQC, November 2022.** Deaf, BSL and lip-reading, autism and epilepsy. Three feeding-tube procedures without a BSL interpreter or adequate consent recording. Two fixed-penalty notices totalling **£8,000** under Regulation 11 (consent). Same trust, different patient, same interpreter-shaped hole. This is a consent case, not an AIS-named case.

**“Samantha” — GP surgery, PHSO finding reported 19 August 2026.** Booked a flu jab; given a Covid vaccine; BSL video that would have flagged the error was not shown; no interpreter. Apology + £450; practice signed an interpreting contract. Northamptonshire ICB apology. This is a **planned, bookable primary-care appointment** — the closest published PHSO match to the anchor case’s second half. PHSO accompanying figure: since April 2020, 623 reasonable-adjustment investigations, 496 upheld or partly upheld (not BSL-only).

**Mrs E — PHSO summary 198, June 2014.** GP practice refused BSL for three years after a funding dispute. Remedy £3,000 plus action plan. Pre-dates AIS (in force August 2016), but the mechanism — commissioner and practice each pointing at the other — is the same mechanism *Still Ignored* still describes.

No named DeafBlind planned-outpatient or end-of-life PHSO inquiry was found in this pass (consistent with the 8 September 2026 EOL brief).

### Anonymised / aggregated testimony that matches the outbound-phone half

Do not present these as named cases.

- **Healthwatch England** (blog trail 2023–24): GP and hospital “insist on phoning me” despite records stating no phone; husband takes the call; privacy lost. Separate story: cannot book except by phone, husband has to call.
- **Healthwatch Devon / Plymouth / Torbay**, Deaf community feedback, 2022: participants received phone calls “despite their notes clearly stating that they are Deaf”; surgeries told patients it was their job to find the interpreter.
- **Healthwatch Sandwell** accessibility report, 2023: consultant held up a paper saying “No BSL interpreter”, signed “cancelled”, and left. Failure-to-book, not VRI-substitution, but the planned-appointment collapse is the same.
- **Deafness Resource Centre** case-study videos (St Helens): “NHS keep calling my phone”; “No interpreter for surgery”.

## 37.2 Advocacy organisations’ published calls on flagging and booking defaults

Use these as **statements of demand**, not as evidence that the legal analysis in prompt 34 is incomplete.

- ***Locked Out*** (BSL Advisory Board, GOV.UK, 27 November 2025), priorities 1 and 2: patient-controlled interpreter booking with confirmation (NHS App); implement and enforce AIS, including s.95; publish self-assessments; EPR rollout must include a prominent AIS flag; GP booking systems audited so phone is not the only channel. Note the factual tension recorded in prompt 34: s.95 was commenced on 7 July 2025; *Locked Out* in November 2025 still described it as not enacted. Treat as statute commenced, enforcement unused.
- ***Still Ignored* / RNID 2025** asks DHSC to make AIS legally enforceable by using the s.95 machinery, name an accountable lead at every level, and direct CQC to put a specific AIS section in its assessment framework with a stated enforcement path.
- **SignHealth open letter, 14 November 2025** (already in project memory): 49 co-signatories; AIS enforcement sits inside the wider “still ignored” frame. Action Deafness, BDA, RAD, ASLI, NUBSLI named. RNID and Sense were not on that letter.

## 37.3 Research and survey data on how common the pattern is

Repeat the *Still Ignored* numbers from prompt 35 rather than inventing a second series. Additional texture:

- **54%** of deaf / hearing-loss respondents had to rely on a partner, family member or friend to relay or interpret at a healthcare appointment — privacy and accuracy cost, not just inconvenience.
- **75%** of sign-language users reported barriers when being called from the waiting room; **74%** when booking. The flag that should have produced a visual or text call, and an interpreter in the room, is not firing.
- Two-thirds of people who need a communication professional rate their overall healthcare-access experience as **poor**.
- Only **16%** think access is better than before AIS.
- Anxiety specifically attached to “no confirmation the interpreter is coming” is recorded in *Locked Out* as a majority experience, with the **80%** no-show figure sitting on top.
- VRS-as-substitute (small SL sub-sample n = 37): 24% agree it is suitable in place of in-person; 54% disagree; 59% say VRS helps at short notice or in an emergency. Do not inflate n = 37 into a national preference poll.

SignHealth ***Sick of It* (2014)** remains the baseline health-outcome paper (BSL users’ worse cardiovascular and mental-health outcomes). *Still Ignored* is the 2025 access-process successor. **Do not quote *Sick of It* figures as if they were 2025 AIS-compliance rates.**

**Sources** — same *Still Ignored* and *Locked Out* URLs as above.

## 37.4 Named services that have fixed part of the problem

A good-practice example, not only a catalogue of failure.

**Royal Trinity Hospice, London — 2025 access-and-inclusion programme.** Staff report patients are now routinely asked about communication needs and the needs are recorded and shared. Email used as the contact method; flashing doorbell on the inpatient room; BSL-interpreted Talk & Tour events so staff practise working with interpreters. Patient quote published by the hospice: “They communicate with me via email, help me lipread and during my inpatient stay even gave me a flashing doorbell for my room, so I knew when people were coming in.” This is a good-practice example on the **contact-method half** of the anchor case. It is a hospice, not an acute trust; do not over-claim.

**St Helena (VRI in palliative settings)** and the Minnesota / Ottawa / Jacksonville Deaf-volunteer hospice models — already logged in the 8 September 2026 EOL brief as improvement examples. They solve presence of communication support. They do not, on published material, prove a sticky flag across an acute EPR.

**Contact Scotland BSL** (24/7 VRS for Scotland-resident users; Sign Solutions from 1 December 2025) — solves the outbound/inbound contact-method problem for public-service calls. Explicitly not a substitute for clinical face-to-face interpreting. England has no equivalent national VRS (*Locked Out* priority 3).

**SWL ICB December 2024** reasonable-adjustments flag rollout in GP systems, noted by Healthwatch Kingston. Direction of travel; impact not yet independently evaluated. Pair with the known SWL GP BSL contract (DAL from October 2024, Sign Solutions VRI subcontractor) and do not assume the new flag has closed the phone-call hole.

**Practices that moved after a PHSO finding** from “no interpreter contract” to a named BSL supplier (Samantha case, Northamptonshire, 2026). Remediation after harm, not design-out of harm.

- Improving access and inclusion for deaf people at Royal Trinity Hospice — Royal Trinity Hospice, 2025/26 news page. <https://www.royaltrinityhospice.london/news/improving-access-and-inclusion-for-deaf-people-at-royal-trinity-hospice>

## What this does — and does not — prove for the anchor case

| Half of the anchor case | What the lived-experience record shows |
|---|---|
| Flag missing or ignored | Repeated Healthwatch testimony that the record is right and the phone call still happens. Staff survey: only 27% always see the need before the meeting. |
| Telephoned instead of texted | Common in anonymised Healthwatch accounts. Not a standalone named PHSO case in this pass. |
| No in-person interpreter for a planned appointment | Named: Samantha (GP, 2026), Mrs E (GP, 2014), Sandwell “No BSL interpreter” paper. Inpatient analogue: Alan Graham, Mr X. |
| VRI offered instead of face-to-face | Survey texture (n = 37) shows majority disagreement that VRS replaces in-person. No named PHSO case whose published summary is only that substitution. |
| Anxiety, missed care, safety | Family used as interpreters; wrong vaccine; cancelled appointments; 54% relying on relatives; 80% booked-professional no-show. |

The case for change does not depend on a new moral frame. It depends on fulfilment: a recorded need that does not produce the recorded contact method or the booked, registered interpreter is a process failure the 2025 Standard already describes and does not currently count.
