# Prompt 34 — Legal status, scope, and enforcement of the AIS

**Master index prompt 34.** Compiled 8 September 2026 from the AIS working brief (prompts 34–37). Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner.

**Anchor case.** A BSL Deaf patient attends a planned, non-emergency hospital follow-up. The trust system does not hold (or does not act on) a BSL flag. The patient is telephoned instead of texted, emailed or relayed. No in-person BSL interpreter is booked. A video relay link is offered instead.

---

## Headline

The Standard is now a “must comply” information standard, but it still does not force in-person BSL for planned appointments, CQC does not assure it, and there is no national count of the exact failure in the anchor case.

The 2025 AIS refresh (**DAPB1605 v4.0**) is treated as the current specification. The 2016/2017 DCB1605 text is cited only where the 2025 documents still rest on it.

---

## 34.1 What the Standard is, and who it binds

The Accessible Information Standard is information standard **DAPB1605** (previously DCB1605 / SCCI1605). It directs a consistent approach to identifying, recording, flagging, sharing, meeting and — in the 2025 refresh — reviewing the information and communication support needs of patients, service users, carers and parents with a disability, impairment or sensory loss.

- British Sign Language (BSL) interpretation is **explicitly in scope**.
- Spoken-language interpreting is **out of scope** of AIS and sits in a separate NHS England community-language framework.

**Organisations in scope:** all providers of NHS care or publicly funded adult social care — NHS trusts and foundation trusts, GP practices, dentists, pharmacies, ambulance services, private hospitals providing NHS care, local-authority adult social care, and ICBs as commissioners. Commissioners “must have regard” and must make contracts, frameworks and performance management enable the Standard.

**Sources**

- DAPB1605: Accessible Information — NHS England Digital. Approved 3 June 2025; release 30/2025 Version 4.0; assurance end date 30 June 2027. <https://digital.nhs.uk/data-and-information/information-standards/governance/latest-activity/standards-and-collections/dapb1605-accessible-information>
- About the standard — NHS England. In force since August 2016; reviewed, updated and re-published 2025. Page restates s.250 HSCA 2012 as the publishing power. <https://www.england.nhs.uk/accessible-information-standard/about-the-standard/>

## 34.2 Is compliance mandatory — and through what mechanism?

Three layers now sit on top of each other. They are not the same thing. Treating them as one “legal duty” is how briefings go wrong.

### Layer A — the information-standard power

AIS is published under **section 250 of the Health and Social Care Act 2012**. Until July 2025 the operative duty on most bodies was to “have regard” to information standards.

**Section 95 of the Health and Care Act 2022** rewrote s.250 so that a person to whom an information standard applies **“must comply with the information standard, except in so far as the requirement to comply is waived”**. That rewrite was commenced on **7 July 2025** by SI 2025/807.

**Factual tension to hold open.** *Locked Out* (BSL Advisory Board / GOV.UK, 27 November 2025) still says “Section 95 … has not been enacted” and lists “bringing section 95 into force” as priority 2. The commencement instrument is dated July 2025. The honest reading is: the statutory “must comply” wording is now in force, but the monitoring, waiver-regulation and penalty machinery that would make AIS feel enforced has not been used in public against named NHS bodies. Advocacy documents published after commencement still describe an enforcement vacuum — and on operational evidence that description remains fair.

**Sources**

- Health and Care Act 2022 s.95. Commencement information I2: in force 7 July 2025 by S.I. 2025/807, reg. 2 (with reg. 3). <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/31/section/95>
- Health and Social Care Act 2012 s.250 as amended. Current text includes new (6A) “must comply” duty and (6D) pointer to enforcement against non-public bodies via s.277E. <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2012/7/section/250>
- Impact assessment: HCA 2022 s.95 information standards — DHSC, March 2025. States the 2022 Act was intended to make information standards binding and extend them to CQC-registered private providers, with financial penalties for private providers. <https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67e13b8a0114b0b86e59f4b7/health-and-care-act-section-95-information-standards-impact-assessment.pdf>

### Layer B — the NHS Standard Contract (trusts, not most GPs)

The 2026/27 full-length Service Conditions contain an explicit clause:

> **SC12.3** “The Provider must comply with the Accessible Information Standard.”

**SC13.2** separately requires appropriate assistance and reasonable adjustments for communication difficulties including hearing impairments.

That is a contractual must for services commissioned on the Standard Contract. It is not, by itself, the GMS/PMS primary-care contract. Commissioners are also required by AIS requirement 2.1 to write the Standard into contracts, tariffs, frameworks and performance management, including incentives and penalties.

**Source**

- NHS Standard Contract 2026/27 Service Conditions (full length) — NHS England, January 2026. SC12.3 and SC13.2. <https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/03-full-length-service-conditions-2627.pdf>

### Layer C — Equality Act 2010, CQC fundamental standards, Care Act

AIS is repeatedly described as supporting the Equality Act 2010 anticipatory reasonable-adjustment duty, including s.20(6) accessible-format information. The Care Act 2014 s.4 requires local-authority information and advice to be accessible.

CQC maps AIS to a long list of fundamental standards (person-centred care, dignity, consent, safety, complaints, governance, staffing, duty of candour) but is explicit that:

> “The AIS is not assured by CQC, that is the role of the commissioner.”

CQC “may take action” if communication needs are not met. There is **no CQUIN indicator** that pays trusts for AIS compliance. Self-assessment is the 2025 accountability tool — organisations are expected to complete the NHS England self-assessment framework, publish a rating and an action plan. That is not the same as a regulator audit.

**Sources**

- Meeting the Accessible Information Standard — Care Quality Commission, page last updated 10 March 2026. “AIS is not assured by CQC.” Assessed under the responsive key question. Adult social care PIR asks how the service meets AIS. <https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-providers/meeting-accessible-information-standard>
- Why it’s important — NHS England AIS pages. “Have regard” language still used on this page even after the 2025 refresh — an example of official wording lagging the s.95 commencement. <https://www.england.nhs.uk/accessible-information-standard/why-its-important/>

## 34.3 What “flagging” is supposed to mean

The 2025 requirements treat flagging as a **must**, not a should.

Organisations must put electronic flags or alerts (or paper equivalents) on the record so staff can see that a need exists and are prompted to act. Flags should, where possible, trigger accessible correspondence automatically and must stop a standard print letter going out when it is the wrong format. Needs must be recorded at first contact or the next interaction, and re-checked at a listed set of events including first appointment, emergency (as soon as practical), health check, care-planning, and when a diagnosis changes the need.

### Where the flag lives

This is a local-system question, not a single national field. The Standard requires SNOMED CT (or legacy Read v2 / CTV3) codes from defined subsets, or the human-readable category if the system cannot take those codes.

- **Primary care:** usually EMIS or SystmOne coded entries plus a local alert.
- **Trusts:** the PAS / EPR alert plus, increasingly, the separate Reasonable Adjustment Digital Flag (**DAPB4019**) on Spine / National Care Records Service.

**AIS and RADF are related but not the same standard.** NHS England’s RADF action checklist still tells providers to reach full RADF compliance by **30 September 2026**. Project verification on 1 September 2026 recorded official DAPB4019 conformance dates of Health **31 March 2027** / social care **31 October 2027**. Use both dates and do not collapse them.

### Who enters the flag

“Health and social care staff” at first or next interaction. Requirement 1.5 says organisations **should** identify an AIS lead. IT / records colleagues must make the system capable of holding and surfacing the codes (requirements 3.1–3.16). There is no named national job-holder who is personally liable if a flag is missing.

### Does the flag follow the patient?

The Standard says organisations must, **where possible**, include recorded needs in existing data-sharing, referral, discharge and handover. NHS e-Referral Service guidance tells the referrer to put accessibility needs in a prominent position on the referral letter and tells the receiving provider to assign an Additional Requirements Manager to act on them.

That is process guidance, not a guaranteed sticky flag across every PAS. Healthwatch Kingston’s SWL fieldwork found GP staff describing EMIS flag overload — too many alerts, so the communication one is missed. That is the operational hole the anchor case falls through.

## 34.4 Appointment booking and contact methods — does AIS require in-person BSL?

**Short answer.** AIS requires the recorded need to be met promptly, by a qualified communication professional, via accessible contact methods. It does **not**, in the 2025 specification, say “in-person BSL is the default for every planned appointment and VRI is only an emergency fallback.”

### What the musts actually say

- People must be able to access services through accessible contact methods (**requirement 1.29**). CQC’s explainer lists email, text and Text Relay as examples. A phone-only outbound contact to a flagged BSL user is a failure of 1.29 if text, email or relay was the recorded method.
- Communication support must be provided promptly, “for example arranging interpreter support … without unreasonable delay” (**1.31**).
- BSL interpreters and deafblind interpreters must have appropriate qualifications, DBS, a professional code, and valid registration (NRCPD or RBSLI cited) (**1.32**).
- Longer appointments are a **should**, not a must (**1.33**).

### What the 2025 implementation guidance says about video

Remote access (VRS / VRI) “enable 3-way conversations” and “are particularly useful in urgent or emergency care settings when it may not be possible to arrange for face-to-face support … in time.”

Then the critical sentence:

> Remote services are not a total replacement for face-to-face interpretation or communication support and may not be appropriate in some circumstances (for example, longer appointments). Where possible, and for routine care, individuals should be given the option of remote or face-to-face interpretation.

That is a **patient-choice / option** framing, not a legal default to in-person. A trust that offers only VRI for a bookable follow-up is therefore in a grey zone: it has not broken a bright-line AIS rule that does not exist, but it has ignored the guidance that routine care should offer the choice, and it will usually also have broken the Equality Act anticipatory-adjustment analysis if the patient has already said they need in-person BSL.

Scotland’s national interpreting policy is tighter than England’s AIS on this point (see prompt 36).

- Improvement framework: community language translation and interpreting services — NHS England. Explicitly excludes BSL; points BSL back to AIS. <https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/improvement-framework-community-language-translation-and-interpreting-services/>

## 34.5 Named CQC / NHSE enforcement against specific trusts

No CQC enforcement notice located in this pass that is titled as an AIS communication-method or interpreter-booking breach. Enforcement that does exist travels under neighbouring regulations.

- **University Hospitals Birmingham NHS FT — November 2022.** CQC served two fixed-penalty notices totalling £8,000 for breaches of Regulation 11 (consent) in the care of “Mr X”, a deaf man who used BSL and lip-reading. On three feeding-tube procedures the Trust arranged no BSL interpreter and did not involve family when obtaining consent. This is the closest named CQC money-penalty case. It is a **consent case, not an AIS-named case**.
- **Same Trust, PHSO Alan Graham investigation published 11 February 2026** (see prompt 37). Interpreters provided on 3 of 11 inpatient weeks; children used to interpret end-of-life news. PHSO found failure to follow national guidance. Remedy: apology plus £900 / £900 / £750. Not a CQC fine.
- **CQC SignLife Project CIC warning notices, August 2025** — a Deaf-led provider, not an NHS trust failing AIS. Out of scope of the anchor case except as a reminder that CQC will use staffing / governance / person-centred-care regulations around BSL services.
- No NHS England published compliance report was found that names individual trusts as AIS-non-compliant on contact method or interpreter booking. The 2025 self-assessment framework is designed to create that visibility locally; a **national scored dataset is not published**.

**Sources**

- CQC fines NHS trust for failing to seek consent — Mills & Reeve note of the UHB November 2022 FPNs. <https://www.mills-reeve.com/blogs/health-and-care/november-2022/cqc-fines-nhs-trust-for-failing-to-seek-consent-to/>
- Teenager had to tell his Deaf mother that her father might die — PHSO news release, 11 February 2026, Alan Graham / UHB. <https://www.ombudsman.org.uk/news-and-blog/news/teenager-had-tell-his-deaf-mother-her-father-might-die-after-hospital-failed>

## Anchor case walked against these rules

| Failure | AIS status today |
|---|---|
| Flag missing or inert | Recording and flagging are musts. Sharing on referral is a must “where possible”. Breach. No automatic CQC finding. |
| Telephoned instead of texted | Requirement 1.29. If text/email/relay is the recorded method, a voice call is non-compliant. |
| VRI offered instead of in-person for a planned follow-up | Not a numbered AIS must. Guidance says offer a choice for routine care. Usually an Equality Act problem if the recorded preference is in-person. |
| Who can force a fix | SC12.3 for Standard Contract providers; ICB contract schedule; CQC only via a fundamental-standard breach; PHSO after local complaint. No named SWL ICB AIS exec lead. |
