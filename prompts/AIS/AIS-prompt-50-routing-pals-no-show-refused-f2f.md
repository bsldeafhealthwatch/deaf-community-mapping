# Prompt 50 — Routing: who to write to, no-show, refused face-to-face

**Master index prompt 50.** Compiled 9 September 2026. Practical companion to prompt 46. Do not draft letters until 51 is also in. Dossier rule: sourced and dated.

**Question.** For the three template jobs (flag my needs; book an in-person interpreter for a dated appointment; complain about a past failure), who is the right addressee, and what changes when the interpreter does not attend or the service offers VRI only?

**Headline.** Upcoming appointments go to **PALS or the booking desk**, not a formal complaint. Past failures go to **one** body — the provider **or** the commissioner, not both. GP first line is the **practice manager**. NHS.uk still says primary-care complaints to NHS England; Age UK (Dec 2025) and Patients Association send them to the **ICB**. Treat that as contested routing and tell the user to check the live ICB/NHS England page before they post. AIS 2025 guidance does **not** ban VRI; it says offer F2F or remote for routine care.

## 50.1 Addressee map

| Situation | First addressee | Formal complaint if that fails | Do not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flag / correct AIS record at a GP | Practice manager (or “AIS lead” if the practice names one) | Practice complaints procedure **or** commissioner — pick one | Write to CQC as if they will fix the flag |
| Flag at a hospital / community trust | PALS or patient-experience team; copy the clinic booking team | Trust complaints manager | CC the chief executive on day one |
| Book an interpreter for a **dated** appointment that has not yet happened | The team that holds the booking + PALS if they stall. Ask them to confirm the booking in writing. | Only if they refuse and the appointment is now unusable — still prefer “urgent concern” via PALS over a 12-month complaint | Start a PHSO file before the appointment |
| Past appointment: no interpreter / wrong method | Provider complaints team (practice manager or trust complaints) | If already used the provider, you generally cannot have the ICB re-investigate the same complaint | Complain to provider **and** ICB about the same incident |
| Mental health trust | That trust’s PALS/complaints (often a combined inbox) | Same trust, then PHSO | Assume the acute hospital PALS covers it |
| NHS 111 / ambulance | Provider PALS/complaints, or ICB as commissioner | PHSO | GP practice manager |
| Public health (sexual health, stop-smoking) | Local authority | Local authority then LGSCO, not PHSO | Trust PALS |
| Interpreter agency conduct (late, no-show, quality) | The **NHS body that booked them** first; they hold the contract. Agency complaints inbox is extra, not a substitute. | Trust/practice complaint + ask the body to raise it with the contractor | Treat the agency as the NHS complaints body |

## 50.2 Interpreter did not attend (no-show / DNA of the interpreter)

No national NHS patient letter or flowchart was found that says “when the BSL interpreter does not arrive, do X.” What exists is **trust operational policy** and the complaints ladder.

| Label | Value | Source name | Source URL | Publication date | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trust-level rule (example, not national) | AWP interpreting policy: if the F2F booking is not confirmed four working days out, chase the provider; if F2F cannot be sourced and phone/video is not suitable, tell the patient and rearrange. Failures (not booked, unavailable, did not attend, quality) to be logged as incidents under “Communication support.” | Avon and Wiltshire Mental Health Partnership interpreting policy | https://www.awp.nhs.uk/download_file/7706/755 | Live document retrieved 2026 | Verified as one-trust policy, not a national rule |
| Patient action that matches PALS purpose | On the day: tell reception / clinic coordinator immediately. Ask them to (a) attempt a same-day replacement (VRI only if you accept it), or (b) stop the appointment and rebook with an interpreter confirmed. Then email PALS the same day with time, clinic, what you were told. That is an urgent concern, not yet a formal complaint. | NHS.uk PALS purpose; AWP “tell the patient and rearrange” | NHS.uk | Live | Verified as synthesis of official PALS purpose + one-trust ops. Not a statutory script. |
| After the event | If the appointment went ahead without you understanding, or was cancelled to your detriment: formal complaint to the provider. Ask them to record it as a communication-support incident and to confirm the next booking. | AWP incident category; RNID “what to do if needs aren’t met” | RNID rights page 2 Jul 2025 | 2025 | Verified as pathway, not a template |
| Agency vs NHS body | The patient complains to the NHS body that made the booking. The body owns the contract. An agency inbox (e.g. Language Empire “complaints@…”) is for the contractor’s QA, not the 2009 Regulations process. | Agency QA pages; 2009 Regulations responsible body | Language Empire example page | Live | Verified |
| National “interpreter DNA rate” letter | Not found. | This pass | — | 9 Sep 2026 | No reliable figure found |

**Template implication:** one short “today’s interpreter did not arrive — please rebook and confirm” PALS/clinic email. Separate from the past-failure complaint letter.

## 50.4 Who holds the booking contract (why the letter target matters)

The patient writes to the **NHS body**, not the commercial agency, unless they are only giving contractor feedback.

Project-local fact (already sourced in the SWL agency tracker, not re-proved here): SWL GP BSL from Oct 2024 sits with DAL, Sign Solutions as VRI subcontractor; ICB not PCNs holds the contract. Hospital packages on LanguageLine notices (ESTH, Croydon, Kingston & Richmond, May 2026) do not itemise BSL. Use that in a *local* letter only when the FOI/award notice is attached. A national template should say “the service that booked the appointment,” not name DAL.

## 50.5 What each future template is *for*

| Working title | Addressee | Timing | Formal complaint? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Record my communication needs | Practice manager / clinic admin | Before any appointment | No |
| B. Confirm in-person interpreter for [date] | Booking team + PALS if no reply | Days before the appointment | No — operational |
| C. Today’s interpreter did not arrive | Clinic coordinator same day; PALS email | Same day | Not yet |
| D. Past appointment with no support | Provider complaints **or** ICB, not both | Within 12 months | Yes |
| E. Do not publish | Letter before action | After D has failed | Separate product (48 + 49) |

## 50.6 Finding help without building a new directory

- PALS: trust website.  
- Practice manager: GP website or NHS.uk GP page.  
- ICB complaints: https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/find-your-local-integrated-care-board plus the ICB “complaints” page.  
- NHS England complaints (still listed for some primary/specialised services): england.nhs.uk contact-us/feedback-and-complaints.  
- Advocacy: search “NHS complaints advocacy” + local authority; Healthwatch; VoiceAbility 0300 303 1660 / helpline@voiceability.org.  
- PHSO: after local resolution, ombudsman.org.uk.

Do not ship a static national address book. Addresses rot. Point at the finder pages.
