# Prompt 48 — Professional/advocate letters vs patient-facing

**Master index prompt 48.** Compiled 9 September 2026. Templates-section batch. Do not draft until 49 also returns.

**Question.** What actually differs when the same AIS/NHS access ask is written by the patient, versus by an advocate, caseworker or solicitor on their behalf?

**Headline.** The first ask should stay a **plain request** (SignHealth / Citizens Advice “short and simple”). A **letter before action** is a later instrument, after a complaint has failed or when a county-court Equality Act claim is actually in view. Putting LBA language in a first booking letter is not required by the Civil Procedure Rules and cuts against the Practice Direction rule that pre-action steps must not be used as a tactic. Professional letters add **consent, “on behalf of”, a correspondence address, and named statutes**. They do not need a 14-day “or we sue” deadline on day one.

A personal letter can name the Equality Act and AIS. It does not need section numbers, limitation, costs, or “this is a letter before action” in the heading.

## 48.2 Deadlines — what is customary, and for which letter

| Letter type | Customary deadline | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| First personal request (flag / book interpreter) | None required. SignHealth: none. Mind GP adjustments: “I hope you can confirm.” CA short request: “I look forward to hearing from you soon.” | SignHealth 2025; Mind; CA | Verified |
| Formal NHS complaint | 3 **working days** to acknowledge (statute + Constitution). No set investigation deadline. 6-month statutory backstop then a delay letter. Local trusts often *aim* at 25–40 working days — not law (prompt 46). | SI 2009/309; NHS.uk | Verified |
| Equality Act letter before action (when a claim is actually contemplated) | PD Pre-Action Conduct: defendant should respond in a **reasonable time — 14 days in a straightforward case, no more than 3 months in a very complex one**. CA LBA page uses 14 days. One published belief-discrimination LBA template uses 21 days. None of these is an NHS-complaints deadline. | PD Pre-Action Conduct para 6(b); CA | Verified |
| Clinical negligence letter of claim | 14 days acknowledge; 4 months reasoned response. Copy NHSR if the defendant is an NHS trust. | Clinical Disputes Protocol 3.23–3.24 | Verified |
| Generic “14 days or else” on a first NHS booking letter | Not supported as an NHS obligation. MK Deaf Zone borrowed 14 days from a Scope-style *service-provider* adjustments letter. Putting it on a GP/trust first request confuses the PD clock with the complaints clock. | MK Deaf Zone 2021; prompt 47 | Verified as mismatch |

If a professional letter needs a date at first-request stage, the honest ones are: “please confirm the interpreter booking **before [appointment date]**” (operational) or “please acknowledge this formal complaint within three working days” (complaints). Not “14 days or we issue.”

## 48.3 When LBA language is appropriate — and when it is premature

| Label | Value | Source name | Source URL | Publication date | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| What an LBA is for | Last step before a **court claim**. Heading “letter before action”; facts; legal basis; remedy; reasonable reply period; “we will start proceedings if you do not.” | CA “writing a letter before action”; PD Pre-Action Conduct para 6 | justice.gov.uk PD; CA housing-discrimination LBA page (same PD rules apply to service-discrimination claims generally) | Current | Verified |
| Litigation is last resort | “Litigation should be a last resort.” Protocols / the PD “must not be used by a party as a tactical device to secure an unfair advantage.” | PD Pre-Action Conduct paras 3 and 4 | https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/rules/pd_pre-action_conduct | Current | Verified |
| EHRC | Good practice to seek information and follow the pre-action PD **before making a claim**. That may avoid a formal claim. It is not a duty to fire an LBA at first contact. | EHRC Services Code ch.14 | https://www.equalityhumanrights.com/guidance/codes-practice/code-practice-services-public-functions-and-associations/chapter-fourteen | Current | Verified |
| CA sequence | Short simple request first. Formal complaint next. LBA if you have time before the court deadline **and** you are going to court. “If you’ve already made a complaint, you could base the letter on that.” | CA asking-for-adjustments + LBA pages | citizensadvice.org.uk | Live 2026 | Verified |
| Equality Act clock | County court: **six months minus one day** from the act (s.118), extendable if just and equitable. An LBA eats into that clock; it does not freeze it. | EA 2010 s.118; EHRC ch.14 | legislation.gov.uk | 2010 | Verified |
| NHS complaints first | PHSO will normally require local resolution exhausted (or six months with no response). A first booking request is not that stage. | Prompt 46; PHSO tips | ombudsman.org.uk | Live 2026 | Verified |
| Disability Justice | Publishes both a **complaint** letter and a separate **LBA** for BSL/support not provided. That split is the right model: do not collapse them into one first-request template. | Prompt 47 | disabilityjustice.org.uk | Guide 2018, site live | Verified |

**For this project’s Templates section:** LBA language is **not** appropriate on the personal/grassroots first-request or appointment-booking letter. It belongs, if at all, on a clearly labelled later rung, after local complaint, with a warning that it is not legal advice (prompt 49).
