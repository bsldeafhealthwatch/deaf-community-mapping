# Prompt 49 — What NOT to put in a public self-help template

**Master index prompt 49.** Compiled 9 September 2026. Hard constraint list for any Templates section. Not legal advice. Distinct from prompt 42 (publishing a scorecard): this is about a **letter an individual sends**, and about a **non-law-firm** publishing the blank form.

**Headline.** A public fill-in letter can stay publishable if it names the **service**, sticks to **facts**, warns about **other people’s data**, and carries a **“not legal advice / you send this”** disclaimer in the Citizens Advice style. It becomes unsafe when it names a nurse as the villain, accuses crime or “illegal non-compliance,” pastes LBA/costs threats into a first ask, or holds itself out as reserved legal work.

---

## Hard don’ts (put these on the ideas-doc “Don’t” list before any draft)

1. Do not pre-fill or prompt the user to name an individual clinician as the wrongdoer. Name the trust, practice or clinic.
2. Do not use “illegal,” “breach of the Act,” “you are non-compliant,” “fraud,” or “misconduct in public office.”
3. Do not put “letter before action,” costs threats, or “we will issue proceedings” on a first-request or first-complaint template (prompt 48).
4. Do not invent statutory deadlines (25 working days; “AIS requires F2F”; “VRI is unlawful”).
5. Do not hold the site out as a law firm, or the letter out as legal advice.
6. Do not invite the user to paste another person’s health details, a child’s name, or a companion’s data without a consent warning.
7. Do not tell the user to copy the letter to the press, social media, or the named staff member’s home/personal email.
8. Do not include a signature block for “your solicitor” or “your advocate” unless the user is actually that person and has enclosed consent.

## Disclaimer block to reuse (draft for the ideas doc, not a letter)

> This is a sample letter for England. It is general information, not legal advice and not a complaints-advocacy service. Check every fact before you send it. Name the GP practice, clinic or trust — not a member of staff unless you need the name to identify the appointment. Do not copy this letter to social media or the press. If you are writing for someone else, attach their written consent. If you want to take legal action, get advice from a solicitor or a Law Centre. Free NHS complaints advocacy: ask your local Healthwatch.
