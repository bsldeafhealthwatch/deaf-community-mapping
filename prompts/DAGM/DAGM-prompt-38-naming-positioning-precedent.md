# Prompt 38 — Naming, positioning, and precedent

**Master index prompt 38.** First file in the Deaf Access Gap Monitor / BSL Policy Maker scoping batch (prompts 38–45). Compiled 8 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Stress-test whether a public, queryable platform that tracks UK Deaf / Deafblind / BSL policy commitments (Equality Act 2010, BSL Act 2022, Accessible Information Standard / DAPB1605) against actual local-authority or NHS spend and delivery already exists, has been attempted, or has a close cousin. A genuine gap in precedent is a finding, not a failed search.

**Working name under test.** “Deaf Access Gap Monitor” / “BSL Policy Maker”. Naming collisions and official-sounding risk are flagged here only where they affect *positioning*; the dedicated name-availability check is prompt 39.

---

## Headline

No public, queryable UK platform was found that does the specific job this idea describes: Deaf / Deafblind / BSL *policy commitment* on one side, *local-authority or NHS spend and delivery* on the other, with the gap flagged as a structured, reusable dataset.

What does exist is adjacent, not equivalent:

- **Official self-reporting** — BSL Act annual reports (central departments only; communications counts, not NHS/LA spend); AIS self-assessment (organisations publish their own RAG, no national dataset).
- **Treaty / recommendation trackers** — EHRC Human Rights Tracker (England and Wales); Scottish Government Human Rights Tracker (launched March 2026). Both track UN recommendations against *government action*, not pound-spend or interpreter fulfilment.
- **Periodic charity / board reports** — *Still Ignored* (SignHealth + RNID, 2025), *Locked Out* (BSL Advisory Board, 27 Nov 2025). Snapshots, not living platforms.
- **Civic-tech cousins that are live and useful as build-path models** — Climate Emergency UK / mySociety Council Climate Action Scorecards; Institute for Government Performance Tracker; WhatDoTheyKnow + WhatDoTheyKnow Projects. None of these is Deaf-specific.
- **Spend scrapers** — council £500+ payment publications, CouncilSpend, one-off FOI campaigns. Interpreting lines are not isolated as a national BSL series.

Closest named collision found on Companies House: **Deaf Access Trust** (02705030), a live charity, not a tracker. That is a naming problem for prompt 39, not a functional precedent.

---

## 38.1 What would count as “this tool already exists”

The search treated a hit as a true equivalent only if all four were present:

1. Public and queryable (not an internal spreadsheet, not a one-off PDF).
2. Deaf / Deafblind / BSL-specific *or* disability-rights with a BSL / language-access slice that can be filtered.
3. Holds the *commitment* (statute, standard, strategy action) in structured form.
4. Holds *spend or delivery* at local-authority or NHS-provider grain, and surfaces the gap.

Anything missing one of those four is logged as a neighbour, not a duplicate.

## 38.2 UK policy trackers and spend trackers for disability rights generally

### Still maintained — closest neighbours

**EHRC Human Rights Tracker (England and Wales).** Launched 2019; relaunched January 2025 with a new method that scores *government action on UN recommendations* rather than a broader “progress” narrative. Searchable; assessments updated periodically. Covers disability as one of several UN-treaty themes (CRPD sits in the UK’s ratified set). January 2025 release: 200+ assessments, including an explicit finding of **no action** by UK and Welsh governments on a UN recommendation for a legal duty on local authorities to fund sign-language learning for parents of deaf children. That is a policy-commitment tracker, not a spend/delivery tracker. It does not hold NHS trust AIS scores or council interpreting invoices.

- Over 200 assessments of government action on human rights released — Equality and Human Rights Commission, 29 January 2025. <https://www.equalityhumanrights.com/media-centre/news/over-200-assessments-government-action-human-rights-released>
- Check on UK and Welsh government action — EHRC Human Rights Tracker landing page. <https://www.equalityhumanrights.com/thehumanrightstracker>
- Historic public URL used in 2021 NHRI briefings: humanrightstracker.com (same product; confirm current canonical host before linking externally).
- EHRC Annual Report and Accounts 2025–26 (PDF), section on developing and updating the Tracker. <https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4ce0694889d85e75ab4115/1167_EHRC_Annual_Report_2025-26_FINAL_ONLINE_ACC.pdf>
- Human Rights Day 2025: Examining progress on human rights in Wales — Senedd Research, 10 December 2025. Notes the January 2025 relaunch. <https://research.senedd.wales/research-articles/human-rights-day-2025-examining-progress-on-human-rights-in-wales>

**Scottish Government Human Rights Tracker.** Official tool at humanrightstracker.gov.scot, launched mid-March 2026. Phase 1: searchable repository of 500+ UN treaty-body recommendations, tagged by theme and affected group (disabled people are one tagged group, 116 recommendations cited in contemporaneous press). Phase 2 (implementation methodology and outcome reporting) was explicitly made subject to the 2026 Holyrood election. Designed with a Tracker Design Group that included Inclusion Scotland, SHRC, Human Rights Consortium Scotland. This is a government-built transparency tool, not an independent gap-monitor, and it does not ingest council or NHS spend.

- Launch post — Scot Gov Fairer, 15 March 2026, pointing to <https://humanrightstracker.gov.scot/>
- Human rights: Tracker Design Group — Scottish Government groups page. <https://www.gov.scot/groups/human-rights-tracker-design-group/>
- Human Rights Tracker Scotland: Equality Impact Assessment — published 12 March 2026. Phase 1 March 2026; phases 2–3 election-contingent. <https://www.gov.scot/publications/human-rights-tracker-scotland-equality-impact-assessment-eqia/pages/3/>
- How a new tool lets you track Scotland’s progress on human rights — *The National*, 15 March 2026. Notes EHRC has the England/Wales counterpart and the UK Government does not. <https://www.thenational.scot/news/25935420.new-tool-can-let-can-track-scotlands-progress-human-rights/>

**Institute for Government — Public Services Performance Tracker.** Annual, live since 2017; 2025 edition with Nuffield Foundation. 250+ indicators on spend, demand, staffing and performance across nine English public services including hospitals, GP, adult social care. National and (from 2025) some geographic variation. **No BSL, AIS, or interpreting series.** Useful as a *form* precedent: a think-tank, not a regulator, publishing a citable annual stocktake that select committees and journalists reuse.

- Public Services Performance Tracker 2025 — Institute for Government, 15 October 2025. <https://www.instituteforgovernment.org.uk/publication/public-services-performance-tracker-2025>
- Trackers index — Institute for Government. Also lists Whitehall Monitor, Parliamentary Monitor, manifesto tracker. <https://www.instituteforgovernment.org.uk/our-work/trackers>

**Climate Emergency UK / mySociety — Council Climate Action Scorecards.** Closest *method* cousin in UK civic tech. All UK councils scored against a published question set (93 questions in 2025, 7 sections); volunteer marking; council right of reply; public site; 2023 and 2025 editions comparable. Built with WhatDoTheyKnow Pro batch FOI and mySociety’s GRACE crowdsourcing tool. Over 1 in 5 councils reported as using the Scorecards internally. This is climate, not disability, but it is the documented UK playbook for “commitment vs local delivery, all councils, public, right of reply”.

- Council Climate Action Scorecards — Climate Emergency UK / mySociety partnership page. 2025 edition published 17 June 2025; average score 38%. <https://climateemergency.uk/action-scorecards/>
- How’s your local council doing on climate action? — mySociety, 17 June 2025. <https://www.mysociety.org/2025/06/17/hows-your-local-council-doing-on-climate-action/>
- mySociety climate programme page (Scorecards + CAPE climate-plan archive + WDTK Pro). <https://www.mysociety.org/climate/>

**Disability Rights UK — Funding Justice.** Live campaign page (updated into 2026) that publishes a funding-flow finding: for every £100 given to disability rights led by non-disabled people, £2.70 reaches disabled-people-led organisations; £460m government contracts to 9 large charities vs £12.5m to 90 DPOs. This is a *funding-justice* snapshot, not a queryable policy-vs-spend platform, and it is not BSL-sliced.

- Funding Justice — Disability Rights UK, page seen current 4 September 2026. <https://www.disabilityrightsuk.org/funding-justice>

### Official self-reporting that is not a public gap-monitor

**AIS self-assessment framework (NHS England).** Organisations are expected to complete a RAG self-assessment and, under the 2025 refresh, to publish annually by March 2027. There is **no national aggregated AIS compliance dataset**. CQC is explicit that it does not assure AIS. This is the opposite of an independent monitor: the body being measured marks its own homework and may or may not publish it.

- Assessing your level of compliance — NHS England. <https://www.england.nhs.uk/accessible-information-standard/assessing-your-level-of-compliance/>
- Accessible Information Standard – self-assessment framework — NHS England long-read. ICBs “should publish assurance evidence”; organisations “should publish annually”. <https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/accessible-information-standard-self-assessment-framework/>
- Meeting the Accessible Information Standard — CQC, updated 10 March 2026. “The AIS is not assured by CQC.” <https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-providers/meeting-accessible-information-standard>
- Still Ignored: the fight for accessible healthcare — SignHealth and RNID, 2025. States DHSC confirmed AIS is not directly assured by CQC and there is no requirement to report AIS compliance into a national dataset. <https://signhealth.org.uk/resources/still-ignored-the-fight-for-accessible-healthcare/>

**Reasonable Adjustment Digital Flag (DAPB4019).** Information standard with conformance dates (health 31 March 2027 / social care 31 Oct 2027 on the official DAPB page as verified in this dossier on 1 September 2026; NHS England checklist pages have lagged). A flag on the Spine is not a public spend/delivery tracker.

- DAPB4019: Reasonable Adjustment Digital Flag — NHS England Digital. <https://digital.nhs.uk/data-and-information/information-standards/governance/latest-activity/standards-and-collections/dapb4019-reasonable-adjustment-digital-flag>

**National Disability Strategy (2021) and Disability Action Plan (Feb 2024).** Strategy commitments were partially paused after a High Court declaration; a 18 September 2023 ministerial deposit listed 47 completed / 54 in progress / 1 paused / 2 dropped. The incoming Labour government (Dec 2024 PQ, Stephen Timms) treated both documents as previous-administration initiatives and did not adopt them as a live tracker. There was never a public queryable implementation dashboard against those 100+ commitments.

- The National Disability Strategy: Content, reaction and progress — House of Commons Library CBP-9599, updated 8 July 2025. <https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-9599/>

**UNCRPD UK implementation.** Monitored through UK Independent Mechanism reports, UN Committee concluding observations, and government follow-up papers — not through a public spend-vs-duty dashboard. Commons Library CBP-7367 is the briefing spine.

- The UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities: UK implementation — House of Commons Library CBP-7367. <https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/CBP-7367/>

### UK spend trackers that exist — but not BSL-sliced

English local authorities must publish payments over £500. That raw feed has been scraped into several public or semi-public tools (CouncilSpend.com; at least one 2026 independent scrape of tens of millions of rows described in Ben’s Bites). Grant Thornton CFO Insights and OHID/PHE Spend and Outcomes Tool (SPOT) compare *broad* council spend to outcomes. None of these isolate BSL / Deafblind interpreting as a structured series. Translation-and-interpretation FOIs on WhatDoTheyKnow typically return a single blended “language services” figure that mixes spoken-language and BSL (example: BCP Council FOI, December 2023).

- Council Spend — <https://www.councilspend.com/>
- Translation, Interpretation and language services costs — WhatDoTheyKnow request to Bournemouth, Christchurch and Poole Council, 2023. <https://www.whatdotheyknow.com/request/translation_interpretation_and_l_304>
- Local authority data: finances — House of Commons Library CBP-10523. Core spending power, not service-line interpreting. <https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10523/>
- Spend versus Outcomes (SPOT) — OHID analytics. Public-health programme spend vs outcomes; not communication support. <https://analytics.phe.gov.uk/apps/spend-and-outcomes-tool/>

**Category finding.** Disability-rights *policy* trackers exist (EHRC, Scottish Government). Public-service *performance* trackers exist (IfG). Local *climate-action* scorecards exist (CEUK / mySociety). Local *payments* feeds exist. A disability-rights tracker that joins those four layers, and then slices BSL, was **not found**.

## 38.3 Prior attempts specifically for BSL / Deaf / Deafblind access

### What the sector actually publishes

**BSL Act 2022 reporting duty — official, narrow, live.** Section 2 requires the Secretary of State to report what *listed ministerial departments* have done to promote or facilitate BSL in communications with the public. Three reports exist:

| Report | Period | Published | URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | 28 Jun 2022 – 30 Apr 2023 | 31 Jul 2023 | <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-british-sign-language-bsl-report-2022> |
| Second | May 2023 – Apr 2024 | 17 Dec 2024 (described on the collection page as voluntary) | collection: <https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/british-sign-language-reports-and-action-plans> |
| Third | case studies May 2024 – Apr 2025; data May 2023 – Apr 2025 | 21 Jul 2025 | <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/british-sign-language-bsl-third-report-may-2024-to-april-2025> |

The reports count BSL *communications products* (announcements, publications, social / web / press conferences) by department. They do not count NHS trust interpreter bookings, ICB contract values, council adult-social-care BSL spend, or AIS flag rates. Scottish and Welsh governments are out of scope of the duty. Ministerial 5-year BSL plans have been requested; they are department communications plans, not a delivery dashboard.

- British Sign Language Act 2022. <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/34/contents>
- BSL reports and action plans collection — GOV.UK, published 21 July 2025, updated 23 July 2025. <https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/british-sign-language-reports-and-action-plans>
- British Sign Language report 2022 and implementation of the BSL Act 2022 — Commons Library CDP-2023-0191, 12 October 2023. <https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cdp-2023-0191/>

**BSL Advisory Board — *Locked Out*, 27 November 2025.** Independent report on GOV.UK. Twelve priorities, including enforce AIS, national VRS for England and Wales, BSL patient-data collection, statutory interpreter guidelines, stop using children as interpreters. A published case for change. Not a queryable monitor, and it does not attach pound figures to named ICBs or councils.

- Locked out: Exclusion of deaf and deafblind BSL users from health and social care in the UK — BSL Advisory Board / GOV.UK, 27 November 2025. <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/bsl-user-experience-of-health-and-social-care-in-uk>

**SignHealth + RNID — *Still Ignored*, April/June 2025.** Survey-based policy report (70% never asked needs; 7% always get a communication professional; 80% of SL users had a booked professional fail to attend — figures already held in this dossier from prompts 34–37). Recommendations, not a living dataset.

- Still Ignored landing page — SignHealth. <https://signhealth.org.uk/resources/still-ignored-the-fight-for-accessible-healthcare/>

**RNID.** Policy pages on AIS and health access; Access to Work experience report 2026 (with DeafATW); campaign monitoring of the Disability Action Plan pledge on in-person BSL at Number 10 briefings (Feb 2024). Campaign monitoring ≠ a public structured tracker.

- Access to health and social care — RNID, page dated 2 July 2025. <https://rnid.org.uk/information-and-support/your-rights/access-to-health-and-social-care/>
- RNID campaign success as UK Government pledges in-person BSL interpreters for major Number 10 press conferences — RNID, 7 February 2024. “RNID will… monitor how reporting elements of the Action Plan are implemented.” <https://rnid.org.uk/2024/02/campaign-success-as-uk-government-pledges-to-have-in-person-bsl-interpreters/>

**British Deaf Association.** Campaign and position statements (BSL Act passage; sign-systems statement). No public BSL-spend or AIS-compliance platform found on this pass.

**Ofcom access-services reports.** Annual counts of TV / on-demand subtitling, audio description and signing against statutory quotas. Live, structured, and citable — but broadcasting, not health or local-authority interpreting.

- Television and on-demand programme services: Access services report January–December 2024 — Ofcom, 19 May 2025 (updated 23 Dec 2025). <https://www.ofcom.org.uk/tv-radio-and-on-demand/accessibility/television-and-on-demand-programme-services-access-services-report--january-to-december-2024>

**This dossier’s own artefacts.** The SWL 878-request series (Oct 2024–Jun 2025), the BSL FOI companion log, the SWL–SEL agency tracker, and the AIS / EOL / DWP / Jobcentre briefs are the nearest *manual* precursor. They are not a public query interface. That is the point of this batch: decide whether they should become one.

### University / research-group platforms

No UK university research group was found running a public, queryable BSL-policy-vs-spend platform. DCAL, Heriot-Watt, UCL Deafness Cognition and Language, and similar centres publish papers and corpora, not a civic-tech monitor. If one exists under a lab name that did not surface on this pass, it is a gap in *this search*, not proof of absence — log it as “not found on 8 September 2026 web + civic-tech directory pass.”

### Companies House / Charity Commission name collisions (positioning only)

**Deaf Access Trust**, company 02705030, confirmation statement filed 15 April 2026, accounts to 31 March 2025. Live. This is a service charity, not a tracker, but it occupies the “Deaf Access …” name-space.

- Deaf Access Trust filing history — Companies House. <https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/02705030/filing-history>

No Companies House hit was found on this pass for “Deaf Access Gap Monitor”, “BSL Accountability Tracker”, “AIS Compliance Monitor”, “Deaf Policy Ledger”, or “Access Gap Register”. That is a prompt-39 job to confirm on the live name-search and trade-mark register, not a finding that those strings are clear.

**Category finding.** BSL-specific *reporting* exists (Act reports, *Locked Out*, *Still Ignored*, Ofcom signing quotas). BSL-specific *queryable spend-vs-duty infrastructure* was **not found**. The sector’s current mode is the periodic report, not the living register.

## 38.4 International equivalents

None of the tools below is a clean template for “UK BSL Act + AIS + Equality Act vs local NHS/LA spend”. Several are usable as *design* references.

### United States

**GSA Section 508 government-wide assessment.** Official, annual, open-data. Federal agencies self-report digital-accessibility maturity and conformance; GSA publishes agency summaries and downloadable response files. Live for FY24 and FY25. This is ICT accessibility inside the federal estate, not ASL interpreter spend at state Medicaid or county level.

- FY24 Governmentwide Section 508 Assessment — executive summary, Section508.gov. <https://www.section508.gov/manage/section-508-assessment/2024/executive-summary/>
- FY25 Assessment Data & Downloads — Section508.gov, reviewed July 2026. <https://www.section508.gov/manage/section-508-assessment/2025/assessment-data-downloads/>

**Center for American Progress — Accessible Democracy Tracker (July 2026).** Interactive state scores for *voting* accessibility, built from a survey of disability and civic organisations. Includes a line on multilingual / ASL interpreter access at the polls. Funded/published by CAP. Active as of the July 2026 interactive. Domain is elections, not health spend.

- Mapping Disabled Americans’ Access to Democracy — Center for American Progress, interactive dated 9 July 2026. <https://www.americanprogress.org/article/accessibledemocracy/>

**Commercial ADA Title II “Accessibility Tracker” products** (Accessible.org; TestPros workbook). Vendor tools for *public bodies to document their own WCAG remediation*. Not independent monitors, and they create a false-authority risk if a civic tool borrowed the same product language.

- Platform Makes Tracking ADA Title II Web Rule Compliance Easier — Accessible.org, 12 August 2025. <https://accessible.org/platform-tracking-ada-title-ii-web-compliance/>

**Seyfarth Shaw / UsableNet ADA Title III lawsuit tallies.** Docket-tracking of website-accessibility suits. Litigation census, not a statutory-duty vs spend register.

**DC Language Access Act annual reports** (Office of Human Rights). Agency-by-agency scores for spoken-language access under a local statute. Closest US *municipal statute-vs-delivery* cousin; ASL is in the language-access family in some US cities but these reports are LEP/NEP-first.

- OHR Language Access FY24 Report — District of Columbia, dated 11 February 2026 on the published PDF. <https://ohr.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/ohr/OHR_LanguageAccess_FY24_Report_2.11.2026.pdf>

**MIT Center for Civic Media — Promise Tracker (Brazil, 2013–2018).** Citizen survey-and-map tool; early campaigns included station accessibility. Project marked active January 2013–December 2018; GitHub builder archived 12 January 2022. See 38.5.

- Promise Tracker overview — MIT Media Lab. “Active from January 2013 to December 2018.” <https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/promise-tracker/overview/>

**Code for Africa — PromiseTracker** (current generation, Kenya-centred, Airtable + AI extraction). Political-promise tracker, not disability spend. Shows the “promise tracker” product category is alive in African civic tech.

- GitHub — CodeForAfrica/PromiseTracker. <https://github.com/CodeForAfrica/PromiseTracker>

### European Union

**EU CRPD Monitoring Framework.** Institutional, not a public query site: European Parliament, European Ombudsman, EU Agency for Fundamental Rights, European Disability Forum. EDF files alternative reports and an annual Human Rights Report on a rotating CRPD article. No public EU-wide dashboard of member-state sign-language interpreting spend.

- EU CRPD Framework — monitoring — FRA. <https://fra.europa.eu/en/cooperation/eu-partners/eu-crpd-framework/monitoring>
- EU not complying with treaty on disability due to lack of political leadership — EDF, 5 March 2025, on its alternative report to the 2025 CRPD review. <https://www.edf-feph.org/eu-not-complying-with-treaty-on-disability-due-to-lack-of-political-leadership/>

**EDF / FRA work** produces indicators (political participation, independent living) as research products, not a living local-spend register.

### Canada

**Accessible Canada Act regime.** Regulated entities must publish accessibility plans, feedback processes and progress reports, and notify the Accessibility Commissioner via My Accessibility Portal. That portal is a *regulator inbox*, not a public gap-query tool. Amended Accessible Canada Regulations on digital technologies published 17 December 2025; in-force dates 5 December 2027 and 5 December 2028.

- Guidance on the Accessible Canada Regulations — ESDC. <https://www.canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/programs/accessible-canada-regulations-guidance.html>
- Direction on ICT Accessibility — Treasury Board / CIO, page current September 2026. <https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/policies-standards/policy-service-digital-announcements/direction-information-communication-technologies-accessibility-towards-regulatory-implementation.html>

**Official Languages Action Plan 2023–2028 dashboard.** Canadian Heritage built a public dashboard of 82 official-language initiatives and money, released spring 2026, annual update promised. This is the cleanest *federal-programme-spend dashboard* found in the search. It tracks English/French official languages, not ASL/LSQ interpreting in health.

- Annual Report on Official Languages 2024–2025 — Canadian Heritage. Describes the dashboard. <https://www.canada.ca/en/canadian-heritage/services/official-languages-bilingualism/publications/annual-report-2024-2025.html>

**3mpwrApp Accountability Tracker (Canada).** Consumer/advocacy app for logging broken promises by employers and agencies. Individual case file, not a national structured dataset.

### Australia

**NDIS public data stack.** The strongest *disability spend* open-data operation found anywhere in this pass: participant dashboards by primary disability, Explore Data interactive, quarterly performance dashboards, CSV downloads. Hearing impairment is a primary-disability slice; Deafblind and Auslan interpreting are not isolated as a national “policy commitment vs utilisation” tracker. Publication cadence for some supplements moved to six-monthly from Q3 2025–26.

- Participant dashboards — NDIA Data and Research. Page current 10 June 2026. <https://dataresearch.ndis.gov.au/reports-and-analyses/participant-dashboards>
- Explore data — NDIA. <https://dataresearch.ndis.gov.au/explore-data>
- NDIS online data updated — 4 June 2026 (data to 31 March 2026). <https://www.ndis.gov.au/news/11559-ndis-online-data-updated>

**Australia’s Disability Strategy 2021–2031 Outcomes Framework.** AIHW annual report plus dashboard against 85 (later 55 reported) measures. National outcomes, not local Auslan-interpreting spend.

- Outcomes Framework 3rd annual report — Australian Institute of Health and Welfare. <https://www.aihw.gov.au/reports/australias-disability-strategy/australias-disability-strategy-outcomes-framework/contents/introduction>

**RMIT Promise Tracker** (with ABC Fact Check until July 2024; continued by RMIT Information Integrity Hub). Election-pledge tracker for ALP 2022 commitments. Closed as an ABC partnership when the unit closed; the dataset survived under RMIT. Political promises, not disability spend.

### Global / multi-country

**Human Rights Measurement Initiative — Rights Tracker.** Cross-country rights scores (rightstracker.org). Country-level, not local spend.

**OHCHR Universal Human Rights Index / National Recommendations Tracking Database.** UN infrastructure that national trackers (EHRC, Scotland) sit on top of.

**Category finding.** Internationally, the mature public tools are (a) digital-accessibility self-assessments (US 508, ACA plans), (b) election-promise trackers, (c) national disability-scheme finance dashboards (NDIS), and (d) treaty-recommendation trackers. A language-access *interpreting-spend vs statutory duty* register at provider grain was **not found** in US, EU, Canada or Australia on this pass.

## 38.5 What happened to tools that are no longer active — or that changed shape

| Tool | What it was | What happened | Apparent cause (as documented, not guessed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| MIT Promise Tracker | Civic monitoring platform; Brazil accessibility and services campaigns; 2013–2018 | Marked ended Dec 2018; GitHub builder archived 12 Jan 2022 | Project end-of-life at MIT Center for Civic Media. No published “we were sued” note found. Typical research-lab sunset: grant cycle + staff moved on. |
| RMIT–ABC Fact Check Promise Tracker | Australian election-pledge tracker, relaunched 2023 | ABC partnership ended July 2024; unit closed; RMIT Information Integrity Hub kept the tracker | Institutional partnership lapse, not a legal challenge. Dataset survived because it sat in a university host. |
| UK National Disability Strategy public narrative | 2021 cross-government commitment list | High Court declaration paused 14 commitments; 2023 deposit restated status; Labour 2024 treated Strategy and Action Plan as previous-administration documents | Legal process + change of government. There was never a separate civic-tech front-end to kill. |
| EHRC Human Rights Tracker 2019 method | Broader “progress” assessments | Relaunched Jan 2025 with a narrower “did the government take action on this UN recommendation?” method | Method change by the operator, still live. Lesson: a tracker that tries to score “how fair is Britain” gets pulled into political weather; a tracker that scores “was action taken on recommendation X” is more defensible. |
| AIS 2016 “be fully compliant” framing | Implied a national end-state | 2025 refresh moved to annual self-publish by March 2027; still no national dataset | Not a dead tool — a duty that never grew the public data layer the 2016 text implied. |
| Scottish Human Rights Tracker phases 2–3 | Implementation methodology and outcomes | Officially contingent on 2026 Holyrood election | Political-cycle risk on a *government-operated* tracker. Independence of host matters. |

No documented case was found, on this pass, of a UK civic-tech disability or language-access tracker being taken down because of a libel action, regulator objection, or ICO enforcement against the *tracker itself*. That is not the same as “legal risk is zero”; it is “no public corpse to autopsy.” Legal/liability practice is the job of prompt 45.

Common death causes that *are* documented across the wider promise-tracker and civic-tech field, and that therefore belong on the de-risk list:

1. **Host institution moves on** (MIT lab; ABC Fact Check unit).
2. **Partnership or grant ends** and nobody owns maintenance.
3. **Method becomes politically expensive**, so the operator narrows scope rather than close (EHRC 2025).
4. **Government-operated trackers inherit the electoral cycle** (Scotland phases 2–3).
5. **Data-maintenance burden** — Climate Scorecards only work because of a volunteer marking army plus WDTK Pro plus a right-of-reply loop. That is labour, not software.

## 38.6 Positioning implications (not a name decision)

These are constraints for prompts 39–45, not branding copy.

- **Do not look official.** EHRC and Scottish Government already occupy the “Human Rights Tracker” slot. NHS England occupies “Accessible Information Standard self-assessment.” A name that reads as an NHS or Cabinet Office product would collide with the thing the tool is supposed to hold to account. mySociety’s plain-English pattern (TheyWorkForYou, WhatDoTheyKnow, FixMyStreet) is the documented UK civic-tech convention that avoids that collision. Tested in prompt 39.
- **Do not claim to be the BSL Act monitor.** The Act already has an official report. The gap the idea would fill is *everything the Act report does not cover*: NHS, social care, councils, ICBs, spend, fulfilment.
