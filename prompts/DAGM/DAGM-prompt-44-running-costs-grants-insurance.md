## 44.1 Cash lines, sourced and bounded

### WhatDoTheyKnow Pro

Priced page printed August 2026: **£20 per user per month, or £200 a year.** Free accounts remain. Batch cap 500. No published extra fee per authority. Cycle 0 (the existing 16-row SWL log) does not need Pro. Cycle 1 does, the moment two people are marking the same batch and the replies need a dashboard.

This is the cheapest line on the table and the one that stops the project building a second FOI site (prompt 43).

### Corrections log

Not software. A sheet with: body, question, first-mark source URL, date the reply window opened, date it closed, whether they replied, the replacement figure, the annotation on the original WhatDoTheyKnow request. CE UK already does the last step in public. Labour is inside the five-week window, not a new product. Cost is hours, plus the postage or email of the offer letter.

Do not treat “we will put a feedback form on the site” as a substitute. Prompt 42: 76% of CE UK’s 2025 councils used a *written, time-boxed* offer. A form that nobody is obliged to answer is not a right of reply.

### Insurance — what the market pages actually say

Three different products get sold as “cover.” Only one of them is the risk prompt 42 described.

1. **CIC / charity package** (Markel Direct). From £3 a month on their worked example: community interest group, turnover up to £10,000, **£250,000 professional indemnity and trustees’ liability.** Also public liability to £10m and employers’ liability. Optional cyber and legal expenses. This is the policy a CIC buys to have volunteers on site and trustees who can sign a grant offer. It is not described as multimedia / defamation cover.
2. **Writer / author / publisher multimedia PI** (Hiscox and peers). Hiscox writer pages advertise cover from £7.20 a month as an *average across business policies sold April 2025–April 2026*, not as a scored quote for a public scorecard. Hiscox publisher pages list defamation and IP as named perils. UK JournoHub (17 May 2026): media-specific professional indemnity for freelancers typically **£250k–£1m** cover at **£200–£600 a year**, NUJ Extra or Hiscox as the usual first calls.
3. **After-the-event / defence-fund products** (Temple ATE; Reporters Shield as a mutual defence fund, GIJN 2026). These attach to a live claim. They are not an annual premium for a volunteer dataset. Reporters Shield’s own description: high-risk jurisdictions such as the UK will likely require **external pre-publication legal review**. That is a process, not a policy number.

**Honest statement for this project.** A freelance-scale media PI policy in the low hundreds a year is the documented band for *text that can be sued over*. A Markel-style CIC package in the low tens a month is the documented band for *having a vehicle that can hold a grant*. They are not substitutes. Neither is a sourced figure for “Climate Emergency UK’s premium.” Do not invent one.

Insurance does not make “non-compliant with the Act” safe. Prompt 42’s drafting rules still apply.

### Counsel — retainer versus an hour when the letter arrives

No cousin in this set published “we keep a defamation silk on £X a month.” What is published:

- Guideline hourly rates, independent 2026 compilation: London 1 Grade A **£579**, National 2 Grade A **£288**, trainees from **£142**.
- A solicitor’s letter generally: **£100–£500 + VAT.**
- Specialist defamation cease-and-desist that *ends* a one-off publication: **£3,500–£9,000 + VAT** (Cohen Davis). Letter-of-claim stage on a more complex publication: low five figures. Issued proceedings: mid-five to six figures. Trial: high-five to mid-six.
- Debt-style “letter before action” mills at £7.50 are the wrong product. A Media and Communications Pre-Action Protocol letter is not a debt letter.

WDTK’s documented response to a legal complaint is: stop at the pre-action letter and take the content down, except dead people and *Derbyshire* public bodies (prompt 42). That implies the running cost is **one specialist read per letter**, not a litigation budget. Budget the read. Do not budget a trial.

A cheap pre-publication hour — methodology page, scoring rules, “we do not print non-compliant with the Act” — is the insurance product that actually changes the text. Reporters Shield says high-risk jurisdictions will require it. Price two to four hours at the National 2 / London 3 band before the first public table, not a standing brief.

## 44.3 What funders and institutional users typically want to see

Not a complete due-diligence manual. The cousins plus the open programme pages.

**Before a small / seed grant (£10–20k).**

- A thing that already exists, even if ugly. Public Whip had a parser. CE UK had a website of declarations. This project has the 878 series and the FOI log. That is the application, not a wireframe.
- A named legal vehicle or a host that can receive the money. Markel’s worked example assumes a CIC or trust. Awards for All lists constituted community groups, charities, CICs, statutory bodies. An unincorporated personal project hits the wall here.
- Living Wage on any paid post JRRT funds.
- A question set that will not change mid-grant so the year can be compared.

**Before a mid-size justice / accountability grant (£50–100k a year).**

- Governance: who marks, who is excluded (CE UK bars current officers), who audits, how a correction enters the log.
- Independence / conflict disclosure. CE UK publishes the exclusion rule. Full Fact is IFCN-registered and runs a corrections page. A monitor funded by a BSL agency, or marked by an ICB comms officer, fails this on sight.
- Right of reply already designed, not promised.
- A partner that looks like a lawyer if the application is to LEF.

**Before anyone treats the table as citable evidence.**

Prompt 41 already split this: journalist reuse ≠ subject reply ≠ select-committee slot ≠ regulator citation. The last of those was not found for the volunteer scorecards. What made WDTK citable to the Justice Committee in 2012 was four years of a public archive, not an accreditation badge. What made Scorecards reusable by the *FT* was the FOI trail on WhatDoTheyKnow. Neither required a university logo. An academic partner is a nice-to-have, not a documented prerequisite in this set.

**What not to claim on an application.**

- “CQC / EHRC / NHS England will use this as assurance.” Prompt 34 and 41: CQC does not assure AIS; EHRC runs its own tracker.
- “Non-compliance rates for every English trust.” Cycle 2, and even then the cell is found / not found / refused.
- “Platform for Parliament to query.” That sentence is the standing-host envelope.

## 44.4 A first-year cash picture that does not invent CE UK’s turnover

Cycle 0–1, SWL cluster plus comparators, one frozen pack, one right-of-reply window, one public table. Cash, not hours:

- WDTK Pro: £0 (free) or £200.
- Domain / hosting / a static page: low hundreds.
- CIC-style package if a vehicle is formed: tens of pounds a month on the Markel worked example, *plus* a separate media-PI quote in the £200–£600 journalist band if the table will be public.
- Pre-publication solicitor read: a few hundred to low four figures at guideline rates, once.
- Contingency for one pre-action response: budget the specialist letter band (£3.5–9k + VAT) as a *risk line you hope not to spend*, not as a grant purpose. WDTK’s cheaper move — take the cell down — is still available and should stay available.
- Volunteer expenses, BSL interpretation for any lived-experience marking session, and the five-week reply admin: this is where a £10–20k seed actually goes. It does not go to a developer.

That picture is compatible with JRRT small / Awards for All / a Crowdfunder of the size CE UK used in 2022. It is not compatible with a Lottery three-year programme bid, an LEF Strengthening Justice bid, or a “platform” day-rate.

Cycle 2 (36 ICBs + ~205 trusts) is the CE UK labour class. The cash lines above do not grow linearly. The volunteer hours do. The grant that has paid for *that* in this set is the partnership-with-a-host grant, not a bigger Awards for All.

## 44.5 Implications for the tool

1. Put WDTK Pro, a media-PI quote, a solicitor who has read the methodology, and the corrections log on the same budget page as volunteer hours. They are smaller than the hours and they are what stop the first table being a campaign PDF.
2. Do not describe insurance as making naming-and-scoring safe. It pays a defence. The drafting rules in prompt 42 are the control.
3. Do not budget a monthly counsel retainer. Budget an hour before publication and an hour when a letter arrives. WDTK’s published practice is still take-down, not fight.
4. Apply for the envelope that matches the object: £10–20k for a first public table; £50k+ only once a vehicle, a reply cycle and a conflict rule exist; host-scale money only with a host.
5. Form the vehicle before the mid-size application, not after. Awards for All and LEF both assume one.
6. Prompt 45 should take the technical object that is *not* being bought with this money — the public query API, the parser, the hosted marking tool — and say what stays in the sheet until a host appears.
