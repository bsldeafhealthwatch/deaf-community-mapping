# Prompt 43 — Collection method: FOI, WhatDoTheyKnow, and the right-of-reply loop

**Master index prompt 43.** Sixth file in the Deaf Access Gap Monitor / BSL Policy Maker scoping batch (prompts 38–45). Compiled 8–9 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps.

**What this prompt is for.** Prompt 42 said legal risk is a cost line and that Climate Emergency UK built reply in because scoring a public body in public *is* the product. This file takes the actual collection loop those cousins run — freeze the questions, trial the FOI, batch it on WhatDoTheyKnow, first-mark, five-week right of reply, audit — and maps it onto the bodies this dossier already names. It does not invent a national BSL scorecard. It says what the loop costs in requests, refusals, and volunteer hours, and which universe of authorities is realistic at each stage.

**Already in this project.** `artifacts/BSL-FOI-companion-log.xlsx` (six copy-paste templates, 16-row first-wave log) and `artifacts/BSL-request-data-gap-tracker.docx` (SWL 878 as gold standard, GM FOI comparator). Those *are* the manual collection. This file is about whether that pack scales the way CE UK scaled.

---

## Headline

Climate Emergency UK did not start by asking 391 councils 93 questions. They started with published plans (2022 Plan Scorecards), then moved to action, and only then sent FOIs — after trial requests, after cutting questions that national datasets already answered, and after making the surviving questions yes/no or a single number. The 2023/2025 Action Scorecards then used WhatDoTheyKnow to send **more than 4,000** FOI/EIR requests, 15 volunteers on the FOI pile alone, 200+ on the rest, **5,000+ hours** on the first mark, and a **five-week right of reply** in which the council’s reply **overrides** the FOI for scoring.

WhatDoTheyKnow Pro batch is capped at **500 authorities per batch** and costs **£20 per user per month or £200 a year**. The ICO does not treat a round-robin as automatically vexatious. Section 12 *does* let an authority aggregate similar requests from one person, or people acting in concert, over 60 days, against a £450/£600 cost limit.

This project’s first honest loop is not “every ICB and every trust in England.” It is the six SWL boroughs, the clustered SWL/SEL ICB, the named local trusts already in the matrix, and one or two comparator ICBs. That is the trial-FOI stage CE UK ran before the 4,000. The ~400-body national set (36 ICBs after the April 2026 mergers, ~205 NHS trusts, 152 adult-social-care councils) is cycle two, after the questions have been frozen and the refusal patterns are known.

## 43.1 The CE UK loop, in the order they actually ran it

Sources for this section: CE UK “Why are we using FOI requests?” methodology blog; 2023 and 2025 Scorecards methodology pages; mySociety CEUK tag post; Scorecards About page; 17 June 2025 press release.

**Stage 0 — published documents only.**  
January 2022 Plan Scorecards: 28 questions across nine sections, marked from plans councils had already put on the web. No FOI. That is the equivalent of this dossier’s current state: the SWL 878 series, AIS guidance, award notices, Charity Commission accounts, NRCPD register counts.

**Stage 1 — trial mark and trial FOI.**  
Before the 2023 Action Scorecards, staff trial-marked a small set of councils. Volunteers then trial-marked a different small set. A small number of FOIs went to a *random* mix of council types, published on WhatDoTheyKnow, explicitly labelled as trials and **not** the final question set. Purpose, in their words: learn how to ask so the work is smallest for the council *and* for the marker.

**Stage 2 — cut anything a national dataset already answers.**  
The FOI blog is blunt. They dropped a planning-permission FOI when they found a government compilation. They cut sub-questions. They pushed remaining questions toward yes/no or a single number. Trial requests were slower; the slimmed live requests came back faster.

**Stage 3 — batch on WhatDoTheyKnow.**  
Live Action Scorecard FOIs (technically EIR for environmental questions) went to all UK councils, January–February 2023, then late summer 2024 for the 2025 edition. mySociety: “in the end, CE UK submitted a total of over 4,000 FOI requests.” Fifteen volunteers worked the FOI responses; the wider 200+ marked the non-FOI questions. All correspondence sits on WhatDoTheyKnow so the raw evidence is public and campaigners do not re-ask the same question.

**Stage 4 — first mark.**  
Volunteers trained to read the FOI and log it against the frozen question. About page: “The first phase of marking alone took over 5,000 volunteer hours.” 391 local and combined authorities in the 2025 set.

**Stage 5 — right of reply, five weeks.**  
Council sees which questions have a mark, **not** the individual scores or the total. 74% replied in 2023, 76% in 2024. If the reply contradicts the WhatDoTheyKnow FOI, **the reply wins** for the score. CE UK then annotates the original FOI so the public record shows the correction. Password-gated or time-limited portal dumps are rejected as not “freely and easily available.”

**Stage 6 — audit.**  
About 50 trained volunteers plus staff review first mark *and* reply. Every plan is audited whether or not the council replied. Then publication (2025 edition released 17 June 2025; work dated July 2024–March 2025).

That is the product. A query API is not in this loop.

## 43.2 WhatDoTheyKnow as the pipe, not the product

Live homepage figures on this pass: **1,394,277** requests to **48,113** authorities. Authority list is maintained by staff and volunteers and published as an open dataset (`pages.mysociety.org/wdtk_authorities_list`). Useful tagged lists already exist: **152** “Councils with Adult Social Services Responsibilities”; London councils as their own list.

**Pro / Batch (priced page, printed August 2026).**  
£20 per user per month, or £200 a year. Free accounts remain. Pro adds private-while-working, a dashboard, and Batch. Batch maximum: **500 authorities**. Help text: “We recommend sending a pilot batch to a sample of authorities to test the request. Once you know the authorities hold the type of data… request from the remaining authorities.” Privacy periods can sit on a whole batch and later be published.

CE UK used Pro. mySociety’s write-up of the partnership is the closest published description of “how to run 4,000 FOIs with three staff.”

**This project does not need to build a request platform.** Using WDTK is the cousin move. Building a second FOI site is PledgeBank-shaped scope (prompt 40).

## 43.3 The legal-process constraints on the loop

Not legal advice. ICO published guidance.

**Section 12 — cost of compliance.**  
Appropriate limit: **£600** central government, **£450** every other authority, charged at £25/hour (so 18 hours outside Whitehall). The authority estimates the cost of finding and extracting. s.12(4) and the Fees Regulations let it **aggregate** two or more similar requests from one person, or from people who appear to be acting in concert or in a campaign, over 60 working days. A six-question BSL pack sent as six emails to the same trust in a fortnight can be treated as one bill.

**Section 14 — vexatious.**  
Round-robin requests are **not** automatically vexatious. Campaigns are **not** automatically vexatious. The ICO distinguishes “using FOI to gather information for a campaign” from “using FOI as a channel of disruption.” Burden can still make a genuine campaign request vexatious if the aggregate load is disproportionate. Upper Tribunal *Craven*: if the real objection is cost, use s.12, not s.14.

**CE UK’s mitigation, which this project can copy.**  
Ask fewer questions. Ask for a number or a yes/no. Trial first. Space waves. Publish the replies so the next person does not re-ask. Do not send the same pack twice inside 60 days.

**Universities remain the awkward FOI-and-libel overlap** (prompt 42, *Duke v Salford*). NHS trusts and ICBs are FOI authorities. Commercial contractors (DAL, Sign Solutions) are not, unless the contract itself is obtained from the commissioner.

The “WDTK at ~400 councils” phrase in prompt 42 is the *cousin scale*, not the year-one target. Year one is the six-plus-cluster plus the named trusts. That is also the only scale at which a solo or small team can offer a five-week right of reply without inventing a volunteer army.

A published 2026 example of the trust-scale FOI: Shabad, University of Liverpool, FOIs to **205 NHS trusts** Nov 2025–Jan 2026 on cyber governance, 170 substantive replies, 20 refusals, 15 no reply, each row carrying a WhatDoTheyKnow URL (Zenodo record 18523179, 10 April 2026). That is the response-rate envelope to budget for, not a promise.

## 43.5 What the frozen question set looks like (method, not the questions)

CE UK’s rule, applied here: every question must be answerable from (a) a document the body has already published, (b) a national dataset, or (c) a single FOI that asks for a number, a yes/no, or a named document. If the answer requires a volunteer to decide “was this good enough,” it is a mark, not an FOI.

Questions this dossier can already freeze without a new FOI:

- Is there a published BSL / AIS contact-method figure for the period DATE–DATE? (SWL 878 is the gold cell.)
- Is the GP-contract holder named on a Find-a-Tender / award notice, and does that notice itemise BSL? (DAL / Sign Solutions / LanguageLine cells already logged.)
- Is there a published AIS self-assessment on the body’s site as at DATE?
- NRCPD RIDB headcount by region as at DATE (already pulled 5 Sep 2026).

Questions that *do* need FOI, and that the existing templates already ask:

- T1 ICB: interpreter requests, fulfilled / unfulfilled / cancelled, F2F vs VRI, spend.
- T2 hospital: same, plus on-the-day failures.
- T3 council: adult social care BSL / communicator-guide / DeafBlind.
- T4 SWL follow-up: the 878 series extended past June 2025.
- T5 mental health: SWLSTG and any ICB talking-therapies BSL contract.
- T6 cost fallback: unit rate if volumes are refused.

Do not add a seventh template until the first six have been through a trial wave and a right-of-reply. CE UK cut questions after the trial. This project should expect to cut T6 or collapse T1/T4 once refusal language is known.

**Scoring rule, copied from CE UK, required by prompt 42.**  
The public cell is “found / not found / refused / figure, source URL, date.” A right-of-reply figure replaces the FOI figure and is annotated on the original request. “Non-compliant with DAPB1605” is not a cell. Prompt 34 already established that CQC does not assure AIS and that s.95 of the Health and Care Act is commenced but unused.

## 43.6 Labour on *this* loop, not CE UK’s 391

Prompt 41 forbade inventing tighter ranges than the cousins published. The honest translation is proportional, and still a range:

- **Trial wave (cycle 0).** Six boroughs + ICB + a handful of trusts + GM. That is the 16-row log already sitting in the companion workbook, plus the replies. One person can send it. The labour is chasing, logging refusals, and writing the five-week reply letter, not building software. Order-of-magnitude: tens of hours to send and file; low hundreds to mark, chase and reply, depending on how many bodies actually answer.
- **First scored public table (cycle 1).** Same universe, frozen questions, published methodology, right of reply offered to every named body, corrections log. This is the CE UK *method* at SWL scale, not CE UK *volume*. Volunteer-hours will look like a marking weekend, not 5,000.
- **National ICB + trust wave (cycle 2).** 36 + ~205 = ~240 FOIs if it is one pack each; more if T1 and T2 stay separate. Fits inside one WDTK Pro batch. At CE UK’s 15-FOI-volunteers-plus-5,000-hours for 391 bodies and 93 questions, a 10-question pack to 240 bodies is still a multi-month volunteer operation. Do not schedule it before cycle 1 has been through reply and audit. Prompt 40: the jump trigger was a grant or a partnership, not a finished architecture.

Refusal and silence are part of the dataset. The Liverpool 205-trust FOI (170 / 20 / 15) is the only recent all-trust response split found on this pass. Use it as a planning assumption, not as a SWL prediction. SWL already answered the 878 request; that does not mean every trust will answer T2.

## 43.7 What not to collect this way

- **Lived-experience case files.** PHSO and CQC published findings are already in prompts 34–37. Do not FOI a trust for “the name of the patient who waited.” That is personal data (prompt 42) and it is not how CE UK built Scorecards.
- **Interpreter identity or pay packets of named individuals.** In scope as sector rates (NUBSLI bands, AtW staff guide) already logged. Out of scope as a named person’s invoice.
- **Duplicate questions already answered on WhatDoTheyKnow.** Search first. CE UK’s public archive is the point of using the pipe.
- **A second FOI platform.** Out of scope. WDTK exists. The companion log is the project’s layer on top.
- **Password-walled disclosure portals.** CE UK rejects them. Copy that. If a trust’s only reply is “log into our disclosure log,” the cell is “not freely available,” not a number guessed from a screenshot.

## 43.8 Implications for the tool

1. The collection method *is* WDTK + a frozen sheet + a five-week reply. That is enough product for cycle 1. A queryable API is cycle-three talk (prompt 40, prompt 44).
2. Right of reply overrides the FOI. Build the log so a later figure can replace an earlier one without silently deleting the first source. CE UK annotates the original request; do the same.
3. Trial one pack on the SWL cluster before any national batch. That is what WDTK Pro’s own help page says. The companion log is already that trial.
4. Section 12 aggregation means the six templates should go as **one letter per authority**, not six. Collapse T1–T6 into a single request body with numbered parts. The workbook can stay six tabs internally.
5. The ~400-body national set is real, and it fits a WDTK batch, and it is not year one.
6. Publish the questions before the scores. That is methodology and it is the s.4 “reasonable belief” story from prompt 42.

Prompt 44 should price WDTK Pro, legal insurance, counsel-on-retainer and a corrections log as running costs next to volunteer hours, and say which grant size has historically attached to “we have a frozen question set and one scored table,” not to “we would like a platform.”
