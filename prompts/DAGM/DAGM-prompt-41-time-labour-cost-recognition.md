# Prompt 41 — Time, labour, cost, and the path to being treated as evidence

**Master index prompt 41.** Fourth file in the Deaf Access Gap Monitor / BSL Policy Maker scoping batch (prompts 38–45). Compiled 8–9 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps.

**What this prompt is for.** Take the hour/year ranges already logged in prompt 40 and attach the *labour model* and the *money* that sat under them. The question is not “how long should our platform take.” It is “what did comparable tools spend, who paid, how many people touched the work, and what counted as being treated as evidence — a journalist reuse, a council right-of-reply, a select-committee appearance, a regulator citation.” Prompt 40 forbade inventing tighter ranges. This file does not.

**Closest cousins used here.** Same set as prompt 40: Public Whip / TheyWorkForYou, WhatDoTheyKnow, FixMyStreet, Climate Emergency UK / CAPE / Scorecards, Full Fact, Institute for Government Performance Tracker and Whitehall Monitor, PledgeBank as the focus-failure case.

---

## Headline

The documented path from a working collection to something a select committee or a national desk will treat as evidence is measured in **years and volunteer-hours**, not in sprints. The cheap part is the first public page. The expensive part is repeating a comparable mark across every body, every cycle, with a right of reply.

Two money facts sit under that:

1. **Seed grants that actually launched the cousins were small.** FixMyStreet’s contemporaneous press figure is **£10,000** (Department for Constitutional Affairs Innovations Fund). WhatDoTheyKnow launched on a Joseph Rowntree Reform Trust grant after an 18-month funded build. Public Whip was never grant-funded; the founders paid the server. TheyWorkForYou’s June 2004 “About” page says one developer was kept fed for a month by a small UK Citizens Online Democracy grant.
2. **The standing organisations that now host those tools are not small.** mySociety group income **£2.628m** in 2024/25 (down 15% on £3.093m). Full Fact income **£2.92m** in calendar 2024 and **£3.05m** in 2025. Institute for Government income **£6.86m** to 31 March 2025, 58 employees, main funder the Gatsby Charitable Foundation. Those are the running-cost envelopes of *hosts*, not the cost of a first scored dataset.

Climate Emergency UK — the closest method cousin — is the one a Deaf-sector project can actually copy on labour: a 2019 website, then a partnership, then **four staff plus 200+ volunteers**, with **5,000+ volunteer hours** cited for one marking phase. Its exact annual turnover is not on the open web in a Charity Commission return (it is a CIC, 11823392, total-exemption accounts). That is a gap, not a clearance to invent a figure.

This project is already in the manual-collection stage that CE UK occupied in 2019–20. The next bill is not a query API. It is the cost of a stable question set, a right-of-reply cycle, and enough people to mark it twice.

## 41.2 What “institutional recognition” actually looked like

The phrase is used loosely. The sources distinguish four different things. Only one of them is a select-committee evidence slot.

### A. Journalist or broadcaster reuse of the dataset

- TheyWorkForYou: Channel 4 branded election version, 2005. GovLab case notes that Number 10 staff used the site, which is why Steinberg was later asked to co-write the Power of Information review. That is reuse, not a committee citation.
- Climate Scorecards: *Financial Times* story pulled from the FOI dataset (mySociety, 27 September 2023). Council environment papers citing the scores (Colchester case, mySociety 2025). CE UK / mySociety’s own figure: over 1 in 5 councils using Scorecards internally. Self-reported uptake is not the same as a select-committee citation. Prompt 40 logged that gap; it is still a gap.

### B. The measured body answering you

Climate Scorecards built this in. 2023 and 2025 editions gave every council a right of reply. 76% of councils used the 2025 right of reply (prompt 40). That is a form of recognition — the subject treats the mark as worth correcting — and it is cheaper to document than a Hansard mention. It is also the step that stops the product being dismissed as an unchecked campaign score.

### C. A parliamentary evidence slot

Clean dated case: **Alex Skene**, WhatDoTheyKnow volunteer, oral evidence to the Justice Committee’s post-legislative scrutiny of FOI, **21 February 2012**. Four years after launch. mySociety has continued to submit written evidence (Scottish Parliament SPPA on FOI reform; UK Procedure Committee on written questions — mySociety democracy pages into 2025–26). Those later submissions are from a funded organisation, not from a two-person parser.

TheyWorkForYou itself became *the thing MPs complained about* (Peter Luff debate on the rise in written questions attributed to the site’s rankings). Complaint is a form of recognition. It is not the same as a committee treating the site as a source of facts about BSL spend.

**Gap.** No select-committee *report* was isolated on this pass that cites Council Climate Scorecards as evidence. Council and press use are documented. Parliamentary citation of the Scorecards is not, on this pass.

### D. Regulator or statutory-body citation

Not found for any of the civic-tech cousins in a form that maps onto CQC, EHRC or NHS England citing a volunteer scorecard as an assurance source. EHRC runs its *own* Human Rights Tracker (prompt 38). CQC is explicit that it does not assure AIS (prompt 34). A Deaf access gap monitor should not plan on being adopted as a regulator product. That would recreate the official-sounding risk prompt 39 flagged.

## 41.3 Labour models

### Two-person parser (Public Whip, 2003)

Francis Irving and Julian Todd. Spare time. Never grant-funded. Server costs paid by the founders. Handed to a new team 1 August 2011. This is the cheapest build in the set and the one that only works when the source is a single, regular official feed (Hansard). AIS self-assessments, council £500+ payment files and FOI replies are not a single feed.

### Volunteer conference launch + later adoption (TheyWorkForYou, 2004 → 2006)

“A dozen or so volunteers” on the June 2004 About page. One developer kept fed for a month by a small UKCOD grant. mySociety adopted the site early 2006. Tobias Escher’s 2011 commissioned report is the first substantial independent evaluation — seven years after launch. Escher’s appendix describes mySociety at that point as employing **12 people**, with sites used by over 5 million in the previous year.

### Funded 18-month build, volunteer operations (WhatDoTheyKnow, 2006–08 onwards)

JRRT grant. Launch line: no name yet, no slick design, half the features missing, but it works. Ongoing model is volunteer request-admin plus, later, a paid support post that mySociety was still fundraising for in 2018 (£15k/year part-time admin; £30–40k/year development). 2011 published running cost ~£12k. 2024/25 impact report: **152,567** FOI requests filed on the site that year; **7.65 million** archive views. The labour that scales is moderation and user support, not the original Rails app.

### Four staff + volunteer army (Climate Emergency UK)

Founder line, on CE UK’s own origin page: “I never started CE UK with the intention of it existing as an organisation with four staff members and real funding.” Environmental Funders Network blog, 17 January 2024, describing the first Action Scorecards: **team of four staff and an army of 200 volunteers over 10 months**; **more than 30,000 unique pieces of data and evidence**. Scorecards about page: first marking phase **over 5,000 volunteer hours**; 256 first-markers and 57 auditors on a later published count; 215 trained volunteers on the 2023 first mark. 2027 volunteer advert: 20 hours over four weeks per assessor, marking one section across at least 10 councils; current councillors and officers excluded.

That is the labour cost of scoring ~400 bodies against a published question set. A BSL / AIS monitor that tried to score every English ICB, every NHS trust and every upper-tier adult-social-care authority on the same cadence would be in the same labour class, even if the question set were shorter.

### Editorial staff, not volunteers (Full Fact)

Launched 2010 as a three-person operation (Will Moy remark, 2016). Charity Commission return to 31 December 2024: **43 employees**, income £2.92m. Press Gazette, 2 June 2026: restructure at the end of 2025 cut **11 posts, about a quarter of the workforce**, after Google withdrew more than £1m. This is not a volunteer-mark model. It is a newsroom. Copying it means copying a fundraising machine, not a marking weekend.

### Already-citable host (Institute for Government)

Charity 1123926. Income **£6,857,604** to 31 March 2025. **58 employees**. Main funder: Gatsby Charitable Foundation (Sainsbury Family). Performance Tracker 2025 is published in partnership with the Nuffield Foundation; IfG says that support “allowed us to substantially expand and deepen our analysis.” Whitehall Monitor 2026 is the 13th edition. There is no “path to recognition” here to copy. The publisher was already the thing journalists phone.

### mySociety as it now exists (the host, not the v1)

Group income 2024/25 **£2.628m**; expenditure £2.529m; staff costs **£1.97m**. Restricted grant income fell 39% to £1.008m. Trading (SocietyWorks) rose 15% to £1.503m; SocietyWorks profit £0.299m. Funding page (seen 2026): SocietyWorks profits contributed **47%** of overall income in FY 2025/26. Named 2024/25 restricted donors include Patrick J. McGovern Foundation £231,533, National Endowment for Democracy £143,603, National Lottery Community Fund £123,960, Joseph Rowntree Charitable Trust £69,289. Climate programme separately described as Lottery Community Fund **£495,907 over three years**. Charity Commission 1076346: government-contract income £1.36m in 2024/25 (SocietyWorks local-authority SaaS, not a grant for TheyWorkForYou).

A new Deaf-sector tool does not inherit this envelope by launching a website.

## 41.5 Running cost is mostly people, not servers

mySociety 2024/25 expenditure note: servers and subscriptions **£79,492** across trading and charitable activities; staff costs **£1.97m**. The ratio is the finding. A Deaf access monitor whose “platform cost” is quoted as a cloud invoice is quoting the wrong line.

WhatDoTheyKnow’s 2011 ~£12k figure is useful only as a historic floor for a thin, volunteer-run site. By 2018 mySociety was saying there was no direct funding for WDTK itself and that a part-time paid assistant had become “increasingly urgent.” Volume is the thing that creates the admin bill: 152,567 requests in 2024/25.

Climate Scorecards’ published cash product is the **data pack**, not a SaaS seat. 2025 prices on climateemergency.uk/action-scorecards: English district / NI council £140; other individual councils £250; complete dataset non-commercial £555; higher commercial tiers £900 and £1,900 for 2023+2025. CE UK’s own gloss: the packs exist “to provide greater financial stability.” That is a cost-recovery tactic after the volunteer mark, not a substitute for the mark.

Full Fact’s 2025 funding page lists running costs as “mostly paying staff and renting office space in London.” Google’s withdrawal of more than £1m (Full Fact, 16 October 2025) forced a late-2025 restructure. Dependency on one class of funder is a documented failure mode, not a hypothetical.

## 41.6 Honest quotes a small Deaf-sector team can use

Use these sentences. Do not decorate them.

- A first public page on top of a working collection has been done in this set in **months**, by two people, on founder cash or a four-figure grant.
- A scored, comparable product covering every relevant public body has taken **about two to three years** in the one close method cousin, and consumed **thousands of volunteer hours** per cycle plus a small paid staff.
- Being asked to give evidence to a select committee took **four years** in the one clean dated case, and the witness was a volunteer from an already-public FOI archive, not from a private spreadsheet.
- Broadcaster reuse can happen inside a year if the underlying feed is already parsed (TheyWorkForYou / Hansard). It has not been shown inside a year for a newly extracted policy-versus-spend dataset.
- The organisations that *look* like the finished article now run on **low-single-digit millions** a year and tens of staff. That is the host, not the first product.
- Volunteer exclusion rules matter. CE UK will not accept current councillors or officers as markers. A BSL monitor that let ICB comms staff mark their own AIS return would be marking homework, which is what the official AIS self-assessment already is.

## 41.7 What this means for the tool in this dossier

The dossier is past idea-stage and inside the CE UK 2019–20 window:

- SWL 878 BSL-request series (Oct 2024–Jun 2025) is a geographically bounded gold-standard collection.
- FOI companion log and six templates already exist.
- AIS, DWP, Jobcentre, EOL and naming briefs exist as sourced markdown.

The next cost that the cousins actually paid is not engineering. It is:

1. **A frozen question set** with published criteria, so a second year can be compared to the first. CE UK spent 2020–21 turning spreadsheets into CAPE before it scored anything.
2. **People to mark, and a smaller set to audit.** 20 hours × N markers is the unit CE UK now advertises. N for “every English ICB + every SWL/SEL trust + six SWL DASSs” is a different N from “388 councils,” but it is not N = 1.
3. **A right-of-reply window** sent to the body being scored. Without it the product is a campaign PDF. With it, 76% of CE UK’s 2025 subjects answered.
4. **A host that will still be there for cycle two.** PledgeBank ran for ten years and closed because the parent would not make it the main job. A monitor parked inside a single campaigner’s evenings inherits that death.

Partnership is the documented jump trigger for pattern-1 tools: mySociety adopted TheyWorkForYou; mySociety approached CE UK in 2020; Nuffield underwrites IfG’s 2025 Tracker expansion. The jump was not a better React app.

Prompt 42 should treat legal risk and right-of-reply as a cost line, not a footnote — CE UK built reply in because scoring a public body in public is the product. Prompt 43 should take the WDTK-at-~400-councils practice and the ~700 FOI-capture hours as the labour unit, not as a software feature.
