# Prompt 127 — How the Equality Act duty and FCA information-barrier rules interact, and which one yields

## Headline

**Nobody has published the answer.** No FCA Handbook rule, no FCA Market Watch or Primary Market Technical Note, no UK Finance or Investment Association product, and no EHRC Code paragraph was found that names a BSL interpreter, live captioner or AI note-taking tool inside a Chinese Wall or a UK MAR insider list.

The two regimes sit side by side. They do not contain a hierarchy clause that tells a compliance officer which one yields.

- **Equality Act 2010** Part 5 / ss.20–21 / s.39 and Schedule 8 put a fact-specific reasonable-adjustment duty on the employer. A BSL interpreter is a textbook auxiliary aid in the Employment Code. The duty is not switched off because the employer is FCA-regulated.
- **SYSC 10.2** lets a firm maintain a Chinese Wall and treat people on the other side as not possessing the withheld knowledge. Last substantive update 3 July 2016. The rule is silent on disability and on third-party communication professionals.
- **UK MAR Article 18** (assimilated Regulation (EU) No 596/2014) requires issuers and persons acting on their behalf to list *all persons who have access to inside information* and who work for them under a contract of employment **or otherwise perform tasks through which they have access**, “such as advisers, accountants or credit rating agencies.” That wording is broad enough that an interpreter in the room is a candidate for the list. No published FCA source confirms or rejects that reading for communication-support professionals.
- Article 18(2) requires written acknowledgement of legal duties and sanctions before or as access is given. That is a process hook, not a ban and not a permission.

Whether the Equality Act requires the employer to extend wall-crossing / restricted-list access to the interpreter is **not a settled legal question**. No named UK judgment, FCA enforcement finding or EHRC investigation was found on these facts. Absence of a judgment is not a finding that the duty does not apply, and it is not a finding that wall-crossing is mandatory.

**Hard-don't for anything public.** Do not print that wall-crossing an interpreter is permitted or prohibited under UK MAR. Do not print that a named bank’s practice is unlawful. Do not treat “we couldn’t wall-cross the interpreter” as automatically a breach — prompt 130 holds the objective-justification point.

## 127.1 Two live duties, no published join

A wall-crossed meeting about an unannounced transaction is, for MAR purposes, a need-to-know event. Everyone who hears the information is a person with access. Article 18 is written to capture employees *and* people “otherwise performing tasks through which they have access.” Advisers and accountants are the worked examples because they are the usual third parties. An interpreter is performing a task in the room. That is why the honest legal question is not “does MAR ban interpreters” — it does not say that — but “must the interpreter be treated as an insider for list and acknowledgement purposes, and does the Equality Act require the firm to run that process rather than exclude the employee.”

SYSC 10.2 does a different job. It lets the firm *withhold* information across internal businesses and treats people on the far side as not knowing it. It is not a rule about who may sit in a wall-crossed meeting on the *informed* side. Once the employee is on the informed side, the wall has already been crossed for that employee. The new party is the interpreter.

The Equality Act duty is independent. s.20 does not contain an “unless MAR” proviso. Reasonableness can still weigh practicability, cost, and the employer’s other legal obligations. That last clause is a factor in the reasonableness analysis, not a published conclusion that MAR always wins.

## 127.2 What “unpublished” means here

This is not a search failure. The FCA has written at length about insider lists, Control Rooms, need-to-know and contractor acknowledgements in the ordinary adviser/accountant sense. Disability adjustments inside that machinery are simply not a published topic. Industry D&I material in financial services in 2024–26 is almost entirely *customer*-facing (SigningBanks, LSB updates, bank VRS pages) or *colleague-culture* (HSBC BSL beginners; Lloyds Access network). Neither product engages SYSC 10 or Article 18.

Prompts 128–130 take the same absence as the starting point: 128 asks what a constructed wall-crossing process for a human professional would look like; 129 isolates the AI-tool record problem; 130 asks whether exclusion has ever been litigated and whether objective justification has been tested.

## 127.3 What this gate does *not* decide

- It does not decide that any named firm’s Control Room practice is lawful or unlawful.
- It does not decide that Article 18 *requires* listing an interpreter — only that the wording is capable of reaching one.
- It does not decide that refusing to wall-cross is a breach. Prompt 130 holds that point.
- It does not import US ADA “effective communication,” Scotland’s NHS interpreting policy, or AIS into an FCA-regulated employment cell.
