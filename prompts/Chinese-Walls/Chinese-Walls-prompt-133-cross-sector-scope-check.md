# Prompt 133 — Scope check: does the pattern recur in other confidentiality-regulated sectors, and should it become a named cross-cutting issue?

**Master index prompt 133.** Seventh and last file in the Chinese Walls / information-barriers batch (prompts 127–133). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier.

**What this prompt is for.** Establish whether the same core tension — a third-party communication-support professional or an AI tool needing access to information a confidentiality or security regime restricts — has been documented, named or resolved in legal professional privilege, medical confidentiality, or UK government security vetting; whether any single published UK source treats “reasonable adjustments in confidentiality-restricted environments” as a named cross-cutting issue; and what is unpublished. Do not fill a UK cell with a US figure. ABA Formal Opinion 500 is international.

**Index check.** This file is the research *answer* to prompt 133. It is the batch’s highest-value prompt if a transferable model exists. Distinct from 128 (FS-specific process) because the question here is whether another UK sector already built the machine.

## Headline

**The tension recurs. It is not a named cross-cutting issue. Two sectors have working machines; one has doctrine; none is a drop-in FCA template.**

1. **Government security — operational model.** Home Office panel interpreters are security-cleared before they hear restricted information and sign an Official Secrets Act / confidentiality declaration. The Crown Commercial Service language-services framework sets BPSS as the floor and lets the buyer add OSA, a specified confidentiality agreement and higher clearance at call-off. This is the closest UK *process* for “vet the interpreter, put a written undertaking on them, destroy the notes.” It is written for Official / Secret government work, not for UK MAR insider lists. It transfers as a pattern, not as a safe harbour.
2. **Legal professional privilege — doctrine, not a Control-Room product.** Privilege can cover communications through an interpreter as a conduit, provided confidentiality is preserved. The Law Society tells solicitors they must know how to book a BSL interpreter for disabled clients and must not pass the cost on (Equality Act s.20 + SRA outcomes). The Law Society criminal practice note covers police-station and court interpreters, including a 2023 sign-language addendum. None of those products is a template for wall-crossing an interpreter into a live M&A disclosure. ABA Formal Opinion 500 (6 October 2021) — **international** — is the cleanest foreign sentence: the interpreter must be qualified, impartial, and must assent to the lawyer’s duty of confidentiality. Do not paste it into a UK cell.
3. **Medical confidentiality — patient-side, not employee-side.** GMC confidentiality guidance and Good Medical Practice require effective communication, including an interpreter. NHS AIS and ordinary clinical-interpreter booking are the working machine for *patients*. They do not tell an NHS trust how to take a Deaf *employee* into a restricted incident meeting, and they do not bind an FCA firm.

**No single published UK source** — EHRC Code, Cabinet Office, TUC, BDF, Law Society, GMC, FCA, UK Finance — treats “reasonable adjustments in confidentiality-restricted environments” as a named cross-cutting topic. Each sector is encountering the same shape in isolation.

Should it become a named cross-cutting issue in this dossier? **Yes as a flag, no as a new Domain.** The Welfare & Employment cluster already holds the adjustment pipeline (92–126). The reusable sentence is: *where a confidentiality or security regime restricts who may hear or record information, an agreed communication or cognitive-processing adjustment has to be run through that regime’s own listing / vetting / undertaking machinery — and no UK regulator has published the join.* Legal privilege, medical confidentiality, government security and financial-services walls are instances. Devices-and-technology batch 134–140 will pick up the AI-record half outside FS.

## 133.1 What “transferable” does and does not mean

A bank Control Room can copy the *shape* of the Home Office pack (clearance-equivalent due diligence appropriate to the information class; written acknowledgement of duties and sanctions; no personal notes; named individual only; off-board when the wall closes) without pretending the Official Secrets Act is UK MAR. A firm can copy the *shape* of privilege practice (the interpreter is a conduit, not an extra client; bind them by contract to the same confidentiality). Neither copy is a published safe harbour. Prompt 127 still holds: the join is unpublished.

## 133.2 What this prompt does *not* decide

- It does not decide that BPSS or SC clearance is required for a City interpreter.
- It does not decide that privilege automatically attaches to a wall-crossed deal meeting because an interpreter is present — privilege is a legal-advice doctrine, not a MAR doctrine.
- It does not treat AIS as the employee-side answer.
- International ABA 500 stays international.
