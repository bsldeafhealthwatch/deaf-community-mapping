# Prompt 130 — What happens when the adjustment cannot be accommodated: exclusion, alternatives, and whether this is a documented detriment

**Master index prompt 130.** Fourth file in the Chinese Walls / information-barriers batch (prompts 127–133). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier.

**What this prompt is for.** Establish whether any documented UK case addresses an employee being excluded from information access, a deal team or an opportunity because a reasonable adjustment (interpreter, AI tool) could not be reconciled with an information-barrier, security-clearance or confidentiality regime; whether any alternative arrangement is documented in practice; and what is unpublished. **Do not print that exclusion in this context is automatically unlawful discrimination.** The Equality Act allows an objective-justification defence for indirect discrimination (s.19) and for discrimination arising from disability (s.15). This prompt records whether that defence has ever been tested on these facts — it has not.

## Headline

**The defence has never been tested on these facts.** No UK employment-tribunal, EAT, High Court, EHRC investigation or FCA enforcement finding was found in which a Deaf, BSL-using or neurodivergent employee was excluded from a wall-crossed meeting, a deal team or a promotion pathway *because* an interpreter or an AI tool could not be reconciled with SYSC 10 / UK MAR / a security-clearance regime.

That absence is the load-bearing finding. It cuts both ways. It does not mean exclusion is lawful. It does not mean exclusion is unlawful. A public sentence that treats “we couldn’t wall-cross the interpreter” as an automatic Equality Act breach is a hard-don’t.

The legal map that *would* be argued, if a case arrived:

- **s.20 / s.21** — failure to make a reasonable adjustment (auxiliary aid; or PCP of “attend wall-crossed meetings without a third party / without an AI record”). Reasonableness can weigh the employer’s other legal duties, cost and practicability. There is no published case applying those factors to MAR.
- **s.19** — indirect discrimination. A PCP such as “only people already on the restricted list, with no additional attendee, may enter the room” applies to everyone and may put Deaf BSL-users (or neurodivergent staff whose adjustment is a tool) at a particular disadvantage. The employer may defend if the PCP is a **proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim**. Market-integrity / MAR compliance is, on ordinary principles, capable of being a legitimate aim. Whether *this* PCP is *proportionate* — including whether a vetted, listed, NDA’d interpreter is a less-discriminatory alternative — has not been decided.
- **s.15** — discrimination arising from disability. Unfavourable treatment because of something arising from disability (need for an interpreter / tool) is unlawful unless the treatment is a proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim. Same untested defence.
- **s.39** — the work provisions that make those sections bite on employees, applicants and some contract workers.

No documented alternative arrangement (standing cleared interpreter for a deal’s life; written-summary-only; on-device no-cloud AI) was found as a named-firm published practice. Those remain constructed options from prompts 128–129, not evidence of use.

Analogous regulated sectors produce *adjacent* not equivalent material: national-security vetting can keep a person out of a post if clearance is refused; that is a different statutory machine (NSV / OSA), not a MAR judgment. Do not file it as this cell.

## 130.1 The sentence this batch exists to stop

“The bank refused to let my interpreter into the wall-crossed meeting, therefore the bank breached the Equality Act.”

That sentence is not available on the public record. The Act supplies three possible claims and two justification/reasonableness gates. Market-integrity compliance is capable of being a legitimate aim. The live question a tribunal would actually try is whether the firm considered a less-discriminatory alternative (list the interpreter, take the acknowledgement, lock the notes) and whether refusing that alternative was proportionate. Nobody has tried it.

## 130.2 Alternatives stay constructed

Prompts 128 and 129 describe the kit a Control Room *could* use. Prompt 130 asked whether anyone has *used* it and written it down. They have not, on the public record. Do not convert the constructed kit into “industry practice.”
