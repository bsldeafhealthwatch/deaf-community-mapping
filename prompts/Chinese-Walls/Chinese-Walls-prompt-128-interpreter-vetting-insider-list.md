## Headline

NRCPD registration gives a firm a **professional confidentiality clause and a complaints route**. It does not give a firm an insider-list process, a deal-specific written MAR acknowledgement, or a Control-Room workflow.

The live NRCPD Code of Conduct (November 2023) requires registrants to treat assignment information as confidential and to share it only with consent or where the law requires or allows (including to prevent harm). That is the same shape as every other UK interpreting code. It is **not** equivalent to legal professional privilege, to the GMC’s common-law medical confidentiality duty, or to a UK MAR Article 18(2) written acknowledgement of insider-dealing and unlawful-disclosure sanctions.

No named UK bank, UK Finance, Investment Association, ICAEW, Law Society, ASLI or NRCPD publication was found that is a template NDA, a wall-crossing protocol, or a case study for putting an interpreter or captioner on an insider list.

The process, if a firm chose to run one, would be built from the ordinary Control-Room kit already used for other third parties (lawyers, accountants, printers): identity capture, reason-for-access, access date/time, written MAR acknowledgement, deal NDA, device and note-taking rules, off-boarding. Whether that kit is *enough* for an interpreter is unpublished. Government interpreting contracts are the closest *operational* analogue — Home Office panel interpreters are security-cleared and sign an Official Secrets Act / confidentiality declaration; the Crown Commercial Service language-services framework sets BPSS as the floor and lets a buyer add OSA and a specified confidentiality agreement at call-off. Those are government-security products, not FCA products. They transfer only as a model of “vet + written undertaking,” not as a MAR safe harbour.

## 128.1 What NRCPD actually gives a Control Room

A compliance officer asking “can I rely on the badge?” gets three things:

1. A published confidentiality rule with a complaints process.
2. A registration check against a public register (RSLI / STTR / notetaker / RIDB as relevant).
3. Nothing about market abuse.

The Code’s lawful-disclosure carve-out (“the law requires or allows”) is the opposite of a MAR wall. It is written for safeguarding and compulsion, which is right for health and criminal work and is a *risk* sentence in a live deal room. A firm that wanted to use NRCPD as one layer would still need a deal-specific instruction that overrides informal peer debrief about *this* assignment and a written MAR acknowledgement that names the sanctions.

## 128.2 The government model is the nearest working machine

The Home Office panel and the CCS framework are the only UK systems found this pass that systematically vet interpreters *and* put a written state-secrecy or confidentiality undertaking on them before they hear restricted information. That is why prompt 133 flags them as the cross-sector analogue. They are not a substitute for Article 18. A bank cannot point to BPSS and sit down. It can point to them as evidence that “vet + written undertaking + destroy notes” is a known UK pattern for communication-support professionals in restricted rooms.

## 128.3 What this prompt does *not* decide

- It does not decide that listing an interpreter is mandatory.
- It does not decide that an NRCPD badge is worthless — only that it is not a MAR product.
- It does not decide that DBS, BPSS or SC clearance is required for a City deal room. No source says that.
- Captioners and speech-to-text reporters are in the NRCPD family and sit under the same Code. They generate a *written* record, which is why prompt 129 is a separate legal problem even when the human is the same professional type.
