# Prompts 127–133 — Reasonable adjustments inside financial-services Chinese Walls / information barriers

Added: 10 September 2026. Compiled as findings: 11 September 2026. Stub under the Welfare & Employment cluster. Not a new Domain. Template flag for other regulated sectors (legal privilege, medical confidentiality, government security clearance) if the pattern proves out — it does, as a recurring *shape* without a named cross-cut (prompt 133).

Checked first: the master index and batches 85–91 (neurodivergence diagnostic / DWP pathways), 92–96 (OH), 97–102 (HR/IT adjustment-ticket SLAs), 103–108 (recruitment), 109–114 (grievance/ET), 115–120 (DeafBlind in-post adjustments), 121–126 (outsourced internal channels). None of those batches engages a sector-specific regulatory regime (FCA SYSC 10, UK MAR insider lists, market-abuse rules) that can independently restrict who is in the room or who may hold a written record after an adjustment has already been agreed. Distinct from 134–140 (workplace devices / AI generally): 129 is only the wall-crossed record collision.

Anchor: a Deaf BSL-using analyst in a UK investment bank’s research or corporate-finance division is invited into a wall-crossed meeting on a live unannounced transaction. Their agreed adjustment is a BSL interpreter for all meetings. The interpreter is not on the deal restricted list, has not signed the deal NDA, and Compliance has no process for wall-crossing a communication-support professional. Separately, a neurodivergent colleague in the same meeting whose adjustment is an AI note-taking / summarisation tool would create a durable, possibly cloud-processed transcript of inside information the barrier policy was never designed to hold.

Gate: prompt 127. Load-bearing: prompt 130 (objective justification untested) and prompt 129 (live-recheck before any public use). Hard-don't: do not print that a named firm, regulator or practice is unlawful, non-compliant or in breach of the Equality Act or FCA rules without a named statute, regulation, tribunal/court judgment or FCA enforcement finding. Do not assert that wall-crossing an interpreter is permitted or prohibited under UK MAR without a named source that actually says so.

Headline across the batch: **no one has published the join.** SYSC 10.2 (last updated 3 July 2016) and UK MAR Article 18 sit beside Equality Act ss.15, 19, 20 and 39. Neither regime contains a hierarchy clause. Article 18’s “otherwise performing tasks through which they have access” wording is capable of reaching an interpreter; no FCA source confirms or rejects that reading. NRCPD confidentiality is a professional duty, not a MAR acknowledgement. No named UK judgment tests exclusion or the objective-justification defence on these facts. No asked foreign regulator has published the join either. Customer-facing bank BSL is abundant and is the wrong object. The closest transferable *machines* are Home Office / CCS interpreter vetting plus an Official Secrets / confidentiality undertaking, and the legal-privilege “interpreter as conduit” doctrine. Neither is an FCA product. Prompt 129 is stale the day it is written.
