# Prompt 131 — International comparative practice (information barriers × reasonable accommodation)

**Master index prompt 131.** Fifth file in the Chinese Walls / information-barriers batch (prompts 127–133). Compiled 11 September 2026. **All findings in this file are international. None of them fills a UK data cell.** Dossier rule: stay unbiased. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier.

**What this prompt is for.** Establish whether any asked jurisdiction has published regulatory guidance specifically reconciling a reasonable accommodation (interpreter, AI tool) with an information-barrier or insider-list regime in financial services; whether any named case, firm policy or regulator statement documents how this has been resolved in practice elsewhere; and what is jurisdiction-specific and does not transfer. Label every row international.

## Headline

**No asked jurisdiction has published the join.** The United States, Canada, Australia and the EU/EEA each have a mature information-barrier / insider-list regime *and* a mature disability-accommodation duty. This pass did not find a securities-regulator document in any of those systems that tells a broker-dealer or bank how to put a sign-language interpreter or an AI notetaker on the informed side of a wall.

The two bodies of law sit on adjacent shelves, as they do in the UK.

- **United States.** SEC / FINRA information-barrier elements (written procedures, wall-crossing logs, restricted and watch lists) are standard. ADA Title I requires a reasonable accommodation including a sign-language interpreter unless undue hardship. SEC’s own staff reasonable-accommodation directive (SECR 6-80, 14 July 2022) lists a sign-language interpreter as an example adjustment *for SEC employees*. That is the regulator as employer, not a broker-dealer Control-Room rule. No SEC/FINRA notice reconciling Regulation FD / Rule 10b-5 / information barriers with an employee interpreter was found.
- **EU / EEA.** MiFID II Articles 16 and 23 plus EU MAR Article 18 use the same “otherwise performing tasks” insider-list wording the UK assimilated. ESMA Q&As on insider lists deal with advisers and delegated list-keeping, not communication-support professionals. No ESMA product on disability adjustments inside MAR was found.
- **Canada / Australia.** NI 31-103 conflicts / information-barrier expectations and ASIC market-integrity rules exist; CHRC / DDA accommodation duties exist. No published reconciling FS guidance found.

Transfer ban: do not paste ADA “undue hardship,” SEC SECR 6-80, ESMA Q&As or Australian EAF dollar caps into a UK SYSC / MAR / Equality Act cell.

## 131.1 Why the silence repeats

Every asked system built its market-abuse machine in the 1980s–2010s and its disability-accommodation machine on a different clock. The worked third parties on an insider list are always the same: lawyers, accountants, rating agencies, printers. Communication-support professionals are a worked example in *health*, *courts* and *government security* (prompt 133), not in securities regulation. That pattern is the international finding. It does not license a UK firm to do nothing; it means a UK Control Room looking for a foreign template will not find one.
