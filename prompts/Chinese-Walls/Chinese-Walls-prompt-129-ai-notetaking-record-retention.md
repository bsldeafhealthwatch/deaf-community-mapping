## Headline

**No FCA rule or guidance addresses this collision.** The FCA’s published AI position in 2024–26 is “existing rules apply; no new AI-specific rulebook.” Those existing rules — Principles 2 and 3, SYSC 9 record-keeping, SYSC 10A recording of certain communications, UK MAR Articles 10, 14 and 18, UK GDPR, operational-resilience and third-party rules — were not written for a neurodivergent employee’s Otter / Fireflies / Teams Copilot / specialist-advice notetaker joining a wall-crossed call.

Two separate problems hide under one product name:

1. **Access.** A cloud AI tool that receives the audio or a transcript is a person-or-system with access to inside information. Article 18 lists *persons*. A vendor’s staff, a model-training pipeline, or a US-hosted processor is not a deal-team member and is not on the insider list. Whether that is “unlawful disclosure” under Article 10 / 14 is **unpublished on these facts**. It is a live compliance question, not a settled prohibition.
2. **Record.** The tool creates a durable transcript the firm’s information-barrier and retention policy may never have classified. SYSC 9 and SYSC 10A can cut *both* ways: the firm may be *required* to retain certain recorded communications, and *forbidden* by its own wall policy from letting an uncontrolled copy exist in a vendor cloud.

Vendor and law-firm commentary in 2025–26 treats AI notetakers as an eComms / privilege / shadow-IT problem. None of it is written as a disability-adjustment analysis. Clifford Chance’s January 2026 boardroom GenAI briefing is the closest senior-meeting product found: treat AI output as a temporary aid, not the official record; lock circulation; be ready to switch the tool off when privileged or sensitive matters arise. That is board governance, not a Control-Room RA protocol.

No FCA Final Notice or published enforcement action was found that turns on an AI notetaker inside a wall-crossed meeting, with or without a disability angle.

**Hard-don't.** Do not print that a named tool is banned in UK banks. Do not print that refusing the tool is a breach of the Equality Act. Do not treat general FCA AI speeches as if they decided this cell.

## 129.1 Why this is not prompt 128 with a different toy

A human interpreter can sign an NDA, go on the list, leave their phone outside and take no notes. An AI tool is an extra copy of the meeting that may live on a vendor’s infrastructure, be used to train a model, be searchable by people who were never wall-crossed, and survive the deal. That is a disclosure-and-retention problem even if every human in the room is cleared. The employee’s legal basis (s.20 cognitive-processing adjustment) does not dissolve the MAR / GDPR / SYSC problem. It also does not dissolve the Equality Act duty. The collision is the finding.
