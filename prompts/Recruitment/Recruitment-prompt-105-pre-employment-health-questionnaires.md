## Headline

s.60 is a **narrow ban with named exceptions**, not a ban on mentioning disability at all.

**The ban.** A person to whom an application for work is made “must not ask about the health of the applicant” before offering work (conditional or unconditional) or, if they cannot yet offer work, before putting the person in a pool of people who will be offered work when a vacancy exists. s.60(13) treats whether someone has a disability as an aspect of health. Sickness-absence questions count. The ban covers written and spoken questions, questions to a third party (including a previous employer), and routing the applicant through occupational health *before* an offer. It applies to employers **and** employment service-providers (s.60(9) includes provision of an employment service).

**Who can sue for the question itself.** Only the EHRC. s.60(2) and s.120(8): a bare s.60 breach is an “unlawful act” under the Equality Act 2006, enforceable **only by the Commission**. An individual cannot take “they asked me if I was Deaf” to an employment tribunal as a s.60 claim. What the individual *can* take is the employer’s **conduct in reliance on the answer** (s.60(3)) — typically direct discrimination or a failure to adjust — and if they do, s.60(5) treats the particulars as facts from which the tribunal could conclude that the employer discriminated (burden shift).

**The permitted exceptions — s.60(6), plus two extras.** A question is outside the ban *in so far as* asking it is **necessary** for:

- **(a)** establishing whether the applicant can undergo an assessment, or whether a reasonable-adjustment duty will arise **in connection with that assessment**. s.60(12): “assessment” = an interview or other process designed to indicate suitability. This is the lawful hook for “do you need a BSL interpreter / tactile interpreter / large print / extra time for the interview or test?”
- **(b)** establishing whether the applicant can carry out a function **intrinsic to the work**. s.60(7): if the employer reasonably believes an adjustment duty would arise for the job itself, read “intrinsic” as “intrinsic once that adjustment is made.” A general “are you Deaf?” is not this exception. “Can you use a telephone unassisted for this call-centre role, with any adjustment in place?” might be, if telephony is truly intrinsic and cannot be designed out.
- **(c)** monitoring diversity of applicants (anonymous, held separately).
- **(d)** disability positive action within s.158 (for example a guaranteed-interview scheme).
- **(e)** establishing whether the applicant has a **particular disability** that the employer has made an occupational requirement of the work — only if s.60(8) is met (occupational requirement + proportionate means of a legitimate aim). The Employment Code’s own example is an employer recruiting a **Deafblind project worker** who has personal experience of Deafblindness.
- **s.60(14)** national-security vetting.
- **Schedule 22 para 1** another statutory requirement (EHRC example: merchant-shipping medical fitness).

EHRC and ACAS both say: collect the process-adjustment answer **separately** from the application that is used to shortlist.

**Named enforcement.** **Elite Careplus Ltd**, EHRC investigation under Equality Act 2006 s.20. Terms of reference published 1 July 2021; news 13 May 2021; investigation report 2021. Finding: between 21 November 2018 and 20 June 2019 the care agency asked general health questions on its application/registration form (TB/asthma/chest; depression/mental illness; dermatitis; current medication affecting attendance or performance; illness keeping the person off work for more than a week in the past year). No s.60(6) exception applied. Unlawful-act notice served. Questions removed; no action plan required. Referral came from the National AIDS Trust. **Not a Deaf/BSL/DeafBlind case.** It is the only concluded EHRC s.60 investigation found this pass.

**No named GB tribunal** was found whose ratio is “employer asked a Deaf or DeafBlind candidate a prohibited pre-offer health question.” O’Sullivan v London Borough of Islington (ET 2017) is a first-instance illustration of interview questions treated as health enquiries (walking distance / evidence of ability); not binding; not Deaf.

**Templates.** ACAS does not publish a pre-offer health questionnaire. It publishes the exception list (page last updated 30 June 2026) and a *worker* reasonable-adjustment request template for people already in post. CIPD HR-inform’s “pre-employment medical questionnaire” products are labelled for use **after** an offer, or for existing employees — that is the correct split on the product page. CIPD’s inclusive-recruitment checklist (2023 PDF; 8 November 2024 guide) lists “a British Sign Language interpreter” under adjustments *to the interview process* — that is the s.60(6)(a) question, not a health screen. BDF (page last updated 20 March 2026) draws the same line. No public Workday / Oracle / SAP SuccessFactors template was opened and scored this pass.

## 105.1 The permitted-exception wording that 103 and 104 depend on

The lawful pre-offer question for a Deaf or DeafBlind candidate is almost always s.60(6)(a):

> necessary for the purpose of establishing whether B will be able to comply with a requirement to undergo an assessment or establishing whether a duty to make reasonable adjustments is or will be imposed on A in relation to B in connection with a requirement to undergo an assessment.

“Assessment” is defined in s.60(12) as an interview or other process designed to indicate suitability. That is why “Do you need a BSL interpreter, a deafblind manual / tactile interpreter, large print, braille, extra time or a different room for this interview or test?” is inside the Act. “Are you Deaf? Are you DeafBlind? What is your diagnosis? How many days were you off last year?” is not, unless another exception is actually necessary.

s.60(6)(b) does not license a general hearing questionnaire. Telephony, lone working with spoken alarms, or driving are the kinds of function that *might* be intrinsic — and even then s.60(7) forces the employer to ask the question as if reasonable adjustments for the *job* were already in place. Prompt 92 (OH) is where post-offer job-adjustment questions live. Mixing the two on the application form is the design error Elite Careplus was found to have made.

The occupational-requirement exception is real and the Code uses a **Deafblind project worker** as the example. That is a specific role whose purpose is lived DeafBlind experience. It is not a licence to ask every DeafBlind applicant, for every role, whether they are DeafBlind.

## 105.2 Enforcement that actually exists

Elite Careplus Ltd is the checkable finding this prompt was expected to surface. General medical-history questions on a care-agency application form, 2018–19, no exception made out, unlawful-act notice, questions taken off the form. The Commission used the power s.60 was written to give it. The case is HIV/health-screening shaped, not BSL-shaped. Do not relabel it as a Deaf case. Do not say “EHRC never enforces s.60.”

No second concluded s.60 investigation naming a Deaf, BSL-using or DeafBlind applicant was found. That is a gap in the enforcement record, not a finding that those questions are being asked lawfully.

## 105.3 Templates

The public official stack does distinguish the two questions.

- Process-adjustment question: ACAS, CIPD inclusive-recruitment checkbox, BDF, NHS Employers, Home Office careers (already in 103).
- General health questionnaire: CIPD HR-inform labels its medical questionnaires as post-offer or in-post. ACAS does not publish one for applicants.

What this pass did not do is open a live Workday / Oracle / council iTrent application form and score it. That remains unpublished. A single “reasonable adjustments / disability / health” combined box on an ATS is the design risk the statute is aimed at; whether any named UK ATS ships that box as default is a prompt 108 cell.

## 105.4 What is unpublished or contested

- Volume of s.60 reports the EHRC receives, and how many concern hearing or dual sensory loss.
- Whether a standard “do you consider yourself disabled?” monitoring box, kept on the same page as the sift, is being used to shortlist — the Code says keep it separate; practice is unpublished.
- Whether “how will you take phone calls?” asked of every applicant is treated as an intrinsic-function question or as a disguised health enquiry. O’Sullivan is first-instance only.
- Named ATS templates.

Do not print that a named living employer’s current form is unlawful. Elite Careplus is the named historical finding, and the Commission recorded that the questions were removed.
