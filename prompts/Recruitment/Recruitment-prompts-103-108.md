Checked first: the project index and existing batches. Prompts 20–26 and 27–33 (DWP/Jobcentre Plus) cover claiming benefits and job-seeking support, not applying for or interviewing at a specific employer. Prompts 92–96 (Occupational Health) and 97–102 (internal HR/IT SLAs) both explicitly assume the person is already employed and an OH recommendation already exists — neither touches the point before a job offer at all. No batch anywhere in the project has asked what happens at application, shortlisting, or interview stage. This is not a duplicate: it is the missing first stage of the employment pipeline, and the natural batch to read before 92–102, even though it is numbered after them.

Anchor: two linked situations every prompt in this batch must stay honest to — (a) a Deaf BSL-using candidate who requests an interpreter for interview through an employer's recruitment portal or agency and must establish, with no prior relationship to the employer, who pays, who books, and what the fallback is if no interpreter is available on the interview date; and (b) a DeafBlind candidate applying for the same role, where the accessible-application-format question (large print, braille, tactile BSL, tactile/manual interpretation at interview) is a materially different request from a BSL interpreter alone, and where a standard "reasonable adjustments" checkbox on an application form is unlikely to capture it.

Proposed domain: stub under the existing Welfare & Employment cluster, alongside 92–96 and 97–102 — this is the pre-employment stage of the same institutional pipeline, not a new numbered Domain.

## Prompts

**103. Legal basis and definitions — recruitment-stage duties, and what "reasonable adjustment" means before a contract exists**
> As a UK employment law researcher, using the Equality Act 2010 and its statutory Code of Practice, ACAS recruitment guidance, EHRC technical guidance on recruitment, and any published tribunal or employment appeal tribunal finding, establish (1) how the Equality Act reasonable-adjustment duty applies at the recruitment stage specifically — before a job offer or contract exists — including whether an employer must provide a BSL interpreter for interview even for an unsuccessful candidate, and whether the duty differs for a role advertised via a recruitment agency versus advertised directly, (2) whether "duty to make reasonable adjustments" at application/shortlisting stage (e.g. accessible application-form formats, extended time, alternative assessment methods) is documented separately from interview-stage adjustments (interpreter booking, venue access) in EHRC or ACAS guidance, (3) any published guidance or case law specifically addressing DeafBlind candidates' access needs (tactile BSL, deafblind manual, braille or large-print application materials) as distinct from BSL-only Deaf candidates', and (4) what is unpublished or contested. Flag thin, unpublished, contested, or anecdotal evidence rather than presenting it as settled. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL. Do not print any claim that a specific recruitment practice is unlawful or discriminatory without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment.

**104. Who pays, who books, and what happens when it fails — the live fulfilment fact at interview stage**
> As a UK workplace-accessibility researcher, using ACAS, Business Disability Forum, CIPD recruitment guidance, Access to Work's own published guidance (Access to Work can fund interpreters for job interviews under its "before you start work" provision), and any documented case, complaint, or news report describing an interview interpreter booking failure, establish (1) the correct, current process and funding route for booking a BSL interpreter for a job interview — is it the employer's cost, or is Access to Work's interview-support provision the primary route, and how quickly must a candidate request it before the interview date, (2) any documented case, charity report (SignHealth, RNID, BDA), or news report where an interview interpreter failed to attend, was booked too late, or was refused by the employer/agency, and what happened to the candidate as a result (rescheduled, withdrawn, proceeded without one), (3) whether recruitment agencies (as distinct from the hiring employer) have any documented published policy or duty regarding interpreter booking, given they often sit between the candidate and the employer, and (4) what is unpublished or contested. Flag thin, unpublished, contested, or anecdotal evidence — this is likely to surface advocacy-sourced or single-case material rather than systematic data. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL.

**105. Pre-employment health questionnaires and the recruitment-stage Deaf/DeafBlind disclosure question**
> As a UK employment law and occupational-health researcher, using the Equality Act 2010 (which restricts pre-offer health questionnaires to specific limited purposes), Business Disability Forum guidance on pre-employment health questions, ACAS, and any Equality and Human Rights Commission enforcement action or tribunal finding on unlawful pre-offer health enquiries, establish (1) the current legal limits on what an employer may ask a candidate about deafness, BSL use, or DeafBlindness before a conditional job offer is made, and the named exceptions (e.g. establishing whether a reasonable adjustment is needed for the recruitment process itself, which is permitted, versus general health screening, which is not), (2) any documented case or EHRC enforcement action where a pre-employment questionnaire unlawfully asked about deafness/disability outside the permitted exceptions, (3) whether standard pre-employment questionnaire templates (published by CIPD, ACAS, or a named HR software provider) distinguish correctly between the permitted "do you need an adjustment for this recruitment process" question and impermissible general health screening, and (4) what is unpublished or contested. Flag thin, unpublished, contested, or anecdotal evidence. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL. Do not print any claim that a named employer or template is unlawful without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment or EHRC enforcement finding.

**106. International comparative practice**
> As a comparative employment and disability-rights researcher, using academic literature, government labour/equality bodies, and Deaf/DeafBlind advocacy organisations from the USA (ADA pre-employment inquiry restrictions and interview accommodation case law), Canada, Australia, and EU member states, establish (1) any jurisdiction with a published standard, regulator guidance, or case law setting a specific process or funding route for interview-stage interpreter or accessible-format provision, (2) any jurisdiction or named employer with a documented distinct process for DeafBlind candidates at recruitment stage (as opposed to Deaf/BSL candidates generally), and (3) what is unpublished, contested, or specific to one jurisdiction's legal system in a way that does not transfer to the UK. Label all findings in this prompt as international — none of it fills a UK data cell; it is context only. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL.

**107. Lived experience and the case for change**

Flag thin, unpublished, contested, or anecdotal evidence rather than presenting it as settled. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL. Do not fill a UK cell with a US or other international figure; label international material as such.

**108. Recruitment agencies and third-party platforms — where the duty sits when the employer is once removed**
> As a UK employment and recruitment-sector researcher, using the Employment Agencies Act 1973 and its regulations, the Equality Act 2010 provisions on agency workers and third parties, REC (Recruitment and Employment Confederation) guidance, and any documented case or complaint involving an online job-application platform (e.g. an applicant-tracking system) that was not accessible to a screen-reader, BSL video-relay, or DeafBlind candidate, establish (1) who carries the reasonable-adjustment duty when a candidate applies through a third-party recruitment agency or an employer's outsourced applicant-tracking system rather than directly to the employer — the agency, the platform provider, or the end employer, or all three concurrently, (2) any documented accessibility failure or complaint involving a named UK applicant-tracking system or recruitment platform and a Deaf, BSL-using, or DeafBlind candidate, and (3) what is unpublished or contested. Flag thin, unpublished, contested, or anecdotal evidence. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL. Do not print any claim that a named agency, platform, or employer breached the Equality Act without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment.

### Notes for this batch

- **Gate prompt: 103.** The recruitment-stage legal-duty split (before offer vs. at interview) governs how 104–105 are read — if the duty is confirmed to apply even to unsuccessful candidates, that is itself a headline finding worth stating plainly, since it is likely to be widely unknown even among HR practitioners.
- **Prompt 105 is the most legally precise prompt in this batch** and the one most likely to surface a genuine, checkable EHRC/tribunal finding — pre-employment health questionnaire restrictions are a well-established area of Equality Act enforcement, unlike the OH/SLA batches which were expected to return largely absence-of-evidence. Do not let this prompt collapse into a generic "employers shouldn't discriminate" statement; it should return the specific permitted-exception wording.
- **Hard-don't for this batch:** do not print any claim that a named employer, agency, platform, or recruitment practice is unlawful, discriminatory, or in breach of the Equality Act without a named statute, regulation, tribunal/court judgment, or EHRC enforcement finding.
- **Held-back legs:** none — six prompts, because the DeafBlind-specific angle (raised in 103 and 106) is distinct enough from the Deaf/BSL angle to need its own sub-question inside the gate and comparative prompts rather than a separate prompt, and prompt 108 (agencies/platforms) is included rather than deferred because it is likely to be a genuinely thin, novel finding (who carries the duty when the employer is once removed) that the rest of the batch depends on being clear about.
- **Relationship to 92–102:** this batch is the pipeline stage before both — an OH referral (92–96) and an internal SLA (97–102) both presuppose the person already has the job. Any write-up should note this batch answers "can they get in the door," not "what happens once they're in."
- **Relationship to 20–33 and 52–54:** Jobcentre and Access to Work interview-support funding may appear in prompt 104. Treat AtW "before you start work" interview support as a *funding route*, not as proof that the employer duty has moved to DWP. Do not restate the £69,260 cap or the 3,210 BSL-interpreter customer count as recruitment-stage findings.
- **Do not conflate with Graeme's personal OH/HR thread** — this batch produces general, citable evidence for the dossier, not advice on an individual application.
