# Prompt 104 — Who pays and who books a BSL interpreter for a job interview, and what is documented when that booking fails?

## Headline

Two routes can pay for the same interview interpreter. They are not alternatives that cancel each other.

1. **Employer (and, if used, the agency) under the Equality Act duty.** If a BSL interpreter, lipspeaker, speech-to-text reporter or deafblind interpreter is a reasonable adjustment for the recruitment process, the duty-holder takes the step. Cost is a reasonableness factor, not a veto and not a reason to wait for DWP. ACAS tells the employer to ask every interviewee whether they need adjustments and names “providing a sign language interpreter for someone who is deaf.” BDF tells employers who use agencies to ask for evidence that the agency itself makes those adjustments, and to book language-support professionals **two to four weeks** ahead of the interview date.

2. **Access to Work Communication Support at Interview (CSI).** This is a separate, candidate-applied, **100% funded, no cost-share** DWP product. The public page (last updated **8 October 2025**) says: apply *before* the interview; tell AtW the employer name, date, expected duration, a contact at the company, and the **total cost** (worker + travel + admin + VAT); AtW pays **after** the interview if the invoice matches the quoted cost. The staff guide (HTML; landing page last listed **13 November 2025**, with a 24 May 2024 CSI-overview update and a later June 2026 refresh of other chapters) describes CSI as a **fast-track**: decision shared with the customer **within 2 working days**; no adviser contact with the employer before the interview; AtW contacts the named company person **after** the interview only to confirm it happened; no cap on the number of interviews, though repeated unsuccessful interviews may trigger a Jobcentre Plus referral. Easy Read AtW material names a **deafblind interpreter** as one of the people CSI can pay for.

CSI does **not** replace the employer duty. Scope, BDF, NDCS and EHRC recruitment guidance all say that in different words. The operational reality the dossier has to stay honest to is the opposite of a clean protocol: the candidate often has to obtain a quote, apply to AtW, *and* tell the employer, while the interpreter market needs two to four weeks and many interviews are offered inside that window.

Documented interview-stage failures are **thin and old**. The named GB case is **Murphy v Sheffield Hallam University** (ET 1998 / EAT 11 January 2000) under the Disability Discrimination Act 1995: application form disclosed profound deafness; interview listed for 16 December 1997 with **no signer arranged**; interview adjourned to 18 December; tribunal awarded **£2,500** for the 16 December failure; he attended the rearranged interview with an amateur signer and was not appointed; EAT dismissed his appeal on the appointment decision. That is a reschedule-after-late-booking pattern, not a 2020s no-show series. **Wynn v Multipulse** (NI, 2007) stays labelled NI. No 2024–26 named job-interview interpreter no-show or employer-refusal judgment was found this pass. Do not import the Still Ignored / Locked Out **80%** healthcare figure into this cell.

REC has no public interpreter-booking protocol found this pass. CIPD’s inclusive-recruitment material (2023 line-manager PDF; 8 November 2024 employer guide) lists “a British Sign Language interpreter” as an example adjustment to offer; it is not a booking SLA.

## 104.1 The two-route split

Prompt 103 established that the Equality Act duty is live at interview, including for a candidate who later loses the job. This file is about money and diaries.

**Route A — duty-holder books and pays.** ACAS puts the ask on the employer. BDF puts the booking on whoever the candidate prefers, with the duty-holder paying “regardless of who organises it.” CIPD tells recruiters to offer a BSL-interpreter checkbox. None of those documents publishes a unit rate or a days-SLA. The market constraint that *is* published is BDF’s two-to-four-week lead time for language-support professionals. A same-week interview invitation and a BSL request are therefore in tension before anyone has spoken to AtW.

**Route B — CSI.** The candidate applies, the candidate (or their chosen provider) obtains the quote, AtW decides in two working days, the candidate books after the decision, AtW pays after the interview, 100%, no cost share, no pre-interview call to the employer. Easy Read names a deafblind interpreter. CSI cannot be used for induction, a job review, a volunteer interview, or a JCP appointment.

The two routes can stack. EHRC’s line is the cleanest public statement: the employer pays for what is a reasonable adjustment; AtW can pick up communication assistance at interview where that extra cost is not reasonable for the employer. NDCS describes the messy lived version: the candidate can book and claim CSI, or tell the employer “I have AtW funding” and let the employer book. Nothing in the official stack says the candidate *must* use CSI first. Nothing says the employer may refuse to book until CSI is approved.

What is unpublished is the **default in a UK private-sector recruitment team**: whether HR treats CSI as the primary route and waits, or treats the interpreter as an employer-funded adjustment and invoices AtW afterwards, or does neither and hopes the candidate arrives with their own interpreter. No official series measures that split.

## 104.2 Notice

Three clocks get confused.

- **Statutory / public-page clock:** apply to CSI *before* the interview. No days figure on GOV.UK.
- **AtW decision clock:** staff guide — share the decision within **two working days**.
- **Interpreter-market clock:** BDF — two to four weeks.

Deaf Unity’s “minimum of 2 working days” is a fair reading of the decision clock, not a third official product. A candidate who is invited on Monday for a Wednesday interview can in principle get a CSI decision, but may not get an interpreter. BDF’s published answer to that clash is rearrange the interview, not proceed without support.

## 104.3 What happens when it fails

The documented GB pattern is **late or missing booking, then adjournment**, not a modern no-show series.

**Murphy v Sheffield Hallam University (DDA 1995).** Facts found by the tribunal and recited by the EAT: the application form stated he was profoundly deaf; the university still listed him for 16 December 1997 with no signer; the interview was put off to 18 December; the 16 December failure was held to be a failure to make a reasonable adjustment and attracted £2,500; on the 18th he attended with Dr Sheppard, “a highly competent amateur signer,” and was not appointed; the EAT would not disturb the finding that disability played no part in the appointment decision. Pre-Equality Act. Still the only named GB recruitment-interpreter judgment located this pass. Outcome for the candidate: **rescheduled, then unsuccessful**.

**Wynn (NI 2007)** remains the cancelled-then-rejected illustration. Other-UK-system.

No 2024–26 news report, charity case study or tribunal was found that names a private employer or UK agency, a job-interview date, and an interpreter who refused, arrived late, or did not attend. RNID 2026 and AWS0095 are about *in-work* AtW delay and interpreter pay. Locked Out / Still Ignored 80% is healthcare. Rimmer is Jobcentre. Limping Chicken (5 August 2024) argues that “the interpreter never turned up” in public services is more often “never booked.” That is industry comment, not a recruitment dataset.

So the honest cell is: **failure at interview stage is expected by practitioners and almost unpublished as named cases.** Do not fill it with healthcare percentages.

## 104.4 Agencies

BDF is the only public protocol-like text found: agencies must provide auxiliary aids including sign-language interpreters; ask who organises; the paying party is the duty-holder. REC’s public-facing material does not add a booking rule. Travers Smith’s careers page is one employer example of asking “either directly or via the recruitment agency.” Prompt 108 takes the statutory duty-holder question. This file only needs the fulfilment finding: **there is no published national agency booking SLA.**

## 104.5 What is unpublished or contested

- Default payer in private-sector HR practice.
- CSI hours cap (2 vs 3) on the live staff-guide paragraph.
- CSI decision-time performance — “within 2 working days” is the staff-guide target, not a published outturn series.
- Volume of CSI awards per year, or a Deaf/DeafBlind split of CSI.
- Named 2020s job-interview no-show.
- Whether an employer who refuses to book and tells the candidate “use AtW” has failed the s.20 duty — that is a fact-specific tribunal question, not a finding this file can print.

Do not paste the £69,260 cap, the 3,210 in-work BSL-interpreter customer count, the NAO 109-day wait, or the RNID 37% four-month figure into a CSI cell. Those are the in-work product.
