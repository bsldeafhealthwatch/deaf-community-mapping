## Headline

The statute does **not** treat the agency as a mere post-box.

**Three possible duty-holders, often at once.**

1. **The end employer** — s.39 (applicants) and s.20. Using an agency does not switch this off. The Employment Code’s recruitment chapter still applies. s.109(2): anything done by an agent *with the authority of the principal* is also done by the principal, whether or not the principal knew. The Code’s own example is a recruitment agency that only puts forward white candidates; the instructing firm “could be liable.”
2. **The employment service-provider** — s.55, with its own s.20 duty (anticipatory, except for “vocational services”). s.56(2)(d) and (e) name the two commercial shapes: a service for *finding people employment* (agency / headhunter) and a service for *supplying employers with people to do work* (employment business). The Explanatory Notes give the website example: “An agency advertises job vacancies on its website. It will need to have the website checked for accessibility and make reasonable changes to enable disabled people using a variety of access software to use it.”
3. **The individual agent** — s.110 personal liability where s.109 treats the act as also done by the principal.

s.111 (instructing, causing or inducing a contravention) is the extra hook if a client tells an agency not to put Deaf candidates forward. The REC Code of Professional Practice principle 4 tells members not to act on a discriminatory client instruction.

**What the statute does *not* do.** It does not print a default rule that “the agency books and the employer pays,” or the reverse. CSI (prompt 104) remains a candidate-applied DWP product; it does not name the agency as applicant. Schedule 8 knowledge rules still apply to each duty-holder separately.

**Named cases that look adjacent and are not this cell.**

- **Murphy v Sheffield Hallam** — employer, not an agency. Prompt 104.
- **Khan v Reed Talent Solutions 1801643/2024** — Reed as agency employer *after placement*; AtW kit delayed on assignment. Prompt 99. Not an interview-interpreter booking.
- **Rimmer v SSWP** — Jobcentre Plus is an employment service-provider (Code 11.59 lists it). That is the DWP/JCP batch, not a commercial agency interview.
- **Zandy v UK Language Solutions 1802264/2020** — interpreter dropped from an agency’s list; not a candidate requesting interview BSL.

**No named GB tribunal or EHRC finding** was located in which a commercial recruitment agency or a named ATS/job board was held to have failed to arrange, or refused to arrange, an interview BSL or tactile interpreter for a Deaf or DeafBlind *candidate*.

**REC / platform policies.** REC publishes Disability Confident videos and a Code principle against acting on discriminatory instructions. No public REC protocol was found that allocates booking or cost of interview BSL. BDF has a member-only “Working with recruitment agencies” note — contents not opened. Indeed’s public accessibility page is WCAG / ADA / AODA / European Accessibility Act language and lists sign-language interpreters for *events*, not a UK Equality Act booking route for a job interview. Home Office Careers is the employer, not an ATS vendor.

The thin finding the batch expected is real: the duty-holder map is in the Act; the money-and-booking map between agency, platform and employer is unpublished.

## 108.1 How the duties sit together

Do not write “the agency is responsible” or “the employer is responsible” as if those were alternatives. On the face of the Act they stack.

- If the agency runs the advert, the portal, the sift or the first interview, s.55 + s.20 attach to *that service*. An inaccessible form, a phone-only booking line, or a refusal to take an interpreter request for *its* interview is the agency’s problem first.
- If the agency is acting with the employer’s authority to recruit, s.109(2) can make the same act the employer’s problem as well.
- If the employer runs the interview after the agency has only sourced CVs, the s.39 / s.20 duty on the employer is the one prompt 103 already mapped; the agency may still have a residual s.55 duty in how it passed the request on.

s.41 is the wrong chapter until someone is *supplied to do the work*. Khan is that later chapter.

CSI does not resolve the stack. The candidate can apply. That does not tell the agency it may sit on its hands, and it does not tell the employer the agency has taken the duty away.

## 108.2 What is actually unpublished

The batch predicted this cell would be thin. It is.

- No public REC, CIPD or major-agency SLA that says “we book, client pays” or “candidate uses CSI, we only pass the date.”
- No public Workday / Oracle / SAP / iTrent configuration note that the vendor, not the client, is the s.55 duty-holder for the application form.
- No tribunal ratio on an agency interview-interpreter no-show.
- BDF’s agency chapter exists and is gated.

What can be said without invention: the Code already tells employers that outsourced recruitment is still theirs to supervise, and it already tells agencies their website is an adjustment problem. The missing document is the one that names the booker and the payer when a Deaf or DeafBlind candidate is in the middle.

## 108.3 Hard-don’t reminder

Do not describe a named living agency, job board or ATS as unlawful, discriminatory or in breach. The finding is absence of a published allocation rule and absence of a named interview-interpreter judgment against an agency — not a finding that every agency interview is non-compliant.
