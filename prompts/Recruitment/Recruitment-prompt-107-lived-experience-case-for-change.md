## Headline

The published first-person stack for *this* cell is thin and interview-heavy. Application-form and DeafBlind-employer-interview testimony is almost absent.

What can be named without invention:

- **Murphy v Sheffield Hallam University** (prompt 104). Profoundly deaf applicant flagged the need on the form. Interpreter not considered for two weeks, sought eight days before the interview, not there on the day. Pressure to continue by lip-reading or writing. He refused after an attempt; interview adjourned a week; university asked him to bring his own interpreter at its expense. Tribunal: late and inadequate; that was not a reasonable attitude given the university’s resources. He was not appointed. The judgment is the first-person record as well as the legal finding. Do not add colour the judgment does not contain.
- **Limping Chicken Insight, 2 October 2024**, “Attending job interviews as a deaf person without speech is challenging.” First-person: access needs stated; company assured a remote-interview interpreter; interpreter and candidate were not a match (newer interpreter, stronger productive BSL than receptive, clunky voiceover). Author felt poorly represented. Deaf charity supported a second interview with a different interpreter; feedback went to the interpreting agency. The piece is signed Insight, not a tribunal. It is the only recent named first-person that is an *employer* interview rather than a Jobcentre appointment.
- **Rimmer judgment, para 221** (keep in its lane). The tribunal recorded that ordinary recruitment is itself a barrier for him — “an example is being offered a telephone interview by an employer” — and that applications made on his behalf since 2015 had failed. That sentence is about the labour market around JCP, not a named employer interview. Do not move the £50,000 award into this cell.

**DeafBlind employer-interview first-person: no reliable figure found.** Deafblind UK’s 27 January 2026 page is how-to advice (Lawrencia Nelson), not a failed-interview account. James Clarke’s March 2024 Limping Chicken piece is a Jobcentre Plus Level-3 “interpreter,” not an employer recruitment interview — prompts 27–33.

**What is not this cell.** AWS0095 and the RNID/DeafATW Dec 2025–Jan 2026 survey (n=358) are Access to Work *in work*. Francesca Bussey and Amanda Casson-Webb are other domains. Sign Solutions / NDCS / Deaf Action pages are advice, not testimony. Jenny Koehring 2025 is a psychometric-testing research call, not a recruitment-failure story.

**Policy ask that follows from 103–108, not from a survey.** The documentary layer already supplies the change-request: keep the process-adjustment question inside s.60(6)(a) and off the health screen; treat CSI as a candidate fallback, not as proof the employer has no s.20 duty; write down who books and who pays when an agency or ATS sits in the middle; do not treat a missing or mismatched interpreter as the candidate’s problem to repair on the day. That is a specification, not a campaign slogan. No official body has adopted it as a recruitment product.

Do not print a national “X% of Deaf interviews have no interpreter.” That number was not found.

## 107.1 What the two usable stories actually show

Murphy is booking failure plus a bad fallback (lip-read / write / bring your own). The 2024 Limping Chicken Insight is booking *success* plus a quality failure (wrong interpreter for a voiceless candidate on a remote interview). Those are different failure modes. 104 already mapped the first. 108’s empty agency cell is the second, one step upstream — the interpreting agency, not the recruitment agency, took the feedback.

Neither story is a DeafBlind tactile-interpreter no-show. Do not write as if they were.

Rimmer’s telephone-interview sentence is the only published description of the *application-channel* problem (phone-only first contact). It sits in a JCP judgment. Treat it as colour on the channel, not as an employer finding.

## 107.2 What the advice pages quietly assume

NDCS and Deaf Action both tell the candidate to drive CSI and, in the Edinburgh PDF, to treat booking as “your responsibility” unless the employer has a budget. That is how the system is *used*. It is not how s.20 allocates the duty (103–104). A write-up that repeats the advice pages as if they were the law will collapse the batch. Quote them as practice, then point back to the statute.

## 107.3 Case for change, kept inside the evidence

The change this batch can honestly support is operational, not rhetorical:

1. Separate the s.60(6)(a) process-adjustment question from any health questionnaire (105; Elite Careplus is the enforcement warning).
2. Name the booker and the payer in the invitation, including when an agency or ATS is in the chain (108 found no public protocol).
3. Treat CSI as a two-working-day candidate fallback, not as the employer’s discharge of s.20 (104).
4. Build a fallback other than “lip-read or come back next week” when the booked interpreter is absent or mismatched (Murphy; 2024 Insight).
5. Stop offering a telephone-only first interview to a BSL-using candidate as if that were a neutral channel (Rimmer para 221, used only for that point).

No select-committee slot, no regulator citation of a volunteer scorecard, and no named DeafBlind employer-interview inquiry was found that would turn this list into an adopted product. Locked Out’s adult neurodevelopmental pathway is a different ask. WPC’s two-week written-reasons recommendation is an *in-post* SLA ask (101), not a recruitment clock.
