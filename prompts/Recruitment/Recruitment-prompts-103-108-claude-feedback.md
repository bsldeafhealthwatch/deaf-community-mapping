## Verdict for any public page

**Curate first. Draft almost nothing.**

The one sentence a page is allowed to lead with, if any:

> An employer (and a recruitment agency) must adjust the recruitment process for a disabled applicant — including a BSL or deafblind interpreter at interview — unless that step is not reasonable on the facts. Access to Work Communication Support at Interview is a separate, candidate-applied, 100% funded fast-track. It does not move the Equality Act duty. General health questions before a job offer are banned except for named exceptions; asking whether someone needs an adjustment *for this interview* is one of those exceptions.

## Don't list (print near any applying-for-a-job / interview page)

- Do not print that a named living employer, agency, job board or ATS is unlawful, discriminatory or in breach without a named statute, regulation, tribunal/court judgment or EHRC finding on those facts.
- Do not print CSI as a transfer of the s.20 duty. Two routes. Employer (and agency under s.55) still carry the adjustment duty. CSI is 100% funded, candidate-applied, paid after.
- Do not print the in-work AtW cap (£69,260), NAO 109 days, PAC 106 days, or RNID 2026 “37% waited four months” as CSI figures. Wrong product.
- Do not print Deaf Unity’s “minimum of 2 working days” as a statutory CSI notice period. Staff guide = decision shared within 2 working days. Public page = apply *before* the interview. Interpreter market = BDF 2–4 weeks. Three clocks.
- Do not print a hard CSI hours cap in public copy until the live staff-guide paragraph is opened. Historic v24 / Deaf Umbrella ≈ two hours; one 2026 HTML summary said up to three. Safe line: “usually a short interview slot, longer if evidenced.”
- Do not print Still Ignored / Locked Out **80%** (booked communication professional failed to attend a *healthcare* appointment) as a job-interview rate.
- Do not print a national “X% of Deaf interviews have no interpreter.” No such official series.
- Do not print Murphy v Sheffield Hallam as an Equality Act 2010 case. It is DDA 1995, ET 1998 / EAT 11 Jan 2000, **£2,500** for the 16 Dec 1997 no-signer; rearranged interview went ahead with an amateur signer; he was not appointed; EAT dismissed the appointment limb. Reschedule pattern, not a 2020s no-show series.
- Do not print Wynn v Multipulse as a GB authority. Northern Ireland, 2007.
- Do not print the EEOC “employer cancelled the interview because it assumed a full-time interpreter” example as a GB judgment. International (prompt 106). Closest *asked* GB fact pattern was not found.
- Do not print Shahbakhshi v Melo 2025 BCHRT 87 ($15,000) as English injury-to-feelings. British Columbia.
- Do not print Australia EAF Auslan Level 2 (uncapped, claimable **up to 5 working days after** the interview) as Access to Work. Opposite window to CSI.
- Do not print Ireland’s Work and Access ISL grant (1 hour prep + 3 hours interview, **private sector only**, public sector employer pays) as the UK split.
- Do not print Ontario AODA’s “notify twice” duty as Equality Act law.
- Do not print Elite Careplus as a Deaf or BSL case. Care-agency general health screen (chest, depression, skin, medication, >1 week off), 21 Nov 2018–20 Jun 2019, unlawful-act notice, questions removed. HIV/NAT-shaped referral.
- Do not print “asking if you are disabled is always unlawful.” s.60(6) exceptions are real. Process-adjustment question is lawful. Monitoring, held separately, is lawful. Occupational requirement to *be* disabled is lawful if s.60(8) is met — Code example is a **Deafblind project worker**, not every role.
- Do not print a combined “disability / health / adjustments” box on an application form as automatically lawful. Elite Careplus is what that design looks like when it is a general screen.
- Do not print CIPD HR-inform medical questionnaires as pre-offer tools. Product page labels them post-offer / in-post.
- Do not recycle Rimmer (£50,000, JCP) or James Clarke (JCP Level-3 “interpreter”) as employer-recruitment testimony. Prompts 27–33.
- Do not recycle Khan v Reed 1801643/2024 as an interview-interpreter case. That is AtW kit *after placement* (prompt 99).
- Do not recycle AWS0095, RNID 2026 AtW quotes, Bussey, Casson-Webb, Elliott or Thomas into this cell.
- Do not generalise the October 2024 Limping Chicken Insight (remote-interview interpreter mismatch, second interview with charity help) into a national quality-failure rate.
- Do not print Deaf Action’s employability line (“this is your responsibility to book via AtW”) as the statutory allocation. Practice, not s.20.
- Do not print “the agency is only a post-box.” s.55 + s.109(2) + s.110 + s.111 stack. Absence of a REC booking protocol is not a finding that every agency interview is non-compliant.
- Do not print Indeed / Workday / Oracle / iTrent as in breach. No public Equality Act booking rule and no named judgment was found.
- Do not print a distinct national DeafBlind *recruitment protocol*. Easy Read CSI names a deafblind interpreter. No asked jurisdiction published tactile / Protactile / deafblind-manual interview rules as a separate legal object (106).
- Do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. Follow money and fulfilment — who obtains the quote, who applies to CSI, who books into the 2–4 week market, who invoices whom after the interview.

## What to do with this finding

| Scenario | Action |
|---|---|
| Someone asks “who pays for my interview interpreter?” | “Two routes. The employer (and the agency, if you applied through one) have an Equality Act duty to adjust the process. Separately, you can apply to Access to Work CSI before the interview — 100% funded, decision aimed at 2 working days, paid after. CSI does not mean the employer can wait.” Link GOV.UK CSI + ACAS interviewing page. |
| Someone asks “do I have to tick disabled on the form?” | “You do not have to disclose deafness to be considered. You can ask for an adjustment *for this interview or test* — that question is allowed. A general health questionnaire before an offer is not, except for named exceptions.” Link EHRC applicant s.60 guide. |
| Someone was asked a long medical list before an offer | Point at EHRC s.60 applicant guide + the Elite Careplus finding as “this is the kind of form the Commission has already investigated.” Do not tell them the living employer is in breach. EHRC still hosts a report form for s.60. Individual remedy for the *question itself* is the Commission, not a tribunal; conduct *in reliance on* the answer can go to a tribunal (s.60(3)–(5)). |
| Someone wants a letter / template | Use the existing template-batch tone (46–51). Ask: date of invitation, what was requested, who was told, whether CSI was applied, whether the interview went ahead without support. Do not cite Murphy as current EA 2010 authority. Point at ACAS early conciliation if they are already past the interview and considering a claim (109–114 owns the clock). |
| Someone wants a new domain page | No. Stub under Welfare & Employment. Link CSI, ACAS, EHRC s.60, NDCS. |
| Someone wants to quote Rimmer or Clarke | Don’t, on an *employer interview* page. Different actor. |
| Someone wants to say “no other country funds this” | Don’t. Australia EAF Level 2 and Ireland Work and Access do. Label international. |

## Cross-batch rules

- **Distinct from 20–26 / 27–33** because those are benefits and Jobcentre desks. CSI is not a JCP appointment. Rimmer and Clarke stay there.
- **Distinct from 52–54** because those are the in-work AtW cap, freeze and processing clock. CSI has no cost-share and a two-working-day decision aim.
- **Distinct from 92–96** because OH starts after an offer (or a pool). A pre-offer OH questionnaire is a s.60 problem (105), not an OH-recommendation problem.
- **Distinct from 97–102** because an internal SLA assumes the person already has the job. Interview booking is not an in-post ticket.
- **Distinct from 109–114** because grievance and the three-month-less-one-day clock start after the door is shut. This batch stops at the door.
- **Distinct from 115–120** (DeafBlind in-post adjustments). Easy Read CSI naming a deafblind interpreter is the recruitment-stage mention; tactile workplace navigation is the later batch.
- Shared facts that must not be reprinted as if this batch discovered them: AtW cap and 3,210 BSL-interpreter customers (20–26 / 52–54); Sch. 8 / s.41 contract-worker split after placement (97–99); Elite Careplus is the s.60 finding, not a new healthcare AIS finding.
