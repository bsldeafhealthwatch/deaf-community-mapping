# Prompt 106 — Do other jurisdictions publish a process or funding route for interview interpreters, or a distinct DeafBlind recruitment protocol?

**Master index prompt 106.** International-context file in the Recruitment / hiring-stage batch (prompts 103–108). Compiled 10 September 2026. **What this prompt is for.** Context only. Establish whether the USA, Canada, Australia or EU member states publish a standard, regulator guide or case that sets a *process or funding route* for interview-stage interpreters or accessible formats, and whether any of them documents a *distinct* DeafBlind recruitment process. **None of this fills a UK data cell.** Do not paste an EEOC example into a s.20 cell. ## Headline

Every jurisdiction asked has the same legal shape as the UK: the employer (or equivalent duty-holder) must adjust the recruitment process, including an interview interpreter or an accessible format, unless a hardship / unjustifiable-hardship / disproportionate-burden defence applies. None of them publishes a days-SLA that would transfer. What differs is **who writes the cheque**.

- **United States.** ADA: employer pays, absent undue hardship. EEOC’s worked example is the fact pattern prompt 103 flagged as missing in GB case law — employer *cancels* the interview because it assumes a full-time interpreter will follow. That is an ADA violation. Process accommodation and job accommodation are assessed separately. Pre-offer disability questions are restricted in a way that is cousin to s.60, not a copy. No federal interview-interpreter *grant*. Braille, large print and readers sit in the same applicant-accommodation list as sign-language interpreters. No DeafBlind-specific recruitment protocol found.

- **Canada.** Duty to accommodate starts at first contact. **Shahbakhshi v Melo, 2025 BCHRT 87**: deaf applicant arrived *with* an interpreter; salon owner refused to proceed because she “could not communicate with clients”; $15,000 injury-to-dignity. Ontario’s AODA adds a *notification* duty — tell applicants that accommodations are available — that the Equality Act does not copy. Canadian Hearing Services’ Ontario Interpreter Services has historically absorbed the cost of a job-interview interpreter if the employer will not. Accessibility Standards Canada CAN-ASC-1.1 (employment, rev. 2025) tells federally-oriented organisations to permit a sign-language interpreter at interview without asking the nature of the disability. No national CSI-equivalent grant.

- **Australia.** Disability Discrimination Act 1992 plus JobAccess guidance: employer must adjust the interview. **Employment Assistance Fund Auslan Level 2** is the closest published analogue to AtW CSI: job interviews and related activities, **no funding cap**, no limit on the number of interviews, applications accepted **up to five business days after** the interview, travel not separately reimbursed at Level 2. Level 1 (in-work) is capped; Level 2 is not. That post-interview claim window is the opposite of AtW CSI’s “apply before.” Still the employer’s DDA duty.

- **EU / member states.** Directive 2000/78/EC Art. 5 reasonable accommodation covers access to employment, which the Commission’s 2024 guidelines read as starting at the advert and the interview. No EU-wide interview-interpreter fund. Member-state money differs. **Ireland Work and Access Communication Support/ISL Grant** (private sector only): 1 hour familiarisation + up to 3 hours for the interview, no cap on number of interviews, public-sector interviews stay with the employer. Germany’s Integrationsamt is the in-work / equipment comparator, not unpacked here. EFHOH’s 2025 18-country survey called recruitment the weakest stage; sign-language interpretation was named as a possible recruitment accommodation in half the country responses.

**DeafBlind as a distinct recruitment process:** no asked jurisdiction published a national protocol that treats tactile / Protactile / deafblind-manual interpretation as a separate legal object from “interpreter or accessible format.” Readers, braille and “other deaf interpreter” appear in lists. That is the same gap as the UK Employment Code.

Nothing here is a UK finding.

## 106.1 Process and funding, jurisdiction by jurisdiction

The legal question is settled in the same direction everywhere asked: the interview is inside the duty. The money question is not.

**Employer-pays model (US, and the default in Canada and the EU).** The EEOC text is the cleanest foreign statement of the 103 gate: do not treat a request for an interview interpreter as proof the job is impossible. Provide the interview interpreter (in person or VRI), then ask about the job. There is no federal cheque. Cost can feed an undue-hardship defence; it does not, on the EEOC’s own example, justify cancelling the interview.

**State-grant model sitting beside the employer duty (Australia, Ireland — and the UK’s CSI).** Australia’s EAF Level 2 is the nearest published cousin: uncapped interview interpreting, claimable after the event. Ireland’s grant is capped in *hours* (1 + 3) not in pounds, private-sector only. The UK product (prompt 104) is closer to Ireland on “apply before / short decision” and closer to Australia on “no cost share / no interview cap.” None of those design choices is required by the ADA or by Directive 2000/78.

**Notification model (Ontario AODA).** Tell applicants, twice, that accommodations exist. That is a process standard the Equality Act does not enact. ACAS *recommends* asking; AODA *requires* notifying. Different legal object.

**Provider-of-last-resort model (historic Ontario OIS).** A Deaf organisation pays the interview interpreter when the employer will not. Useful as a comparator for who actually writes the cheque when the duty-holder stalls. Confirm live OIS rules before using it in anything public; the published CHS booklet may lag the contract.

## 106.2 DeafBlind

The prompt asked whether any jurisdiction documents a *distinct* recruitment process for DeafBlind candidates. The finding is absence.

What exists internationally is the same stack the UK already has: braille and large print as application-format examples (EEOC); “reader or interpreter” as an auxiliary aid; Ireland’s “other deaf interpreter” in a grant list. No asked government published a tactile-signing / Protactile / deafblind-manual interview protocol with a booking route and a payer. Do not invent one by analogy with ASL or Auslan.

## 106.3 What does not transfer

- ADA “undue hardship” is not Equality Act “reasonableness.”
- AODA notification is not s.60 or s.20.
- EAF Level 2’s five-day *retrospective* window is the opposite of AtW CSI’s “apply before.”
- Shahbakhshi’s $15,000 is a BC dignity award, not an English injury-to-feelings band.
- Ireland’s public-sector-pays / private-sector-grant split is not how CSI works.
- CAN-ASC-1.1 binds the organisations in its scope, not every Canadian private employer.

Use this file to stop a UK write-up saying “no other country funds interview interpreters” (Australia and Ireland do) or “the ADA requires a days-clock” (it does not).
