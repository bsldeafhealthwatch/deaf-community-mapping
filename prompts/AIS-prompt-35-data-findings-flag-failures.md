# Prompt 35 — Data findings: how often the flag fails

**Master index prompt 35.** Compiled 8 September 2026 from the AIS working brief (prompts 34–37). Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. Cream / MODELLED labels are estimates, not measured series.

**Anchor case.** A BSL Deaf patient attends a planned, non-emergency hospital follow-up. The trust system does not hold (or does not act on) a BSL flag. The patient is telephoned instead of texted, emailed or relayed. No in-person BSL interpreter is booked. A video relay link is offered instead.

This prompt looks for quantified or documented evidence of that pattern: (a) contacted by phone rather than an accessible method, and/or (b) no in-person BSL interpreter booked for a routine appointment, including VRI offered instead of face-to-face.

---

## Headline

There is **no published national rate** for “correct contact method used” or “interpreter booked as requested” against AIS. NHS England does not run a mandatory AIS out-turn collection. What exists is survey proxy, local Healthwatch, FOI fill-rates, and casework. Flag that clearly: the data is thin.

---

## 35.1 National or regional statistics on the two exact failures

Best current quantified proxy is the SignHealth / RNID *Still Ignored* survey.

- Fieldwork: 13 November–26 December 2024.
- England sample: **n = 1,114** adults who are deaf or have hearing loss.
- Staff poll: Censuswide for RNID, **n = 404** patient-facing NHS staff, 9–20 December 2024.

### Figures that map onto the anchor case

| Measure | Figure | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Never asked about information / communication needs when accessing NHS care | **70%** | AIS step 1 failure |
| Need a communication support professional at appointments | 19% of sample | Of those, 58% need a BSL interpreter |
| Support always provided when needed | **7%** | 27% rarely; 36% never |
| Sign-language users who have had a booked professional fail to attend | **80%** | Despite being told one was booked. SL-user n = 121 |
| NHS staff who know how to book communication support | 78% | Staff knowledge ≠ patient receipt. 32% of staff unaware of AIS |
| Staff always made aware of communication needs before the meeting | **27%** | Flag / referral alert working only for a minority of staff encounters |
| Healthcare more accessible now than 10 years ago (pre-AIS) | 16% agree | Patient judgement of the Standard’s decade |
| VRS suitable in place of an in-person interpreter (SL users asked) | 24% agree / 54% disagree | Small sub-sample n = 37. 59% say VRS helps in emergency / short notice |

*Locked Out* (BSL Advisory Board, GOV.UK, 27 November 2025) repeats the 80% no-show figure and cites joint charity research that **67% of deaf people had no accessible method of contacting their GP**.

## 35.2 Trust-level FOI — missed bookings and wrong contact method

FOI responses on WhatDoTheyKnow typically answer “how many BSL jobs were requested / filled / unfilled”, **not** “how many times a flagged patient was telephoned” or “how many planned appointments went ahead on VRI against a recorded in-person need”. Those last two fields are almost never in a reportable system.

### Published examples (fill-rate, not AIS incident count)

- **Warrington and Halton Teaching Hospitals FT (2025 FOI).** Deafness Resource Centre BSL 251 requests, fill rate 96.54%, 9 unfilled, 2 interpreter DNA, one complaint about BSL availability. LanguageLine 25 BSL jobs. Useful as a fill-rate; silent on contact-method failure.
- **An unnamed 2024/25 trust FOI in the same wave.** 120 BSL / VI / DeafBlind appointments arranged, 8 unmet, 7 last-minute patient cancellations. Again a fill-rate, not an AIS incident count.
- **Northern Care Alliance historic FOI (SRFT era).** Action on Hearing Loss 1,419 BSL requests 2014–17 with cancellation totals but no fulfil split; Language Empire 1,732 requests 2017–19, 1,503 fulfilled, 66 supplier-unable-to-fulfil. Trust said it did not record AIS consent/procedure breaches as structured data.

### SWL-specific

The project already holds the Oct 2024–Jun 2025 BSL request gold standard of **878** across six boroughs. That series is **demand, not AIS-failure**. Healthwatch Kingston’s AIS fieldwork is the local qualitative counterpart: flag overload in EMIS; new SWL GP BSL provider from late 2024 (DAL / Sign Solutions) flagged as needing evaluation against user-defined measures. South West London ICB rolled out a new reasonable-adjustments flagging system in December 2024; impact not independently evaluated in this pass.

**Sources**

- Interpretation and Translation Service Contract Details — WhatDoTheyKnow request to Warrington and Halton, 2025. <https://www.whatdotheyknow.com/request_event/18905433>
- Healthwatch Kingston AIS report summary. Notes EMIS flag overload and Dec 2024 SWL ICB reasonable-adjustments flag rollout. <https://healthwatchkingston.org.uk/sites/healthwatchkingston.org.uk/files/AIS%20report%20summary.pdf>

## 35.3 Emergency versus planned — what NHS bodies actually say

**England AIS implementation guidance.** VRI/VRS particularly useful in urgent or emergency care; not a total replacement; for routine care offer a choice. That is the closest official England distinction. It is **guidance, not a must**.

**Scotland is explicit.** Public Health Scotland *Interpreting, Communication Support and Translation National Policy*:

- Face-to-face BSL is “the preferred and main method”.
- Video interpreting may be used in an emergency, for short routine appointments (bloods, immunisations) where the BSL user has agreed in advance, or while waiting for a face-to-face interpreter.
- A face-to-face interpreter should still be sought as soon as possible in emergencies even when online is available.
- Contact Scotland BSL is for making contact, not for clinical conversations.

No England trust FOI located in this pass that publishes a written rule “VRI is acceptable for planned outpatients”. Local interpreting policies (for example Avon and Wiltshire Mental Health) typically say face-to-face is best practice in mental health, with telephone/video for triage, booking confirmation or while waiting for F2F.

**Sources**

- AIS implementation guidance — remote access — NHS England long-read, published 30 June 2025. <https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/accessible-information-standard-implementation-guidance/>
- Interpreting, Communication Support and Translation National Policy — Public Health Scotland / NHS Scotland, sections 4.3.3–4.3.5. <https://publichealthscotland.scot/media/3059/interpreting-communication-support-and-translation-national-policy.pdf>

## 35.4 PHSO / CQC cases that match “phoned instead of contacted accessibly”

No PHSO investigation was found whose published summary is only “the trust rang a BSL user and they missed a routine outpatient slot”. The published PHSO Deaf cases cluster on missing interpreters **at the appointment itself**, or on using family/children as interpreters — the second half of the anchor case rather than the outbound-phone half.

- **PHSO summary 198, June 2014** — “Deaf patient denied access to BSL interpreter at GP practice” (Mrs E). Three years without a practice interpreter after PCT funding was withdrawn. Remedy £3,000 plus action plan. Pre-dates AIS coming into force (August 2016) but is the template GP-booking failure.
- **PHSO “Samantha”, reported by BBC 19 August 2026** — wrong vaccine at a GP surgery because no BSL interpreter and a BSL explainer video was not shown. Remedy apology + £450; practice then signed an interpreting contract. Northamptonshire ICB apology. PHSO accompanying figure: since April 2020, 623 reasonable-adjustment investigations, 496 upheld or partly upheld — **not BSL-only**.
- **PHSO Alan Graham / UHB, 11 February 2026** — inpatient, not outpatient, but the same “need known, interpreter not produced” pattern. Interpreters on 3 of 11 weeks.
- **Healthwatch England blog (2023–24 trail)** — the outbound-phone half, anonymised: “Both GP and hospitals insist on phoning me – even though I have made it clear I don’t use a phone as I am profoundly deaf. I have asked them to either email or text me instead. I know it’s on my records, but it’s ignored.” Aggregated / anonymised, not a PHSO finding.

**Sources**

- Deaf patient denied access to BSL interpreter at GP practice — PHSO case summary 198, June 2014. <https://www.ombudsman.org.uk/about-us/our-casework/how-our-casework-makes-difference/case-summaries/198>
- Deaf patient given wrong jab due to sign language error — BBC News, 19 August 2026, reporting PHSO “Samantha”. <https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cn8nmm51l49o>
- Teenager had to tell his Deaf mother that her father might die — PHSO, 11 February 2026, Alan Graham / UHB. <https://www.ombudsman.org.uk/news-and-blog/news/teenager-had-tell-his-deaf-mother-her-father-might-die-after-hospital-failed>
- Deaf people’s experiences — Healthwatch, 10 April 2024 (stories also dated 2023). <https://www.healthwatch.co.uk/blog/2024-04-10/deaf-and-hard-hearing-peoples-healthcare-experiences>

## What is thin, unpublished, or only anecdotal

- No national or ICB-published rate for “correct contact method used”.
- No national or ICB-published rate for “interpreter booked as requested”.
- No SWL trust AIS self-assessment ratings located as published documents in this pass.
- No FOI series that counts “wrong outbound contact method” as an incident type.
- Healthwatch phone-call testimony is real and repeated, but anonymised / aggregated — do not present it as a named case.
- Still Ignored VRS-vs-F2F table is a **small sub-sample (n = 37)** — do not treat 24%/54% as a national preference poll.
