# Prompt 154 — Does Access to Work, or any adjacent DWP / local-authority fund, pay for the employee's caring labour toward other disabled household members?

**Master index prompt 154.** Fifth file in the Household-Leave batch (prompts 150–156). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Prompt 152 said this file must not treat Access to Work as a household-leave fund. This file tests that instruction against the live staff guide and the products that are commonly confused with a bridge: Carer’s Allowance, Carer’s Credit, a Care Act carer’s assessment, Carer’s Leave, s.57A, an AtW Support Worker for the *employee’s own* job, Universal Credit childcare, and short-breaks / direct payments for the person who needs care. ## Headline

**There is no bridging provision.** Access to Work pays additional costs that arise from the *customer’s own* disability, illness or health condition at work. It does not pay for the customer to care for a disabled partner or an EHCP child. The staff-guide Support Worker category labelled “Carer” is help for the customer’s own physical mobility or toilet use **at work**, starting “at the front door.” A support worker “cannot give nursing care or help get the customer up and ready in the morning.” That sentence faces the wrong way for this household: it stops AtW becoming personal care for the employee, and it has nothing to say about care the employee gives to someone else.

The adjacent products are separate systems:

| Product | What it actually funds | Whose need? | Employment-retention? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Access to Work | Extra workplace costs of the customer’s own disability | The employee as disabled worker | Yes, for *their* job |
| AtW “Carer” SW | Mobility / toilet support for the customer at work | The employee | Yes, for *their* body at work |
| Carer’s Allowance | £86.45 a week (2026/27) if 35+ hours’ care and net earnings ≤ £204 from 6 Apr 2026 | The carer’s time | **No.** Cash benefit with a cliff-edge earnings limit that *constrains* paid work |
| Carer’s Credit | Class 3 NI credits toward State Pension | The carer’s NI record | **No.** Not cash, not workplace |
| Care Act s.10 carer’s assessment | Assessment of the carer’s needs; support *may* include replacement care so the carer can work. Duty to have regard to whether the carer works or wishes to work | Adult carer of an adult | Closest statutory “have regard to work” sentence. Not a workplace grant. Provision is local and means-tested. Parent-carers of disabled children sit under Children Act assessments, not s.10 |
| Carer’s Leave / s.57A / parental leave / FW request | Time off or a change of pattern (150) | The employee as carer | Time, not money |
| UC childcare / 30 hours | Help with registered childcare costs while working | The child | Childcare-for-work, not disability-household care |
| Direct payments / short breaks | Care or respite for the person who needs it | The dependant / family as a care unit | May *indirectly* free the employee to work. Not an AtW-style workplace grant in the employee’s name |
| Employment Allowance (care worker) | Employer NI relief if you employ a care-and-support worker from your own funds | Whoever employs the worker | Tax admin, not DWP AtW |

**No named first-person** was found this pass of a Deaf or BSL-using employee who is also carer of a disabled partner or EHCP child describing AtW, Carer’s Allowance, or a carer’s assessment as the thing that kept them in post. Contact and Carers UK workplace stories remain single-axis parent-carer or all-carer (152). The absence of a bridge is the finding.

A government *Employment rights for unpaid carers review* (terms of reference published 19 November 2025) is looking at how unpaid carer’s leave is working and at options for paid carer’s leave. That is policy in motion, not a live fund.

**Hard-don’t for anything public.** Do not print that AtW covers household caring. Do not reprint the cap or BSL customer counts here. Do not print that DWP should build the bridge. Do not describe Carer’s Allowance as an employment-retention product — the earnings limit is the opposite mechanism.

## 154.1 Why the confusion keeps happening

Four labels sound like they might pay for this household:

1. **AtW “Carer.”** Official word. Means a person who helps the disabled employee use the toilet at work.
2. **Carer’s Allowance.** Official word. Means £86.45 a week if you care 35 hours and keep net pay under £204. The earnings limit is how the benefit *punishes* remaining in better-paid work, not how it funds it.
3. **Carer’s assessment.** Official word. Means the council must look at whether you work. What you then get is local, often replacement care for the *other* person, and only after an eligibility test.
4. **Carer’s Leave.** Official word. Means one unpaid week (150).

None of them is a workplace grant, in the employee’s name, for the fact that the partner and the child are also disabled.

## 154.2 What 155–156 should not invent

Prompt 155 may find a foreign workplace-care grant. Do not paste it into a UK AtW cell. Prompt 156 may find a first-person who cobbled CA + holiday + a council short break + an employer’s goodwill. That cobble is not a bridge product. Name the pieces.
