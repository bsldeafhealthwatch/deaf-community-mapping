# Prompt 151 — What must a Deaf or BSL-first employee produce and say to use the five rights, and does any guidance build the process for them?

**Master index prompt 151.** Second file in the Household-Leave batch (prompts 150–156). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Take the five unstacked rights from prompt 150 and ask a different question: what the employee is actually expected to *produce and communicate* at each stage (written request, notice, evidence, consultation or appeal meeting), and whether ACAS, GOV.UK or the EHRC Employment materials address how a BSL-first or Deaf employee is meant to complete those steps. The object is the **process burden**, not whether the underlying entitlement was refused. Do not treat a written form, an email, or English captions as discharging a documented interpreter request for a negotiation meeting. ## Headline

The statutory toolkit is **written-English and, where a meeting exists, spoken-or-video English**. None of the ACAS model letters, the ACAS Code of Practice on requests for flexible working (in force 6 April 2024), the ACAS carer’s-leave or parental-leave pages, or GOV.UK’s employee-facing leave guides tells a BSL-first employee how to file the request, how to give short-notice s.57A notice, or how the consultation meeting is interpreted. The closest published sentences sit on *other* pages: ACAS’s general reasonable-adjustments page names “a sign language interpreter” as an example auxiliary aid (last updated 30 January 2025); EHRC’s hybrid-working employer guidance tells the employer to ask, before an adjustments conversation, whether the worker needs a BSL interpreter (page live this pass). Those sentences are not joined to the leave-and-flexible-working templates.

The burden is not the same on all five rights:

- **Flexible working** is the heavy process. The request **must be in writing** and must contain four statutory particulars. Unless the employer grants it in full, there **must** be a consultation meeting (in person, video, or, only if those are impossible, telephone). The Code’s companion is a fellow worker or trade-union representative — **not** an interpreter. There is no statutory right of accompaniment and no statutory appeal; both are “good practice.” Reason for the change is optional on the ACAS template. No evidence of a dependant’s condition or EHCP is required. The Code (para 11) separates the Equality Act reasonable-adjustment duty from the flexible-working duty; it contemplates flexible working *as* an adjustment for the employee’s own disability, not an interpreter *for the consultation meeting about caring leave*.
- **Carer’s leave** is the lightest planned process. Notice need not be in writing. Evidence of the dependant’s care needs **must not** be required before grant (Regulations 2024 and GOV.UK). No meeting is required. The employee must still communicate that the absence is carer’s leave and the days wanted, inside a short notice clock (3 days for a day; twice the length of the leave if longer).
- **Unpaid parental leave** sits in the middle. 21 days’ notice of exact start and finish dates. Proof of parental responsibility (birth / adoption / parental-order / guardianship document) is what ACAS now lists. The default scheme’s day-by-day exception still turns, in MAPLE Sch. 2 para. 7, on the child being entitled to **disability living allowance**; ACAS’s parental-leave page, last updated **7 April 2026**, has widened the employee-facing sentence to “Disability Living Allowance [or] Personal Independence Payment.” Neither text names an EHCP. No ACAS model letter exists for ordinary parental leave (shared parental leave has a template pack; ordinary parental leave does not).
- **s.57A time off for dependants** is oral-first by design: tell the employer the reason and the expected length as soon as reasonably practicable. No prescribed channel (phone, text, email, Relay, VRS). No evidence. No meeting. In an emergency that is the point — and it is also the point at which a BSL-first employee whose workplace still expects a voice call is left without a published route.
- **Article 8** has no application form, notice clock, or meeting. It is not a process the employee operates.

**No named UK first-person, charity case study, or tribunal** was found this pass whose object is a Deaf or BSL-using employee describing the *request / evidence / negotiation process itself* for flexible working, dependants leave, parental leave, or carer’s leave as the barrier. RNID 2026 and RNID workplace-adjustments pages cover AtW case-managers and the employee’s own workplace communication support. They do not document this object. Contact and Carers UK publish how-to pages in English; no BSL version of those workplace-rights pages was found this pass. That absence is the finding, not a failed search.

Templates last-updated dates: ACAS flexible-working request / acknowledgement / outcome letters and example policy, **6 April 2024**. ACAS carer’s leave page, **5 April 2024**. ACAS parental leave page, **7 April 2026**. ACAS time-off-for-dependants page marker **13 March 2025**. A live URL is not a 2026 rewrite.

**Hard-don’t for anything public.** Do not print that a named employer’s written-English process is unlawful. Do not print that emailing the request “discharges” a BSL meeting-interpreter request. Do not treat the ACAS Code companion (colleague / union) as a BSL interpreter. Do not recode AtW or recruitment-interview interpreter stories as leave-request-process stories.

## 151.1 The process the guidance actually designs

Flexible working is the only one of the five that **forces a meeting** unless the request is granted in full. That meeting is designed as a spoken or video conversation about hours, place and business impact, with an optional colleague or union companion. The Code knows what a reasonable adjustment is — paragraph 11 exists — and still does not say “book an interpreter for this meeting.” Telephone is the residual channel. For a BSL-first employee, the residual channel is the worst channel.

Carer’s leave and s.57A are lighter on paper and sharper in real time. Carer’s leave can be given orally with no evidence, which is the opposite of a written-English trap — and still assumes the employee can *reach* the person who records absence, on a three-day clock, in a form that person will accept. s.57A is “as soon as reasonably practicable,” which in most workplaces still means the same-morning absence line. No official page lists text, Relay UK, or VRS as an acceptable way to do that.

Parental leave is a dated written notice plus, if the employer asks, a birth certificate and, if the employee wants days rather than weeks, a benefit letter the regulation still calls DLA. An EHCP is not on that list. ACAS’s April 2026 widening to PIP is guidance sitting on top of an unamended regulation. That is a 150 contestable that becomes a 151 evidence problem: a BSL-first parent producing a PIP letter for a rule that names DLA, with no published translation path.

Article 8 adds no form.

## 151.2 Where BSL *is* named — and why that does not fill this cell

ACAS can name a sign-language interpreter when it is writing about reasonable adjustments generally. EHRC can name a BSL interpreter when it is writing about the conversation *before* an adjustments meeting. The 2026 draft disciplinary-and-grievance Code can name an interpreter as an adaptation of *that* process. The Employment Code’s palantypist example is a disciplinary hearing. None of those sentences is printed on the flexible-working Code, the flexible-working templates, the carer’s-leave page, the parental-leave page, or the dependants-leave page. The join is available as a general Equality Act argument. It is **not** documented as part of this toolkit’s own machinery.

A written request in English and a video consultation with English captions are not the same thing as an interpreted meeting. The prompt’s hard-don’t on that point stands.

## 151.3 What is unpublished or contested

- Any official statement that s.57A notice may be given by text, Relay, VRS, or email.
- Any official statement that a flexible-working consultation must, or even should, be BSL-interpreted when the employee is a BSL-first communicator.
- Whether MAPLE’s DLA-only evidence rule is treated in practice as satisfied by PIP, CDP, or an EHCP. ACAS has already moved the employee-facing sentence; the regulation has not.
- A first-person Deaf or DeafBlind account of *this* process. Not found. Lived-experience policy asks about household stacking belong in 156; they do not exist yet as process-barrier testimony.
- Whether any large employer’s leave policy publishes a non-voice absence line. Out of scope as a census; absence of a national rule is the finding.
- NI procedure remains different for flexible working (150). This file does not paste GB templates into NI.
