**What this prompt is for.** First-person of an employee — ideally Deaf, BSL-using or DeafBlind — using flexible working, s.57A, parental leave or carer’s leave for *more than one* disabled household member at once, and any account of the request process itself as the barrier. ## Headline

**No named UK first-person was found of a Deaf, BSL-using or DeafBlind employee who used the statutory toolkit for two or more independently qualifying household members at once.** The published Deaf workplace testimony that exists is the employee’s own access (AtW, interpreters, isolation) or a single caring relationship (one partner, one parent, one child). The published parent-carer workplace testimony that exists is one disabled child plus an employer’s *contractual* flexibility or paid carer’s policy — not the statutory one-week / s.57A / 18-week-per-child menu used as a stack.

**No named campaign, consultation response or policy ask was found whose object is “statutory leave should scale for a household in which the employee, the partner and an EHCP child each independently qualify.”** The live 2026 ask from Carers UK, Usdaw and the TUC is **paid** carer’s leave and a longer unpaid period. The live government product is the *Make Work Pay* consultation (9 June–1 September 2026) on paid carer’s leave, a right-to-return after intensive caring, guidance, and Hugh’s Law for parents of a *seriously ill* child. Those are adjacent. They do not name the multi-disabled household as a distinct statutory unit.

Carers UK’s own explainer of the 2023 Act is the closest published sentence to this object, and it restates the cap rather than campaigning against it: “One week per year is the **maximum**… regardless of how many dependants they may be supporting… you cannot take a week of carer’s leave for each dependant.” That is how the sector describes the law (150, 152). It is not a demand to rewrite the unit of account.

ONS sandwich-carer statistics (8% food insecurity; 53% unable to work as much as they would like because of caring for someone they live with) are **general-carer evidence**. They describe a dual-generation care load (dependent child + adult relative), not a Deaf employee + disabled partner + EHCP child, and they are not a leave-stacking rate. Do not recode them.

**The case for change that *can* be written from this batch, without inventing a right:** the four statutory rights exist individually (150); the request process is written-English and meeting-based (151); practice has not built an unofficial aggregate (152); Article 8 is not a fifth leave product (153); AtW does not pay for the caring labour (154); a minority of foreign systems scale a *named product* per person in need of care (155); and nobody in the published UK Deaf or parent-carer record has yet described using the toolkit as a stack (this file). That is a policy-gap finding. It is not a finding that a named employer’s refusal was unlawful.

## 156.1 What the near-misses show, and what they do not

The record is not empty of Deaf people who care, or of parent-carers who work. It is empty of the *join* this batch isolated: a Deaf employee, in post, drawing on the statutory menu because the partner and the child independently qualify. The near-misses keep collapsing into one of four other objects already owned elsewhere:

1. The employee’s own workplace access (AtW, interpreters) — 20–26, 52–54, 134–140.
2. One dependant (partner *or* parent *or* child).
3. A hearing family member interpreting for Deaf relatives (Gemma; health, not employment leave).
4. Exit from work (Contact 62%; iNews; ONS sandwich 53%) — labour-market outcome, not toolkit use.

Hedger is the only named Deaf-employee flexible-working judgment found this pass. Recoding it as a multi-disabled-household case would be a citation error: the ratio is sex discrimination after maternity, and the child is not an EHCP household member on the published facts.

## 156.2 The live policy window, accurately described

Between 9 June and 1 September 2026 the government consulted on unpaid-carer employment rights. Carers UK, TUC and Usdaw used that window to push **pay** and **length**. The consultation text also takes parents of a *seriously ill* child as a distinct group (Hugh’s Law). None of the public launch documents isolated a household in which every member independently qualifies. If a consultation response from a Deaf or parent-carer organisation did so, it was not on the open web this pass. The government response is unpublished at 11 September 2026.

That window is the place a stacking ask *could* have been made. Absence from the public campaign pages is the finding, not proof that no unpublished submission exists.

## 156.3 What a public write-up may and may not say

May say: published first-person of this exact object was not found; the sector’s published ask is paid and longer carer’s leave; sandwich-carer and parent-carer exit figures are real and general; the statutory toolkit is unstacked (150–152).

May not say: the toolkit is “not fit for purpose” as a legal conclusion; a named employer’s process breached Article 8 or the Equality Act; ONS 53% is a Deaf rate; Hedger, Gennery, Whittaker, Gemma or Tom stand in for this household; Ireland’s 104 weeks or Norway’s extra days are what the UK “should” copy.
