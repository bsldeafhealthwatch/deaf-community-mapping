# Prompt 152 — When two-plus rights and two-plus household members are used at once, does anyone treat that as one situation?

## Headline

**Practice has not built the unofficial aggregate that statute refused to write.** ACAS and GOV.UK now print “instead of or in addition” sentences — parental leave is “instead of or in addition to carer’s leave”; s.57A emergencies can be followed by parental leave or holiday; carer’s leave “complements” s.57A because one is planned and the other is not. That is a **menu**. It is not a household-level product. Nobody publishes a worked example of one employee drawing flexible working *and* carer’s leave *and* parental leave *and* s.57A in the same year because a disabled partner and an EHCP child each independently qualify. The only official multi-dependant sentence remains the carer’s-leave cap already recorded in 150: one week per employee per 12 months, usable across dependants, not multiplied by them.

The units still do not add:

- Flexible working: two statutory requests per employee per 12 months, one live at a time. A second change in the household (partner’s condition, then child’s EHCP annual review) consumes the cap. The request does not have to name the household; the cap does not grow with it.
- Carer’s leave: one week, all dependants. Planned only.
- Parental leave: 18 weeks **per child**, default four weeks per child per year. A disabled partner generates none. A second EHCP child does generate another 18 weeks.
- s.57A: no annual cap, per incident, “reasonable” hours-to-days. GOV.UK’s unofficial friction sentence: “Your employer may want to talk to you if they think time off is affecting your work.”
- Article 8: no process and no extra leave (153).

**Sandwich-carer research is the wrong object unless labelled.** ONS (6 November 2024) and Carers UK describe people caring for a child *and* an older adult — typically a parent. That is two caring directions. It is not “the employee, the partner, and the child are each independently registered disabled / EHCP.” Contact’s *Counting the Costs* figure (62% of parent-carers gave up a job or reduced hours; average income loss cited as £21,174) is a **single-axis parent-of-a-disabled-child** finding. Named Contact workplace stories this pass (Cheryl Abrahams; Jade and Finn; Jaime and Jess) are one disabled child each. Do not recode them.

**Named friction cases that look close and still travel on a different statute:** *Dobson* (two disabled children + weekend rostering) is Equality Act s.19. *Johnson v Bronzeshield Lifting* 2303313/2022 (menopause + elderly parents + Friday off) joined a flexible-working refusal to disability and sex claims; the published ratio is not household stacking. *Pullin v Neovia* (Down’s-syndrome daughter + hours increase) is constructive dismissal / failure to accommodate part-time working for one disabled child. *Ghebrehiwt* is s.57A scope for one parent. No GB judgment found this pass whose ratio is “the employer mishandled the combination of two-plus statutory rights for two-plus independently qualifying household members.”

CIPD’s carer-friendly guide (5 April 2024) and family-leave manager guide (6 April 2026) recommend flexible working plus leave plus short-notice adaptability as **good employer practice**. That is a retention product, not a statutory aggregate view of this household.

**Hard-don’t for anything public.** Do not print that using four rights in one year is a recognised entitlement. Do not print that a named employer’s performance conversation after repeated s.57A absences is unlawful. Do not treat sandwich-carer percentages as Deaf or as this household type.

## 152.1 What “instead of or in addition” actually does

ACAS and GOV.UK have noticed that an employee might be a parent *and* a carer. The sentence they wrote is: parental leave is separate from, and can sit beside, carer’s leave. TUC adds: s.57A does not eat the carer’s-leave week because the two rights cover different temporal facts (emergency vs planned). That is good menu design. It does not produce a combined entitlement, a combined notice rule, a combined evidence pack, or a combined decision-maker. The employee still makes four separate communications, against four separate clocks, with four separate units of account (150–151).

The practical pinch points that follow from that menu, without anyone writing them down as a household rule:

1. **Two flexible-working requests a year.** A pattern that works for the partner may fail at the child’s annual review. The second statutory request is the last one until the 12-month window rolls. After that the employee is asking a favour.
2. **One week of carer’s leave.** Clinic day for the child and a planned support-plan meeting for the partner compete for the same five days.
3. **Parental leave only moves the child’s side.** Whole weeks unless the DLA/PIP exception applies (150/151 contestable). The partner’s planned care still has to fit in the carer’s-leave week or in holiday.
4. **s.57A has no cap and a published “we may need to talk” sentence.** Repeated emergencies — the reality of an EHCP child plus a disabled partner — are how a performance conversation starts. The statute does not set the threshold. *Qua* and *Ghebrehiwt* still limit the right to the immediate crisis plus making arrangements, not ongoing personal care.
5. **Holiday becomes the unofficial fifth right.** GOV.UK and ACAS both point to annual leave when the statutory pots are the wrong shape. Holiday is finite and is also how the employee rests.

## 152.2 Sandwich carers are not this household

ONS sandwich carers are people providing care to a child and to an older adult, often a parent. The employment harm figures (53% unable to work as much as they would like; 74% at 20+ hours) are real and citable **as sandwich-carer research**. They do not measure “employee + independently disabled partner + EHCP child.” Carers UK’s 2.6 million / 47% tipping-point figures are all-carer. Contact’s 62% / £21,174 is parent-carers of disabled children. Three different populations. This batch’s object is a fourth.

The honest finding for 156, if nothing else appears, is that this object is unpublished.
