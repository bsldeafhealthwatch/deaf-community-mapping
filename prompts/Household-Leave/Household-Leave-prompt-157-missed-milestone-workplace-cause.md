# Prompt 157 — When the workplace access route fails, does the employee miss the family milestone or decision — not just the leave?

**Master index prompt 157.** Eighth file in the Household-Leave batch (prompts 150–157). Added and compiled 11 September 2026 after 150–156 had already closed. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Consequence question. 150–152 established that the toolkit is unstacked and that planned care sits awkwardly against it. 151 established process clocks (FW up to two months; carer’s leave minimum notice; parental leave 21 days). This file asks whether anyone has *named* those clocks, or a refused request, as the reason an employee missed a specific family event or decision — diagnosis, EHCP annual review or transition meeting, hospital discharge planning, safeguarding or best-interests, a significant medical decision — rather than merely losing pay or goodwill. Deaf/BSL disaggregation is question (2) and is expected to be empty. Do not infer causation from a general delay. Do not recode Locked Out / AIS missed *patient* appointments, CODA child-interpreting, or EOL exclusion as this object.

## Headline

**No named source this pass states that an employee missed a specified family milestone *because* flexible working, s.57A, parental leave or carer’s leave was refused or too slow.** The architecture that would make that outcome likely is documented elsewhere in the batch and in live GOV.UK/ACAS text. The outcome itself is unpublished for this object.

Two structural facts, already in 150 and not reopened here, matter for how the gap should be read:

1. **The milestones 157 lists are mostly planned.** GOV.UK *Time off for family and dependants* says, in terms, “You cannot have time off if you knew about a situation beforehand. For example you would not be covered if you wanted to take your child to hospital for an appointment.” ACAS *Time off for medical appointments* (24 Jan 2025): no legal right to time off for a medical appointment; dependants leave is “unlikely to apply for a diabetes clinic that has been booked 4 months in advance.” Hampshire Local Offer restates: “There is no statutory right to time off to attend planned appointments with dependents.” An EHCP annual review, a scheduled diagnosis appointment, a planned discharge meeting and a booked best-interests meeting sit on that planned side. s.57A is the day-one, no-evidence right, and it does not cover them. Carer’s leave can cover a planned disabled-child hospital appointment (ACAS example) but needs **three days’ notice for a single day**. Parental leave needs **21 days**. A flexible-working request has a **two-month** decision clock. A school or hospital that gives shorter notice than those clocks is not, on any source found, recorded as “therefore the parent missed the review because of the statutory process.”
2. **The published carer “missed appointment” numbers face the wrong way, or the wrong cause.** Carers Week 2025: **40% of current carers** postponed or cancelled a medical appointment, test, scan, treatment or therapy **because of caring** — the carer’s *own* health, not the dependant’s milestone, and the reasons given are appointment times, lack of support to attend, and lack of replacement care, not a named employer leave refusal. State of Caring: only 19% agreed they were given relevant information at hospital discharge; 60% in an earlier wave disagreed they were asked about ability and willingness to care. That is an **NHS consultation** failure, not a workplace-leave clock. Contact’s SEN-support work describes parents *leaving work* because school support failed, not missing the EHCP review because a FW request was still inside its two months.

**Deaf / BSL cross-cut: none found.** Locked Out (80% booked interpreter failed to attend; one-third missed appointments because they could not communicate to reschedule) and the BBC Hull 10 Feb 2026 cases (Millie Neadley surgery delayed; Rachel Duke’s son’s GP slot “missed” when a display screen did not show the name) are **patient-access / AIS** failures. They are owned by prompts 34–37 and the EOL brief. Recoding them as “the Deaf employee missed the child’s EHCP review because the FW consultation had no interpreter” would be a citation error.

Working Families published “precedents” are claim-form templates, not adjudicated missed-event findings. Precedent 6 (autistic child, dependant leave, refused part-time) does not say the parent missed a named review.

**Hard-don’t.** Do not print that a named employer caused a named missed family event. Do not promote the 40% Carers Week figure as “parents miss EHCP reviews because of work.” Do not paste Locked Out into this cell.

## 157.1 Why the architecture makes the outcome plausible without making it a finding

A planned EHCP annual review is a decision meeting. The parent’s attendance is expected (Scope: the LA must consult the parent; the child’s voice is part of the review). The statutory route that does not need evidence and starts on day one (s.57A) is closed to that meeting because it is planned. The statutory route that is designed for planned care (carer’s leave) needs notice the school may not give. The statutory route that rearranges the job (flexible working) can take two months. That is the gap 150–152 described. 157 asked whether anyone has published the next sentence: “and so I missed the review.” That sentence was not found.

Absence of the sentence is not proof the meetings are being attended. It is proof the published record has not isolated workplace-leave process as the named cause.

## 157.2 What must not be written in public

- “40% of carers miss their child’s EHCP review because of work.”
- “Deaf employees miss more family decisions because interpreter booking adds time to the FW meeting.”
- “The employer caused [named person] to miss [named meeting]” without a source that says exactly that.
- Locked Out 80% as a workplace-leave statistic.
