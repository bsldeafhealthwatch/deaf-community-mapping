## Headline

**Household-level stacking is not a recognised statutory or guidance category.** The five rights exist as five separate machines. None of ACAS, GOV.UK, CIPD-facing government pages, the Employment Rights Act 1996 as amended, the Maternity and Parental Leave etc. Regulations 1999, or the Carer’s Leave Regulations 2024 describes “an employee + disabled partner + EHCP child under one roof” as a single qualifying situation with a combined entitlement. That absence is the gate finding. It is not a finding that any named employer has applied the rules badly.

The units of account differ, which is why the silence matters:

- **Flexible working** is a *right to request*, capped at **two statutory requests per employee in any 12-month period**, one live request at a time. The cap is per employee, not per dependant and not per reason. Day-one in Great Britain from **6 April 2024**. The employee no longer has to explain the business impact. The employer may refuse only on one of eight listed business grounds, after consultation, inside a two-month decision period (including any appeal). ERA 2025 s.9 will add a “reasonable to refuse on that ground” test and a duty to explain why the refusal is reasonable; ACAS (2 September 2026) and the government’s own implementation pages place that amendment in **2027**. It is **not live** on 11 September 2026.
- **Time off for dependants (s.57A)** is a *per-incident* right to a “reasonable amount” of unpaid time off for five listed emergency purposes. There is **no annual cap** and **no per-dependant cap** in the statute. Multiple household members can each generate separate incidents because “dependant” is a class (spouse/civil partner, child, parent, same-household member, plus a person who reasonably relies on the employee). Planned appointments are outside the right on GOV.UK’s own example. *Qua v John Ford Morrison Solicitors* [2003] IRLR 184 / [2003] ICR 482 EAT is the named reasonableness case: the right is to deal with the immediate crisis and make longer-term arrangements, not to provide ongoing personal care; “reasonable” is usually hours to one or two days; the employer’s operational inconvenience is irrelevant to that assessment.
- **Unpaid parental leave** is the **only right in the set that scales with the number of children**: **18 weeks per child** until 18, default **four weeks per child per year**, 21 days’ notice, employer may postpone by up to six months except immediately after birth/placement. Day-one in Great Britain from **6 April 2026** (ERA 2025; Paternity Leave (Amendment) Regulations 2026 / associated parental-leave commencement). A disabled partner generates **no** parental-leave weeks. The default scheme still requires whole weeks **except** where the child is “entitled to a disability living allowance” (MAPLE 1999 Sch. 2 para. 7 — the regulation has not been rewritten for PIP, DLA-to-PIP migration, or EHCP). GOV.UK’s looser sentence (“unless … your child is disabled”) is guidance, not the regulation. An EHCP is **not** named as a statutory key.
- **Carer’s leave** is the **only right that contains an explicit multi-dependant sentence, and the sentence is a cap, not an uplift.** GOV.UK: “If an employee needs to care for more than one person, they cannot take a week of carer’s leave for each dependant. They can only take one week every 12 months. They can use the week of leave on more than one dependant.” Regulations 2024 reg. 5(1): “one week of carer’s leave during the relevant period.” Day-one from 6 April 2024. Unpaid. No evidence may be required before grant. Employer cannot refuse; may postpone once, to a date within one month, if absence would cause serious disruption. Disability under the Equality Act 2010 is one of three “long-term care need” routes — so a disabled partner *and* a disabled child can each qualify as a dependant, but the employee still has one week.
- **Article 8** is not a sixth employment claim against an ordinary private employer. Public authorities are bound directly by HRA s.6. Private-sector reach is indirect: HRA s.3 (interpret other employment legislation compatibly so far as possible) and the tribunal’s own s.6 duty as a public authority. Deep case-law is prompt 153. This file records the mechanism so the originating table’s “protects private-sector workers” line is not repeated as if it were a standalone right.

**EHCP status does not, by itself, open any of the five rights.** Eligibility sits in the named statute or regulation (employee status; dependant class; long-term care need; parental responsibility; child under 18). An EHCP may be useful *evidence* that a child is disabled for Equality Act or MAPLE purposes; that is a 151 question, not a 150 entitlement.

**ERA 2025 commencement check (run date 11 September 2026).** Live for this batch: unpaid parental leave and paternity leave as day-one rights from 6 April 2026. Not live for this batch: the flexible-working “reasonable to refuse + explain why” amendment (ERA 2025 s.9), placed by ACAS and government timeline pages in 2027. Do not write the 2027 test as current law.

**Hard-don’t for anything public.** Do not print that an employer who limits these rights is acting unlawfully. Do not print that the toolkit is “not fit for purpose” as a legal conclusion. Do not treat Association discrimination as a stacking rule. Do not paste NI, Scottish social-care carer products, or a 2027 commencement into a live GB cell.

## 150.1 What each right is for, in one sentence

Flexible working rearranges the *contract* (hours, times, place) after a written request and a two-month process. s.57A covers a *short emergency* involving a dependant. Parental leave is *planned unpaid time with a child under 18*, 18 weeks per child. Carer’s leave is *planned unpaid time to provide or arrange long-term care*, one week per employee-year. Article 8 is an interpretive constraint on how other employment law is read, and a direct duty only where the employer is a public authority. They were not designed as a household care-package.

## 150.2 The stacking question, answered in the statute’s own units

- Flexible working: **employee-year request cap.** Two household members do not produce four requests.
- s.57A: **per incident, no numerical cap, reasonableness cap.** Two household members can produce two incidents on the same day; the statute is silent on that coincidence.
- Parental leave: **per child.** The only scale-up in the set, and it does not reach a disabled partner.
- Carer’s leave: **per employee-year, expressly not per dependant.** The only place the draftsman looked at “more than one person” and wrote a sentence — and the sentence forbids a week each.
- Article 8: **not an entitlement unit at all.**

That pattern is why later prompts must not invent an aggregate “household leave allowance.” The rules were never built to account for more than one qualifying dependant as a single situation. Whether that produces friction in real workplaces is prompt 152. Whether a BSL-first employee can operate the request machinery is prompt 151.

## 150.4 What is unpublished or contested

- Whether MAPLE Sch. 2 para. 7’s “disability living allowance” still means DLA only, or is read in practice as PIP / any disability benefit / “disabled child” as GOV.UK says. Contested; the regulation has not been rewritten.
- How a tribunal would apply s.57A “reasonable” where emergencies involving two disabled household members land in the same week. No named judgment found on those facts.
- Whether any large employer’s contractual policy creates an unofficial household stack on top of the statute. Not a statutory finding; out of scope here.
- Article 8 case-law on flexible working / caring hours: reserved to 153.
- NI Good Jobs family-leave package: proposal, not treated as law.
