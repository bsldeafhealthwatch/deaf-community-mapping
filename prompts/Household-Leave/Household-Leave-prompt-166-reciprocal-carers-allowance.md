## Headline

**There is no express statutory bar on two people each claiming Carer’s Allowance for caring for the other.** The written bar is about the *same* severely disabled person, not about reciprocity.

SSCBA 1992 s.70(7): no person is entitled, on the same day, to **more than one** carer’s allowance (or to CA and Scottish Carer Support Payment together). That stops one claimant collecting two CAs.

SSCBA 1992 s.70(7ZA): where two or more people would have a “relevant entitlement” (CA or the Universal Credit carer element) **for the same day in respect of the same severely disabled person**, only one of them has it — by joint election, or by the Secretary of State if they cannot agree.

A claims CA because A cares for B. B claims CA because B cares for A. Those are two different severely disabled persons. (7ZA) does not apply. (7) does not apply, provided each partner claims only one CA.

Carers UK’s own FAQ states the same structure in plain English: only one CA per cared-for person; “If you care for someone who also cares for someone else however, you can both make a claim for Carer’s Allowance if you meet the criteria.” That is advocacy restatement of s.70, not a second statute.

Each claim must still stand on its own feet: 35 hours a week for *that* person, a qualifying disability benefit on the cared-for partner (PIP daily living is enough), claimant aged 16+, not in full-time education, residence, and **not gainfully employed** — earnings at or under **£204 a week** after the allowed deductions, from 6 April 2026. PIP on the *claimant* does not itself disqualify them.

The live constraint for the anchor couple (both employed) is usually the earnings cliff, not a reciprocal-claim ban. A named Upper Tribunal or ADM chapter that walks through this exact two-PIP-partner pattern was **not found** this pass. Forum commentary is not used.

## 166.1 What the statute actually forbids

| Situation | Statute | Result |
|---|---|---|
| A claims two CAs (for B and for C) | s.70(7)(a) | A can be paid only one |
| A and C both claim CA for caring for B | s.70(7ZA) | Only one relevant entitlement |
| A claims CA for B; B claims CA for A | s.70(7) and (7ZA) as written | **Not the situation those subsections describe** |
| A is paid CA and State Pension | Overlapping Benefits Regs 1979 | CA usually not paid; underlying entitlement may remain |

## 166.2 What still has to be true for each partner

Independently, each of A and B needs:

1. 35 hours’ care for the other in that Sunday–Saturday week.
2. The other on a s.70(2) qualifying benefit (PIP daily living is enough).
3. Age 16+, not full-time education, residence/presence.
4. Earnings at or under £204 after the allowed deductions — the usual stopper if both remain employed above that line.
5. Not already paid another CA or CSP that same day.

PIP on the claimant is not on that list.

## 166.3 What a public write-up may and may not say

May say: the Act stops two CAs for one disabled person, and stops one person collecting two CAs; Carers UK states that two people who each care for the other may both claim if they meet the criteria; each claim still faces the 35-hour and £204 tests; SDP-type extras can fall if CA is paid.

May not say: “reciprocal CA is always paid”; “DWP bans couples from both claiming”; “PIP on the carer blocks CA”; “the overlapping-benefits regs forbid two CAs in one household.” May not treat a forum thread as ADM.
