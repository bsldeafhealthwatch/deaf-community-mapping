# Prompt 167 — Emergency leave for a parent on DLA/PIP, statutory cover before and after a parent’s death, and the employee’s own PIP

## Headline

**The parent’s DLA/PIP is not an eligibility key for s.57A.** A parent is a dependant because s.57A(3)(c) says “a parent,” not because they hold a benefit. Carer’s leave long-term care need can be met by Equality Act disability or a 3-month care need or old age (s.80J(2)(b)) — DLA/PIP is one way a disability may be evidenced in life, not a statutory ticket. Carer’s Leave Regulations 2024 **reg. 7(4):** “An employer cannot require an employee to supply evidence in relation to a request for carer’s leave before granting the leave.” GOV.UK: “Employees do not need to give evidence of their dependant’s care needs.” s.57A has no evidence subsection at all — only a duty to tell the employer the reason and expected length as soon as reasonably practicable (s.57A(2)). Whether an employer may *ask* for a benefit letter is unpublished as a judgment; making grant of carer’s leave conditional on it would sit against reg. 7(4). Do not print that asking is lawful or unlawful.

**There is no live statutory “bereavement leave for a parent” in Great Britain.** Parental Bereavement Leave (Jack’s Law) is the death of a **child under 18** or a stillbirth after 24 weeks — Parental Bereavement (Leave and Pay) Act 2018 / ERA 1996 ss.80EA–80EE. It does not cover a parent. What *is* live after a parent dies is **s.57A(1)(c)** — reasonable unpaid time off “in consequence of the death of a dependant.” ACAS treats that as the statutory hook and points anything more to the contract or a discretionary policy. ERA 2025 creates a future unpaid general bereavement right; ACAS (2 Sep 2026) places it in **2027**. It is **not live** on 14 September 2026. Do not print it as current law.

**Repeated terminal crises and “reasonable.”** *Qua v John Ford Morrison Solicitors* [2003] ICR 482 / [2003] IRLR 184 EAT is the named case. The right is to deal with an unexpected event and make longer-term arrangements, not to provide ongoing personal care. Reasonable is usually hours to one or two days; the employer’s inconvenience is irrelevant. Previous number, length and dates of absences *are* relevant. The Act says “falls ill,” not “is ill.” Recurring known illness may mean a later episode is not unexpected. That is guidance on the facts of each occasion — not a published days-cap, and not a rule that terminal decline automatically takes every later fall outside s.57A. Carer’s leave is the planned-care machine (one week per year, 3-day notice for a day) and is the product designed for foreseeable care during a known decline. The two remain separate (150).

**SREL has no documented bearing on the employee’s employment-leave rights.** Special Rules for End of Life are a DWP fast-track for the *patient’s* PIP/DLA/AA/UC/ESA. They can speed a qualifying benefit that then supports a *Carer’s Allowance* claim (166). They do not extend s.57A, add carer’s-leave weeks, or create bereavement leave.

**The employee’s own PIP does not change s.57A or carer’s-leave eligibility.** The statute does not treat a PIP-claiming employee as a different class of requester. *Qua* says the employee’s circumstances (including whether anyone else can help) go to “necessary” and “reasonable.” That is fact-specific, not a PIP uplift. The **spouse**, if employed, holds their **own** s.57A and carer’s-leave pots because the employee is a spouse/civil-partner dependant (s.57A(3)(a); s.80J(2)(a)(i)) and may have a long-term care need. Those pots are for action necessary in respect of *the employee*, not a second bite at the parent. Whether “I need to go with my spouse to the parent’s hospital because they cannot manage without me” fits a s.57A limb is unpublished. Do not print an automatic accompanying right. 150’s no-stacking finding stands: two employees, two dependants, two separate machines — not one household pot.

Deaf status of the three people does not create an extra statutory week. Hospital BSL stays in 34–37 / EOL.

## 167.1 Map of the five questions

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| (1) Parent’s DLA/PIP as eligibility or compulsory evidence | Not an eligibility key. Carer’s leave: employer **cannot** require evidence before grant (reg. 7(4)). s.57A: no evidence clause. Asking vs making grant conditional are different; the second is the one the regulation addresses. |
| (2) Bereavement leave for a parent | No live parent product. Jack’s Law = child. After death: s.57A(1)(c) unpaid reasonable time. ERA 2025 general right = 2027. |
| (3) Repeated terminal crises | *Qua*: each occasion; previous absences count; known recurrence may not be unexpected; no days-statute. Carer’s leave is the planned-care week. |
| (4) SREL | Benefits machine for the patient. No employment-leave join found. |
| (5) Employee’s PIP / spouse | PIP does not change the employee’s eligibility. Spouse has an independent pot for the *employee* as dependant. Accompanying-to-parent’s-hospital is untested. No household stack. |

## 167.2 What a public write-up may and may not say

May say: a parent is a dependant without a benefit test; carer’s leave cannot be conditioned on evidence; Jack’s Law is not for parents; after a parent dies the live statute is s.57A(1)(c); *Qua* is the recurring-absence case; SREL is DWP not HR; the spouse has their own leave for the employee, not a second week for the parent.

May not say: the employer must grant unlimited crisis days through a terminal illness; later falls are automatically outside s.57A; SREL creates extra leave; the spouse must be released to interpret at the parent’s bedside; ERA 2025 bereavement is already in force.

**150’s no-stacking finding stands.**
