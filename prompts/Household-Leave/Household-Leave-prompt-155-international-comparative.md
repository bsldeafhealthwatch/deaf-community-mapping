# Prompt 155 — International comparative: does any asked jurisdiction publish a multi-dependant scale-up, or an accessible leave-request process?

**Master index prompt 155.** Sixth file in the Household-Leave batch (prompts 150–156). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** International context only. Look for (1) a statute or collective agreement that *explicitly* recognises or scales for an employee supporting more than one disabled or dependent family member at once — per-dependant caps, household additional leave, or “more than one relevant person” language — not a generous single-dependant scheme an analyst might infer would help, and (2) a published accessible-process rule for the employee navigating the request themselves (sign-language equivalent, plain language, disability-specific support for the *process*). Do not paste FMLA, ADA, EEOC, Fair Work / EAF, NZ HRA, EU 2000/78, Canadian federal or Nordic social-insurance sentences into 150–154 or 156. Do not treat a foreign “family status” or “carer” protected characteristic as a stacking rule.

## Headline

**Four asked systems publish an explicit per-person scale-up. The US federal floor, the EU Work-Life Balance floor, Australian NES carer’s leave and Canadian EI caregiving benefits do not design a household stack.**

Explicit scale-ups found this pass:

- **Ireland — Carer’s Leave Act 2001 s.6 and s.7.** Leave of up to **104 weeks for each relevant person**. Default: one relevant person at a time, and six months must elapse before leave for a second person. **Exception, once only:** if the two relevant persons **reside together**, the employee already on leave for one may start leave for the second; total cap **208 weeks** (104 + 104). That is the cleanest statutory “two people under one roof” sentence in the asked set. It is unpaid job-protected leave for *full-time* care, medically certified by the Department of Social Protection — a different machine from the UK’s one unpaid week.
- **Norway — extra omsorgsdager (Folketrygdloven § 9-6).** On top of ordinary sick-child days (10 for 1–2 children under 12; 15 for 3+), a parent can apply for **10 extra days per chronically ill or disabled child** (20 if sole carer), to age 18, if the condition creates a markedly higher risk of work absence. **Several such children = 10 extra days for each.** One NAV application per child, with a medical certificate. Paid through national insurance, not the employer’s leave pot.
- **Sweden — two per-person pots, different facts.** *Föräldrapenning:* **480 days per child** (twins 660, triplets 840). *Närståendepenning:* **100 days per severely ill person** cared for. *VAB* (temporary parental benefit for a sick child): 120 days per year as the ordinary cap; unlimited days if the child’s life is distinctly threatened. Social-insurance days, not an employer stacking rule.
- **Italy — Legge 104 monthly permits.** Standard three paid days a month to assist a person with certified serious disability. Assisting **two different people** can produce **six days**. Two caregivers assisting the *same* person share the three days; they do not each get a new pack. Per-beneficiary, not per-household.

What is **not** a household stack:

- **US FMLA:** 12 workweeks per eligible employee per 12 months for all ordinary qualifying reasons combined. Caring for a spouse *and* a child with serious health conditions draws on the **same** 12 weeks. Fact Sheet 28K expands *who* counts as a son or daughter over 18 (incapable of self-care because of a disability); it does not add weeks. Spouses at the same employer *share* 12 weeks for birth or for a parent — a cap, not an uplift. Military caregiver leave is 26 weeks and still a combined total.
- **EU Directive 2019/1158:** five working days of carers’ leave **per worker per year**. Commission / academic mapping: most Member States allocate per worker; Ireland and Luxembourg are the “per beneficiary” outliers.
- **Australia NES:** paid personal / carer’s leave is **one bucket** (sick + carer). Unpaid carer’s leave is two days **per occasion**. The flexible-working request lists “are a carer” as one eligibility status, not a per-dependant count.
- **Canada EI caregiving benefits:** three streams — 35 weeks (critically ill child), 15 weeks (critically ill adult), 26 weeks (end of life). Weeks for one certified person can be **shared among caregivers**. That is splitting one pot, not multiplying pots because two household members are disabled. Critical-illness / end-of-life certification is a different fact from long-term disability.

**Accessible process for the request itself.** The only standing, named sign-language duty found on a leave-adjacent *process* is the US EEOC rule that an employer must provide an ASL interpreter for an interview or an ADA interactive-process / mediation conversation when the *employee* is deaf, absent undue hardship. That is the employee’s own disability in the process, not a carer-of-two protocol. Fair Work, Service Canada EI caregiving pages, Försäkringskassan and NAV publish written / digital applications; no jurisdiction in this pass publishes “book a sign-language interpreter for the flexible-working consultation” as a leave-statute requirement.

**Do not transfer.** Irish 104-week full-time leave, Norwegian insurance extra-days, Swedish 100-day närståendepenning, Italian 104 permits and FMLA’s 12-week combined cap are products of those systems. None of them fills a UK cell.

## 155.1 What “explicit” meant in practice

The prompt asked for language that scales for more than one disabled family member, not a generous scheme an analyst might stretch. Ireland’s reside-together exception, Norway’s “10 extra days for each such child,” Sweden’s “100 days for each person cared for,” and Italy’s “two different people → six days” meet that test. FMLA’s 12 weeks, the EU’s five days, Australia’s one sick/carer bucket and Canada’s shared-weeks-for-one-certified-person fail it.

Ireland is also the only asked jurisdiction that writes the *household geometry* into the leave statute (two relevant persons who reside together). That geometry is still a full-time-care product with a DSP medical gate. It is not a UK-style one-week planned-care right multiplied.

## 155.2 What does not transfer

- Nordic extra days and närståendepenning are social-insurance replacements for lost earnings, paid by NAV / Försäkringskassan, not an employer duty to grant more statutory weeks.
- Irish 104 weeks is a career-break-length unpaid absence for certified full-time care.
- FMLA eligibility (1,250 hours, 50 employees / 75 miles) and Australia’s 12-month qualifier are not UK day-one architecture.
- EEOC ASL is Title I reasonable accommodation for the deaf employee. The UK already has EA s.20 for that fact (151). It is not a foreign stacking rule.

## 155.3 What 156 should not do

Do not write “other countries give extra leave when two household members are disabled” as a campaign sentence. The accurate sentence is: a minority of asked systems scale a *named product* per person in need of care; most, including the EU floor and FMLA, cap the worker. The UK already scales unpaid parental leave per child and caps carer’s leave per employee (150). That pattern is not unique.
