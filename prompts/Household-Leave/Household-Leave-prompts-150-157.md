Anchor: a Deaf or DeafBlind BSL-using employee, in a household where their partner is also registered disabled and their child holds an EHCP — three independently qualifying people under one roof — trying to use the ordinary statutory toolkit (flexible working request, time off for dependants, unpaid parental leave, carer's leave) to manage appointments, emergencies, and planned care across all three at once, while the request-and-evidence process itself (write a letter, attend a meeting, negotiate with a manager) is not built for a BSL-first communicator and does not appear anywhere to account for more than one qualifying dependant.

Proposed domain: stub under the existing Welfare & Employment cluster, alongside 92–149 — the statutory-leave-and-flexible-working slice of the same in-post pipeline, not a new numbered Domain.

### Notes for this batch

- **Gate prompt: 150.** If none of the five rights show any recognition of multi-dependant stacking (plausible — this looks like an unrecognised policy category), that absence is the headline finding and should shape how 152–154 are read: not "the rules are being applied badly" but "the rules were never built to account for more than one qualifying dependant." Later prompts must not invent an aggregate cap or an unofficial stacking rule to fill the silence.
- **Prompt 153 must actively interrogate the premise it was given, not confirm it.** The Human Rights Act Article 8 framing in the source table this batch grew from ("protects public-sector workers… from excessive workplace interference") is a simplification of an indirect, tribunal-mediated mechanism in the private sector. This prompt exists to correct or confirm that framing with named case law, not restate the table. Public-authority duty (s.6) and private-sector interpretive obligation (s.3) are different machines; keep them in different rows.
- **Prompt 154 is the bridge check.** It tests whether the welfare-funding side (Access to Work, already well covered elsewhere for the employee's own adjustment) has any crossover into the employee's caring responsibilities. Expected finding: no, these are separate systems, which is itself useful for showing the household falls into a gap between two well-funded single-purpose systems. Do not reopen the cap, the 3,210 BSL-interpreter customer count, or the staff-guide bands.
- **Still-alive scepticism.** Check last-updated dates on ACAS model letters, GOV.UK leave pages, the AtW staff guide, Carers UK / Contact how-to pages, and ERA 2025 commencement orders. A 2023 Act title is not proof the 2026 live rule is unchanged. Volunteer and campaign pages go stale without formally closing.
- **Hard-don't for this batch:** do not print any claim that an employer's refusal, limitation, or process for these five rights is unlawful, discriminatory, or a breach of Article 8 without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment. This is very likely a policy-gap batch (rights exist individually; nothing addresses their combination), not a rights-violation batch — the two are different findings and must not be conflated in the write-up. Do not print "the toolkit is not fit for purpose" as a legal conclusion. Do not treat Association discrimination as a stacking rule. Do not treat an EHCP as automatic evidence that carer's leave or dependants leave is available; eligibility is in the named statute, not in the education plan.
- **Held-back legs:** none after 157. Eight prompts. 157 was added the same day once 150–156 had closed the legal and first-person legs; it is the workplace-caused missed-milestone consequence, not a second lived-experience pass and not a general “Deaf people are excluded from family life” theme (EOL and CODA already own adjacent ground).
- **Prompt 157 is deliberately narrow.** It does not ask whether Deaf people miss family communication in general. It asks whether the specific gap 150–152 document (unstacked rights + process clocks) has a *named* knock-on of the employee missing the family event or decision itself. Inferring that from Carers Week “40% cancelled their own appointment” or from Locked Out patient-interpreter failure is a citation error.
- **Relationship to 141–149 (EAP) and 92–149 generally.** This batch does not repeat the EAP-access question (144) or the single-employee compounding-needs-in-service-design question (145). Those ask whether support *services* coordinate around one employee's multiple needs. This batch asks a different, prior question: what the statutory entitlements themselves actually provide when it is the *household*, not the employee alone, that carries multiple independent qualifying conditions, and whether a BSL-first employee can access that entitlement framework at all.
- **Do not conflate with Graeme's personal OH / HR thread.** This batch produces general, citable evidence for the dossier, not advice on an individual flexible-working request.
- **England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland** are separate systems. Parental leave, flexible working and carer's leave have UK-wide employment-law roots but NI procedure and some accompanying rights differ; Scottish social-care carer products are not an England employment cell.
