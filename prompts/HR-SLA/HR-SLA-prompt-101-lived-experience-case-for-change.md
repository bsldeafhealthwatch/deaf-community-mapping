# Prompt 101 — Lived experience and the case for change

**Master index prompt 101.** Lived-experience file in the Internal HR/IT SLA batch (prompts 97–102). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. Named first-person first; anonymous survey quotes second; AtW quotes stay in the AtW cell.

**What this prompt is for.** Documented first-person testimony about an *employer* HR/IT queue for an agreed adjustment — not DWP Access to Work processing, not Jobcentre BSL, not OH diagnosis. Any comparison the speaker themselves draws between in-house and agency treatment. Named policy asks about turnaround transparency.

**Index check.** Prompts 52–54 and RNID 2026 already hold AtW waits. Prompts 27–33 hold Rimmer (Jobcentre). Prompt 98 holds the BDF/TUC/UNISON numbers. Prompt 99 holds Khan and Toth. ## Headline

**Named first-person testimony from a Deaf or BSL-using employee about their employer’s HR or IT ticket — how long the ticket sat, whether they could see an SLA, whether agency status changed the queue — was not found.** Limping Chicken and Disability News Service searches this pass did not produce that file.

What exists, and must be labelled:

1. **Deaf / BSL first-person that *is* published is Access to Work, not employer HR.** RNID April 2026 survey and RNID written evidence AWS0095 (25 Mar 2026) quote BSL users waiting months for an AtW *award or renewal*, including a female BSL user aged 45–54 who had used the scheme since the mid-1990s and was told up to 30 weeks for a case manager. That is DWP. It does not describe the employer’s internal ticket after OH agreed an interpreter.
2. **Anonymous employer-side quotes exist in BDF 2023**, not Deaf-split, not contract-type-split: “I did all the organisation myself as I waited 2 years for my manager to do something. In the end, I paid for it myself.” And “Working from home became much easier once I had adjustments implemented – I was off for 8 months waiting for them to be sorted out.”
3. **Named employer-delay judgments that are not Deaf/BSL:** Rose Davies (Gloucestershire Health) — eight OH reports, more than a year on a quieter desk / headphones / flashing telephone, her own words “the amount of time and effort for a small request is astonishing and exhausting,” then resignation. Miss R Duggal v HBOS — tribunal found **11 months** to fix office lighting “excessive and wholly unreasonable.” Khan (prompt 99) is agency-employer delay on AtW-funded kit, then a move to direct employment; she is not Deaf.
4. **The live policy ask is a statutory *response* deadline, not a published employer SLA product.** Work and Pensions Committee *Disability at Work* (HC 1227, 21 May 2026) recommends employers **respond within two weeks** and give written reasons for refusal, aligned with the Employment Rights Act flexible-working reply clock. Lords Public Services Committee asked **four weeks**. TUC May 2025 asks employers to respond within two weeks and to adopt its passport. Disability Employment Charter evidence to the same inquiry asked for a set timeframe. None of those texts is a campaign about “SLA transparency” as a named product. Costigan’s Private Member’s Bill (prompt 97) remains first-reading, not law.
5. **No first-person comparison of in-house versus agency ticket treatment** was found. Prompt 99’s absence stands.

**Hard-don't.** Do not print RNID AtW quotes as employer-SLA testimony. Do not generalise two anonymous BDF sentences into a national Deaf rate. Do not print delay as unlawful without the named judgment that actually decided that claimant’s facts.

## 101.1 What the prompt asked for, and the gap

The batch anchor is a Deaf BSL employee whose OH report is already agreed (prompts 92–96) and who then sits in HR’s or IT’s queue. Prompt 101 asked that person to speak, in public, about that queue.

They have not, under a name, in the sources opened this pass. Deaf public testimony in 2026 clusters on **Access to Work** — case-manager waits, renewal cliffs, invoices, VRS-not-BSL letters — because that is the visible national scheme. Employer-side ticketing is local, rarely minuted, and almost never bylined.

That is a documentation gap, not a finding that Deaf employees do not wait on employer desks. BDF and TUC show disabled employees as a class do wait. The class is not BSL-split and not contract-type-split.

## 101.2 How to use Davies and Duggal

Both files are employer-side delay with a named person and a tribunal clock. They are the right *kind* of evidence for this batch. They are the wrong *population* for a Deaf Network write-up if printed as if they were BSL. Use them as “this is what a documented internal delay looks like when it reaches a tribunal,” then state the Deaf-named equivalent was not found.

Davies is also the cleanest published employee-voice line on process friction: a small request, many months, exhaustion, resignation. That sentence is hers.

## 101.3 The case for change that *is* on the record

The live, sourced ask is narrow:

- a **response** deadline (two weeks WPC / TUC; four weeks Lords PSC);
- written reasons when the answer is no;
- tell new starters that the right exists, whether or not the employer already knows they are disabled.

It is not a sourced ask for a published multi-tier HR SLA, and it is not a sourced ask that agency tickets be time-stamped separately. Those remain untested FOI questions from prompt 99.

University of York research commissioned after the Lords PSC recommendation had not surfaced as a public report this pass.
