## Don't list (print near any workplace-adjustments / HR page)

- **Do not repeat Grok's Khan v Reed timeline.** Grok's finding files (99, 101, 102) and its own self-review all state the adjustments/kit "arrived 22 June 2023" and imply a fairly clean two-step move (Reed agency → direct Leeds City Council employment) driven by that delay. Having read the judgment PDF directly: there is **no "22 June 2023" date anywhere in it**. The tribunal's own delivery evidence sits in **August–October 2023** (chasing 28–30 Aug 2023; estimated chair delivery 9 Oct 2023 quoted from a 19 Sep 2023 communication). The employment chain is also more layered than Grok's summary suggests: Khan moved through **three different employing/booking entities** before reaching the Council directly — PayStream (umbrella, from Nov 2022), then Reed itself as PAYE temp (Jan–Apr 2023, ending 25–30 Apr 2023), then SWES (umbrella, Apr–Nov 2023) — and she did not resign from the Reed assignment chain until **17 October 2023**, with the booking ending **3 November 2023**, the same day direct Council employment began. The tribunal's causal finding ("had the adjustments been put in place by the end of March the claimant would have continued") is real and citable, but the specific delivery date and the two-party simplicity are not. **Any future use of this case must cite delivery as "August–October 2023 (exact date not specified in the judgment; estimated chair delivery 9 Oct 2023)" and describe the chain as multi-entity, not agency-to-council in one step.**
- Do not print a statutory days-clock for fulfilling an agreed adjustment. There isn't one (verified live).
- Do not print the WPC two-week figure, the Lords PSC four-week figure, or Costigan's PMB as law.
- Do not print "NHS Employers two weeks" as a national NHS SLA — it's a self-assessment checklist question.
- Do not print AWR's 12-week qualifier as an RA waiting period.
- Do not print AtW figures (109 days, £69,260 cap, RNID quotes) as employer-ticket data.
- Do not print BDF/TUC/UNISON wait bands as BSL-split or contract-type-split. They are not.
- Do not print BDF 2019's 25% and 17% over-a-year figures as if they agreed with each other.
- Do not print "HR serves in-house clients first" as a documented rule — no published contract, FOI, CIPD/REC report or judgment found.
- Do not print *Toth v HR Go / Mayr-Melnhof* as national practice — the claim **failed**; note the judgment header spells the party **Toth**, not "Torth" as the source filename has it.
- Do not print the ADA 15-day figure as US private-employer law — that's a federal-agency procedure example under EO 13164.
- Do not print Australia's 21-day or New Zealand's one-month flexible-working reply clocks as disability-adjustment law.
- Do not file Rimmer, Iqbal, Bussey, Elliott, Thomas, or Graeme's own OH thread as HR-SLA testimony.
- Do not print a Workplace Adjustment Passport as a legal guarantee binding the next manager, contractor or employer. Royal Mail's passport expressly excludes agency, contractors and the self-employed.
- Do not collapse 102 (intra-employer passport) into 161 (TUPE passport), or 97–102 into 121–126 or 109–114.
- Do not print any named delay or in-house/agency difference as unlawful without a named statute, regulation, or judgment on those facts.
- Do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. Follow money and fulfilment.
