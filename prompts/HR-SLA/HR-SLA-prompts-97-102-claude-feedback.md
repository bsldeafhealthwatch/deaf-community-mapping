## Verdict for any public page

The one sentence a page is allowed to lead with, if any:

> There is no UK statutory clock for how fast an employer must fulfil an agreed reasonable adjustment. The Equality Act duty applies to agency and contract workers as well as employees. Whether HR actually serves those tickets in the same queue is unpublished.

## Don't list (print near any workplace-adjustments / HR page)

- Do not print a statutory days-clock for fulfilling an agreed adjustment. There isn’t one. “Reasonable time” is fact-specific (prompt 97).
- Do not print the WPC two-week figure, the Lords PSC four-week figure, or Costigan’s PMB as law. Two-week is a *response* recommendation aligned with flexible-working reply; it is not a fulfilment SLA. Costigan remains first-reading on the 97 pass.
- Do not print “NHS Employers two weeks” as a national NHS SLA. The May 2025 checklist *asks* processed within two weeks. NHS Lothian 20/30 working days is one board. CSWAS review-route contact in 4 working days is a Civil Service review door, not fulfilment.
- Do not print AWR’s 12-week qualifier as an RA waiting period. Different statute (prompt 97).
- Do not print AtW 109 / 106 working days, the £69,260 cap, or RNID AtW quotes as employer-ticket data (52–54 / 101).
- Do not print BDF, TUC or UNISON wait bands as BSL-split or contract-type-split. They are not.
- Do not print BDF 2019’s 25% and 17% over-a-year figures as if they agreed. Contested inside the same source (prompt 98).
- Do not print RNID’s £69,620. Official cap is £69,260.
- Do not print “HR serves in-house clients first” as a documented rule. Prompt 99: no published contract, FOI, CIPD/REC report or judgment. Perception stays perception.
- Do not print *Khan v Reed* as “Leeds City Council served permanents first.” Reed delayed. She then took direct employment. Not Deaf/BSL.
- Do not print *Toth v HR Go / Mayr-Melnhof* as national practice. The RA claim **failed**. Witness: 18 years, no agency worker had needed RA; hirer “does not send them any agency worker that would require any adjustments.” Filename says Torth; party name is **Toth**.
- Do not print “slower for agency workers” and “unlawfully slower for agency workers” as the same claim.
- Do not print the ADA 15-day figure as US private-employer law. That is an EO 13164 *federal agency procedure example*. Commerce is 20 business days to decide + 10 to implement (prompt 100).
- Do not print Australia’s 21-day Fair Work flexible-working reply, or New Zealand’s one-month flexible-work reply, as a Disability Discrimination Act / Human Rights Act adjustment clock.
- Do not print EEOC joint-employer guidance as a UK finding that agency and payroll already share one ticket.
- Do not file Rimmer, Iqbal, Bussey, Elliott, Thomas, or Graeme’s personal OH thread as HR-SLA testimony.
- Do not generalise two anonymous BDF sentences (“waited 2 years… paid for it myself”; “off for 8 months”) into a national Deaf rate.
- Do not print a Workplace Adjustment Passport as a legal guarantee that the next manager, next contractor, or next employer must implement the old list. NHS Employers: a new role may need different adjustments. Civil Service: hard kit still hits procurement.
- Do not print that agency and bank staff hold the NHS or Civil Service passport as of right. Coverage language is employees / civil servants. **Royal Mail Care and Responsibility Passport expressly excludes agency, contractors and self-employed.**
- Do not collapse 102 (intra-employer passport) into 161 (passport on TUPE). Different legal employer.
- Do not collapse 97–102 into 121–126. This batch is the *adjustment ticket*. That batch is whether the outsourced *helpdesk you ring* is reachable in BSL.
- Do not collapse 97–102 into 109–114. This batch stops at the queue. Grievance is what happens after the date is missed.
- Do not print any named SLA, delay, or in-house/agency difference as unlawful without a named statute, regulation, or judgment on those facts.
- Do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. Follow money and fulfilment — whose budget, whose ticket system, whose signature on the passport.

- **Distinct from 92–96** because those stop at the OH recommendation. This batch starts when HR/IT/Facilities must fulfil it.
- **Distinct from 52–54** because AtW is a DWP grant. Employer delay and AtW delay can stack; they are still two clocks.
- **Distinct from 103–108** because recruitment is before a contract. Interview interpreter booking is not an in-post ticket.
- **Distinct from 109–114** because grievance is the missed-date ladder (Acas, time limits, hearing BSL). Do not preview tribunal tactics here.
- **Distinct from 121–126** because that batch is whether the *outsourced desk you telephone* (IT, payroll, whistleblowing) is reachable. This batch is the adjustment *request* sitting in a queue.
- **Distinct from 158–163 / prompt 161** because TUPE changes the legal employer. 102 is the same employer, new manager or new role, or agency→permanent *without* a transfer of undertaking.
- Shared facts that must not be reprinted as if 97–102 discovered them: AtW cap £69,260 and NAO/PAC waits (52–54); BSL-interpreter AtW customers 3,210 in 2024/25 (20–26); Iqbal v Home Office (92–96); Rimmer (27–33); ESR IAT “not designed for temporary movements” (99, also relevant to 158).
