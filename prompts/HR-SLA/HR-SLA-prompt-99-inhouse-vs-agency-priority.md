# Prompt 99 — Is there a documented service-priority gap between in-house staff and agency, contract, or outsourced staff?

**Master index prompt 99.** Load-bearing file in the Internal HR/IT SLA batch (prompts 97–102). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Test whether the perception that “HR serves its in-house clients first” is documented in a named UK contract, policy, FOI or case — including any named Deaf, BSL-using or disabled worker. Do not manufacture confirmation from generic outsourcing literature. Absence is the finding if nothing specific is sourced.

**Index check.** Prompt 97 gate: the Equality Act duty applies to agency and contract workers (s.41(4); ACAS 30 Jan 2025). AWR’s 12-week qualifier is not an RA waiting period. Prompt 98’s survey stack does not split by contract type. “Slower for agency workers” and “unlawfully slower for agency workers” are different claims.

## Headline

**No published UK contract, FOI disclosure, CIPD/REC report or tribunal judgment was found that documents a structural ticket-priority rule** — “in-house HR requests are actioned before an identical request from an agency, contract or outsourced worker.” Separate ticketing systems and slower managed-service SLAs for agency staff are **plausible operationally**. They are **not sourced** on this pass as a written term.

What *is* documented is adjacent, and must not be collapsed into that claim:

1. **Split duty-holders, not split queues.** EA s.41(4) and Schedule 8 put some adjustments on the agency-employer and some on the principal. Two organisations can each wait for the other. That is a dual-duty map (prompt 97), not a published “serve permanents first” SLA.
2. **Khan v Reed Talent Solutions Ltd** 1801643/2024 (Leeds ET, 11 Feb 2025). Agency social worker placed with Leeds City Council, moving through three employing/booking entities before reaching the Council directly (PayStream umbrella from Nov 2022; Reed PAYE temp Jan-Apr 2023; SWES umbrella Apr-Nov 2023). Reed's chain failed to put AtW-funded kit in place in a reasonable time (duty from Nov 2022; tribunal said end of March 2023 was achievable; delivery evidence in the judgment sits August-October 2023 -- chasing 28-30 Aug 2023, estimated chair delivery 9 Oct 2023 -- no single named date of "22 June 2023" appears in the judgment). Causal link found: she resigned from the Reed assignment chain (17 Oct 2023) and took **direct** employment with the Council on 3 Nov 2023, the same day the booking ended, *because* the adjustments were not in place. **Not Deaf/BSL.** The judgment does not find that Council HR served its own permanents first; it finds Reed's chain delayed.
3. **Toth v HR Go (Liverpool) Ltd and Mayr-Melnhof Packaging UK Ltd** 1602052/2019 (Mold ET, reserved 2022). Agency-worker RA claim **failed**. Witness for the agency: in 18 years **not one** agency worker had required reasonable adjustments; she did not know if the agency used Occupational Health. Tribunal recorded the hirer’s expectation that the agency would monitor RA “and in reality does not send them any agency worker that would require any adjustments,” and that the hirer had **no monitoring or arrangements** for an agency worker who needed RA. That is a **no-process / screening** finding on those facts, not a ticket-priority SLA, and the claimant lost.
4. **NHS policy text includes bank, agency and sessional workers in the duty** (NHS Scotland guide 6 Aug 2025; NHS Employers toolkit 14 Nov 2025 lists bank staff and contractors). Inclusion in the policy heading is not evidence of a shared ticket queue or of equal measured turnaround.
5. **ESR Inter-Authority Transfer** passports permanent NHS-to-NHS moves and is “not designed for temporary movements of staff.” That is a workforce-record architecture fact. It is **not** a published RA-ticket priority table.

**No named Deaf or BSL-using worker** was found in a case, FOI or report describing in-house tickets being served before theirs.

**Hard-don't.** Do not print the Khan or Toth files as proof that “HR serves in-house first.” Do not print Toth’s failed claim as a finding that screening-out agency workers who need adjustments is a national practice. Do not print the pattern as unlawful without a named judgment that actually decided that point.

## 99.1 What the premise needed, and what was found

The load-bearing claim is not “agency workers sometimes wait a long time for adjustments.” Prompt 98 already shows long waits across disabled workers as a group. The claim is **structural priority**: the same request, two contract types, in-house served first because of the way HR/IT is organised (separate tickets, a slower MSP SLA, a written rank).

That document was not found. Shared-services and outsourcing texts talk about HR as a transactional service with an SLA. They do not publish a disability-ticket grade that differs by whether the requester is on the payroll or on an agency timesheet.

The statutory structure *can* produce delay without anyone writing a priority rule. Kit that travels with the worker sits with the agency; a flashing fire alarm on the principal’s floor sits with the principal; AtW may be paying; each party can treat the file as the other party’s. Khan is that fact-pattern on one social-worker assignment. It is still not a published “permanents first” SLA.

## 99.2 Khan and Toth — use the files, do not over-read them

**Khan** is the cleanest recent agency-RA judgment located. Reed was the employer. Leeds City Council was the placement. The tribunal used s.41(4). Delay ran from a November 2022 start, through December 2022 AtW approval, past a March 2023 “should have been done” point, to delivery evidence sitting August-October 2023 in the judgment (chasing 28-30 Aug 2023; estimated chair delivery 9 Oct 2023 -- there is no "22 June 2023" date in the judgment; that date was an earlier, uncorrected error in this project's own research and must not be repeated). She moved onto the Council’s books on 3 November 2023, via a three-entity employment chain (PayStream, then Reed PAYE, then SWES), not a single agency-to-council step. The reason the tribunal accepted was the missing kit, not a comparison with how fast Council HR processed a permanent colleague’s identical request. Do not invent that comparison.

**Toth** is useful only as a window onto process emptiness: an agency witness who had never seen an RA request in 18 years, and a hirer described as not receiving agency workers who would need adjustments and as having no arrangements if one arrived. The claims failed. Treating that paragraph as national practice would be the manufacturing the prompt forbids.

Neither file is a Deaf or BSL file. Rimmer remains a Jobcentre case (prompts 27–33). Iqbal remains an office-hours Home Office BSL+OH file (prompt 92–96). Do not recycle them here.

## 99.3 Unpublished, contested, anecdotal

- “HR will serve their clients first” as Graeme framed it: **anecdotal** until a named contract, FOI or case says so. This pass did not find that source.
- Dual-queue delay as a *lived* pattern is plausible and is consistent with split duty-holders. Plausible is not documented.
- NHS bank vs agency vs substantive measured RA turnaround: **unpublished**.
- Outsourced facilities / cleaning / catering contractors whose staff work on an NHS or council site and raise an adjustment through the contractor’s own HR desk: **no published SLA comparison** found.
- REC and CIPD have not published the comparison this prompt asked for.
- Generic “outsourcing creates two masters” literature is out of scope as proof.

FOI that would actually test the premise belongs in the existing companion log, not a new tracker: ask named ICBs, trusts and councils (a) whether agency/bank/contractor staff use the same workplace-adjustment ticket as substantive staff, (b) whether the published SLA differs, (c) measured median days by contract type for the last 12 months. Yes/no-or-number questions only.
