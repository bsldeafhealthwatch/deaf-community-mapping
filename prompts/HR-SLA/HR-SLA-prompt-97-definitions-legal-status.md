## Headline

There is **no statutory or regulator-set timescale** for an employer to process or fulfil a reasonable-adjustment request. Equality Act 2010 s.20 requires “such steps as it is reasonable to have to take.” That is a fact-specific test. ACAS (page last updated 30 January 2025) lists factors — will it remove the disadvantage, is it practical, is it affordable, health and safety — and does not publish days or weeks. The only clocks that sit nearby are **different legal objects**: the two-month flexible-working decision period (Employment Rights Act 1996 as amended; ACAS Code on flexible working, 11 January 2024) and the three-months-minus-one-day tribunal limit for a discrimination claim (EA s.123; from 1 October 2026 most ET claims move to six months minus one day). Neither is an HR/IT SLA for an adjustment ticket.

The duty is **not confined to directly-employed staff**. ACAS states it covers employees and workers, contractors and self-employed people hired to personally do the work, and job applicants. For agency / contract workers supplied by another employer, EA s.41(4) puts the reasonable-adjustment duty on the **principal (the hirer / end-user) as well as the employer of the contract worker**. Schedule 8 splits *who* must do *which* adjustment; the Employment Code (from para 11.9) says the length of the assignment is a factor in what is reasonable for the principal. The Agency Workers Regulations 2010 are a **separate** equal-treatment scheme (day-one facilities; 12-week basic working conditions). AWR is not the source of the disability-adjustment duty and does not create a 12-week wait before an agency worker can request BSL or a visual alarm.

No published CIPD, Institute of Customer Service, or NHS Employers **framework SLA** was found that names disability-adjustment tickets as their own national category with a target. What exists is employer-level or good-practice text: NHS Employers’ May 2025 self-assessment asks whether requests are “processed within two weeks”; NHS Lothian’s own guidance uses 20 / 30 working days; Civil Service Workplace Adjustment Service review-route contact is “within 4 working days.” Those are not regulator clocks. Deirdre Costigan’s *Reasonable Adjustments (Duty on Employers to Respond) Bill* (Ten Minute Rule, first reading 20 May 2025, Bill 247) would have created a statutory response deadline; as of the 5 May 2026 bill page it had not left first reading and is **not law**.

**Hard-don't for anything public.** Absence of a published SLA is not a finding that a named employer is in breach. A delay is “unreasonable” only if a tribunal so finds on those facts. “Slower for agency workers” is a practice question for prompt 99, not a legal conclusion from this file.

## 97.1 No regulated SLA — timing is “reasonableness”

s.20 is three requirements (PCP; physical feature; auxiliary aid). Each is “take such steps as it is reasonable to have to take.” The Act does not define a number of days. The EHRC Employment Code’s reasonableness factors (effectiveness, practicability, cost, disruption, resources, external help such as Access to Work) are the same shape as ACAS’s shorter list. Tribunals have treated delay as relevant to whether the *step* was taken, and s.123 uses “when A might reasonably have been expected to do the thing” as the start of the *claim* clock for an omission. That is how delay enters the statute — as a limitation rule and as evidence on reasonableness — not as an SLA.

Do not collapse the nearby two-month flexible-working clock into this cell. ACAS’s flexible-working Code is explicit that the Equality Act duty is separate. A BSL-using employee who asks to change hours may trigger both; missing the two-month flexible-working deadline is a flexible-working breach, not proof that s.20 has a two-month clock.

Charity and union language that “employers should implement any adjustments in a timely fashion” (e.g. CSP FAQ, June 2026) is good-practice wording. The same CSP page tells the member it “may be a good idea to request a response … within a certain timescale such as 14 days.” That is a suggested chase letter, not a legal target.

## 97.2 Contract type does not switch the duty off

Three layers, not one:

1. **Direct employees and limb (b) workers / personal-service contractors** — EA Part 5 plus ACAS’s three-bullet list. Day one. No 12-week wait.
2. **Contract workers supplied by another employer** (the classic agency temp, and many outsourced placements) — s.41(4) puts the duty on the *principal* as well as on the worker’s employer. Schedule 8 and Employment Code chapter 11 split the work: portable / assignment-generic adjustments sit with the employer (the agency); site-specific adjustments sit with the principal. Assignment length is a reasonableness factor for the principal, not a qualifier that turns the duty off.
3. **AWR 2010** — equal treatment on pay, hours, leave and (from day one) collective facilities and vacancy information. It does not list BSL interpreting or a visual fire alarm as an AWR “basic working condition.” Treating AWR’s 12-week qualifier as a waiting period for a disability adjustment would be a misread of the wrong statute.

Who “the employer” is for s.20 purposes is therefore fact-specific: the employing entity for its own PCPs and kit; the hirer as principal for its premises, rotas and on-site processes; sometimes both (Sch. 8 para 2(5)). That is a duty-holder map, not a service-priority rule. Whether HR *tickets* are in practice queued by contract type is prompt 99.

## 97.3 Published HR / shared-services SLAs do not name a disability tier at national level

What this pass actually found:

- **No** CIPD workforce-SLA standard that carves out “disability adjustment” as a ticket type with its own target.
- **No** Institute of Customer Service published product of that kind.
- **NHS Employers** (19 May 2025) asks organisations whether they *have* a two-week processing provision — a maturity-checklist question, not a national contract term.
- **NHS Lothian** (March 2024) is a named local 20 / 30 working-day response policy.
- **Civil Service Workplace Adjustment Service** review route: 4 working-day contact after internal routes are exhausted.
- **CIPD’s own** reasonable-adjustment documents that do carry numbers are about CIPD *assessments* (apprentice EPA extra time; exam special consideration within seven days), not employer HR shared-services.

Internal shared-services contracts are usually unpublished. Prompt 99 is the place to look for FOI-disclosed NHS / local-authority managed-service schedules. This gate file records the absence of a *national published* disability-ticket SLA, not a finding that no employer has one internally.

## 97.4 Unpublished, contested, or not yet law

- Costigan PMB: the live legislative *ask* for a response deadline. First reading only. Do not write it as commenced.
- TUC May 2025 82% / 55% figures: advocacy survey of 1,000 disabled workers. Use in prompt 98 with the “who had made a request” qualifier. Not a statutory clock and not an official statistic.
- “Unreasonable delay” in any one tribunal judgment is fact-specific. *Fernandes v DWP* [2023] EAT 114 restates how s.123 works for omissions; it does not set a national number of weeks after which delay is automatically a breach.
- Northern Ireland is a different statute (DDA / NI equality legislation). Do not paste EA 2010 Part 5 into an NI cell.
- USPS 45-calendar-day Rehabilitation Act rule located in the same search is **international** and does not fill a UK cell.
