# Prompt 102 — Do Workplace Adjustment Passports travel across contract type, manager, and role change?

**Master index prompt 102.** Final file in the Internal HR/IT SLA batch (prompts 97–102). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment.

**What this prompt is for.** Whether a Workplace Adjustment Passport (Civil Service, NHS Employers, TUC/CIPD equivalent) is documented as applying to agency, contract and outsourced staff as well as directly-employed staff, and whether any named case shows an agreed adjustment having to re-enter the processing queue after a manager, department or employment-status change.

**Index check.** Portability of an already-agreed adjustment. Not the original OH recommendation (92–96). Not AtW award portability (52–54), except where a DWP passport is the mechanism. Khan (99) is a candidate fact-pattern, not a passport case.

---

## Headline

A Workplace Adjustment Passport is a **voluntary, worker-owned record**. Its published purpose is to stop the worker having to re-explain an agreed adjustment when the **line manager or role changes inside the same employer**. It is **not a statute**, not an SLA, and not a ticket that HR must honour in a set number of days.

**Agency / contract / outsourced coverage is not written as “applies equally.”**

- Civil Service language is **employees / civil servants**. The staff-transfer guidance that tells an importing manager to read the passport is for civil servants moving between departments.
- NHS Health Passport (NHS Employers, 24 Nov 2022) is for “individuals working in the NHS.” Later NHS Employers guidance (19 May 2025) says a passport means staff who move role or manager “do not have to re-explain,” **and** that a new role may need different adjustments. Bank, agency and contractors appear in the *duty-coverage* toolkit (prompt 99), not as named passport-holders.
- TUC / GMB product is for **workers / members**, designed to be negotiated into a workplace agreement. The 2019 launch text also talks about people who switch employers; the mechanism is a document the person carries, not a duty on the next employer.
- **Royal Mail Care and Responsibility Passport: explicit exclusion** — “does not apply to agency staff, contractors or self-employed individuals.”
- College of Policing: officers, staff **and volunteers**.
- DWP Health Adjustment Passport and BSL Adjustments Planner are **person-owned job-change / AtW tools**, optional to show a prospective employer. AtW staff guide: contractors and freelancers may document an award on a passport. That is AtW portability, not an employer ticket.

**Named case that an agreed adjustment had to re-clear an SLA from scratch solely because of a manager, department or contract-type change: not found as a tribunal or Ombudsman holding.** GMB says members “are having to fight to keep existing adjustments when they get a new manager” — union assertion, not a named judgment. Khan left agency for direct employment because kit was missing; the file does not mention a passport. BDF 2023: 62% of disabled employees did not have a passport; 36% said their organisation used them.

Even inside the Civil Service, **hard adjustments do not skip procurement.** Importing-manager guidance: current manager decides which kit transfers; software compatibility; new items “may take time” and should shape the start date.

**Hard-don't.** Do not print a passport as a legal guarantee that the next manager, next contractor or next employer must implement the old list without a fresh reasonableness assessment. Do not print Lisa’s 2016 Civil Service blog (BSL interpreters on a CS passport) as proof that agency BSL users have the same instrument.

## 102.1 What a passport actually does

It is a conversation aid and a memory. The worker decides whether to show it. A new manager in the same organisation is *asked* to treat the list as the starting point. A new role can still need a new assessment — NHS Employers says so in terms. Hard kit can still hit a procurement queue — Civil Service transferring-manager guidance says so in terms.

It does not:

- create an SLA clock (prompt 97–98 still hold);
- bind a different legal employer (agency → hirer, contractor A → contractor B);
- override Schedule 8’s split of who pays for what (prompt 99);
- survive as a guarantee when the job’s essential functions change.

## 102.2 Agency and contract type

The documents that bother to draw a line, draw it at **employees**. Royal Mail draws it expressly against agency, contractors and self-employed. Everyone else is silent. Silence plus “length of contract” as a reasonableness factor (NHS Tayside) is the closest published structure: a short assignment can make an expensive adjustment less “reasonable” for that principal, which is already in the EHRC Code, not a passport rule.

DWP HAP / BSL Planner are the instruments that *are* meant to travel between employers, and they travel with the person into an AtW conversation, not into an HR ticket with a numbered SLA.

## 102.3 Unpublished or contested

- Measured rate at which a passport actually prevents a re-queue: **not found.**
- NHS bank or agency worker issued the NHS Health Passport as a matter of policy: **not found.**
- Tribunal that treated failure to honour a passport as a free-standing breach: **not found.** The duty remains s.20; the passport is evidence that the employer already knew.
- Whether SWL ICB, SWL trusts or the six boroughs issue passports to bank/agency BSL users: local FOI, not a national finding.
