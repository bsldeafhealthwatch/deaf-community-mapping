# Prompt 98 — How long does it typically take a UK employer to process an internally-agreed reasonable adjustment?

**Master index prompt 98.** Second file in the Internal HR/IT SLA batch (prompts 97–102). Compiled 10 September 2026. ## Headline

There is **no official UK statistical series** for how long employers take to process or fulfil a reasonable adjustment. ONS, EHRC and NHS England do not publish a national mean or median “ticket-to-kit” figure. What exists is a **survey stack**, not an administrative dataset:

- Business Disability Forum *Great Big Workplace Adjustments Survey* **2023** (nearly 1,500 disabled employees + ~400 managers): **1 in 8** waited **over a year**; speed “improved by 4 per cent” on the 2019 wave; only **10 per cent** said it was easy to get the adjustment. No public wait-band table for the 2023 wave comparable to 2019’s 32% under one month / 17% over a year.
- BDF **2019** wave (1,200+): **32%** under one month; the same executive summary also prints **25% over a year** in the headline bullets and **17% over a year (8% two or more years)** in the body. Treat the 25% vs 17% clash as **contested inside the source**.
- TUC **12 May 2025** (n=1,000 disabled workers): of those who had *made a request*, **82%** waited **between four months and over a year** for *agreed* adjustments; **55%** had the request only partly met or not met.
- UNISON local-government disabled members **2023**: when adjustments were agreed, **26%** waited up to six months, **12%** over six months, **8%** over a year; **11%** of requests got **no response**. An earlier 2023 conference paper used **23% waited a year or more** — figure drift, flag it.

**No published UK series splits those waits by BSL interpreter booking vs assistive technology vs workstation/software.** BDF 2023 names the mix of adjustments people *have* (flexible hours 47%, ergonomic kit 42%) but not how long each type took.

Employer-published *targets* are not measured fulfilment: NHS Employers May 2025 checklist asks whether requests are processed within two weeks; NHS Lothian policy is 20 / 30 working days for a *response*; Disability Employment Charter and the Work and Pensions Committee (21 May 2026, HC 1227) want a **two-week legal response** deadline. None of those is current law. The House of Lords Public Services Committee asked for four weeks; in September 2025 the government said it had commissioned University of York “first stage of research.” Costigan’s PMB remains at first reading (prompt 97).

Named internal-delay cases exist, but they are **fact-specific tribunal files**, not a national rate. Do not use Paul Rimmer or AtW-renewal BSL stories as employer-SLA evidence — those are DWP processes already covered elsewhere.

**Hard-don't.** A long wait in a survey is not a finding that a named employer is in breach. Do not print TUC 82% as an ONS rate. Do not paste NAO’s 109-day AtW average into this cell.

## 98.1 What the numbers actually measure

Three different clocks get collapsed in commentary. Keep them apart.

1. **Time to a decision / a response** — Charter, WPC and Costigan PMB. NHS Lothian’s 20 working days is this clock. A two-week *response* is not two-week *fulfilment*.
2. **Time to implement an agreed adjustment** — TUC 82% and UNISON 26% / 8% are this clock, on self-report. BDF “waited over a year to get their adjustments in place” is also fulfilment, not acknowledgement.
3. **AtW funding time** — DWP. An employer can agree the adjustment on day one and still be waiting for AtW to pay an interpreter. That wait is not the employer’s SLA. Point at 52–54; do not add the 109 days into an employer cell.

BDF 2019 is the only public source located this pass with an under-one-month band (32%). The 2023 wave only publishes the tail (1 in 8 over a year) plus a vague “4% faster.” Without the 2023 band table, do not invent a 2023 median.

TUC’s 82% is the figure the WPC then repeated. It is a union survey of people who had already made a request, with a bundled “4 months to over a year” band. It is not 82% of all disabled workers, and it is not 82% of BSL-using workers.

## 98.2 Named cases where *internal* delay is on the face of the judgment

Use these as documented examples. Each is one file. None is a national rate. None is a BSL-interpreter booking file.

- **Davies (Rose) v Gloucestershire Health and Care NHS Foundation Trust** — administrator after a 2001 subarachnoid haemorrhage. Tribunal found a failure to make reasonable adjustments between June 2018 and 8 July 2019, and separately September 2015 to March 2017. She resigned 24 October 2019. Award reported as £27,208.74 (injury to feelings £18,000). DSE assessment delayed; action column left blank. Source: *Employee Benefits* write-up 14 Oct 2024 of the reserved judgment. **Verified** as a named NHS internal-process case. Full judgment not re-read end-to-end this pass.
- **2400129/2017** (judgment 2 Nov 2017) — line manager knew mid-2015 exactly what workstation chair was needed; correct chair in place by 24 May 2016 or shortly after; employment ended 17 October 2016 on redundancy; tribunal treated the failure as continuing while the agreed workstation was not provided. **Verified** from the published GOV.UK PDF. Not a Deaf/BSL file.
- **Abertawe Bro Morgannwg University Local Health Board v Morgan** [2018] EWCA Civ 640 — Court of Appeal: the duty is to take the step when the employer is able to, to prevent the relevant detriment. Cited in practitioner guides as “do it when you can,” not as a days-SLA. **Verified** as the CA proposition. Not a wait-band.
- Practitioner recap *Mitchell v Marks and Spencer plc* (undue delay on a small adjustment) appears in DavidsonMorris’s April 2025 guide. The underlying judgment was not opened this pass — treat the name as **thin** until the transcript is pulled.

## 98.3 Unpublished or contested

- No BSL-specific employer booking-time series. Meeting-by-meeting interpreter fulfilment inside an employer is operationally different from a one-off chair order; the data has not been published that way.
- No public shared-services contract appendix with measured “disability ticket vs generic HR query” turnaround. That is prompt 99’s structural question.
- BDF 2019 25% vs 17% over-a-year clash on the same page.
- UNISON 23% vs 8% over-a-year across two 2023 texts.
- WPC citing TUC as “2024” when the report is 12 May 2025.
- CIPD has no days-to-fulfil product on this pass.

- “How long is reasonable” practitioner blogs (e.g. an unnamed ADHD/probation file with a two-month wait to a workplace-needs assessment) are secondary write-ups until the judgment code is attached.
