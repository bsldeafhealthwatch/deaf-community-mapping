Added: 10 September 2026. Covers internal employer Service Level Agreements (SLAs) — the target response/turnaround times HR, IT, and Facilities set themselves for processing requests — specifically where an employee's request is a disability-related reasonable adjustment (BSL interpreter booking, assistive technology, workstation change) rather than a standard/generic request, and whether HR's own internal-client structure (in-house staff vs. externally-contracted or agency/outsourced staff) produces a documented difference in whose request gets served first.

Checked first: the project index and existing batches. Prompts 92–96 (Occupational Health) cover what OH recommends and the legal basis for the reasonable-adjustment duty itself, across different working patterns (office, home, night shift, lone working) — they do not touch how fast, or in what order, HR/IT actually process a request once OH has recommended it, or whether internal SLA/ticketing structures treat a disabled employee's request differently from a standard one. Prompts 20–26 and 27–33 cover DWP/Jobcentre Plus processes, which are a different institutional actor entirely (the state, not the employer). Prompts 52–54 cover Access to Work's own funding-cap and processing-time figures — AtW is a DWP grant, not the employer's internal ticket queue. This batch is new ground: it is about the employer's own internal service-delivery machinery — the queue, the ticket, the SLA clock — not the legal duty or the diagnostic/referral layer already covered.

Anchor: a Deaf BSL-using employee whose OH report recommends an interpreter for meetings and a visual fire alarm. The recommendation is agreed, but HR's own internal SLA for processing an "adjustment request" ticket is not published anywhere the employee can see, in-house permanent staff appear (anecdotally) to get requests actioned faster than agency, contract, or outsourced staff raising the identical request through a third-party HR/managed-service provider, and there is no escalation route if the SLA is missed. This batch tests whether that pattern is documented anywhere, or is currently only an assumption.

Proposed domain: stub under the existing Welfare & Employment cluster, alongside 92–96 — this is the internal service-delivery layer that sits between an OH recommendation (92–96) and the adjustment actually existing on the ground. Not a new numbered Domain.

## Prompts

**97. Definitions and legal status — is there any regulated SLA for reasonable adjustments, and does contract type change the duty**
> As a UK employment law researcher, using the Equality Act 2010 and its statutory Code of Practice, ACAS guidance on reasonable adjustments and grievance timescales, CIPD guidance on HR service delivery, and any published tribunal or EHRC finding, establish (1) whether "reasonable adjustment" requests are subject to any statutory or regulator-set timescale at all, or whether timing is judged case-by-case under "reasonableness" with no fixed clock, (2) whether the Equality Act reasonable-adjustment duty differs in any way for an agency worker, contractor, or outsourced/third-party-employed staff member working alongside directly-employed staff (the Agency Workers Regulations 2010 and any case law on who "the employer" is for adjustment-duty purposes), (3) whether any published HR/shared-services SLA framework (CIPD, Institute of Customer Service, NHS Employers, or a public-sector shared-services contract) explicitly names disability-adjustment requests as a distinct SLA category with its own target, or whether they are typically folded into a generic "HR query" or "IT request" SLA with no disability-specific tier, and (4) what is unpublished or contested. Flag thin, unpublished, contested, or anecdotal evidence rather than presenting it as settled. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL. Do not print any claim that a specific delay or SLA structure is unlawful, discriminatory, or a breach of duty without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment — "unreasonable" under the Act is a fact-specific finding, not a fixed clock, and this prompt must not manufacture one.

**98. Documented processing-time data — reasonable adjustments and assistive technology specifically**

Flag thin, unpublished, contested, or anecdotal evidence rather than presenting it as settled. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL.

**99. In-house/permanent staff versus agency, contract, and outsourced staff — is there a documented service-priority gap**
> As a UK employment and workforce-model researcher, using the Agency Workers Regulations 2010, published outsourcing/managed-service contracts where terms are public (e.g. NHS or local-authority shared-services contracts, Freedom of Information disclosures), CIPD and Recruitment and Employment Confederation (REC) reporting on agency-worker treatment, and any tribunal or Employment Appeal Tribunal case concerning an agency or contract worker's reasonable-adjustment request being delayed or refused, establish (1) whether any published contract, policy, or case documents a structural reason why an in-house/directly-employed worker's HR request would be actioned before an agency, contract, or third-party-managed worker's identical request (e.g. separate ticketing systems, a managed-service provider with its own SLA that is slower or lower-priority than internal HR's), (2) any specific named case, FOI disclosure, or reporting describing this happening to a Deaf, BSL-using, or disabled worker specifically, and (3) what is unpublished, contested, or anecdotal — a general perception that "in-house is easier" may be widely believed but this prompt is testing whether it is documented anywhere with a named source, not restating the perception as fact. Flag thin, unpublished, contested, or anecdotal evidence rather than presenting it as settled. For each element give source title, publishing body, publication or last-updated date, and a direct URL. Do not print any claim that this pattern constitutes unlawful discrimination without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment.

### Notes for this batch

- **Gate prompt: 97.** If there is no regulated SLA for reasonable adjustments and no legal distinction by contract type (both likely), prompts 98–99 must be read as "what actually happens in practice" rather than "what is legally required," and that distinction must not collapse in any write-up.
- **Prompt 99 is the load-bearing/most-sceptical prompt for this batch.** The premise in Graeme's own framing — "HR will serve their clients [i.e. in-house] first" — is a widely-held perception, not yet a documented fact. This prompt must not let Grok manufacture a plausible-sounding confirmation from generic outsourcing literature; if nothing UK-specific and Deaf/disability-specific is found, log the absence as the finding, not a failure to search harder.
- **Hard-don't for this batch:** do not print any claim that a specific SLA structure, processing delay, or in-house/agency service difference is unlawful, discriminatory, or a breach of duty without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment. "Slower for agency workers" and "unlawfully slower for agency workers" are different claims and must not be conflated.
- **Held-back legs:** none — the batch is six prompts because the topic splits cleanly into legal basis, documented processing-time data, the in-house-vs-agency claim specifically, international comparison, lived experience, and passport portability across contract-type changes.
- **Relationship to 92–96:** this batch assumes the OH recommendation already exists (per 92–96) and picks up at the point where HR/IT must actually fulfil it — do not re-ask the OH scope question here.
- **Relationship to 52–54:** AtW processing times and the £69,260 cap are already sourced. Do not paste AtW backlog figures into an "employer SLA" cell.
