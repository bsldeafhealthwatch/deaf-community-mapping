# Grok XYZ prompt batch — Deaf/DeafBlind end-of-life & palliative care (UK-focused + international)

## 1. UK statutory services — NHS, hospice, and DWP intersection

As a UK health policy researcher specializing in disability access and the Accessible Information Standard (AIS), using NHS England guidance and policy documents, CQC inspection reports, Hospice UK and Marie Curie publications, Sign Health research, and DWP guidance on benefits assessment for terminally ill and Deaf/DeafBlind claimants (including the Special Rules for End of Life), identify the specific barriers BSL-using Deaf and DeafBlind people face when accessing end-of-life and palliative care in the UK. Cover: (1) NHS Trusts' compliance and non-compliance with the AIS for terminally ill Deaf patients, (2) hospice and palliative care providers' BSL interpreter provision and booking practices in emergency/rapid-decline situations, (3) DWP's handling of BAD deaf/DeafBlind claimants applying under Special Rules for terminal illness, including interpreter access at assessments, (4) any CQC or Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman findings specifically naming Deaf or DeafBlind patients in end-of-life contexts. For each finding, give the source title, publishing body, date, and direct URL.

## 2. DeafBlind-specific: tactile and Deafblind Manual communication in end-of-life care

As a researcher specializing in DeafBlind communication access and palliative care, using Deafblind UK, Sense (the DeafBlind charity), Sign Health, and any peer-reviewed or grey-literature sources, identify what is documented about DeafBlind people's experience of end-of-life and palliative care specifically — focusing on tactile BSL, Deafblind Manual, hands-on signing, and social haptics as communication methods with hospice, hospital, and district nursing staff. Address: (1) whether hospices and NHS palliative teams have any documented training or protocol for tactile communication with dying DeafBlind patients, (2) how pain, distress, and consent are communicated and verified when a patient is losing or has lost the ability to use tactile sign due to declining physical function, (3) family and interpreter fatigue in DeafBlind end-of-life care given the intensity of hands-on interpreting, (4) any documented cases, reports, or inquiries into DeafBlind people dying without adequate communication access. Flag clearly where evidence is thin or absent rather than filling gaps with general Deaf-community findings — the DeafBlind experience is distinct and under-researched. Cite title, source, date, and URL for each item.

## 3. International comparative research

As a comparative health policy researcher, using peer-reviewed journals (e.g., Palliative Medicine, BMJ Supportive & Palliative Care, Journal of Deaf Studies and Deaf Education), international Deaf health research bodies (e.g., National Center for Deaf Health Research in the US, European Union of the Deaf), and government or NGO health equity reports from the US, EU, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand, identify research and policy approaches to Deaf and DeafBlind end-of-life/palliative care outside the UK. Cover: (1) any studies measuring health outcome disparities for Deaf/DeafBlind patients in hospice or palliative settings, (2) countries or health systems with documented good practice (e.g., dedicated Deaf-signing palliative teams, sign-language-fluent clinicians, national interpreter-access mandates in end-of-life care), (3) any research on Deaf people's understanding of prognosis, consent, and advance directives when interpreters mediate emotionally difficult clinical conversations, (4) cross-national findings on family/caregiver burden when family members act as unpaid interpreters in a dying relative's care. For each source, provide title, author/institution, country, publication date, and URL, and note which findings might translate usefully to the UK NHS/hospice context.

## 4. Lived experience, harm, and the case for change

As a researcher documenting health inequality and patient safety in end-of-life care, using Deaf and DeafBlind advocacy organization reports (British Deaf Association, Sign Health, RNID, Deafblind UK, Sense), patient safety and ombudsman investigations, academic qualitative research, and first-person or advocacy-published accounts, identify documented cases and testimony of Deaf or DeafBlind people (or their families) experiencing harm, distress, or failures of dignity in end-of-life and palliative care due to communication access failures. Approach this with an open, non-clinical lens — the goal is to surface what has gone wrong and been felt by real people and families, not just measurable service gaps. Include: (1) any formal complaints, inquests, or ombudsman findings where lack of BSL/tactile access contributed to a poor death or distressing final days, (2) advocacy organizations' own calls for reform in this specific area, (3) any research on the emotional and psychological toll on Deaf/DeafBlind patients and families navigating dying without full language access, (4) examples — anywhere in the world — of what meaningful improvement has looked like, so this isn't only a catalogue of failure. For each source, give title, organization/author, date, and URL. Where testimony is anonymized or aggregated, note that clearly rather than presenting it as a named case.
