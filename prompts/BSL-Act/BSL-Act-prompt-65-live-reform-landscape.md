# Prompt 65 — Live legislative and policy landscape since 2022: has anyone tried to close the gap?

**Master index prompt 65.** Last file in the BSL Act 2022 scope-gap batch (prompts 62–65). Compiled 9 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow the record; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. “Strengthen the Act” rhetoric is not a Schedule-extension proposal. Do not treat Wales or Northern Ireland statutes as Westminster amendments.

**What this prompt is for.** Between Royal Assent (28 April 2022) and today, has anyone tried to put non-ministerial departments on the section 2 Schedule? Four tests, asked separately:

- (a) a Private Member’s Bill, amendment or government bill seeking to extend section 2 or the Schedule to non-ministerial departments;
- (b) an Early Day Motion, written question or oral question that specifically names the ministerial / non-ministerial distinction or HMRC’s exclusion;
- (c) a published BSL Advisory Board recommendation that the Schedule be extended;
- (d) a government commitment, in any annual BSL report or ministerial letter, to review the scope of the Schedule beyond machinery-of-government changes.

If all four are no, say so. Prompt 62 is the 2021–22 Hansard answer. Prompt 63 is the campaign archive. Prompt 64 is the live provision map. This file is the post-enactment Westminster answer, with the devolved cousins parked in their own section so they are not mistaken for a UK Schedule change.

**Index check.** `SuperGrok_Research_Prompt_Pack.md` already lists 62–65 and sets **NEXT PROMPT NUMBER TO USE: 66**. This file closes the batch.

**Method.** bills.parliament.uk (the 2022 Act page is last updated 29 April 2022 — no successor UK BSL bill); legislation.gov.uk textual amendments to the Schedule; TheyWorkForYou / questions-statements.parliament.uk written questions and Westminster Hall; edm.parliament.uk; GOV.UK BSL Advisory Board terms of reference, meeting-summary collection (to 14 January 2026) and achievements 2023–25; four annual BSL reports plus the 15 July 2026 news notice; SI 2023/424 and SI 2024/991; Wales and NI statute pages.

## Headline

**All four tests are no.**

Nobody at Westminster has, since 28 April 2022, tabled a bill or amendment to put non-ministerial departments on the Schedule. No EDM names the cut. No written or oral question located on this pass names HMRC’s exclusion or asks the Secretary of State to use — or to change — section 2(9). No published BSL Advisory Board recommendation says “extend the list.” No annual report or ministerial written statement commits to reviewing who is listed beyond machinery-of-government renaming.

What *has* happened is the thing the Delegated Powers Memorandum said would happen:

- **SI 2023/424** (3 May 2023) renamed BEIS / DCMS entries and inserted DESNZ, DSIT and DBT — new ministerial departments, not HMRC.
- **SI 2024/991** (30 October 2024) substituted “Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government” for the old DLUHC line.

That is section 2(9) working as designed. The same Memorandum said the power “has been designed so that it could not be exercised to add a non-ministerial department or other public body to the list.” Closing the gap still needs **primary legislation**. Nobody has introduced it.

The activity that looks like reform is real and is a different object: more frequent reporting (voluntary annual reports through 2027), 5-year departmental comms plans, *Locked Out*, the BSL GCSE, Access to Work evidence, and — outside Westminster — the **British Sign Language (Wales) Act 2026** and the **Sign Language Act (Northern Ireland) 2026**, both of which list public bodies. Those statutes are cousins. They do not amend 2022 c. 34.

**Finding: none located.**

bills.parliament.uk still treats the 2022 Act as a completed 2021–22 ballot bill. Last updated 29 April 2022. No 2023, 2024, 2025 or 2026 UK Public Bill with “British Sign Language” in the title that would amend section 2 or the Schedule was found on this pass.

## 65.2 Test (b) — EDM, written question or oral question that names the cut

**Finding: no question or EDM located that names HMRC’s exclusion or the ministerial / non-ministerial distinction as a problem to fix.**

What *does* exist, so the negative finding is not “Parliament never mentioned the Act”:

**Westminster Hall, 17 October 2023** (debate on the first BSL report). Tom Pursglove, then Minister for Disabled People, restated the cut in operational language — about No. 10 and the parliamentary estate, not HMRC, but the same legal fact:

> “The BSL Act places a duty on the Secretary of State for Work and Pensions to collate and publish a report on BSL use in the ministerial Departments listed in the schedule to the Act. … No. 10 and the parliamentary estate are not ministerial Departments, and there is no statutory requirement on them to report on their use of BSL.”

https://www.theyworkforyou.com/whall/?id=2023-10-17a.27.0

That is the closest post-enactment parliamentary sentence to the gap. It defends the existing list. It does not propose widening it.

## 65.3 Test (c) — published BSL Advisory Board recommendation to extend the Schedule

**Finding: none located in the published record.**

Terms of reference (updated 4 December 2025) scope the Board to ministerial departments:

> “provide advice to ministerial departments on how to initiate or enhance their use of BSL in public communications”
>
> “policy or delivery areas relating to the use of the BSL across all ministerial departments”

https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/british-sign-language-bsl-advisory-board-terms-of-reference/bsl-advisory-board-terms-of-reference-including-bsl-version

The information pack for the 2025 recruitment restates the Act as reporting “by the ministerial departments named in the schedule.”

Meeting-summary collection on GOV.UK, last updated 14 January 2026 (summaries through 3–4 December 2025). Published items cover GCSE, Access to Work, AI translation, health and social care / *Locked Out*, interpreter supply, 5-year plans. **No published summary records a recommendation to amend the Schedule.**

*Achievements 2023 to 2025* (21 May 2026): GCSE subject content, Pathways to Work response, *Locked Out*. No Schedule item.

https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/british-sign-language-bsl-advisory-board-achievements-2023-to-2025/bsl-advisory-board-achievements-2023-to-2025-bsl-and-english

*Locked Out* (27 November 2025) restates the Act as ministerial-department reporting and then spends its 12 priorities on NHS and social care. That is a different reform object (prompt 63).

**Caveat, kept visible.** Published summaries are not a complete private minute. The finding is “no published recommendation,” not “the Board never discussed it.”

Fourth report landing page (15 July 2026) still describes the duty as reporting “by the ministerial departments named in the schedule.” The accompanying news notice claims +41% year-on-year and +161% since 2023 — a communications-count story, not a scope-review story.

https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/british-sign-language-bsl-fourth-report-may-2025-to-april-2026

What government *has* committed to, so the negative finding is not “they did nothing”:

- Voluntary **annual** reports through 2027 (the Act only requires a report at least every three years). Written statements HCWS347 (19 December 2024) and HCWS861 (21 July 2025).
- A 5-year BSL *communications* plan per listed department, published July 2025. MoJ’s plan is the only one located that lists its own agencies — still films, not a VRS duty, and still plan text, not a Schedule change (prompt 64).
- A refreshed Advisory Board from December 2025.

None of those is a review of who is listed.

## 65.6 What would actually close the Westminster gap

From prompt 62, still live:

1. **Primary legislation** amending section 2(8) and the Schedule, or replacing section 2(9) so that the regulation power can add a non-ministerial department. The 2022 Delegated Powers Memorandum is explicit that the current power cannot.
2. A government bill is the realistic vehicle. A ballot PMB that tried to impose new duties on HMRC would hit the same public-expenditure / ways-and-means constraint that stripped the 2021 draft of its Council and public-bodies clauses.
3. Operational VRS at HMRC, Companies House and DVLA already exists without the Act (prompt 64). Adding a body to the Schedule would create a *reporting* duty on public-facing comms films, not an automatic extra-support VRS. Do not sell Schedule-extension as a contact-centre fix.
4. Equality Act reasonable-adjustment and Charter complaints remain the live individual hooks for a Deaf person facing HMRC (prompts 55–57, 61). Those hooks do not depend on the Schedule.

Safe sentence for the HMRC brief: “Four years after Royal Assent, the ministerial-only Schedule has been edited twice for machinery-of-government reasons and has not been the subject of a published parliamentary or Advisory Board proposal to include HMRC. Closing that specific gap still requires primary legislation.”

Unsafe sentence: “Parliament has refused to include HMRC.” Refusal implies a vote. There has been no vote.
