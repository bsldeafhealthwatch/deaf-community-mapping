# Prompt 63 — Campaign and consultation record: did BDA, RNID, SignHealth or individual campaigners raise the non-ministerial gap?

**Master index prompt 63.** Second file in the BSL Act 2022 scope-gap batch (prompts 62–65). Compiled 9 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow the record; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps. Do not manufacture a controversy the archive does not support. A genuine published silence on this specific point is the finding.

**What this prompt is for.** Establish whether any published commentary — from the Bill’s drafting (2021) through the fourth annual BSL report (15 July 2026) — *specifically names* non-ministerial departments, HMRC, or the ministerial / non-ministerial distinction as a scope limitation to be fixed. Keep that question separate from the already-documented criticism (no enforceable individual right; later non-compliance by *covered* departments). Prompt 62 is the Hansard answer. Prompts 64–65 are the provision map and the live reform landscape.

**Index check.** `SuperGrok_Research_Prompt_Pack.md` already lists 62–65 and sets **NEXT PROMPT NUMBER TO USE: 66**. This file is the research answer to prompt 63.

**Method.** Live sites and search indexes for: bda.org.uk (including BSL Act Now and press releases); rnid.org.uk; signhealth.org.uk; Disability News Service BSL tag (full run, not only the two first-pass articles); limpingchicken.com; GOV.UK BSL Advisory Board meeting summaries and *Locked Out* (27 November 2025); the four annual BSL reports. Targeted strings: HMRC, “non-ministerial”, Charity Commission, Land Registry, “Crown Prosecution”, Ofsted, “Food Standards”, “extend the Schedule”, “arms-length”. Wayback was not required for the live pages that still carry the 2021–22 campaign copy. No formal pre-legislative consultation document for this Private Member’s Bill was located — that absence is recorded below, not filled.

## Headline

**Nothing found on the specific point.**

No published BDA, RNID, SignHealth, Disability News Service or Limping Chicken piece located on this pass names HMRC, the Charity Commission, HM Land Registry, the CPS, Ofsted, the Food Standards Agency, or the ministerial / non-ministerial split as a gap in the BSL Act 2022. No Deaf-led organisation located here has, since April 2022, published a policy ask that the Schedule be extended or that an equivalent reporting duty be created for non-ministerial departments.

That is not “campaigners said nothing about the Bill.” They said a great deal. The two documented lines of criticism are different from this question and must not be conflated:

1. **2022, Jeff McWhinney and DNS — “tokenistic / no new rights.”** The objection is clause 1(2) (“does not affect the operation of any enactment or rule of law”) plus the drop from a statutory Council and duties on public bodies to a reporting-and-guidance PMB. McWhinney *did* notice that the Bill only reports on “20 of its departments.” He used that as evidence the Bill was thin, not as a demand to add HMRC.
2. **August 2023, DNS — “abysmal / betrayal.”** The objection is that **11 of the 20 departments already on the Schedule** produced zero BSL in the first reporting period. Treasury is named *as a covered department that did nothing*. HMRC is not mentioned. The target is non-compliance inside the list, not exclusion from the list.

Post-2022 organisational energy went to implementation of the existing duty (annual reports, 5-year plans, BSL Advisory Board, GCS guidance), to *Locked Out* health-and-social-care recommendations, to Access to Work, and to the **Wales and Northern Ireland bills**, which *do* list public bodies. That last fact is the nearest cousin: when Deaf organisations had a later chance to design a list of obligated bodies, they argued for public bodies. They have not been recorded applying that argument back to the 2022 UK Schedule.

The first-pass working hypothesis survives the deeper pass: the gap exists; it was probably not concealed; it was not the campaignable failure anyone chose.

## 63.1 What the two documented criticisms actually say

### A. February 2022 — “tokenistic,” no enforceable individual right

**Disability News Service, John Pring, 3 February 2022**, “Deaf campaigner calls on BSL-users to reject ‘tokenistic’ new bill.”  
https://www.disabilitynewsservice.com/deaf-campaigner-calls-on-bsl-users-to-reject-tokenistic-new-bill/

Jeff McWhinney (former BDA chief executive; founder of SignVideo):

> “They believe they are getting extra rights for BSL. They are not.”
>
> “Deaf people must ask parliament to translate the bill into BSL so they can really understand what they are not getting, and then they can discuss it.”
>
> The bill was “a compromise too far.” “Maybe at the beginning there was a very clear objective that we wanted to achieve and see in the bill, but it has been eroded. It is better to say no to the bill and force people into action to make sure it is done right.”

DNS’s own gloss, in the same piece:

> Cooper’s bill would recognise BSL as “a language of England, Wales and Scotland”, but the bill also states that this “does not affect the operation of any enactment or rule of law”.
>
> “This appears to mean that the bill would provide a symbolic recognition of the language rather than offering any new rights for BSL-users who want the right to BSL interpreters when accessing public services such as the NHS, local government and education.”
>
> McWhinney “pointed out that the bill appears to promise only new guidance on the ‘promotion and facilitation’ of BSL, and regular reports by the government on what **20 of its departments** have done to ‘promote or facilitate the use of British Sign Language in its communications with the public’.”

**That “20 of its departments” sentence is the closest the 2022 archive comes to the Schedule.** It is a count of the ministerial list, used to show thinness. It does not name a missing body and does not ask for the list to be widened.

Same week, Limping Chicken reprinted the DNS article (4 February 2022).  
https://limpingchicken.com/2022/02/04/john-pring-deaf-campaigner-calls-on-bsl-users-to-reject-tokenistic-new-bill/

**DNS, 10 February 2022**, “BSL bill ‘will not provide Deaf people with any new rights.’”  
https://disabilitynewsservice.com/bsl-bill-will-not-provide-deaf-people-with-any-new-rights

Facebook panel after Second Reading. McWhinney again: the bill “should be more robust” and “should go further”; it “does not mention individuals’ rights.” The amendment he is recorded as calling for is:

> “McWhinney called for an amendment to the bill that stated that having access in BSL should be a reasonable adjustment under the Equality Act when accessing public services.”

That is an Equality Act hook, not a Schedule-extension hook. BDA, RNID and NDCS had “failed to comment on the concerns” when DNS first approached them (3 February piece). Mark Atkinson (RNID CEO) had already called Second Reading “a momentous day.”

**DNS, 28 April 2022**, “Controversial BSL bill set to become law.” Repeats the “no new rights” warning; records Rosie Cooper saying the Act creates “a mechanism for Deaf people to achieve equal access to public services.” Still no NMD.

**Confidence.** Verified that these articles exist and say what is quoted. Verified that they do not name HMRC or the ministerial/non-ministerial distinction.

### B. August 2023 — “abysmal / betrayal,” non-compliance by covered departments

**Disability News Service, 3 August 2023**, “‘Abysmal’ first year figures show government’s BSL Act ‘betrayal’.”  
https://www.disabilitynewsservice.com/abysmal-first-year-figures-show-governments-bsl-act-betrayal/

The first BSL report (31 July 2023) showed 11 of 20 Schedule departments produced no BSL translation of a public announcement, publication or press conference in the first ten months. Named zeros include DEFRA, DLUHC and **the Treasury**.

Lee Starr-Elliott: the Act was a “token vanity project.” Kerena Marchant: “This is a betrayal of the Deaf community. The figures are abysmal and prove the worthlessness of this legislation.” David Buxton (former BDA chair, BSL Act Now lead): “disappointed” by how few departments had acted; still argued the Bill unlocked later wins (BSL GCSE, Deaf jurors); wanted “a stronger BSL Act with extra rights.” BDA spokesperson: the report was “quite ambiguous” with “very shallow efforts”; government “continues to fall short in adequately engaging with BSL users.”

**Treasury is in this article because it is on the Schedule and scored zero.** HMRC is the operational tax body that sits next to Treasury and is *not* on the Schedule. DNS does not make that distinction. Neither do the named campaigners in the piece.

**Confidence.** Verified. Do not write “campaigners attacked the exclusion of HMRC in 2023.” They attacked zeros *inside* the list.

## 63.2 Organisational archives, 2021–2026

### British Deaf Association

**BSL Act Now page (live 9 September 2026).** Campaign launched 18 March 2021. Coalition named: RNID, RAD, Signature, iBSL, SignHealth, NDCS, NRCPD, Black Deaf UK. Outcome copy:

> “The Act legally recognises British Sign Language as a language of England, Wales and Scotland. It also requires UK Government departments to report on how they promote and facilitate the use of BSL and provides for guidance on meeting the needs of people who use BSL as their first or preferred language.”

No HMRC. No “non-ministerial.” No ask to extend the Schedule.  
https://bda.org.uk/bsl-act-now/

**BDA press release after Lords Second Reading (25 March 2022).** Lists the three enacted limbs (recognition; report by ministerial government departments; guidance). One sentence then over-reaches the statute:

> guidance “setting out how government departments and **public bodies** must meet the needs of the estimated 87,000 Deaf people…”

That is the campaign’s hoped-for reach of *guidance*, not a published demand to amend the Schedule. David Buxton in the same release: “If the BSL Bill passes into law, it won’t be the end of the road – it will be the start of the road.”  
https://bda.org.uk/british-sign-language-bill-passes-2nd-reading-house-lords/

**BDA comment on the second annual report (press release page “BSL Advisory Board”, text addresses the 2023–24 report figures).** Welcomes Labour continuing annual reports. Wants the numbers “contextualised against a wider annual review of implementation of the BSL Act 2022.” Calls 176 BSL outputs in a year “a drop in the ocean” against the volume of English-language government communications. Criticises departments that will produce BSL “where there is opportunity to do so.” Still a criticism of *how the existing duty is used*, not of who is outside it.  
https://bda.org.uk/bsl-advisory-board/

**BDA annual report 2023–24 (PDF).** Proud of leading BSL Act Now. Priorities going forward: bilingual practice, language-planning research, professional training. No Schedule-extension ask.  
https://bda.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/BSL-Annual-Report-2023-2024-min.pdf

**Negative finding, BDA.** No page located that names HMRC or asks to add non-ministerial departments.

## 63.3 Was there a pre-legislative consultation to search?

**No formal government consultation on the British Sign Language Bill 2021–22 was located.** It was a ballot Private Member’s Bill. CBP-9446 records that Public Bill Committee took **no oral evidence and received no written evidence**. Stakeholder forums happened (Chloe Smith, Second Reading: “my Department and others have held a number of stakeholder forums”), but those are not a published consultation response document.

What exists instead:

- BSL Act Now campaign letters to MPs (BDA: 1,358 letters, 572 MPs, 88% reached).
- The 6 October 2021 Rosie Cooper / Deaf-sector meeting (SignHealth note).
- The government-supported rewrite of the clauses between the June 2021 ballot title (Council + public bodies) and the January 2022 print — the rewrite itself is the “consultation” outcome, and it is the moment the public-bodies framing left the Bill (see prompt 62).

Absence of a published consultation response is therefore expected. It is not a failed search.

The documented 2023 objection was zero output from 11 of the 20 departments already on the Schedule (DNS; Kerena Marchant; Lee Starr-Elliott). Those are not the same claim as ‘HMRC is missing from the list.’”
- “McWhinney did note that the Bill only required reports from ‘20 of its departments.’ He used the number to show the Bill was thin. He did not name a body that should have been the 21st.”

Unsafe sentences:

- “Campaigners were unaware of the gap.” (Awareness is not knowable from published pages. The finding is *they did not publish this ask*.)
- “BDA covered up the NMD cut.” (No evidence.)
- “SignHealth says the Act already covers public services, therefore they think HMRC is in scope.” (The Healthcare-in-BSL rights box is loose wording, not a legal reading.)
