# Prompt 71 — Lived experience and the case for change (later life)

**Master index prompt 71.** Sixth and last file in the Deaf/DeafBlind older people batch (prompts 66–71). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Put named and anonymised testimony next to the structural findings from 66–70, then state what would have to change for Domain 17 to be more than a folder label. Where testimony is anonymised or aggregated, that is said in the same sentence. Do not invent a composite “Deaf pensioner told us.”

**Cross-reference, do not duplicate.** Domain 16 / AIS prompt 37 already hold the dying and hospital cases: **Alan Graham** (PHSO 11 February 2026), **UHB Mr X** (CQC FPNs November 2022), **PHSO Samantha** (August 2026), **PHSO Mrs E** (2014), **Francesca Bussey** and **Pearl Clinton** (family-as-interpreter at the deathbed / breaking news that a parent or grandmother is dying). Those are about dying and acute care. This file uses them only as a pointer. The later-life question is what happens in the years *before* that bed.

**Index check.** Batch 66–71 is now complete as markdown. Proposed domain, once verified: **Domain 17 — Ageing, retirement, and later-life isolation.** Do not fold it into Domain 16.

## Headline

The published later-life record is short, and that shortness is the case.

There is **one** Independent Case Examiner investigation that names a deaf customer in the same breath as State Pension and Pension Credit (ICE annual report 2024/25): payments stopped for years after an address failure; combined redress **nearly £55,000**. There is **one** Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman decision that walks an older BSL-first resident through a mainstream home with no signing staff and a DoLS visit done in **pen and paper** (24 017 923, Hammersmith & Fulham, 29 October 2025, Mr Y). There is **one** named 83-year-old club member on the national news saying she will feel “lonely and lost” if the building closes (Brenda Maynard, Portsmouth, January 2025). *Locked Out* prints the care-setting line “I’m lonely. No one to talk to” and the housebound line about **£100** outings, both anonymised.

That is not a literature. It is a handful of official files plus club-closure reporting plus one government-commissioned qualitative report. The advocacy demand that follows is already written down, and does not need new rhetoric: *Locked Out* priority 12 asks each UK nation for a strategy to increase specialist BSL residential care, day care and BSL in-home support. Prompt 67 shows why — Care Act guidance gives deafblind adults a specialist assessor and does not give the same named duty to BSL-using Deaf adults who are not deafblind. Prompt 68 shows there is no official loneliness rate for this group. Prompt 70 shows other countries have built a campus, a 65+ interpreting fund, or a mobile Deaf care team instead of waiting for the rate.

The case for Domain 17 is therefore: **later life is visible in law and in clubs, and almost invisible in official statistics.** Closing the domain means counting, then commissioning, not adding another awareness week.

## 71.1 Named and official cases that belong in later life

Use the name only where the person, the family, or the official body has put it in the public domain.

### ICE 2024/25 — anonymised deaf customer, State Pension and Pension Credit

Independent Case Examiner annual report 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025, “When urgent action is needed.”

The customer is **anonymised** and described as **deaf**, with very poor physical health and housing problems, long-term DLA. In 2018 DWP started the DLA-to-PIP migration. Extra support / a visiting officer was not arranged; DLA closed. A new address was not put on the record. An important letter went to the old address, came back, and was not chased. Payments were suspended and the suspension was not reviewed. From **2019** the customer received no pension or benefit. By the time they were over State Pension age they could not claim PIP; an Attendance Allowance form was issued instead. ICE recorded:

- State Pension reinstated with arrears of **just under £30,000** (2019–2023)
- Pension Credit mishandled when arrears were treated as capital
- Combined redress **nearly £55,000** plus a **£3,000** consolatory payment

This is **not** a finding that Pension Service refused a BSL interpreter on a call. It is a finding that a deaf pensioner sat outside the payment system for years after an address and suspension failure. Logged in full in prompt 66. It is the only ICE case in that report that names deafness next to State Pension.

### LGSCO 24 017 923 — Mr Y, Hammersmith & Fulham, 29 October 2025

Profoundly deaf, BSL first language, placed in a mainstream care home whose staff had **no BSL or deaf-awareness training**, contrary to the care plan. A Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards visit was completed with **pen and paper**. He died shortly after transfer back to another home. Fault found. £450 to the granddaughter and a process review.

This is the later-life isolation case. It is not SWL. It is not a CQC rating. Recording the BSL need in the plan was not fulfilment. Full write-up in prompt 67.

### Brenda Maynard, 83, Portsmouth Deaf Club — BBC, 24 January 2025

Named. Profoundly deaf, BSL. Member since she was seven — **76 years**. About 50 members used the Arundel Street centre. The Portsmouth Deaf Association said the building was no longer viable; close 1 February 2025. Her line, on the record: she will feel “lonely and lost”; “all my favourite people are here.”

This is club infrastructure, not a care-home placement. It is what prompt 68 measured as the thinning peer network. The association was looking for other premises; replacement of the *room* is not the same as replacement of the 76-year relationship.

## 71.2 Anonymised and aggregated testimony — later life, not dying

Do not present these as named cases.

### *Locked Out* lived-experience lines (27 November 2025)

Published by the BSL Advisory Board on GOV.UK. Anonymised.

- Care setting: “I’m lonely. No one to talk to. I watch people laugh and talk. I have to watch and wait for my moment to ask for a drink. I can’t say to staff, ‘I’d like a little more or less in my drink’. I try to teach staff BSL but they don’t understand.”
- Housebound / cost of the peer network: “As of now, I’m housebound. I would go on outings or socials but it costs me over £100 each time I go out to meet friends, and often I can’t find anyone to take me.”

These two lines are the isolation-plus-transport pair that prompts 68 and 69 quantified as a mechanism without a national rate.

### Manchester Deaf Centre ageing survey case study “Margret, 73”

Lottery-funded report, circulated March 2020. The name in the report is a **case-study name**. Treat as anonymised composite / permitted case-study identity, not a consenting public figure.

Born outside Stockport, residential Deaf school in South Wales, husband Martin died, son in Vancouver. Never learned to drive. “Shies away from the ‘hearing’ world.” Struggles with NHS, bank, HMRC. Local Deaf centre taught FaceTime/Skype while Martin was alive. After the death, isolation increased. Survey n=96 around that centre: 60% knew other Deaf people who were lonely; 74% stayed out of the hearing community.

### Wales older-Deaf focus groups (University of Manchester / older people in Wales paper)

Anonymised focus-group speech, published in an older-people-in-Wales research PDF:

- “It’s your first language and if you’re in a care home and you’re the only deaf person, then you’re going to have no communication with anybody.”
- “I feel it would be lonely. There’s no staff that can sign or no nursing staff can sign.”
- “I think if there was no Sign Language there then I think you might actually deteriorate quickly.”
- Preference, if a Deaf home is impossible: “two or three other Deaf people” in the same home so staff might learn some sign.

This is anticipated later life, not a placement complaint. It matches Mr Y after the fact.

### Simcock — eight older adults ageing with deafblindness (UK, interviews 2014–16)

Anonymised. Themes already in prompts 68 and 70: human contact as “medicine for loneliness”; Deaf peers walking away because tactile signing is effortful; fear of being cut off from “the rest of the world.” First UK study of *felt vulnerability* in this group. n=8.

### RNID *It Does Matter* BSL-user quotes (Dec 2024)

Anonymised, mixed ages, n BSL = 180 inside a larger deaf/HoH sample. Not later-life specific. Texture only: “People find it hard to talk to me so prefer to avoid me.” 36% of those who had faced negative attitudes felt lonely or isolated. Do not age-band it.

### BDA England Loneliness Project (to March 2022)

BDA says Deaf people “of all ages and especially… Deaf men” were willing to share experiences at 14 club and school visits (~300 people). The end-of-project report promised to DCMS was **not found as a public statistical product** on the 68 pass. Treat the page as delivery evidence, not as published testimony.

## 71.4 What the batch actually found — six lines

1. **Pensions (66).** VRS button exists. Pension Credit take-up is **62%** nationally and is **not** published by BSL or deafness. One ICE deaf-and-pension file. No PHSO Pension Service / Deaf case.
2. **Care homes (67).** Deafblind adults have a specialist-assessor duty; BSL-using Deaf adults who are not deafblind do not. CQC does not tag BSL-specialist homes. SWL named specialist bed-stock found: Longley Road, six places. Anchor isolation case is LGSCO Mr Y, not SWL.
3. **Loneliness (68).** No official UK rate. Closest numbers: Manchester n=96 (60% know lonely Deaf peers) and RNID 36% lonely/isolated in a BSL stigma subset. Club closures are local stories, not a register.
4. **Transport (69).** Profoundly/severely deaf (70 dBHL+ both ears) is a statutory Freedom Pass category. There is no London companion Freedom Pass. Taxicard is not a Deaf product. TfL does not run live next-stop BSL. Dual-sensory travellers lose audio and screens together.
5. **International (70).** Named alternatives exist: De Gelderhorst (117 apartments + nursing), Bob Rumball (64 beds), Australia NSLP (65+ not-on-NDIS interpreting fund), Swedish mobile Deaf team. No country publishes an ONS-style signer-loneliness rate either.
6. **Testimony (71).** Official later-life files can be counted on one hand. Dying files are longer, and they are the wrong domain.

## 71.5 The case for change — without a banner

What would have to be true for Domain 17 to earn the number.

**Count first.** The FOI list already written in this batch:

- Six SWL local authorities: placements in the last three years that recorded a BSL / Deaf / DeafBlind communication need (prompt 67).
- Six SWL boroughs: Disabled Freedom Passes in issue under “profoundly or severely deaf” and under “blind or partially sighted”; any companion / +C concession (prompt 69).
- Six community-transport operators + Dial-a-Ride: how a BSL user books, and how many such trips in 12 months (prompt 69).
- ICE / PHSO / LGSCO: keep the search live for a *named* DeafBlind later-life file. Absence is still the finding until one appears.

**Close the Care Act split, or say you will not.** *Locked Out* has already asked for BSL-user recognition “similar to that granted deafblind individuals.” That is a DHSC / statutory-guidance decision. Domain 17 should track whether the next Care Act guidance revision names BSL users in the 6.91–6.96 style, or leaves them on 6.80.

**Choose a delivery model.** Prompt 70 left four spends on the table: a campus, a mobile signing workforce, a 65+ interpreting fund (the Australian NSLP shape), or rights inside generic homes (the AIS / Equality Act theory). England currently runs the last of those plus a handful of specialist beds. A Domain 17 briefing that does not pick among those four is a reading list.

**Do not wait for a loneliness prevalence.** Prompt 68’s instruction stands: thinness is the headline. A Community Life Survey cut by BSL would be useful. It is not a precondition for commissioning a Croydon-style room or a Longley Road-style house.

**Keep dying in Domain 16.** Family-as-interpreter at the deathbed is already evidenced. Re-using Alan Graham as an ageing story dilutes both domains.
