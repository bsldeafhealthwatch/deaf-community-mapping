# Prompt 70 — International comparative research: later-life Deaf / DeafBlind provision

**Master index prompt 70.** Fifth file in the Deaf/DeafBlind older people batch (prompts 66–71). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Place the UK findings from prompts 66–69 next to named overseas products — specialist sign-language care homes, 65+ interpreting funds, mobile signing care teams, personal interpreter rights — so the batch is not only a catalogue of UK absence. Domain 16 already holds international *palliative* comparators (Minnesota / Ottawa / Jacksonville volunteer models; Canada / Australia sign-language palliative video packs). This file is **ageing and everyday later life**, not dying.

**Index check.** `SuperGrok_Research_Prompt_Pack.md` lists 66–71 as pensions, care homes, isolation, transport, **international comparative**, lived experience. This is the international file. Prompt 71 carries testimony.

**Do not substitute.** A US “deaf-friendly senior living” marketing page that means hearing-aid loops is not a BSL/ASL cultural home. NDIS Specialist Disability Accommodation is not automatically available to every Deaf adult. A Finnish interpreter *right* is not a UK Freedom Pass.

## Headline

The United Kingdom is not empty of later-life access products. Prompt 66 already logged an inbound Pension Service VRS button. Prompt 67 logged one CQC-Outstanding BSL home inside the SWL footprint (SignHealth Longley Road, six places, mental-health / learning-disability registration). What the UK does **not** publish is a national later-life *estate* or a 65+ interpreting fund for people who age out of working-age disability schemes.

Four overseas products are specific enough to sit on a briefing slide without being a general “they do disability better” claim:

1. **De Gelderhorst, Ede, Netherlands** — the country’s only housing-and-care centre for older Deaf people. Live stock on the operator’s own pages / LinkedIn: **117** independent-living apartments, **33** care-home places, **51** psychogeriatric nursing places. Nederlandse Gebarentaal is the house language; staff are required to sign. Academic description (2019): atrium and open balconies designed for visual conversation across floors. There is a waiting list.
2. **Bob Rumball Home for the Deaf, Barrie, Ontario** — described by the operator as **the only long-term care facility in Canada specifically designed for Deaf seniors**. **64** beds, opened 2006, one-storey, visual design (large windows, courtyards). Sits on top of earlier Toronto supportive-housing floors that had nowhere to send residents when they needed nursing care. Canadian Association of the Deaf policy position: at least one such residence per province.
3. **Australia National Sign Language Program (NSLP)** — a Commonwealth fund, delivered by Deaf Connect, giving **free** Auslan / First Nations sign / tactile and hand-over-hand interpreting to Deaf, Deafblind and hard-of-hearing people **aged 65+** (50+ for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people) who are **not eligible for the NDIS**. Covers aged-care services, and from **1 July 2024** Medicare-rebate health appointments, plus social, banking, funerals. This is the cleanest analogue to the UK problem of “too old for working-age disability support, too language-specific for generic older-people’s services.”
4. **Sweden, regional mobile team of Deaf assistant nurses** — NGO / Deaf-club pilot in one southern county. First Swedish study: Karlsson, Mahmud, Andersson, Fristedt et al., *BMC Geriatrics*, 15 December 2025; Jönköping University write-up 10 March 2026. Qualitative (four participants, 15 interviews over two years). Participants described everyday tasks, authority conversations and “a chat” becoming possible in Swedish Sign Language. Authors say scale it; municipal sign-language training for care staff remains **voluntary**, not statutory.

The United States has a **directory**, not a system: NAD / Deaf Seniors of America list independent-living and nursing sites (Apache ASL Trails, Arizona; New England Homes for the Deaf, Danvers MA, founded 1901 — 51-bed rest home + 30-bed skilled nursing, staff educated in ASL and Deaf culture). Access is Fair Housing law plus Medicaid / private pay, not an NHS-style entitlement.

Finland’s Kela interpreter service is a **personal right** for people with hearing, dual-sensory or speech impairment who have a Finnish municipality of residence — closer to an individual budget than to a care home. Scotland, inside the UK, is the tighter legal comparator on language (BSL Act 2015, Contact Scotland BSL 24/7 VRS) and still does not run a Gelderhorst-scale home.

None of these products has a published RCT that “solves loneliness.” They are fulfilment models. The UK question they pose is: which of them is copyable under Care Act / NHS / DWP rules, and which is a different welfare state.

| United Kingdom | SignHealth Longley Road (SWL) plus the small named list in prompt 67 | No national Deaf-elder campus. Locked Out still wrote “only 2 residential homes that cater specifically to BSL users” — contested against the named list in prompt 67, still a thin estate | Longley Road: six places, Outstanding, MH/LD registration — not a generic 85-year-old BSL pathway | BSL-using staff on that site. No UK equivalent of Gelderhorst’s 117 apartments |

**What “good” looks like in the Dutch and Canadian buildings, stripped of advocacy tone:** the language of the house is the residents’ language; a material share of staff are Deaf or required to sign; the architecture assumes visual conversation (atrium, balconies, sightlines, lighting); there is a step from independent apartments into nursing *without* leaving the language community. The UK care-home market can buy loops and flashing fire alarms. It does not, on the published stock, buy that package at national scale.

Canadian Association of the Deaf position (page last dated 2014 on the live URL, still the published policy): governments should fund at least one Deaf seniors’ residence per province, prefer Deaf caregivers, and write visual alarms / videophones into nursing-home law. That is a demand, not a delivered map. Barrie remains the named LTC site.

## 70.2 Interpreting and VRS as a later-life entitlement

The design choice that matters is whether an older signer can book language support for **aged care, a GP, a bank and a funeral** without being on a working-age disability scheme.

| Country | Product | Who pays | Who is in | Hours / scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Australia | **National Sign Language Program (NSLP)**, delivered by Deaf Connect | Commonwealth. Replaces the old NABS medical-interpreting offer for this age group | Deaf, Deafblind or hard of hearing; **65+** (50+ Indigenous); **not NDIS-eligible** | 7 days, including after hours and emergencies. F2F and VRI. Auslan, First Nations sign, ASL, International Sign, Signed English, tactile / hand-over-hand. Aged-care services; Medicare appointments from 1 Jul 2024; social and professional (bank, funeral) |
| Finland | **Kela interpreter service for persons with disabilities** | Social-insurance institution. Separate rule: when the *authority* summons the person, the authority pays | Hearing impairment, dual sensory, or speech impairment; can use a working communication method; Finnish municipality of residence | Personal right, not a 65+ bolt-on. Social-care client law also requires mother tongue and culture to be taken into account |
| United States | FCC-funded **VRS** | Telecoms levy, 24/7 | ASL users, any age | Phone-equivalent. Does not by itself staff a nursing home |
| Canada | **VRS 24/7** (CRTC comparison classifies Canada with US, France, Germany, Sweden) | Telecoms / public | Provincial overlay varies | VRS ≠ a care-home language policy |
| Sweden | Interpreter access plus the **mobile Deaf care team** (below) | Mixed | Team was a regional pilot, not a national right | Care-staff signing is voluntary for municipalities, with some state training support |
| Scotland (UK) | **Contact Scotland BSL** | Scottish Government contract (Sign Solutions from 1 Dec 2025; historic envelope £3m ex VAT — project memory) | Scotland-resident users, any UK number, **not** international or work calls | 24/7 VRS. Social-landlord good-practice examples (Ayrshire Housing homepage link) exist. Does not create a specialist care home |
| England / SWL | AIS in health and adult social care; EA reasonable adjustment elsewhere; inbound Pension Service VRS (prompt 66); no 65+ interpreting fund for people outside social-care packages | Fragmented | AIS does not hard-default F2F BSL for planned appointments (prompt 34–37) | No published NSLP equivalent |

Australia’s cut at 65 is the policy fact to steal or reject, not to moralise. The NDIS generally does not take new participants at that age; older Deaf Australians who never held a disability plan would otherwise drop into generic My Aged Care. NSLP is the patch. The UK equivalent gap is: Care Act assessment (prompt 67) plus Pension Service VRS (prompt 66) plus whatever a local authority will fund as a communication need — no named national 65+ interpreting budget for GP, bank and funeral.

Deaf Connect **Ageing Well** is the matching home-care offer: Auslan-delivered personal care, Support at Home, and NSW Social Support Individual hours. That is a commissioned aged-care provider working in sign, not a volunteer lunch club.

## 70.3 Workforce models that stay in the person’s language

**Sweden — mobile Deaf assistant-nurse team**

- Who ran it: a regional Deaf association (NGO), as a complement to ordinary municipal old-age care, in one southern county.
- Who delivered it: Deaf assistant nurses working in Swedish Sign Language, in people’s homes and in residential settings.
- What the study did: 15 interviews with four participants over two years; Deaf signing interviewers, no interpreter in the research conversation.
- What participants said, as reported: everyday tasks (cleaning, shopping, walks), help that ordinary care does not always do (sorting the home, hobbies, sitting down to talk), and support in conversations with authorities and health staff. Authors report better psychological wellbeing, happiness and participation in decisions.
- Limits: n=4. Pilot, not a national programme. Jönköping University (10 March 2026) says scale it; also says municipal sign-language training for care staff is voluntary.
- UK analogue that does **not** yet exist as a published ICB or local-authority product: a peripatetic BSL-using home-care team attached to a Deaf organisation, visiting people who will never get a place at Longley Road.

**United States — Fair Housing as the legal hook inside generic homes**

NAD points at the Fair Housing Justice Center film *A Home for Henry* (nursing homes and assisted living). That is a rights-enforcement model: the home is ordinary, the duty is not to discriminate, visual alarms and effective communication are demanded *into* the mainstream bed. It is the opposite of Gelderhorst (build a Deaf house) and closer to what AIS + Equality Act are supposed to do in an English care home. Prompt 67 already showed how thin that looks in CQC data.

**Finland — personal interpreter hours rather than a Deaf ward**

Kela hours travel with the person. Social-care meetings the *office* initiated are the office’s bill. That splits the invoice. It does not create a signing night staff.

## 70.4 Isolation programmes and older-Deaf research abroad

Already used in prompt 68, restated only as comparators:

- Finnish Association of the Deaf survey 2012, n=421, **36%** lonely at least sometimes — **Finland**, not UK.
- JDSDE November 2025 qualitative study of **12** older sign-language users — **Finland**. Authors call loneliness in this group under-studied.
- WFDB report on older people with deafblindness (2023): isolation mentioned by virtually all interviewees; notes the UK pattern of disability services to 65 then generic older-people’s services (citing Simcock).
- Simcock UK IPA (n=8) remains the named *British* qualitative set on ageing *with* deafblindness.

No country found on this pass publishes an official later-life loneliness rate for sign-language users that ONS could copy. The research gap is international, not only English.

Men’s Sheds (Australia-origin, now Canada and UK) is a retirement-isolation product for men. It is not a Deaf product unless a local shed signs. Do not list it as a BSL comparator.

## 70.5 What transfers — and what does not

Copyable *ideas* under current UK law, without pretending the funding vehicle exists:

| Overseas product | Closest UK hook | Why it is not plug-and-play |
|---|---|---|
| De Gelderhorst campus | Care Act placement + specialist residential commissioning; CQC rating already used at Longley Road | Needs a capital estate and a staffed NGT/BSL house language. England has six-place and similar homes, not 117 apartments + nursing on one site |
| Bob Rumball 64-bed LTC | Same | One home for a whole country is also a travel-and-uprooting cost (prompt 69). Canada’s “only one” is a warning as well as a model |
| Australia NSLP | AIS booking + Care Act communication need + a DHSC or DWP 65+ interpreting line | NDIS age-cut created the political case. UK has no equivalent single cut-off speech; the gap is still real for people with no social-care package |
| Sweden mobile Deaf team | Local-authority home care / direct payments; VCSE contract; possible ICB prevention spend | Evidence base is four people. Needs a Deaf-led provider and signing assistant nurses, not a generic “sensory support” visit |
| Finland Kela hours | Personal budget / direct payment to buy interpreters | Kela is a national social-insurance right. England’s interpreter is still booked setting-by-setting |
| Scotland Contact Scotland BSL | Already exists for Scotland-resident users | Does not staff a care home at 02:00. England has no equivalent any-number VRS for public services |
| US Fair Housing inside generic homes | Equality Act + AIS + CQC | That *is* the UK theory. Prompt 67 is the UK practice |

*Locked Out* (27 November 2025) already asked England to “develop a national strategy… to significantly increase the number of specialist BSL residential care homes, day care centres and BSL in-home support.” The overseas list above is the content that strategy would have to choose between: **campus**, **mobile team**, **personal interpreter hours**, or **rights inside generic homes**. They are different spends.

## What not to write

- Do not write “other countries have solved Deaf-elder isolation.” They have named buildings and funds. Loneliness rates for signers are missing there too.
- Do not write that every Deaf Australian gets SDA housing. SDA is high-need NDIS accommodation.
- Do not treat the NAD directory as a count of accessible US nursing-home beds.
- Do not import AIS language into Australian aged care or Finnish Kela.
- Do not recycle Domain 16 palliative examples (Trinity Hospice, St Helena VRI, Minnesota volunteers) as if they were later-life housing policy.
- Do not present the Swedish n=4 study as a national rollout.
