# Prompt 69 — Transport, mobility, and getting to appointments and social life

**Master index prompt 69.** Fourth file in the Deaf/DeafBlind older people batch (prompts 66–71). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Test whether the concession products that look generous on paper (Disabled Person’s Freedom Pass, Older Person’s Freedom Pass, ENCTS, Taxicard, Dial-a-Ride, borough community transport) actually move an older BSL-using or DeafBlind person to a hospital, a GP, or a Deaf lunch club. Four questions: (1) what TfL and national rail actually provide in BSL or dual-sensory form; (2) who qualifies, in SWL, and whether the application is usable; (3) documented missed trips; (4) whether SWL community-transport schemes can take a BSL booking.

**Do not assume.** Profoundly or severely deaf is a statutory ENCTS / Freedom Pass category. Moderate age-related hearing loss is not. A companion pass is a local extra under the Transport Act 1985, not a London-wide right. Taxicard is a mobility / severe-sight scheme, not a Deafness scheme. Prompt 68 already logged the £100-outing and “nobody to take me” mechanism; this file tests the products that are supposed to close it.

---

## Headline

On paper, an older Deaf Londoner has two doors in. **Profoundly or severely deaf** (DfT: **70 dBHL or more in both ears**) is a statutory category for the Disabled Person’s Freedom Pass and the English National Concessionary Travel Scheme. **State pension age** (66 in 2026) opens the Older Person’s Freedom Pass for every London resident, Deaf or not. If both apply, London Councils tell people to take the **Disabled** pass — it works on TfL at all times, not from 09:00.

That is eligibility, not usability.

- There is **no London-wide companion Freedom Pass**. Carers do not get a pass of their own. ENCTS guidance lets travel concession authorities fund companion travel as a *discretionary* extra; East Riding and some shire councils print a “+C” on the pass. SWL borough pages found on this pass do not advertise a companion travel concession for Deaf or DeafBlind residents. Croydon’s “Companion Badge” is a **parking** anti-theft product for Blue Badge holders, not a free extra seat on the bus.
- **Taxicard** automatic entry is higher-rate DLA mobility, PIP moving-around 8+, registered severely sight-impaired / blind, or War Pension mobility. Deafness alone is not on that automatic list. A DeafBlind person with a CVI as SSI/blind is in; a BSL user with usable sight and no walking limitation is not, unless their borough runs a wider discretionary assessment.
- TfL’s live passenger-information stack is **audio plus visual screens plus induction loops**. Network-wide next-stop BSL on trains does not exist. What exists are **trials**: Arriva Rail London “Luna” / Signapse at five Overground stations (2024), SWR whoosh.media BSL player, c2c BSL totems at Fenchurch Street and Southend Central (April 2025 trial), LNER BSL on some customer-information screens. TfL’s own BSL product is a **YouTube explainer** of how to travel, not a live disruption feed.
- A DeafBlind older person who has lost usable hearing *and* usable sight falls off both channels at once: they cannot hear the announcement and cannot read the screen. Tactile paving is incomplete. Dial-a-Ride bookings default to a **phone line** (0343 222 7777) or an app whose published accessibility work is VoiceOver / large text, not BSL video relay.
- Missed-appointment evidence in this population is still mostly **booking and interpreter**, not “missed the bus”: *Locked Out* — one-third of BSL users missed an appointment because they could not communicate to reschedule; three-quarters struggle to book. No named SWL safeguarding or ombudsman case was found that isolates transport-inaccessibility as the reason a Deaf elder missed a clinic. The mechanism is documented; the SWL count is not.

All six SWL boroughs have a named community-transport operator on TfL’s directory (CAT, MCT, RaKAT, Sutton CT, WCT). None of the pages pulled on this pass advertise BSL-fluent drivers or a VRS booking route. Wandsworth Community Transport still lists a Minicom number.

## 69.1 What does TfL / rail / bus actually provide?

### Concession products (the ticket)

| Product | Who it is for | What it does | Time limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Disabled Person’s Freedom Pass | London resident + one of seven Transport Act 2000 categories, including **profoundly or severely deaf** and **blind or partially sighted** | Free TfL modes; ENCTS buses in England | TfL: any time. National Rail in London: from 09:30 weekdays |
| Older Person’s Freedom Pass | London resident who has reached **state pension age** (66 in 2026) | Same TfL modes | Typically from 09:00 weekdays on TfL; weekends unrestricted |
| 60+ London Oyster photocard | London resident aged 60 to SPA | Freedom-Pass-like inside Greater London; **not** valid on buses outside London | Paid product (Wikipedia / press: £36 band; confirm live fee on TfL before quoting) |
| ENCTS disabled bus pass (outside the Freedom Pass overlay) | Same seven categories, issued by the travel concession authority | Free off-peak local buses in England | Statutory window 09:30–23:00 weekdays; local extras vary |
| Taxicard | Severe **mobility** difficulty or **severe sight impairment** making mainstream public transport hard | Subsidised black cab / some PHV | Trip caps set by borough |
| Dial-a-Ride | Long-term disability, TfL membership | Door-to-door booked trips, 07:00–23:00 | Phone and app booking rules below |
| Disabled Persons Railcard | National, paid | 1/3 off rail for the holder and a companion | Not a London concession; companion is built into *this* product |

**Deafness threshold (DfT guidance, used by Richmond and the ENCTS category-B forms):** severe **70–95 dBHL**, profound **95+ dBHL**. Passes “should be issued to people in these categories.” Mild or moderate loss in the better ear is outside the statutory deafness category. There is **no statutory deaf register** in England (already logged in prompt 67); councils may use a voluntary social-services list but must still check the person is profoundly or severely deaf.

**DeafBlind:** two statutory doors (sight *or* hearing). Dual-sensory people with a CVI and/or an audiogram over 70 dBHL both ears should clear eligibility. That is not the same as being able to *use* the network.

### Information on the move

TfL’s published offer for “people with sight or hearing loss”:

- Induction loops at many ticket offices, Help Points, platforms, black cabs and buses.
- Visual customer-information screens and the usual audio PA.
- Tactile paving “being fitted” — “until we have this at all stations, please do not assume it is there.”
- Large-print and audio Tube maps; step-free guide; travel support card; travel mentoring.
- A BSL version of the “Accessible travel in London” explainer on YouTube.

That last item is a **static film about the system**. It is not next-stop BSL in the carriage, and it is not a disruption alert in BSL when a District line train is reversed at Wimbledon.

Live or near-live BSL passenger information, as of this pass, is **trial geography**:

- Arriva Rail London / GoMedia **Luna** (Signapse) at five Overground stations, demonstrated at Camden Road, 2024 — BSL on the passenger’s own device, fed from the same data as text and audio.
- SWR **whoosh.media** BSL player, soft-launched August 2024 — platform descriptions and next-station clips.
- **c2c** interactive BSL totems at Fenchurch Street and Southend Central, trial announced 3 April 2025.
- **LNER** BSL on customer-information screens, Doncaster first, with a stated intent to roll out.

None of those four is a SWL-borough bus or Tube standard. A Croydon Tramlink or Northern line passenger does not get Luna by default.

### The dual-sensory cut

A DeafBlind traveller who has lost usable hearing *and* usable sight cannot use the PA *or* the screen. Loops do nothing without residual hearing and a working aid. Phone-first booking (Dial-a-Ride, most hospital transport lines, most community-transport offices) is the same barrier already logged against DWP, HMRC and Pension Service in prompts 20–26, 55–61 and 66. TfL Dial-a-Ride’s published app accessibility statement lists VoiceOver, TalkBack, dynamic type and contrast. It does not list BSL video relay.

## 69.2 Do older Deaf / DeafBlind people in SWL qualify — and can they apply?

### Statutory yes, with a threshold

All six SWL boroughs issue the Disabled Person’s Freedom Pass against the same seven Transport Act 2000 categories. Richmond’s published evidence list is typical:

- **Blind / partially sighted:** CVI, BD8, or local-authority registration.
- **Profoundly or severely deaf:** audiological report or aural-specialist report, or registration; 70–95 / 95+ dBHL.
- **Without speech:** cannot make basic oral requests in any language. BSL users who *can* vocalise some words are not automatically in this category; they come in through deafness, not through “without speech.”

Wandsworth, Merton and Croydon pages repeat the same seven categories. Croydon adds a **discretionary** Disabled Freedom Pass for some 18–59-year-olds supported by Croydon social services or SLaM whose pass would deliver care-plan outcomes — London-only, withdrawn on leaving the borough. That discretionary route is not the older-Deaf product.

Older Person’s Freedom Pass at SPA does not need an audiogram. A 67-year-old BSL user who never applied under the deafness category still travels free after 09:00. They do not get the Disabled pass’s all-day TfL window unless they also qualify and apply for it.

### Companion travel — not a London Freedom Pass right

- London Councils / Wikipedia summary of the scheme: **Disabled Person’s Freedom Passes are not available for carers.**
- DfT ENCTS guidance: free companion travel is a **local discretionary concession** under s.93 Transport Act 1985, funded from local resources.
- Shire examples (East Riding “+C” logo) exist. **No SWL borough page found on this pass that offers a companion seat on the Freedom Pass for a Deaf or DeafBlind holder.**
- Croydon “Companion Badge” (£40) is a Blue Badge *parking* clone that shows the vehicle registration so the Blue Badge can stay at home. It does not put a second person on the tram for free.

The Disabled Persons **Railcard** *does* include a companion discount. That is a paid national rail product, not the Freedom Pass.

### Taxicard — mobility and severe sight, not Deafness

London Councils automatic Taxicard list: higher-rate DLA mobility; PIP moving-around **8+**; registered **severely sight-impaired / blind** (partially sighted is not automatic); War Pension mobility supplement. Some boroughs add higher-rate Attendance Allowance (Kingston and Sutton are on that extra list) or an age rule (Kingston 90+).

A mobile, sighted, profoundly Deaf BSL user is outside automatic Taxicard. A DeafBlind person registered SSI/blind is inside. Boroughs can assess “severe mobility impairment” case by case; “cannot use public transport without extreme difficulty” is Merton’s wording. That assessment is not a published BSL-access product.

### Application channel

Freedom Pass and Taxicard applications go through the **borough**, usually an English-language web form plus posted evidence (audiogram, CVI, proof of address). London Councils do not offer walk-in counters for these products. Wandsworth still tells people they can phone 020 8871 8871 and have a form posted. Croydon’s travel-passes page has a “translate this page to BSL” widget on the council site — a page translation, not a BSL application interview.

**ABSENT:** a published figure, for any SWL borough, of how many Disabled Freedom Passes are in issue under the “profoundly or severely deaf” category, or how many DeafBlind residents hold both a Freedom Pass and a Taxicard. That is an FOI to each of the six.

## 69.3 Documented missed appointments and abandoned social trips

**What is published is booking-and-interpreter failure, not a transport-failure series.**

*Locked Out* (27 November 2025):

- Three-quarters of BSL users struggle to book an NHS appointment because the channel is English-online or telephone.
- Just under half have given up without booking.
- **One-third missed appointments because they could not communicate with services to reschedule.**
- 80% have had a booked communication professional fail to attend.
- Poor access “contributes to the estimated £1.2 billion annual cost of missed appointments and unnecessary repeat visits” — that is a national missed-appointment pot, not a Deaf-transport subset.
- Lived line already used in prompt 68: housebound, “over £100 each time I go out to meet friends,” and often nobody to take them.

Still Ignored / RNID–SignHealth appointment figures (7% always get a communication professional; 80% no-show) are **clinic-room** failures. They explain why a person might turn around and go home once they have arrived. They do not count people who never left the house because the bus stop announcement was audio-only or the patient-transport line was a phone number.

Guy’s and St Thomas’ patient-transport page is typical of the hospital offer: eligibility is mobility / safety / cannot use public transport; assessment at least 48 hours ahead; booking line **020 7188 2888**; “a friend or relative can contact us on your behalf.” That last sentence is the family-as-channel pattern. No BSL VRS is advertised on that page.

Moona Mohammed (LBC, June 2025, Barts): waited two hours, did not hear her name, marked DNA. That is a **waiting-room call**, not a missed bus — but it is the same sensory channel failure.

**ABSENT:** a named SWL ombudsman, CQC or safeguarding finding that an older Deaf or DeafBlind resident missed a clinic or abandoned a social trip *because* transport information or booking was inaccessible. Log it. Do not invent one from the national booking figures.

Holding the pass is treated as having the means. That only works if the pass is usable in the person’s language and senses.

## What not to write

- Do not write “Deaf older people automatically get a Freedom Pass.” They do if they meet 70 dBHL both ears *or* have reached SPA. Late-deafened moderate loss is outside the deafness category.
- Do not write that Taxicard is a Deaf product.
- Do not write that London issues companion Freedom Passes. It does not, on the published scheme rules.
- Do not describe TfL as having live BSL announcements. It has an explainer film and other operators have *trials*.
- Do not recycle Locked Out’s one-third missed-appointment figure as a transport statistic. It is a booking-channel statistic.
- Do not assume community transport can take a BSL booking because the minibus is wheelchair-accessible.
