# Prompt 66 — State Pension and retirement-benefit access: DWP, Pension Service, Pension Credit

**Master index prompt 66.** First file in the Deaf/DeafBlind older people batch (prompts 66–71). Compiled 9 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Test whether BSL-using Deaf and DeafBlind pensioners are visible in DWP/Pension Service access data at all — separately from the general older-disabled population. Covers four questions: (1) whether Pension Service BSL VRS is offered at the point of need or only on a general accessibility page; (2) whether Pension Credit take-up is ever broken out by deafness/BSL; (3) what DWP actually offers as accessible correspondence for State Pension awards and uprating notices; (4) whether any CQC, PHSO or Independent Case Examiner finding names a Deaf or DeafBlind pensioner failed by Pension Service communication.

**Duty reminder.** DWP does not operate under the NHS/social-care Accessible Information Standard. The live duty is the Equality Act 2010 reasonable-adjustment duty (and, for public communications about policy, the BSL Act 2022 reporting duty on DWP as a named ministerial department). Do not write “AIS applies to the Pension Service.”

**Index check.** `SuperGrok_Research_Prompt_Pack.md` already lists 66–71. This file is the research answer to prompt 66, not a new numbered batch. Proposed later-life domain title (once the batch is verified): Domain 17 — Ageing, retirement, and later-life isolation. Do not fold this into Domain 16 (end-of-life).

## Headline

The Pension Service **does** publish a one-click BSL video-relay button on its own contact page. The destination on 9 September 2026 is InterpretersLive! at `https://connect.interpreterslive.co.uk/vrs?ilc=DWP`. The same component sits on Pension Credit, Attendance Allowance and Winter Fuel Payment contact pages. It is not buried only on the general accessibility guide. That is a fulfilment fact, not a campaign claim.

Three hard gaps sit next to that button:

1. **VRS is inbound only.** DWP’s own design system (updated 20 January 2026) states the department cannot use VRS to call a customer. Award letters, annual uprating notices and “we tried to phone you” chasers still leave the building as print unless the claimant has already asked for an alternative format.
2. **Pension Credit take-up is not published by deafness or BSL.** Official caseload take-up for Great Britain in FYE 2024 is **62%** (up to 910,000 entitled families not claiming). DWP’s October 2025 ad-hoc analysis uses a **disability-benefit proxy** (AA / DLA / PIP in the household). No official table, FOI or research found in this pass splits take-up by BSL use, deafness or dual sensory loss.
3. **No PHSO investigation naming a Deaf or DeafBlind pensioner failed by the Pension Service** was found. The closest official finding is an Independent Case Examiner 2024/25 case study of an anonymised deaf customer whose State Pension and Pension Credit were suspended after DWP failed to update an address and failed to make further reasonable adjustments — ending in nearly £55,000 of arrears and a £3,000 consolatory payment.

CQC does not regulate DWP. Treating a CQC care-home finding as a Pension Service finding is a citation error.

## 66.1 Is Pension Service BSL VRS offered at the point of need?

**Yes, on the live contact pages — not only on the general accessibility guide.**

The Pension Service contact page, checked 9 September 2026, lists three channels in the same block:

- Telephone: **0800 731 0469**
- Relay UK: **18001** then 0800 731 0469
- British Sign Language (BSL) video relay service if you’re on a computer — destination **`https://connect.interpreterslive.co.uk/vrs?ilc=DWP`**, with a YouTube how-to for mobile/tablet (`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oELNMfAvDxw`)

Hours: Monday to Friday, 8am to 6pm except public holidays. Welsh-language voice line 0800 731 0453. No textphone is published on this page (the department-wide accessibility guide removed textphone wording on 30 September 2025 and points people to Relay UK instead). Postal address: The Pension Service, Post Handling Site A, Wolverhampton, WV98 1AF.

**Supplier stack.** InterpretersLive! is a Sign Solutions product. Dals (formerly DA Languages) still publishes a “DWP BSL video interpreter service” landing page stating the specialist VRS “is provided by Dals and InterpretersLive!” and “can only be used to make calls within DWP departments.” DA Languages was awarded the DWP language-services contract effective **May 2022** (CCS RM6141 Lot 1 National Managed Service; original term to 8 May 2025). Treat the £540k figure as the published notice value for that award, not as a verified all-DWP BSL spend line. The live GOV.UK button still resolves to InterpretersLive with the DWP customer code.

**What the button does not do.**

- DWP Design System, “Available formats”, updated **20 January 2026**: VRS “is only available for inbound calls (DWP cannot use it to make calls to customers).”
- An older internal Universal Credit VRS spotlight (released on WhatDoTheyKnow) describes the interpreter introducing themselves as being from **SignVideo** before ringing the service line. That is a UC-era staff note, not current Pension Service public copy. Do not write “Pension Service uses SignVideo” off that PDF. Live pension pages name InterpretersLive.
- No published service-level target was found for Pension Service VRS wait, drop-off, or out-of-hours cover. Hours follow the voice line (weekday office hours). That absence is a gap, not a failed search.
- No SWL-borough or London Pension Centre BSL volume was found.

**Duty.** Equality Act 2010 s.20 reasonable adjustment, not AIS. DWP is a BSL Act scheduled ministerial department, so it reports BSL *communications products* in the annual BSL reports. Those reports do not count operational VRS calls.

## 66.2 Pension Credit take-up — is there a deafness or BSL split?

**No reliable BSL- or deafness-specific take-up figure found. Official take-up is published for all entitled pensioner families, with a disability-benefit proxy that is not a BSL flag.**

### Official all-pensioner figure (use this, do not proxy it as Deaf)

Income-related benefits take-up, financial year ending 2024 (published 30 October 2025):

- Caseload take-up of Pension Credit in Great Britain: **62%** of those entitled received it (down from 65% in FYE 2023; difference not statistically significant).
- Families entitled but not claiming: **up to 910,000** (up from up to 760,000 in FYE 2023).
- Unclaimed expenditure: **up to £2.5 billion**.
- Average unclaimed amount per entitled non-claiming family: about **£2,600 a year**.
- Expenditure take-up (share of money claimed): **71%**.
- By family type, FYE 2024 caseload take-up: single males 67%, single females 64%, couples 48%.

Regional receipt-rate analysis published the same day (first of its kind at sub-national level): England 63%, London **69%**, South West **55%** (lowest English region). That London figure is the nearest official geography to the SWL ICB footprint. It is **not** a Deaf figure and must not be printed as one.

### Disability proxy — still not Deaf

DWP ad-hoc statistics, 30 October 2025, “Analysis of households potentially eligible for Pension Credit, and eligible households in receipt, 2023 to 2024”:

> “The receipt of a pensioner disability benefit (Attendance Allowance (AA) / Disability Living Allowance (DLA) / Personal Independence Payment (PIP)) within the household, has been used as a proxy to there being a disabled person present.”

Findings under that proxy:

- 37% of potentially eligible non-claiming households have someone on a pensioner disability benefit, versus 58% of eligible households that *are* in receipt.
- DWP itself flags undercount: the proxy is narrower than the Family Resources Survey equality definition of disability.

Deafness, dual sensory loss and BSL-main-language are **not** columns. Attendance Allowance can be awarded for a hearing condition (tabloid and advice-site recycling of “hearing issues” AA caseloads is common; those figures count age-related hearing loss as well as BSL-using Deaf people and cannot be used as a BSL take-up rate).

### Campaign and research layer

DWP’s Pension Credit toolkit (HTML stakeholder guide, updated through 2025 including a 1 July 2025 video refresh) now embeds a BSL overview film and still quotes the “up to 910,000” non-claiming estimate. The toolkit’s phone block lists Relay UK; the *claim* page, not the toolkit, is where the VRS button lives. The toolkit still tells advisers that “if English is not the claimant’s first language, they can ask a friend or family member to help” and that a phone interpreter can be arranged — family-as-interpreter language that this project already treats as a risk in health settings. Flag it; do not moralise it.

No FOI response found on this pass that releases Pension Credit caseload or take-up by “deaf”, “BSL”, “hearing impairment” or “deafblind”. Independent Age, Age UK and RNIB comment on the headline 62% / 910,000 figures; none of those comments in this pass is a BSL-user take-up study.

**ABSENT (this is the finding):** a published Pension Credit take-up rate, underclaim count, or invitation-to-claim trial result restricted to BSL-using Deaf or DeafBlind pensioners.

## 66.3 Accessible correspondence — award letters and uprating notices

**What is published as available, if the customer asks.** The 30 September 2025 accessibility guide lists, for people who have difficulty reading letters or filling in forms:

- larger typesize
- Braille, grade 1 (uncontracted) or grade 2 (contracted)
- audio on CD or as an MP3
- email in a suitable format where assistive technology is used
- a phone call to explain
- different paper colour
- a home visit

Signed video is **not** on that public list. It *is* listed as a possible “written communication” alternative on the internal-facing DWP Design System (signed video with subtitles), alongside the note that “not every letter can be replaced with an email: there may be legal requirements for some communications to be sent as a printed letter.” Large print is “by far the most commonly requested alternative format… over 90% of all recorded alternative format needs in DWP.” The same page warns that some people coded as large-print may be better served by another format and have not been asked to update preferences.

**State Pension invitation letter (2022 ministerial answer — still the last explicit PQ found).** Guy Opperman, 28 March 2022, UIN 145077: the invitation letter to claim State Pension opens with:

> “If you would like Braille, British Sign Language, a hearing loop, translations, large print, audio or something else, please phone 0800 731 0469 or textphone 0800 731 7339.”

Two limits on that sentence:

- “British Sign Language” in an invitation letter is an offer to *contact the department in BSL* (or to request a BSL adjustment), not evidence that the award notice or the annual uprating notice is itself issued as a BSL video.
- The textphone number in that 2022 answer is now out of step with the 30 September 2025 guide, which dropped textphone and points to Relay UK.

The online Get your State Pension service is described in the same answer as allowing on-screen text enlargement. A paper BR1 exists in large print and Braille. Selecting an alternative-format need during the claim is said to flag the central system so later letters go out in large print automatically.

**2025 readability PQ.** Sir Stephen Timms, 5 November 2025, UIN 86084, on New State Pension calculation letters: the department “is committed to making communications clear, understandable and accessible”; Easy Read products “are specifically created to help and support disabled customers” and are tested with Mencap. The answer does not mention BSL video versions of calculation or uprating letters.

**BSL information products that do exist (communications, not personalised award letters).**

- DWPsign YouTube channel — departmental BSL channel. GCS blog 27 September 2023: launched after a 2018 Ministerial Taskforce on Alternative Formats; then “over 2,000 subscribers and 166 videos”. Subscriber count on later retrievals in this pass ~2.9–3.0k.
- “Get Help from Pension Credit” — DWPsign, published 17 June 2021, 11:40. Explains eligibility, online / phone / Relay UK / VRS routes, and points to Citizens Advice and Age UK.
- “Pension Credit: A British Sign Language guide for people with hearing impairments” — DWPsign, published 4 March 2025, 3:39. Added to the stakeholder toolkit in September 2024 / refreshed 2025. Title uses “hearing impairments”; content is a Pension Credit explainer, not a personalised award.
- “Video Relay Service British Sign Language” — DWPsign, 22 April 2022. How-to for InterpretersLive.

No public catalogue was found that lists a BSL-video version of the individual State Pension award letter, the annual uprating notice, or the Pension Credit decision letter. **ABSENT** as a published product, not as a proof that no individual has ever been sent one after asking.

**Blind-claimant litigation is not a Deaf finding, but it is the nearest correspondence-duty judgment.** *CO/2101/2022* (High Court declaration extracted on Rightsnet): from 21 May 2020 to 13 September 2022, hard-copy print letters, scanned-image PDFs and unreasonably delayed alternative-format letters to registered blind / sight-impaired ESA and PIP claimants who had requested email, Word, accessible PDF or Braille put those claimants at a substantial disadvantage. The declaration is about visual impairment and specified benefits, not BSL pension letters. Do not recycle it as a Deaf Pension Service authority. It is useful only as evidence that DWP’s default print channel has already been found wanting for another sensory group.

### Independent Case Examiner — one official anonymised case that *does* join deafness to State Pension and Pension Credit

ICE annual report 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025 (HTML updated 6 November 2025), section “When urgent action is needed”:

An anonymised customer, described as **deaf**, with very poor physical health and housing problems, had been on DLA for years. In 2018 DWP started the DLA-to-PIP move. The customer did not claim PIP in time. ICE says DWP needed to consider additional support, including a home visit; that did not happen; the DLA claim closed. The customer later wrote, including a new address. DWP did not update the address. An important letter went to the old address, came back, and was not followed up. Payments were suspended and the suspension was not reviewed. From 2019 the customer received no pension or benefit payments. A 2020 contact was missed. In 2023, now over State Pension age and unable to claim PIP, they were sent an Attendance Allowance form; ICE says DWP did not consider further reasonable adjustments or review the suspended State Pension. The customer had to prompt again. Outcomes recorded by ICE:

- State Pension reinstated with arrears of **just under £30,000** for 2019–2023
- Pension Credit later processed with **just over £2,000** for a six-month period — then found wrong, because DWP treated the State Pension arrears as capital and applied a tariff instead of disregarding them
- Combined redress of **nearly £55,000** covering lost DLA 2018–2023, lost Pension Credit 2019–2023, erosion of the value of arrears, and the incorrect tariff deduction
- Consolatory payment **£3,000**

ICE is not PHSO. It is the DWP-specific independent examiner that sits under the departmental complaints ladder before an MP can refer a case to PHSO. The customer is anonymised. The report does not say the original failure was “no BSL interpreter on a Pension Service call”; the recorded failures are address, suspension review, home-visit / extra-support consideration, and capital treatment of arrears. Deafness is the vulnerability ICE chose to name. That is enough to log it here. It is not enough to write “DWP refused this pensioner a BSL interpreter.”

No other ICE case in that annual report names deafness, BSL or the Pension Service communication channel.

## 66.5 Related retirement benefits — Attendance Allowance and Winter Fuel / heating payments

- **Attendance Allowance** is the pension-age extra-costs benefit in England and Wales (Scotland: Pension Age Disability Payment). Claim page publishes VRS, Relay UK and a surviving textphone (0800 731 0317). Online claims exist but exclude appointees and attorneys. No published AA caseload split for BSL-using Deaf people was found. Recycled “hearing issues” AA headcounts mix age-related hearing loss with culturally Deaf BSL users — do not use them as a BSL figure.
- **Winter Fuel Payment** (England and Wales) is automatic for people already on State Pension, Pension Credit, UC, AA or PIP. The claim route for people who must apply publishes the same BSL VRS component. Scotland’s Pension Age Winter Heating Payment is a separate Social Security Scotland product and was not checked as a live BSL channel on this pass — log as a follow-up if Domain 17 needs a Scotland row.
- **Pension Credit severe-disability and carer additions** (toolkit “Pension Credit and help for disabled people”) increase the PC award where the person gets AA / qualifying DLA/PIP care or a carer benefit. That is the mechanism by which a Deaf pensioner’s extra-costs benefit, *if awarded*, feeds PC. It is not evidence of take-up.

## What not to write

- Do not write that the Pension Service has no BSL VRS. The button is on the contact page.
- Do not write that AIS applies to DWP or the Pension Service.
- Do not use the 62% / 910,000 Pension Credit figures as if they were a Deaf underclaim rate.
- Do not use “hearing issues” Attendance Allowance caseloads as a BSL-user count.
- Do not describe the ICE 2024/25 deaf customer as a PHSO case, and do not invent a missing-interpreter fact the report does not state.
- Do not import Samantha, Alan Graham, or Paul Rimmer into this file as Pension Service findings.
- Do not treat the 2025 Contracts Finder £540,000 language-services award as proven all-DWP BSL expenditure.
