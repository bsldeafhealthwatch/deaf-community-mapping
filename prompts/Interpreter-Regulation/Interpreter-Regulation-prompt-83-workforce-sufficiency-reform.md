# Prompt 83 — Workforce sufficiency, geographic distribution, and the case for reform

**Master index prompt 83.** Third file in the Interpreter Regulation & Agencies batch (prompts 81–83). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. Targets are not delivered outcomes.

**What this prompt is for.** Scale and shape of the registered-interpreter shortage; what government has actually funded; why registrants decline NHS/DWP/legal work; which reform proposals exist and what has become of them. Generalise the EOL brief’s RIDB geography to the *general* RSLI register. Do not reprint the £15k / 68 pair as if the general BSL workforce has an equivalent plan.

**Depends on 81–82.** Live NRCPD table (1,664 SLI, 19 RIDB, regional cells) and the agency-layer pay gap sit underneath this file.

---

## Headline

There is still **no official national interpreter-to-Deaf-population ratio** and **no funded England workforce plan for general RSLI**. What exists is a live register, two contested denominators, one tiny RIDB assessment grant, and a set of advocacy recommendations that have not been converted into training-place numbers.

On this pass the register shows **1,664** Sign Language Interpreters and **241** regulated trainees (duals included). Census 2021: BSL is the **main language of 22,000** usual residents aged 3+ in England and Wales (ONS). *Locked Out* uses a wider BDA-derived pair — “around 151,000 BSL users, of whom 87,000 are deaf” and “25,000 who use BSL as their main language.” Those denominators are not interchangeable. A crude E&W sum of the NRCPD SLI regional cells is about **1,500** against ONS 22,000 main-language users — roughly **15 main-language users per published SLI cell**, before anyone subtracts duals, part-timers, people who do not take healthcare work, or people who live in the South East and will not travel to Lincolnshire. Using the 87,000 deaf-BSL-user denominator produces a much thinner ratio. Neither calculation is an official workforce standard. Do not print “the NHS says 1:X.”

Geography is not the same problem as RIDB. **RIDB zeros remain East Midlands, South West, Yorkshire and The Humber.** General SLI is present in every UK nation and English region. The thin SLI cells are **North East 40, Northern Ireland 43, Wales 63.** South East 239 and London 220 are the dense cells. Wales’s own government correspondence (November 2025) quotes ASLI July 2025: **1,558 RSLI nationally, 54 based in Wales** — consistent in direction with NRCPD’s Wales cell of 63 (different date, different dual rules).

Willingness is a separate shortage from headcount. *Locked Out* prints **20.6% of interpreters accept work in healthcare settings and only 7.65% in mental health.** Those percentages are the Board’s, footnoted to its own evidence pack, not an NRCPD extract. NUBSLI’s April 2026 fee guidance and the 2016–17 LanguageLine boycott dossier are the documented pay/terms reasons. RNID/DeafATW 2026 survey (n=358): one BSL user contacted “over 70 qualified interpreters” who would not work at the AtW rate once travel was included. That is claimant-side evidence of refusal, not a union census of “% who decline NHS.”

Government money aimed at *growing the registered bench*:

- **Delivered as a published grant:** £15,000 for RIDB competency-assessment weekends, announced on GOV.UK **23 June 2026**, target **68** RIDB against a historic baseline of **8**. Live register on this pass: **19**. Target ≠ delivered.
- **Not found:** an equivalent DHSC/NHS England/DWP funded training-place programme, bursary scheme, or headcount target for general RSLI.
- BSL Act departmental 5-year plans (July 2025) are communications-product plans, not interpreter-workforce plans. Fourth BSL Act report (15 July 2026) still counts communications products.
- MoJ’s October 2026 court-interpreting contracts expand a **spoken-language** DPSI trainee scheme and add CPI-linked pay. That is not a BSL training-place programme.

Reform asks that are live on paper and not enacted as a national workforce strategy: *Locked Out* priorities 1 (patient-controlled booking) and 8 (statutory guidelines stipulating NRCPD-registered interpreters); ASLI’s Wales Bill evidence (ring-fenced training funding, bursaries, time-bound workforce targets); Lords Public Services Committee *Lost in translation?* (minimum pay, annual review, single register — written for court interpreting, mostly spoken). NRCPD’s own long-term aim remains statutory regulation (prompt 81). None of those has a funded delivery line for general RSLI on this pass.

## 83.1 Two shortages stacked on each other

Treat these as different facts.

**Stock shortage.** Not enough names on the register in some categories and some regions. RIDB is the extreme: 19 people, three English regions at zero, a £15k assessment grant aimed at 68. General SLI is not at zero anywhere, but the North East / Wales / NI cells are thin, and the South East / London cells cannot be assumed to travel.

**Allocation shortage.** Of the names that exist, *Locked Out* says only one in five take healthcare and fewer than one in twelve take mental health. AtW claimants report being unable to spend an awarded rate. NUBSLI documents a decade of agency-rate disputes. Adding 100 new RSLI in Wolverhampton does not fill a Croydon mental-health slot if the rate, notice period and travel rule make that slot unviable.

A workforce strategy that only funds course places addresses stock. A strategy that only raises framework rates addresses allocation. The UK currently has neither, except the RIDB assessment weekends.

## 83.3 What has been proposed, and what has actually been funded

**Funded, small, RIDB-only.** £15,000, 23 June 2026, competency assessment weekends, target 68. Live 19. This is the only named growth pound found.

**Proposed, not funded as a national RSLI programme.**

- *Locked Out* statutory interpreter guidelines + patient-controlled booking.
- ASLI Wales: bursaries, ring-fenced training, time-bound targets.
- NRCPD: statutory regulation of the profession.
- Lords Committee: minimum court pay, single register (spoken-led).
- SignHealth open letter 14 November 2025 (already logged): access failures, not a numbered training-place ask.

**Adjacent, not this workforce.** Departmental BSL 5-year communications plans; BSL GCSE assessment rules (Ofqual, November 2025); MoJ spoken DPSI trainee expansion; Contact Scotland £3m VRS contract (supply of *minutes*, not of new RSLI).

ASLI’s 2021 professional census remains the last dedicated demographic study. It is useful for *who the profession was* (White, female, uneven geography). It is not a 2026 sufficiency model.

## Gaps logged

- No official interpreter-to-Deaf ratio.
- No national BSL booking-fulfilment series (trust or DWP).
- No 2025–26 ASLI/NUBSLI census that repeats the 2021 SLTI exercise.
- Locked Out 20.6% / 7.65% underlying microdata not re-opened.
- No quantified “% of RSLI who refuse NHS / hospice / CJS work” from the register itself.
- No funded general-RSLI training-place target in England.
- Wales route-map workforce actions are mapping exercises with an 18-month short-term clock — not yet a delivered headcount.
- Unique-person NRCPD totals by region after stripping duals: unpublished.

## What 81–83 jointly support, and what they do not

Safe to write:

- NRCPD and RBSLI are voluntary registers. Unregistered practice is not a criminal offence. AIS 1.32 and purchaser contracts are the live hooks.
- Live RIDB = 19, three English regions at zero, £15k grant targeting 68.
- Live SLI = 1,664 published cells; no English region at zero; NE/Wales/NI thin.
- Public bodies buy through agencies. Duty stays with the provider. Agency unit rates and margins are unpublished.
- There is no England workforce plan for general RSLI equivalent to the RIDB assessment grant.

Not safe to write:

- “Unregistered interpreter is illegal.”
- “There are now 68 RIDB.”
- “The official ratio is 1:X.”
- “Agencies take 37% of every BSL booking.”
- “92% framework fulfilment is an official CCS KPI” (until the schedule is read).
- “Ofqual regulates interpreters.”

Prompt 84, if run later, is the lived-experience / advocacy case for statutory regulation versus better enforcement of the voluntary registers and the purchaser layer. It was held back from this batch on purpose.
