# Prompt 82 — Agency and procurement model: how NHS and public bodies actually buy interpreters

**Master index prompt 82.** Second file in the Interpreter Regulation & Agencies batch (prompts 81–83). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. Gaps stay gaps.

**What this prompt is for.** Map the interpreter-supply chain between a registered individual and a patient/claimant/appointment. Keep this layer distinct from ICB/commissioner *spend* already logged under Public Spending & Contracts. Do not treat agency marketing copy as independently verified rates.

**Depends on 81.** NRCPD/RBSLI are voluntary registers. AIS 1.32 and purchaser contracts are what require registration. That distinction is assumed here.

---

## Headline

Public bodies almost never book a named NRCPD registrant as a first move. The dominant model is: the trust, ICB, GP federation, council or MoJ contractor holds a contract with an **interpreting agency** (sometimes via a Crown Commercial Service or regional framework); the agency then subcontracts a freelance registrant, or supplies its own VRI platform, or fails to fill the slot. AIS implementation guidance tells organisations that when they use an agency they “should confirm with the agency that only NRCPD registrants will be provided” and write that into the specification. That sentence is the closest official text to an accountability split — and it still leaves the **AIS / Equality Act duty on the commissioning body**, not on the agency.

Duty does not transfer. SC12.3 binds “the Provider” (the NHS organisation on the Standard Contract). Equality Act s.20 binds the service provider the patient is trying to use. An agency failure is a supplier-performance event inside that duty. PHSO findings in Alan Graham (UHB, published February 2026) and Samantha (GP flu/Covid jab mix-up, reported August 2026) hold the **trust or practice**, not the agency, responsible for the missing or unused interpreter.

Rate transparency stops at the agency’s invoice. CCS RM6302 (Language Services, live 7 May 2025 – 6 May 2028, optional +12 months) has a Framework Schedule 3 prices file that is **not published**. Project note of 6 September 2026 already logged: RM6302 Lot 4 unit rates unpublished; no UK public-body F2F-versus-VRS spend split found. What *is* published is the freelancer side (NUBSLI April 2026 medians: London RSLI full day £375, most GB regions £335–£365) and a handful of agency *customer* tariffs from earlier project work (Bristol April 2025 short £158.60 / half £195.20 / full £390.40; Lingo on-site £235/3hrs then £70/hr; Sign Solutions/ABTC VRS £1.65–£3.15/min). The gap between those two columns is the agency margin. *Locked Out* (27 November 2025) states the qualitative case — large multilingual agencies apply “large mark-ups,” do not grow the BSL pool, and can hit a 92% all-language fulfilment KPI while missing every BSL booking — but does not publish a verified pence-in-the-pound split.

Named fulfilment failures that reach an ombudsman almost always describe the **commissioner not booking**, booking late, using family, or not acting on a flag — not a tribunal finding that “Agency X sent an unregistered trainee.” Limping Chicken (5 August 2024) records the practitioner counter-claim: “the interpreter never turned up” is often “nobody was actually booked.” Both can be true in different cases. Neither is a national fulfilment series.

## 82.1 The actual chain

```
Patient flagged (or not) on the AIS record
        ↓
Ward / GP / Jobcentre / court booking clerk
        ↓
Contracted agency or framework supplier
   (DAL, LanguageLine, Sign Solutions, Clarion/TBW, thebigword, Action Deafness, RAD…)
        ↓
Freelance NRCPD/RBSLI registrant
   or VRI platform seat
   or unfilled request
        ↓
Appointment proceeds / is delayed / uses family / is cancelled
```

Four consequences of that extra layer:

1. **The patient rarely has a contractual relationship with the interpreter.** Complaint about competence goes to NRCPD (prompt 81). Complaint about “nobody came” goes to the trust/practice PALS and then PHSO. Complaint about the agency’s booking desk sits in a no-man’s-land unless the specification names KPIs and the commissioner enforces them.
2. **Registration checks are delegated.** AIS says write “NRCPD only” into the agency specification and check IDs. Whether that check happens is a contract-management fact, not a register fact.
3. **Fulfilment KPIs that mix BSL with 200 spoken languages hide BSL failure.** That is *Locked Out*’s central procurement claim. The public RM6302 page does not print the 92% figure the Board attributes to “the NHS national tender framework.” Leave that number contested until the schedule is read.
4. **VRI can be the product the agency actually wants to sell.** InterpretersLive (Sign Solutions) and SignVideo/SignLive directory seats are easier to scale than a 3-hour F2F booking in Sutton. AIS 2025 guidance permits remote for routine care. The agency layer is one reason remote becomes the default even where the patient asked for F2F.

Direct booking still exists: AtW customers with their own interpreter list; some specialist clinics (Moorfields / DBE in the EOL brief); some Deaf-led agencies that are themselves the framework supplier (Sign Solutions, Action Deafness, RAD on the South East Lot 2). Direct booking does not remove AIS duty from the provider. It just shortens the chain.

## 82.2 Accountability when the booking fails

Write the failure mode first, then the respondent.

| Failure mode | Who PHSO/CQC/EA actually reach | What the agency can be | What NRCPD can be |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nobody booked / flag ignored | The trust, GP, or commissioner | Not in the chain | Not in the chain |
| Request placed, no interpreter assigned | The provider, for not confirming a named booking (Wales SOP is explicit) | Contract KPI / service-credit counterparty | Nothing — no registrant was involved |
| Named registrant does not attend | Provider still owes the patient a reasonable adjustment; may recover against the agency; may complain about the registrant | Contract breach vis-à-vis the provider | Professional-conduct complaint if the no-show is the registrant’s |
| Trainee or unregistered person sent without disclosure | Provider (AIS 1.32) | Specification breach | If the person *is* a registrant/trainee, NRCPD process; if not, NRCPD has no respondent |
| Family used instead | Provider (Alan Graham) | Usually unused | Nothing |

No published case on this pass has made the agency the ombudsman respondent. That is a gap in the public record, not proof agencies never fail. It is why prompt 84 (if run) should look for WDTK FOIs that ask trusts for agency-KPI dashboards rather than for more PHSO narratives.

## 82.3 Money: what is published and what is not

Three price lists sit on different sides of the same transaction.

- **Freelancer ask:** NUBSLI April 2026 medians. London full day RSLI £375.
- **Customer tariff:** occasional agency or council rate cards (Bristol 2025, Lingo, VRS per-minute). Bristol’s full-day customer tariff £390.40 sits only £15 above the London NUBSLI median — which would imply a thin margin *if* the interpreter on that booking is paid the London median, and a fat one if they are paid a regional or trainee rate. That “if” is not evidenced.
- **Framework / invoice price:** RM6302 Schedule 3, ICB call-off unit rates, DAL’s SWL GP schedule. Unpublished.

*Locked Out* and NUBSLI agree on the *direction* of the problem (agency terms and public-sector rates push registrants off NHS work). Neither publishes a national “margin % on BSL bookings” series. The working-brief DAL 37% gross margin is company-wide and must not be pasted onto a Sutton GP booking.

CCS RM6302 is the live national on-ramp. Lot 4 is the correct lot for BSL/Deafblind. Using Lot 1 (spoken managed service) for BSL is the procurement error *Locked Out* describes. Whether an individual ICB has done that is a FOI question, not a finding on this pass.
