# Grok research prompt batch — Prompts 81–83: Interpreter Regulation & Agencies

Numbered per the project's master prompt index (`prompts/SuperGrok_Research_Prompt_Pack.md` in the DeafCommunityMapping repo — confirmed next free number was **81** at time of writing, per the index's own "⚠️ NEXT PROMPT NUMBER" line; **update the index and that line in the same edit that adds this batch**).

**Why this batch exists.** NRCPD and RBSLI registration is cited as the qualification standard across multiple existing batches — AIS requirement 1.32 names "NRCPD or RBSLI" registration as the must-have for a BSL/deafblind interpreter (`claude/grok-findings-templates-section.md`), and the EOL/palliative brief treats live NRCPD headcount (19 registered interpreters nationally as of 5 September 2026, against a government grant target of 68) as the binding supply constraint behind the whole domain. But no batch has looked at the regulator itself, how registration/agency procurement actually works, or what "NRCPD or RBSLI" means as a live regulatory system rather than a citation. Interpreter Regulation & Agencies sits on the dashboard with 7 figures and no dedicated prompt file. It is also the connective tissue between several already-built findings: RIDB scarcity (EOL brief), NHS interpreter procurement (templates section, NHS Standard Contract SC12.3), and Access to Work funding caps (52–54) — strengthening this domain should sharpen those, not duplicate them.

**Checked against existing prompts first.** No existing batch runs a dedicated pass on interpreter regulation, registration, or agency procurement as its own topic. AIS prompts 34–37 and the templates-section prompts (46–51) cite NRCPD/RBSLI registration as a *requirement* but do not examine the regulator's own standards, complaints process, or the size/shape of the registered workforce. The EOL/palliative brief's RIDB figures are DeafBlind-interpreter-specific (a narrower registration category) and were sourced for that domain's own purpose, not as a general interpreter-supply finding — this batch should confirm and generalise that figure rather than assume it already covers BSL/English interpreters broadly. Prompts 55–61 (HMRC) and 27–33 (Jobcentre Plus) each separately name interpreter-booking failures at their own body but do not examine the agencies or regulatory framework supplying those interpreters. This is the first dedicated batch on the regulatory/supply layer itself. Flag clearly anywhere the evidence is thin, unpublished, or anecdotal only — do not fill a gap with a plausible-sounding generalisation, and do not treat a professional body's own self-description of its standards as proof those standards are enforced or complied with in practice.

**81. NRCPD and RBSLI — regulatory status, standards, and registered workforce size**

As a UK interpreter-regulation researcher, using the National Registers of Communication Professionals working with Deaf and Deafblind People (NRCPD), the Regulatory Body for Sign Language Interpreters and Translators (RBSLI), the Institute of Translation and Interpreting, and any published Department of Health and Social Care or NHS England guidance referencing either register, establish exactly what these bodies are and what they actually regulate. Cover: (1) confirm whether NRCPD and RBSLI are statutory regulators (with a legal underpinning comparable to a healthcare regulator) or voluntary/independent registers that NHS and public-body contracts choose to require by policy (via AIS requirement 1.32 and NHS Standard Contract SC12.3) rather than by law — this distinction matters because it changes what "using an unregistered interpreter" actually breaches; (2) the current published registered headcount for each register, broken down by category if published (Registered Sign Language Interpreter/RSLI, Trainee Sign Language Interpreter/TSLI, Registered Sign Language Translator, Registered Interpreter for Deafblind People/RIDB, lipspeaker, notetaker, speech-to-text reporter) and by UK nation/region if the data is broken down that way — cross-check the RIDB figure already logged in this project's EOL/palliative brief (19 registered nationally as of 5 September 2026) directly against the current NRCPD public register rather than assuming it is still current; (3) NRCPD's and RBSLI's own complaints and fitness-to-practise process — what happens when an interpreter is found not competent or in breach of the professional code, whether outcomes (suspensions, removals) are published, and how many such cases have occurred in the last three years if that data is public; (4) whether Ofqual or any UK qualifications regulator oversees the interpreter-training and assessment pathway feeding these registers (the project's existing BSL Act batch already flags a "Signature Ofqual recheck" as an open item — confirm what that refers to and its current status rather than treating it as resolved). For each finding, give the source title, publishing body, date, and direct URL.

**82. Agency and procurement model — how NHS and public bodies actually buy interpreters**

As a UK public-sector procurement researcher, using published NHS Standard Contract terms (SC12.3), NHS trust and ICB tender/framework documents, Crown Commercial Service or regional procurement framework listings for language/BSL interpreting services, and named agency providers already identified elsewhere in this project (e.g. Sign Solutions, Language Empire, InterpretersLive, SignLive, DAL), establish how the interpreter-supply chain between a registered individual interpreter and a patient/claimant/appointment actually works. Cover: (1) whether NHS trusts and other public bodies typically contract with interpreting agencies as intermediaries (who then subcontract or directly employ registered interpreters) rather than booking a named registered interpreter directly, and what that layer means for accountability when a booking fails — does the trust's AIS/Equality Act duty transfer to the agency, or does it remain with the commissioning body regardless of agency failure; (3) any published framework agreement, tender document, or procurement notice naming the rate paid per hour/booking to agencies versus what is understood to reach the interpreter, and whether any documented dispute or workforce concern exists about agency margins reducing interpreter pay and therefore willingness to take NHS/public-sector bookings (cross-reference but do not duplicate the project's existing "Public Spending & Contracts" domain figures — this prompt is specifically about the interpreter-agency layer, not ICB commissioning spend generally); (4) any documented case, FOI response, or ombudsman finding describing an agency failing to fulfil a booking (interpreter did not attend, unregistered/trainee interpreter sent without disclosure, booking cancelled without adequate notice) and how responsibility was apportioned between the agency and the commissioning body. For each source, give title, publishing body/organisation, date, and URL, and flag clearly wherever a figure is agency-published marketing material rather than independently verified.

**83. Workforce sufficiency, geographic distribution, and the case for reform**

As a researcher documenting workforce sufficiency and regional access to registered BSL/deafblind interpreters, using NRCPD/RBSLI published registration data (per prompt 81), Association of Sign Language Interpreters (ASLI) publications, Deaf advocacy organisation reports (British Deaf Association, SignHealth, RNID), Freedom of Information disclosures already made by NHS trusts or DWP on interpreter booking fulfilment rates, and any Parliamentary or select committee scrutiny of interpreter workforce sufficiency, establish the scale and geographic shape of the UK's registered-interpreter shortage and what is being proposed to close it. Cover: (1) any national or regional data on interpreter-to-Deaf-population ratio, or on the specific geographic gaps already partially identified in this project (the EOL brief notes zero RIDB coverage in South West, East Midlands, and Yorkshire & Humber as of September 2026 — confirm whether a similar regional gap exists for general BSL/English (non-DeafBlind) registered interpreters, not just the DeafBlind-specific register); (2) any published government, NHS England, or DWP workforce plan, grant scheme, or training-place funding commitment aimed at increasing the number of registered interpreters (the EOL brief references a "68" RIDB grant target — establish what funds that target, over what timeframe, and whether any equivalent commitment exists for the general BSL/English interpreter workforce, which the EOL brief's figures do not cover); (3) any documented account (advocacy report, academic research, ASLI workforce survey) of why registered interpreters decline NHS, DWP, or legal-sector bookings — pay rate, agency terms, travel distance, emotional demands of specific settings (e.g. end-of-life, mental health, criminal justice) — and whether this is quantified anywhere rather than only anecdotal; (4) any specific reform proposal from an advocacy body, workforce body, or Parliamentary source calling for a national interpreter workforce strategy, and what has become of it. For each source, give title, organisation/author, date, and URL. Flag clearly where a figure is a stated target/aspiration versus an actual delivered/funded outcome — this project's standard is what is delivered, not what is announced.

### Notes for this batch specifically

- Prompt 81 is the gate for the whole batch — establish plainly whether NRCPD/RBSLI are statutory or voluntary registers before any later finding characterises "unregistered interpreter" as a breach of law versus a breach of contract/policy. Getting this wrong would repeat the DAGM lesson about not writing a legal conclusion ("non-compliant with the Act") that only a regulator can make.
- Prompt 81's RIDB cross-check should be treated as a live-data recheck, not an assumption that the EOL brief's 19/68 figures are still current by the time this batch runs — NRCPD's public register is the primary source, not the project's own prior brief.
- Prompt 82 should keep the agency layer distinct from the ICB/commissioner spend already logged under "Public Spending & Contracts" — this is about the interpreter-supply relationship specifically, not duplicate the domain that already exists for procurement spend generally.
- Prompt 83 is the most likely to surface only agency-side or advocacy-side estimates rather than an official national workforce figure — flag confidence per finding rather than presenting a target as an achieved outcome, matching the discipline already applied to the RIDB grant-target figure elsewhere in this project.
- As with every prior batch, flag confidence per finding rather than presenting anything as settled — this project's standard is "sourced and dated," not "asserted."
- A fourth prompt (lived experience / advocacy case for reform, matching the pattern in every completed batch — AIS 37, CODA 77, Deaf-older 71, VRS/Telecoms held back as 81 in that batch's own numbering) was deliberately left out of this batch given ongoing usage constraints. If usage allows a follow-up, that would be prompt 84, run and reviewed as its own pass.
