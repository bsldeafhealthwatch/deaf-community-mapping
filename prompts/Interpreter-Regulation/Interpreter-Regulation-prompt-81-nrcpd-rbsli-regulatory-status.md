## Headline

NRCPD and RBSLI are **voluntary independent registers**, not statutory regulators. There is no UK statute that makes it a criminal offence, or a reserved-title offence, to practise as a BSL/English interpreter without being on either register. NRCPD says so in its own words: it currently holds “voluntary Registers”; statutory regulation “will make it illegal for someone who isn’t registered to practise”; that change is a “long term aim” with no published timetable. RBSLI’s own FAQ: “Regulation of the interpreting profession is currently non-statutory or self-regulatory. RBSLI is the same in this respect.”

What *does* bite is policy and contract, not the registers’ own legal power:

- AIS requirement **1.32** (DAPB1605 v4.0) says organisations **must** use communication professionals with “valid recognised professional registration (for example, NRCPD, RBSLI)”. That is an information-standard “must comply” duty on NHS and publicly funded adult social care bodies, not a licensing statute.
- NHS Standard Contract 2026/27 **SC12.3** requires the provider to comply with AIS. It does not itself name NRCPD or RBSLI.
- The Ministry of Justice Language Services contract requires BSL interpreters to be registered with “a voluntary National Regulator for Language Service professionals,” with a Level 6 BSL minimum for court work (MoJ written answer 22 September 2025).
- NRCPD’s own statutory-regulation page is explicit about the enforcement hole: if a registrant is removed, “currently there is nothing to stop them from continuing to practise.”

Do not write “using an unregistered interpreter is illegal.” Write: it can breach AIS 1.32 / SC12.3 / a named contract / the Equality Act reasonable-adjustment duty. Only a court, ombudsman, or CQC/commissioner finding can convert that into a decided case.

**Live RIDB recheck (this pass, 10 September 2026).** NRCPD’s published registration-figures table still shows **19** Registered Interpreters for Deafblind People nationally. That matches the EOL brief’s 5 September 2026 snapshot. Regional zeros on that same table: **East Midlands 0, South West 0, Yorkshire and The Humber 0.** London 1, South East 3, West Midlands 4, North West 3, Scotland 3, East of England 2, North East 1, Wales 1, Northern Ireland 1. The government competency-assessment grant of **£15,000** is an *aspiration* to grow the RIDB bench to **68**. It is not a delivered headcount. The “8 RIDB” baseline cited in 2025–26 government/Board copy is the pre-grant figure; the live public table is 19.

**Workforce on this pass.** NRCPD homepage live feed: **2,087 professionals in total** on the registers. Category totals on the figures page (which **include dual registrations**, so they do not sum to unique people): Sign Language Interpreter **1,664**; Regulated Trainee SLI **241**; Sign Language Translator **96**; Relay Intralingual Interpreter **62**; Trainee Relay Intralingual **19**; Trainee SLT **20**; Lipspeaker **54**; Speech-to-Text Reporter **32**; Electronic Notetaker **9**; Manual Notetaker **2**; Deafblind Interpreter **19**. RBSLI does not publish a current headcount. The last sourced count found is an academic 2019 figure of 52; treat current RBSLI size as **no reliable figure found**. Scotland’s separate voluntary register (SRLPDC) published, in accounts to 31 March 2025: 61 registered BSL/English interpreters, 8 trainees, 2 Deafblind Manual interpreters, 2 lipspeakers, 1 guide communicator, 7 registered agencies.

**Complaints.** NRCPD runs a published Professional Conduct Complaints Procedure (v1.3, reviewed March 2026). Outcomes that restrict practice, suspend, or remove a registrant are named on the website. One named recent sanction: Darren Neil Adams, NRCPD 1018172, **12-month suspension 25 November 2025 – 24 November 2026** for dishonesty about a cancelled booking. There is **no published three-year aggregate** of complaints received, upheld, suspensions or removals. RBSLI has a complaints procedure and sanctions list including suspension and removal; **no published case outcomes or volumes** were found.

**Signature / Ofqual.** Signature is an Ofqual-recognised awarding organisation. The Level 6 Diploma in Sign Language Interpreting and Translation (INTRA6, RQF **603/5484/7**) is an Ofqual-regulated vocational qualification. Ofqual regulates the *qualification and the awarding organisation*. It does **not** regulate the professional registers, fitness to practise, or the right to work as an interpreter. University routes (Wolverhampton, Heriot-Watt, UCLAN, QUB) sit outside Ofqual’s vocational register and are approved separately by NRCPD as courses. That is the live meaning of the project’s “Signature Ofqual recheck”: Ofqual oversees Signature’s quals in England; it is not a substitute regulator for NRCPD/RBSLI.

## 81.1 Statutory regulator or voluntary register — the distinction that later prompts must not blur

Four layers get collapsed in briefings. Keep them separate.

**Layer 1 — statute that creates a reserved title or a licence to practise.**  
This is what the GMC, NMC and HCPC have. Sign-language interpreting does not have it. NRCPD has asked for it since at least November 2015. RBSLI says it wants statutory regulation for the interpreting profession as a whole. No Act, Order in Council or regulation found on this pass creates that licence. Ireland is the nearest islands comparator: the Irish Sign Language Act requires public bodies and courts to use RISLI-registered ISL interpreters. That is Irish law, not UK law.

**Layer 2 — voluntary register with a complaints process.**  
NRCPD, RBSLI and SRLPDC. They can remove a name from *their* list. They cannot stop the person taking the next NHS or agency booking unless the *purchaser* checks the list and refuses to book them.

**Layer 3 — purchaser policy and contract.**  
AIS 1.32, NHS Standard Contract SC12.3 (via AIS), MoJ Language Services specification, ICB/trust frameworks, Access to Work staff-guide product lists. This is where “must be NRCPD or RBSLI” actually lives. Breach is a contract/policy/Equality Act problem for the commissioning body, not a regulatory offence by the interpreter.

**Layer 4 — qualifications regulation.**  
Ofqual (England), SQA/SCQF (Scotland), university degree-awarding powers. These bodies accredit courses. They do not licence practitioners.

*Locked Out* priority 8 asks government to publish **statutory guidelines** stipulating NRCPD-registered qualified interpreters. That recommendation is itself evidence that the current architecture is policy-not-statute. Do not treat the recommendation as if the guidelines already exist.

## 81.2 What the live NRCPD table actually says

Read the published cells; do not average them into a “UK shortage” slogan in this file (that is prompt 83).

- The RIDB scarcity the EOL brief uses is still current: **19**, with three English regions at zero.
- General BSL/English SLI is a different shape. Every UK nation and every English region has a non-zero SLI cell. The thin regions are North East (40), Northern Ireland (43) and Wales (63), not the same three zeros as RIDB.
- Trainee SLI (241) is a large share of the pipeline sitting on the same register. RBSLI does not register trainees; NRCPD does. Purchasers that accept TSLI and purchasers that do not are running different risk rules.
- Dual registration means 1,664 + 241 + 96 + 62 + 19 + … is not a headcount of people. Use the homepage live feed (2,087) when a single total is required, and always footnote duals.

Scotland is the only nation with a second published year-end census (SRLPDC). The two Scotland numbers are not additive: many practitioners can sit on both NRCPD and SRLPDC.

## 81.3 Complaints and fitness to practise — process is public, volume is not

NRCPD rebuilt the procedure after a 2022 consultation, dropped the phrase “Fitness to Practice” as the product name, extended the complaint window to one year, and still publishes named outcomes only for restriction / suspension / removal. The Adams 12-month suspension is the clean worked example that the process can bite. Several 2025–26 summaries close with no finding or are discontinued after referral. That is normal case-examiner traffic, not proof the register is toothless — and not proof it is effective either.

What is missing, and should stay missing until a source appears:

- annual received / closed / upheld series
- a three-year removal count
- any RBSLI published determination
- any finding that an *agency*, rather than a named registrant, was the respondent (agencies are not on the NRCPD professional registers; that is prompt 82)

Charity Commission figures confirm NRCPD is a going concern (£436k income in 2024) and that it received one small government grant that year (£6,150). That grant line is not, on the face of the Commission page, the £15k RIDB competency grant.

## 81.4 Signature, Ofqual, and the pathway into the registers

The project’s open “Signature Ofqual recheck” item resolves as follows.

1. **Signature is an awarding organisation.** It designs and awards BSL language qualifications and the Level 6 interpreting/translation diploma (INTRA6, RQF 603/5484/7). Centres pay an annual approval fee (INTRA6 approval £674 in the 2025/26 fees booklet) plus per-assessment learner fees. Signature’s own site says its qualifications are Ofqual-accredited.
2. **Ofqual regulates that qualification in England** — validity of assessment, recognition of the AO — and separately regulates the forthcoming BSL GCSE, which is a school-language product, not an interpreter ticket.
3. **Passing INTRA6 (or an NRCPD-approved university award) makes a person *eligible to apply* for RSLI/RSLT.** Registration is a second gate: DBS, insurance, Code, fee, CPD. Course pass ≠ registered practitioner.
4. **NRCPD, not Ofqual, decides which courses count.** The approved-courses list includes Signature products and several university awards and the SRLPDC SCQF Level 10 customised award. A Signature-only story understates the pipeline.
5. **RIDB is a different Signature product plus a language prerequisite.** The Level 6 Deafblind Manual / Visual Frame / Hands-On certificate is not itself RSLI. Applicants also need Level 6 BSL (or equivalent). That is why a £15k assessment-weekend grant can exist alongside a 19-person live register: the bottleneck is the second qualification plus the assessment events, not Ofqual recognition of Signature as an AO.

Ofqual is therefore in scope for “is the diploma a regulated qualification?” and out of scope for “who may work in an NHS clinic tomorrow.” Mixing those two questions is how a briefing invents a statutory regulator that does not exist.
