## Headline

UK non-emergency VRS is **not** a portable service tied to the Deaf user that can reach any UK telephone number. The dominant commercial model is: an organisation (bank, utility, NHS body, council, employer) buys a seat, minutes package or directory listing from a VRS supplier; the Deaf user then calls *that organisation* through the supplier’s app or web directory. SignVideo’s own FAQ states the reachable set as “any organisations listed on the SignDirectory.” Ofcom’s live consumer page tells users to “check your bank’s website… or look it up on the directory pages of the video relay service providers.” That is the regulator describing a directory of opted-in organisations, not a user-tied number.

Two publicly funded exceptions exist and should not be collapsed into the commercial model:

1. **999 BSL** — mandated by General Conditions C5.11–C5.12 from 17 June 2022; approved provider Sign Language Interactions; BT acts as wholesale payer for industry; funded by regulated telephone and broadband companies; free and 24/7; emergency numbers only.
2. **Contact Scotland BSL** — Scottish Government-procured national VRS since 2016; Sign Solutions from 1 December 2025; published award value £3 million (ex VAT); Scotland-resident users can call public, third-sector, private and personal numbers in the UK and receive calls if registered. Explicitly **not** an emergency service.

Ofcom considered and **declined** to mandate non-emergency video relay when it designed 999 BSL. The 2019 EECC consultation said emergency communications “are in a different category from other communications and we are not proposing video relay is made available for non-emergency communications.” The 2012 relay-services statement left a follow-on video-relay review and a DCMS voluntary-provision initiative; no later Ofcom statement was found that converts that review into a universal non-emergency VRS duty.

No UK court or tribunal judgment was found that has decided an Equality Act claim specifically about a business or public body refusing to take a VRS-relayed call. The nearest adjudicated cousin is Ireland (WRC, Sofiya Kalinova v Permanent TSB, 2022). UK material is advocacy and press (Barclays / “Claire” fraud-alert story circulated by deafPLUS). The EHRC 2026 services Code of Practice lists “video interpreting services” as an example of a reasonable adjustment. That is guidance, not a decided case. Do not write “refusing a VRS call is unlawful” as if a UK court has said so.

The BSL Advisory Board’s *Locked Out* report (27 November 2025) is the named UK review that treats the absence of a national VRS in England and Wales as a structural gap and recommends “national video relay services (VRS) for England and Wales with 24/7 access.” NHS England accepted a pandemic-era recommendation for a national health VRS after BSL Health Access closed in March 2021; *Locked Out* records that recommendation as still unimplemented.

## 78.1 How the commercial model actually works

Three channels sit side by side. Mixing them is how the “Deaf people have VRS” claim becomes uncheckable.

**Channel A — Organisation directory (the default inbound path).**  
A bank, utility, council, NHS body or helpline contracts SignVideo, Sign Solutions / InterpretersLive, or Convo / SignLive. The organisation is added to that supplier’s directory. A Deaf BSL user opens the app, taps the organisation, and an interpreter places a voice call into the organisation’s published number. The caller is not charged; the organisation is. SignVideo states this in the FAQ. Ofcom’s consumer accessibility page points users to those same directories rather than to a single national number.

**Channel B — User-funded minutes (Access to Work, Direct Payment, personal subscription).**  
Convo’s 21 May 2026 help page lists AtW, Direct Payments, PIP and “service providers” as payers. SignVideo’s Play Store listing describes a separate subscription product for VRS / VRI / translation when the user is not calling a directory listing. This is the closest the commercial market comes to “call any number.” It is not free at the point of use unless a public fund or the user pays, and it is not an Ofcom-mandated service. Whether PIP lawfully funds VRS minutes is Convo’s wording, not a DWP circular found on this pass — keep that clause contested.

**Channel C — Public-service one-click hosts.**  
HMRC Extra Support and Debt Management SignVideo hosts (prompt 55), NHS 111 / 119 listings, DWP inbound VRS via DA Languages / Sign Solutions (UIN 160360, 14 March 2023: “All VRS is provided by our current supplier, DA Languages, via SignSolution. We no longer use… SignVideo”). These are still organisation-tied. They do not let the same user dial a GP, a taxi firm, or a neighbour.

The UKCoD directory and VRS Q&A are the industry-body documents that treat Channel A as the intended architecture: organisations purchase, providers quote a per-minute price, purchasers check the UKCoD specification. That is as close as this pass comes to a “contract/procurement model cited directly.” Individual NHS-trust or bank contracts were not opened; RM6302 Lot 4 unit rates remain unpublished (already logged in the F2F cost-stack note of 6 September 2026).

## 78.2 What 999 BSL is — and what Ofcom said it is not

999 BSL is the only UK VRS that is:

- imposed on communications providers by General Condition;
- approved against published criteria;
- free at the point of use including data;
- available 24/7 without pre-registration;
- funded by the telecoms industry rather than by the called organisation.

It reaches emergency services only. Ofcom’s 2019 consultation is explicit that the EECC equivalence language used to justify the duty “only applies to emergency communications,” and that Ofcom was “not proposing video relay is made available for non-emergency communications.” The 2025 evaluation celebrates outcomes (downloads, ~20,000 calls, wait times, lives-saved judgement) and does not reopen the non-emergency question.

Wholesale mechanics: BT contracts with SLI and recharges regulated providers. Ofcom did not mandate that wholesale structure; it mandated the outcome and left implementation to industry. The public evaluation does not print the industry bill.

Do not write that 999 BSL “proves” a universal VRS would be cheap or that Ofcom has “refused” a national service in 2025. What is sourced is a 2019 scope decision that has not been reversed.

## 78.3 Contact Scotland BSL is the UK’s only everyday “dial any number” VRS

Scotland funds a national VRS so that a Scotland-resident BSL user can place short interpreted calls to public bodies, third-sector organisations, private services and friends/family, and can receive calls if registered. The 2025 retender was awarded to Sign Solutions, go-live 1 December 2025, published envelope £3 million. The service is not 999. The FAQ still requires “a good internet connection.”

*Locked Out* uses this service as the “excellent practice which is not available elsewhere in the UK” and states, as a finding not a slogan, that England’s provision is limited to NHS 111 and 999 BSL. That sentence is the cleanest sourced statement of the England/Wales structural gap.

## 78.4 Third-party refusal — what can and cannot be printed

Print this stack, in this order:

1. **No UK judgment found** that a named business or public body unlawfully refused a VRS-relayed call.
2. **EHRC 2026 services Code** names video interpreting as an example reasonable adjustment. Anticipatory duty under EA 2010 ss.20 and 29 applies to service providers. That is the legal hook. It has not been tested on these facts in a published UK decision found on this pass.
3. **Ireland, 2022:** Permanent TSB lost an Equal Status Act complaint for treating an interpreter as a “third party.” Useful as a comparator, not as UK law.
4. **UK anecdote:** the Claire / Barclays fraud-alert story (directory hours + rejected interpreter call + redirection to text relay). Single case, advocacy-circulated.
5. **Relay UK hang-ups** have reached an English employment tribunal (Wilton). Different product.

US bank-refusal ADA settlements (Wells Fargo / NAD) and Canadian bank complaints are out of scope for this file except as a pointer that prompt 80 will need them. Do not import them into a UK legal-exposure sentence.

## Do-not-print list

- Do not print “UK VRS works like a phone number.”
- Do not print “refusing a VRS call is illegal” as a decided UK proposition.
- Do not print a national count of VRS-contracted organisations as if UKCoD or SignVideo had audited one on a dated census. “Over 150” is SignVideo Play Store copy.
- Do not print PIP as an official VRS funding stream without a DWP source.
- Do not collapse Relay UK (mandated text, any number) with commercial VRS (directory or user-paid).
- Do not treat Contact Scotland as available to England-resident users as of right. The FAQ is written for a Scottish service that can *place* UK-wide calls for its users.
- Do not use US Interstate TRS Fund numbers in this file.
