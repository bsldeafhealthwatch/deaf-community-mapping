# Prompt 84 — International Deaf and DeafBlind people travelling in the UK: what actually exists

**Master index prompt 84.** Inbound travel counterpart to VRS 78–80 (UK resident at home / at work / leaving the UK) and to the airline/airport addendum in `VRS-Telecoms-prompts-78-80-claude-feedback.md`. Compiled 13 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Establish what a **non-UK-resident** Deaf or DeafBlind visitor — whose sign language is usually **not** BSL — can actually use while physically in the UK: 999, 101, Relay UK, Contact Scotland, airport/Border Force desks, NHS care, hotels, taxis, retail. Do not treat that person as a Contact Scotland user, an AtW customer, or an AIS-flagged English GP patient. Do not assume ASL, ISL, LSF, DGS or International Sign can ride a UK BSL directory listing.

## Headline

The inbound product is thinner than the outbound UK-BSL story, and it is **BSL-shaped**.

A visitor who already uses **BSL** (or can make themselves understood in it) can, without a UK phone contract:

- open **999 BSL** from the website or a pre-downloaded app, with **no registration**;
- use **Heathrow Convo Access QR** or the Heathrow/Manchester directory listing to talk to *airport staff* in BSL;
- tap any organisation that has bought a SignVideo / Convo / InterpretersLive listing (NHS 111 BSL, some police 101 lines, some airlines);
- use **Relay UK** as **text**, if they can link a phone number.

A visitor whose language is **ASL, LSF, DGS or International Sign** does **not** have a published public VRS product in the UK. Ofcom has **not** required 999 BSL to be answered in Irish Sign Language, let alone ASL. AIS requirement **1.32** is a **qualification rule for BSL and deafblind interpreters** (NRCPD / RBSLI), not a visitor-scope clause. Other national sign languages sit with spoken-language interpreting: **out of AIS scope**. Equality Act s.29 still binds the service; it does not conjure an ASL roster.

Zero-rating of 999 BSL data is a duty on **UK regulated communications providers**. Ofcom’s own FAQ answers “can I call from *outside* the UK” (calls still land in a UK 999 room) and “hotel Wi-Fi registration.” It does **not** answer “I am visiting from another country on a foreign SIM.” Treat foreign-SIM zero-rating as **no reliable figure found**.

Contact Scotland BSL is funded for Deaf BSL users to call Scottish services and UK numbers. The FAQ does not print “Scotland residents only,” and the Scottish Government’s BSL page includes the word “visit.” That is **not** the same as a tourist product for an ASL user in a London hotel. Do not send an England-staying visitor there as of right.

Do not write “the UK is accessible for Deaf tourists.” Do not write “999 BSL works in ASL.”

## 84.1 Emergency and non-emergency calling

Three channels, three languages-of-work.

**999 BSL** is the only mandated video path. It is built for **BSL**. The visitor-friendly facts are real: no account, website or app, 24/7, free at the point of the call. The visitor-hostile facts are also real. The interpreter on the other end is a registered **BSL** interpreter. Ofcom has expressly declined to require even **Irish** Sign Language. The in-app text channel is the published fallback for fingerspelling, not a substitute language. Zero-rating is written onto **UK** communications providers. A tourist whose packets are billed by AT&T, Orange or Deutsche Telekom is outside the sentence Ofcom can enforce. Download the app on airport Wi-Fi *before* you need it; do not file that sentence as a guarantee that roaming data is free.

**Relay UK** is the only mandated *any-number* product, and it is **text**. A visitor who types English (or who can use a textphone) is closer to functional equivalence than an ASL user staring at a BSL interpreter. App onboarding wants a phone number that can complete a verification call to 0800 500 888. That is a UK-number-shaped door. EmergencySMS also needs a prior REGISTER-to-999 handshake — useless if the visitor learns about it in the ambulance bay.

**Contact Scotland BSL** remains a Scotland-funded BSL relay. The FAQ’s “any Deaf person… Scottish services… anywhere in the UK” sentence is wider than “Scotland resident,” and the Scottish Government likes the word “visit.” That still does not make it an inbound tourist service, and it does not make it an ASL service. An England-staying visitor has no entitlement. A Scotland-staying BSL visitor *might* get through; log that as contested, not as a product.

No official FAQ titled “I am visiting from another country” was found on 999 BSL, Ofcom, Relay UK or Contact Scotland.

## 84.2 Airports, ports, Border Force

Regulation 1107 is the floor: get the person to the gate and tell them the essential facts in an accessible format. It is not a language roster.

Heathrow (May 2026) and Manchester (2023, QR 2024) are the two sourced **airport-operator** BSL VRI products. Both are marketed to **BSL users**. Heathrow’s QR path is the rare UK VRS object that does not need a pre-existing app account — that helps a visitor who has data and can see a green poster. It still interprets into **BSL**. It does not interpret the passenger to Border Force, and it does not follow them to the taxi rank.

Home Office interpreting is a spoken-language panel plus ESP contracts that **name BSL**. That is better than nothing and worse than a published walk-up IS desk. Do not write that Border Force “has International Sign.” Do not write that it “refuses BSL.” The public document trail supports only: spoken panel exists; BSL is an ESP line item; on-the-day fulfilment at a given desk is unpublished.

Airline listings from the 78–80 addendum still apply, with one inbound-specific wrinkle: **Ryanair lists ISL as well as BSL.** That is the only sourced non-BSL airline directory row. It is Irish Sign Language to Ryanair, not a universal visitor key.

## 84.3 Healthcare while visiting

Do not flatten three stacks.

1. **Who pays** — overseas-visitor charging. A&E and NHS 111 advice are generally free if the person did not travel to obtain the treatment. Admission after A&E is often not.
2. **Who must communicate** — AIS + Equality Act. AIS attaches to the **patient/service user** of NHS or publicly funded adult social care. Nothing in the v4.0 requirements text opened this pass says “ordinarily resident only.” A visitor in A&E is a service user. AIS 1.32 then says the BSL or deafblind interpreter the trust books must be registered. It does not say the trust must hold ASL.
3. **What language** — AIS is explicit that “language other than English… for reasons other than disability” is out of scope. A French Deaf visitor’s LSF is, on that drafting, closer to community-language interpreting than to AIS BSL. The trust may still have to make a reasonable adjustment under the Equality Act. That is a duty, not a bench.

NHS 111 BSL via SignVideo is the nearest thing to a national urgent-care door. It is BSL, 24/7, and the SignVideo 111 page asks the user to register. There is no NHS England “Deaf visitor” page.

Same-day A&E for an ASL user is the honest stress test. The live booking path is still “trust contractor tries to find a registered professional.” The registered professional class in 1.32 is BSL / deafblind. Do not invent a national ASL list to close that sentence.

## 84.4 Hotels, transport, retail, 101

Fulfilment is the directory. A visitor does not need AtW or a GP record to tap a listing. They need the app, data, hours, and a language match.

101 is not 999. It is a local force purchase. West Yorkshire (InterpretersLive), Hampshire & IoW (SignVideo, press-claimed 24/7), Avon and Somerset (Convo) are examples, not a map. A tourist in a force that has not bought a listing has Relay UK text, the force’s web form, or a hearing companion.

VisitBritain’s consumer line is one bullet: Britain uses BSL. The toolkit tells *attractions* to sell bookable BSL tours. Royal Yacht Britannia is a named example of BSL **and ASL** multimedia guides — an attraction content product, not a phone system. Do not generalise it.

International Sign is not a published UK public VRS SKU on SignVideo or Convo UK.

## 84.5 DeafBlind visitors

The RIDB bench is 19 people. Three English regions sit at zero. Communicator-guides are a different occupation and are not an NRCPD category (prompt 81 / DeafBlind 115). Contact Scotland’s Braille-display path helps a **BSL-using** deafblind person calling a UK number from a Scottish-shaped service. It does not create an airport communicator-guide. Regulation 1107 will move a person through a terminal. It will not manual-interpret a hotel check-in. AIS will, in theory, require a registered deafblind interpreter once the person is an NHS service user and the need is identified. Identification on a first A&E night, with no prior flag, is the gap the AIS lived-experience files already describe for residents. It is worse for a visitor.
