# Grok research prompt 84 — International Deaf visitors in the UK

Numbered per `SuperGrok_Research_Prompt_Pack.md`. **Next free number at issue was 84.** Interpreter-regulation lived experience is deferred to **85** so this inbound-travel hole can be run first. Do not start a new batch at 1.

**Why this prompt exists.** VRS 78–80 and the airline/airport addendum in `VRS-Telecoms-prompts-78-80-claude-feedback.md` asked what a **UK BSL user** can do at home, at work, and when *leaving* the UK. They did not ask the reverse: a Deaf person who lives in another country, whose sign language is not BSL, landing at Heathrow or Manchester, staying in a hotel, needing 999, a GP, Border Force, an airline desk, or a taxi. That person is not a Contact Scotland user, not an AtW customer, and not automatically an NRCPD-bookable BSL patient. Prompt 79’s “no reliable figure found for UK VRS while abroad” is the outbound gap. This prompt is inbound.

**Checked against existing prompts.** 78 = per-org UK VRS model. 79 = UK resident home/office/travel. 80 = US/Canada/Australia/NZ comparators. Airline addendum = UK carriers’ BSL *directory listings* for UK-resident or BSL-using passengers. AIS 34–37 assumes an NHS patient inside the Standard. Do not treat a two-week visitor as if they had an AIS flag on an English GP record. Do not assume ASL, ISL, LSF, DGS, or International Sign can be served by a BSL VRS listing.

**84. International Deaf and DeafBlind people travelling in the UK — what actually exists**

As a UK access-and-telecoms researcher, using CAA/airport assistance pages, airline help pages already opened in the 78–80 addendum, 999 BSL and Relay UK rules, Home Office/Border Force accessibility material, NHS visitor-and-migrant charging plus AIS scope text, NRCPD categories (prompt 81), and any published tourist-board or VisitBritain Deaf-access page, establish what a non-UK-resident Deaf or DeafBlind visitor can actually use while physically in the UK. Cover:

(1) **Emergency and non-emergency calling.** Can a visitor use 999 BSL without a UK phone plan or a pre-downloaded app; is 999 BSL BSL-only or does it accept International Sign / ASL / ISL; what happens on a foreign SIM for data zero-rating; is Relay UK usable by a visitor; is Contact Scotland available to a tourist. Flag any official FAQ that answers “I am visiting from another country.”

(2) **Airports, ports, Border Force.** What assistance product exists at LHR/MAN and any other sourced airport for a passenger who does not use BSL (PRM assistance under Regulation 1107/2006 is not the same as a BSL VRS QR). Any sourced Border Force / Home Office BSL or International Sign path at immigration. Do not import ADA/ACAA as UK law.

(3) **Healthcare while visiting.** AIS applies to NHS care — confirm whether a visitor receiving NHS treatment (EHIC/GHIC, paid-for, or A&E) is inside AIS 1.32, and whether the live booking path is still “trust agency books a BSL RSLI.” What exists if the visitor needs ASL or International Sign rather than BSL. Any published NHS “Deaf visitor” page. Do not invent a national ASL interpreter roster.

(4) **Hotels, transport, retail, police 101.** Directory VRS listings that a visitor could open without a UK AtW award or a UK GP record. Hours. Language. Whether International Sign is offered by any named UK VRS supplier as a public product.

(5) **DeafBlind visitors.** RIDB live headcount is 19 with three English regions at zero (prompt 81). Any sourced airport/hotel/NHS path that is not “hope a communicator-guide is on shift.”

For each finding give source title, publishing body, date, and URL. Flag marketing vs independently verified. Use the standard table: **Label | Value | Source name | Source URL | Publication date | Confidence (Verified/Contested/No reliable figure found).**

Do not write “the UK is accessible for Deaf tourists.” Do not write “999 BSL works in ASL” unless an official FAQ says so. Do not treat a UK BSL directory listing as a universal visitor product.
