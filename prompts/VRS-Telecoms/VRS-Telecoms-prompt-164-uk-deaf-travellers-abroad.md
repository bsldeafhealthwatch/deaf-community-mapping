# Grok research prompt 164 — UK Deaf and DeafBlind travellers abroad

Numbered per `SuperGrok_Research_Prompt_Pack.md` — confirmed next free number was **164** at time of writing, per the index's own "⚠️ NEXT PROMPT NUMBER" line; **update the index and that line in the same edit that adds this prompt.**

**Why this prompt exists.** VRS 78–80 established the UK-resident home/office/travel picture and the international comparator landscape; prompt 79 explicitly flagged "no reliable figure found for UK VRS while abroad" as an outbound gap it could not close. Prompt 84 then answered the mirror question — what an *inbound* international visitor can use in the UK. This prompt closes the remaining side of that pair: what a **UK-resident BSL user** can actually use once they are the one abroad — on a foreign SIM, in a foreign airport, calling a foreign 999-equivalent, or trying to get NHS-standard healthcare communication support in another country. This is the domain's last unanswered structural question before the batch's lived-experience leg (still unnumbered — see prompt 79/84 notes) could ever be usefully run.

**Checked against existing prompts first.** 78 = UK per-org VRS model. 79 = UK resident home/office/travel (flagged the outbound-abroad gap this prompt closes). 80 = what the US/Canada/Australia/NZ/EU systems deliver *for their own residents* — it did not test whether a UK BSL user, as a foreign visitor to those countries, could actually use them. 84 = the reverse direction (international visitor inbound to the UK). No existing prompt asks what a UK BSL user can do once they land in Paris, New York, or Sydney. Do not re-litigate the domestic UK model (78) or re-describe what other countries' systems are (80) — this prompt tests **UK-traveller access to those foreign systems as an outsider**, which is a different and unanswered question. Flag clearly anywhere the evidence is thin, unpublished, or anecdotal only — do not fill a gap with a plausible-sounding generalisation, and do not assume that because a country has a national VRS (prompt 80), a UK tourist can register for or use it.

**164. What a UK-resident Deaf or DeafBlind traveller can actually use once abroad**

As a UK access-and-telecoms researcher, using the FCC TRS/VRS rules and any published visitor/registration eligibility text (prompt 80), CRTC/CAV Canada VRS eligibility rules, Australia's National Relay Service and New Zealand Relay eligibility pages, EU/EEA national relay service pages and the EENA 2024 relay paper (prompt 80), UK government travel-advice pages (GOV.UK "Foreign travel advice" per-country pages), UK travel insurance and EHIC/GHIC guidance, and any published Deaf-travel advocacy material (RNID, British Deaf Association, Deaf travel bloggers/forums cited as lived testimony, not statistics), establish what a UK BSL-signing traveller can and cannot do once physically outside the UK. Cover:

(1) **Registration and eligibility barriers.** For each of the user-tied VRS systems already mapped in prompt 80 (US FCC VRS, Canada CAV, Australia NRS Video Relay, New Zealand Video Interpreting), does the published eligibility text require the user to be a resident, a citizen, or hold a domestic phone number/address to register — or could a UK visitor register and use the service during a short stay? Quote the actual eligibility wording rather than assuming residency is required. Flag clearly where no eligibility text was found either way.

(2) **Emergency calling abroad.** Confirm whether 999 BSL can be used by a UK traveller once on foreign soil (prompt 84 already established that 999 BSL calls made from outside the UK connect only to **UK** control rooms, per Ofcom — this prompt tests the reverse: does the destination country's own emergency number (112 in the EU, 911 in the US/Canada, 000 in Australia, 111 in NZ) offer any sign-language-equivalent access route a UK BSL signer could use, or is a hearing companion / text-only / nothing the only option). Do not assume International Sign is accepted by any named emergency service without a source.

(3) **Healthcare abroad.** For a UK traveller needing medical treatment in the EU/EEA (GHIC), the US, or another country with reciprocal healthcare arrangements, is there any published guidance — from GOV.UK, NHS, a UK travel insurer, or the destination country's own health system — addressing Deaf/BSL-signing patient communication support specifically? Do not assume AIS travels with the patient; it is an NHS/English-social-care standard and does not bind a foreign hospital. Flag as a gap if no such guidance exists.

(4) **Airports and transit abroad.** Do any foreign airports publish a BSL or International Sign VRI/QR product comparable to Heathrow's or Manchester's (prompt 84)? Regulation (EU) 1107/2006 assistance obligations (the EU's own version of the UK-retained regulation in prompt 84) apply at EU airports — confirm what they actually deliver for a Deaf passenger versus what they deliver for mobility-impaired passengers, since the two are often conflated in airport accessibility literature.

(5) **Lived experience.** Any published first-person UK account (blog, vlog transcript, advocacy case study, forum thread specifically attributed to a named or clearly identified individual) of a UK Deaf or DeafBlind traveller's actual experience trying to communicate in an emergency, healthcare, or transit situation while abroad. Label clearly as single-account testimony, not a statistic, and do not generalise from one account to "Deaf travellers abroad face X."

For each finding, give the source title, publishing body, date, and direct URL. Flag confidence per finding (Verified / Contested / No reliable figure found) rather than presenting anything as settled — matching the discipline already applied throughout this domain.

### Notes for this prompt specifically

- This is a single prompt, not a numbered sub-batch — it closes one specific, previously-flagged gap (prompt 79's "no reliable figure found for UK VRS while abroad") rather than opening a new multi-prompt domain.
- Do not import US ADA/ACAA rules as if they applied to a UK traveller in the US — the question is what the *destination country's* system offers a foreign registrant, not what it owes its own residents (already covered in prompt 80).
- The lived-experience item (5) is deliberately folded into this single prompt rather than held back as a separate numbered leg, since the whole domain's dedicated lived-experience prompt remains unissued (flagged in the 78–80 and 84 reviews) — if a genuinely rich vein of UK first-person testimony turns up here, it may be worth a dedicated follow-up prompt from 165+ rather than overloading this one.
- As with every prior batch, flag confidence per finding rather than presenting anything as settled — this project's standard is "sourced and dated," not "asserted."
