# Prompt 79 — The home / office / travel access gap

**Master index prompt 79.** Second file in the VRS & Telecoms Equity batch (prompts 78–80). Compiled 10 September 2026. Read prompt 78 first. This file does not re-litigate the per-organisation contract model; it asks where that model, plus AtW and connectivity rules, actually break for a BSL user trying to place a call from home, from work, or while mobile.

**Dossier rule.** Stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. Gaps stay gaps. Do not collapse home, workplace and travel into one “access gap” percentage — the payer, the cost driver and the fix differ by context.

**Cross-reference, do not duplicate.** Funding-cap, freeze and processing-time figures for Access to Work live in prompts 52–54. This file uses them only where they have a *calling* consequence.

---

## Headline

There is **no published UK statistic** for the share of Deaf BSL users who have working VRS from a home broadband or mobile connection. There is **no published study** that counts how many UK telephone numbers a hearing person can reach versus how many a BSL user can reach via VRS. Both absences are findings.

What *is* sourced, split by context:

- **Home.** Directory VRS (SignVideo SignDirectory, InterpretersLive, Convo Community Directory) is free to the caller only for organisations that have bought a listing. Everyday calling to anyone else requires a user-paid or AtW-paid minute bundle, Contact Scotland BSL (Scotland-resident users), or a fall-back to Relay UK text. The broadband Universal Service Obligation is 10 Mbit/s down / 1 Mbit/s up and is not written as a VRS-grade duty. Ofcom General Conditions mandate text relay and emergency video relay; they do not mandate household VRS. 999 BSL itself needs “a good internet connection” and the operator FAQ contemplates users being billed for data they should not have been charged.

- **Workplace.** Access to Work will pay for “British Sign Language (BSL) interpreters, video relay service support, lip speakers, palantypists and note takers.” VRS funded that way is tied to the current award and the current employer. DWP’s employer factsheet is explicit: support-worker awards (the bucket VRS sits in) **do not automatically transfer** when the person changes employer. RNID’s April 2026 AtW survey records advisers pushing VRS as a cheaper substitute for booked interpreters, renewal cuts to VRS minutes, and AtW’s own caseworkers failing when *their* VRS queue is over capacity. The cap (£69,260, frozen) and the 100-plus-day processing times are the calling consequence of prompts 52–54, not a new number.

- **Travel / unplanned.** The only UK VRS that is designed to work from any data connection without a pre-arranged organisational contract is **999 BSL** (emergencies) and, for Scotland-resident users, **Contact Scotland BSL**. An unplanned call to a GP, a 101 non-emergency police line that is not in a directory, a delayed-train desk, or a taxi firm has no Ofcom-monitored universal video path. Relay UK remains the mandated any-number product — in text, not BSL. No mobile network operator was found that sells a “dial any number in BSL” add-on.

## 79.1 Home — connectivity is assumed, reachability is not

A home broadband line plus a smartphone is enough to open SignVideo, Convo or Contact Scotland. That is not the same as being able to place the call the hearing member of the household can place.

What a home user can do without opening a wallet:

- tap any organisation that has bought a listing;
- call 999 BSL;
- if they live in the Contact Scotland scheme, call a UK number through that service;
- use Relay UK in text.

What they cannot do on that same connection, unless they have bought or been awarded minutes:

- phone a GP practice that is not on a directory;
- phone a neighbour, a tradesperson, a school office, or a workplace that has not bought VRS;
- receive an arbitrary inbound voice call in BSL, unless they have registered for call-back with a supplier that offers it and the caller knows how to use that supplier.

The USO and the General Conditions do not close that gap. The USO is a 10/1 floor. The GCs give text relay and emergency video relay. Treating “has broadband, therefore has VRS” as a sourced statement would be a citation error.

999 BSL’s own FAQ is the cleanest official admission that even the mandated service still depends on a “good” connection and that zero-rating sometimes fails in the billing stack.

## 79.2 Workplace — AtW buys minutes, not a number

Access to Work will pay for VRS. That sentence is true and insufficient.

The award is:

- capped (£69,260, frozen — prompt 52);
- slow to decide (100-plus working days — prompt 52);
- reviewed, and on RNID’s 2026 evidence sometimes cut, including VRS minutes;
- tied to the current job. Support-worker awards do not automatically follow the person to a new employer;
- in tension with booked face-to-face interpreting. RNID’s survey records advisers “demanding” VRS. That is a calling-quality issue, not just a funding-cap issue: the substitute product cannot carry specialist or customer-facing work for every user.

Firewall and device-management blocks (corporate MDM forbidding the VRS app, guest Wi-Fi that will not pass WebRTC) are reported as a class of problem in the existing AtW staff-guide note. No official count exists. Do not print one.

The workplace gap is therefore not “AtW does not fund VRS.” It is: the funded product is a minutes bundle attached to one employment relationship, approved on a timescale measured in months, and not accepted by AtW advisers as equivalent to F2F in every role.

## 79.3 Travel — the only portable BSL “phone” is 999

From a train, a hotel Wi-Fi, or a supermarket car park, the published BSL options are:

1. **999 BSL** if it is an emergency and the data path works.
2. **A directory app** if the needed organisation happens to be listed *and* the app will load on that connection.
3. **Contact Scotland BSL** if the user is in that scheme.
4. **Relay UK** if the user will accept text rather than BSL.
5. **Convo Access QR** if they are standing in a partner venue.

There is no Ofcom-approved, MNO-provided, or government-funded BSL service whose purpose is “call whoever you need to call, from wherever you are.” That is the travel finding. It is the same structural fact as prompt 78, observed from a platform rather than from a sofa.

Unplanned healthcare is the worked example *Locked Out* already quantified: three-quarters struggle to book, almost half give up, one-third miss a booked appointment because they cannot call to rearrange. Those figures are about the telephone system as a whole, not a VRS-specific survey. They are still the best published measure of what happens when the reachable set is a directory rather than a number.

## 79.4 The missing comparison number

Prompt 79 question (4) asked for a study quantifying the gap between the numbers a hearing person can reach and the numbers a BSL user can reach via VRS.

**No such study was found.** SignVideo’s “over 150 services,” UKCoD’s “growing number,” and Ofcom’s advice to look the bank up on a supplier directory are descriptions of a list, not a ratio against the UK numbering plan.

Print the absence. Do not derive a fake percentage from 150 ÷ (millions of UK numbers).

## Carry into prompt 80

The features that would close these three gaps, if a foreign model actually delivered them, are:

- a user-tied credential that works from any broadband or mobile connection (home);
- funding that is not reset when the employer changes (workplace);
- the ability to place and receive a call to an unpublished number while away from a partner venue (travel).

Prompt 80 must test whether the US TRS Fund, Australia’s NRS, Canada’s VRS, New Zealand, or the EU Accessibility Act actually deliver those three features — not whether their policy documents say “functionally equivalent.”
