# Prompt 53 — What to actually say: practical scripts for requesting AtW and PIP interpreter support

**Master index prompt 53.** Second file in the AtW / PIP plain-language guide batch (prompts 52–54). Compiled 9 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Claimant-facing wording for the public “Know Your Rights” AtW and PIP guide pages. Prompts 20–33 already cover DWP’s own process, contractors and complaints ladder. Prompt 47 already catalogues NHS letters. This file only asks: what can a BSL user copy or adapt, from a published guide, when they book an assessment or fill a PIP form?

**Guide-writing rule.** Published sentences first, in quotation marks, with the organisation and URL. Anything this project would add is labelled **adaptation, not a published script**. Do not invent a “RNID official script” that RNID did not print. Do not tell people a family member can interpret a PIP assessment — BDA advises against it.

---

## Headline

There is **no national one-page “say this to Access to Work / PIP” card** from RNID, SignHealth, BDA or Disability Rights UK that covers both schemes.

What does exist, and is usable:

- **PIP assessment booking and no-show:** the strongest Deaf-led script is the British Deaf Association PIP guide (English transcript of a BSL film). It tells the person to request the interpreter themselves, to refuse to go ahead if none arrives, and to reject notes or lip-reading as a substitute.
- **PIP form, Question 9 / Activity 7 “communicating verbally”:** Citizens Advice and a handful of advice-site example paragraphs. None of the four named Deaf organisations publish a fill-in Activity 7 paragraph. Official PIP language still matters: “communication support,” “basic” vs “complex” verbal information, and **lip-reading does not count**.
- **Access to Work assessment booking:** no published copy-paste sentence from RNID, SignHealth, BDA or DR UK. Closest tools are the official **BSL Adjustments Planner** (DWP, 27 November 2024) and Sign Solutions’ commercial how-to (be specific about hours). DeafATW letters are about **award cuts**, not booking the assessment interpreter.
- **VRS offered instead of in-person:** assessment providers (Capita, Ingeus, Maximus) now treat a BSL request on a “telephone” PIP appointment as a **SignVideo / VRS booking by default**. BDA’s no-show advice is about a missing interpreter, not about refusing VRS. That refusal script is a gap. Capita’s own clinician guide says an assessment should not go ahead without the requested BSL/ISL interpreter or lip speaker.

Default posture for the guide: **curate and credit first**. Draft only the two holes — AtW assessment request, and “I asked for in-person, you offered VRS.”

## 53.1 Requesting a BSL interpreter when booking an AtW or PIP assessment

### PIP — published wording

**British Deaf Association, “Personal Independence Payment (PIP)” guide.** English page with BSL film. Page live 2026 (site footer © BDA 2026). NDCS points to this film as the BSL PIP guide.

Simon, in the transcript:

> “When you get your letter it will say something like, ‘Dear Lisa, You have been invited for an assessment…’ It will list the date and time for your assessment. The letter will not contain any information about an interpreter. It’s your responsibility to let them know whether you’d like an BSL interpreter, lip speaker, speech to text reporter or a deaf blind hands on interpreter. You have to contact them with the request and they’ll arrange it ahead of your appointment.”

Contact routes named on the same page: Next Generation Text / minicom, SignVideo, or a hearing person making the call.

<https://bda.org.uk/pip-guide/>

**National Deaf Children’s Society, “Personal Independence Payment (PIP) for deafness.”** Live 2026. Not a script; a checklist line:

> “If your assessment is over the phone and you’re worried about it, you can request: a face-to-face assessment; a language or British Sign Language (BSL) interpreter.”

Also: tell the office arranging the assessment as soon as possible if you cannot communicate by phone. BSL contact for the PIP claim line: textphone 0800 917 7777; Relay UK 18001 then 0800 917 2222.

<https://www.ndcs.org.uk/advice-and-support/all-advice-and-support-topics/money-and-benefits/personal-independence-payment-pip>

**Scope, “Preparing for your PIP assessment.”** Example adjustments list includes “an interpreter or signer.” Contact the assessment centre as soon as you get the date.

<https://www.scope.org.uk/advice-and-support/preparing-for-pip-assessment>

**Assessment providers — how they currently book BSL (not claimant scripts, but the person needs to know what “request a BSL interpreter” triggers):**

**Finding.** Asking for “a BSL interpreter” on a telephone PIP appointment is, on current contractor pages, likely to produce **VRS**, not an in-person interpreter at a centre. If the person wants face-to-face BSL they must ask for both: a face-to-face assessment **and** an in-person registered interpreter. NDCS is the only named-charity page that puts those two requests next to each other.

### Access to Work — published wording

**Gap.** RNID’s Access to Work page (updated 15 April 2026) tells people how to apply, points to the official BSL Adjustments Planner, and lists BSL VRS for the helpline. It does **not** print a sentence to send when DWP books an AtW workplace or telephone assessment.

<https://rnid.org.uk/information-and-support/benefits/access-to-work/>

**DWP, Access to Work BSL Adjustments Planner.** Published 27 November 2024. 12-page PDF + large-print + BSL YouTube explainer. It is a workplace-needs checklist (communication, meetings, documents), not an assessment-booking script. RNID correctly points BSL users here. Credit and link it; do not rewrite it.

<https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-work-bsl-adjustments-planner>

**Sign Solutions, “How to apply for Access to Work support.”** Commercial provider, not a Deaf-led charity. Two usable lines:

> “If you use BSL, arrange for an interpreter for any telephone assessments that might be part of the application process. You can request this when you apply or contact Access to Work in advance to make arrangements.”

> “Be specific when describing your support needs. Instead of stating ‘I need an interpreter’, specify ‘I need a BSL interpreter for weekly team meetings (2 hours), monthly all-staff meetings (3 hours) and quarterly performance reviews (1 hour)’.”

<https://www.signsolutions.uk.com/how-to-apply-for-access-to-work-support/>

**Deaf Umbrella, Deaf Resource Pack / workplace emails.** Deaf-led. Scripts are for asking an *employer* for an adjustment, not DWP:

> “I need a BSL interpreter to take part in meetings.”

Email 1: “I am Deaf and need [BSL interpreter / captions / etc.] to access [meetings / training / job tasks]. This helps me do my job properly and fully take part.”

<https://www.deafumbrella.com/_files/ugd/69a578_cbb5a0070b834bbd9ed56a16989dbf75.pdf>

**Adaptation, not a published script — AtW assessment request.** No RNID / SignHealth / BDA / DR UK sentence was found. If the guide ships one, keep it this short and label it as adapted from the BDA PIP request + Sign Solutions specificity rule:

> “I am a Deaf BSL user. British Sign Language is my first language. Please book a registered (NRCPD yellow-badge) in-person BSL/English interpreter for my Access to Work assessment on [date]. Video relay is not an adequate alternative for this appointment. Please confirm the interpreter’s name and registration in writing.”

That paragraph is this project’s draft. It is not a quotation.

## 53.2 PIP form wording that tracks the descriptors

Focus is claimant-facing phrasing for Question 9 / Activity 7 “communicating verbally.” Not DWP’s internal scoring manual (prompt 21).

### What the legal words mean (so the form answer can use them)

PIP regulations, as explained in DWP / advice-sector guides:

- **Communication support** = support from a person trained or experienced in communicating with people with specific communication needs — a sign-language interpreter is the worked example — or someone who knows the claimant well.
- **Basic verbal information** = a simple sentence in the person’s native language (“Can I help you?”).
- **Complex verbal information** = more than one sentence, or one complicated sentence.
- Verbal information includes information interpreted from speech into sign and back.
- People who *would* need that support score the descriptor even if they do not currently have an interpreter.

Citizens Advice Q9 page: assessment is of communication in the person’s **first language**, not English. “If you’re deaf or partially deaf this is the most important question on the form.” Tick yes if “you need an interpreter or signer.”

<https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/benefits/sick-or-disabled-people-and-carers/pip/help-with-your-claim/fill-in-form-pip/9-communicating-verbally/>

**Lip-reading does not count.** Upper Tribunal CPIP/315/2018: ability to lip-read should not be taken into account when deciding whether the person can communicate. Capita’s clinician information report on deafness (CIR-Deafness) states the same operational rule: “within the scope of PIP, lip reading is not considered to be an acceptable means of communication.” If an assessor offers lip-reading instead of BSL, that is the rule to name.

Capita CIR-Deafness: <https://haas.capita.co.uk/dam/documents/cir-ni/CIR-Deafness.pdf>

### Published example paragraphs

**Citizens Advice Q9 — the only example they print** (tiredness / speech, not a BSL-first paragraph):

> “I'm deaf, and sometimes I find it hard to make myself understood when I’m tired. When someone can’t understand me it gets me down and makes me want to not try anymore. I just stay at home and don’t get dressed for a few days and I don’t eat properly.”

**Money Wellness, “Help filling in your PIP form: Q9 communicating verbally.”** Not Deaf-led. Closest published BSL-first example found:

> “I have been profoundly deaf since birth. Even though I wear two hearing aids, I rely primarily on sign language to understand other people. … British Sign Language (BSL) is my first language and my partner interprets conversations for me. I rely heavily on my partner to help me communicate. Without my partner, I’m unable to communicate with other people except for friends and family who know BSL. … Unless I write down what I want to say using a pen and paper, I’m excluded from conversations with people who don’t know BSL.”

<https://www.moneywellness.com/money-booster/benefits-help/disability-sickness-and-carer-benefits/pip/pip-help-with-form-questions/9-communicating-verbally>

**Gap.** RNID, SignHealth, BDA and Disability Rights UK do **not** publish a Question 9 model answer. BDA’s PIP film tells people to get help filling the form (CAB — with the warning CAB will not fund an interpreter — a local deaf organisation, or an adviser). It does not dictate the English paragraph. NDCS publishes a separate “guide to filling in the PIP form for deaf young people” (download + online tool) aimed at families; that is the nearest Deaf-sector form walkthrough.

<https://www.ndcs.org.uk/advice-and-support/all-advice-and-support-topics/money-and-benefits/personal-independence-payment-pip>

### Adaptation, not a published script — Activity 7 box

If the guide offers a starter paragraph, keep the official words in and credit it as an adaptation of Citizens Advice structure + PIP definitions + BDA “BSL is my first language” stance:

> “British Sign Language is my first language. I cannot follow or take part in a spoken English conversation without a registered BSL/English interpreter. That is true for simple one-sentence information and for longer or complicated information. Hearing aids / lip-reading / written notes do not give me access to spoken English. Without an interpreter I miss what people say, I cannot make myself understood, and I avoid speaking to people I do not know.”

Do not tell people which descriptor letter to claim. That is advice, not a script, and it belongs with a named adviser.

## 53.3 Interpreter fails to attend, or VRS is offered instead of in-person

Two different problems. Keep them on separate lines in the guide.

### A. Nobody is there (or the person who arrives is a trainee on their own)

**BDA PIP guide — the usable no-show script.** Simon, answering Lisa (“If I arrive on the assessment day and there is no interpreter – what do I do? I don’t want to risk losing out if I cancel or postpone it.”):

> “If no interpreter is at your appointment it’s your right to refuse to go ahead with it on the day. The DWP have a duty to provide an interpreter or whichever communication preference you have requested. If there is no communication support then you should not go any further with the appointment. The advisor may indicate to you they write down information as a substitute, or they may ask if you can lipread instead. Make it clear by your refusal that those are not acceptable options. Explain to the advisor that you will attend at another time when the correct provisions are in place. I would strongly advise against bringing a family member to come along and ‘interpret’ for you. The best way is to have impartial communication support for your appointment.”

Badge rule on the same page:

> “Any interpreter who attends your appointment must hold a yellow badge as seen here. A yellow badge is proof that the interpreter is qualified to the acceptable standard. If the interpreter presents a blue or purple badge it means they are a trainee interpreter which is not suitable. However, if the trainee interpreter is accompanied by a fully qualified interpreter then that would be acceptable. A trainee interpreter who attends PIP appointments on their own would not be appropriate at all.”

<https://bda.org.uk/pip-guide/>

**Capita CIR-Deafness (assessor-facing, not claimant-facing, but it is the rule the assessor is supposed to follow):**

> “If the customer has requested a BSL/ISL interpreter or lip speaker, the assessment should not go ahead without this. Failure to provide an interpreter or lip speaker if requested or needed is a breach of the Equality Act.”

<https://haas.capita.co.uk/dam/documents/cir-ni/CIR-Deafness.pdf>

**Adaptation, not a published script — one spoken line on the day:**

> “I asked for a registered BSL interpreter. There isn’t one here. I will not go ahead. Please book a new date with a yellow-badge interpreter and confirm it in writing. Writing notes or lip-reading is not an alternative.”

### B. They offer video relay instead of the in-person interpreter already booked

**Published claimant script: not found** from RNID, SignHealth, BDA or DR UK.

What is published is the other direction: contractors *default* a BSL telephone PIP to VRS (Capita and Ingeus pages above; DWP easy-read “may be asked to have a video assessment using VRS”). BDA’s film does not discuss refusing VRS. RNID’s 2026 AtW survey records people being “previously demanded that I use VRS” at work — evidence of the pressure, not a script.

**Disability Justice** letter generator covers “BSL interpreter or other type of communication support was not provided” by a public body, including DWP. That is a complaint / letter-before-action tool, not a same-day spoken line. Use it after the appointment, with credit.

<https://www.disabilityjustice.org.uk/take-action/accessible-information-and-communication/>

**DeafATW sample letters** (hosted on deafatw.com; mirrored by Deaf Unity) are for reconsideration and complaints about **cut awards**, not for a no-show or a VRS switch. Do not reuse them as an assessment script. They remain the right tool for the AtW complaints ladder already mapped in prompts 20–26.

<http://www.deafatw.com/how-to-complain.html>

**Adaptation, not a published script — VRS-instead-of-booked-F2F:**

> “I booked an in-person registered BSL interpreter for a planned assessment. Video relay is not what I asked for and it is not an adequate replacement today. Please rearrange with an in-person yellow-badge interpreter and confirm the new date in writing. I am not refusing the assessment. I am refusing to go ahead without the adjustment I asked for.”

Flag clearly on the guide page that this paragraph is this project’s wording, and that refusing VRS on a contractor-default “telephone + SignVideo” invite is a thinner evidence base than refusing a no-show.
