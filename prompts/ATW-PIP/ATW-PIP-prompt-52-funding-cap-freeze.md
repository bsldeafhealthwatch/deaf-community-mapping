# Prompt 52 — Access to Work funding-cap freeze: current numbers for a plain-language stat block

**Master index prompt 52.** First file in the AtW / PIP plain-language guide batch (prompts 52–54). Compiled 9 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Feed the public-facing “Know Your Rights” AtW guide stat block. Prompts 20–26 already cover national DWP claims data, interpreter provision at assessments, and complaints. This file does not repeat that ground. It only confirms the four figures a guide page can quote: the frozen cap and its real-terms cut; typical BSL day rates versus that cap; processing-time target versus NAO / PAC waits; and whether DWP has announced a cap or wait change since March 2026.

**Guide-writing rule.** Quote the official cash figure first. Put the real-terms cut second, with the CPI months used. Do not write “the cap was cut to £X” — the cash cap has not moved. Do not write “DWP pays £375 a day” — that is a union mid-market fee, not a DWP tariff.

---

## Headline

The Access to Work annual grant cap is **£69,260**. It has sat at that cash figure since **8 April 2024** and the official customer factsheet now lists the same amount for 2024/25, 2025/26 and 2026/27 (to 31 March 2027). That is a two-year freeze after nine years of annual uprating.

CPI rose **6.4%** between April 2024 and April 2026 (ONS index D7BT: 133.5 → 142.1). Holding the cap still is therefore a **real-terms cut of about 6.4% over two years**. A cap that had kept April 2024 purchasing power would be about **£73,720** in April 2026 prices.

DWP’s published processing target is still **25 working days**. The National Audit Office’s most cited average is **109 working days in November 2025**. The Public Accounts Committee (12 June 2026) recorded a slightly lower departmental figure of **106 days** and confirmed the helpline still tells callers the wait may be **up to 37 weeks**. No announced increase to the cap has been found since March 2026. The post-March change that *was* announced is staffing, not the cap: 480 extra case staff, backlog clearance aimed at September 2027.

### How the freeze became public

Disability News Service, 7 May 2026, John Pring: “Labour ministers secretly imposed Access to Work cut last year, DWP update quietly reveals.” DNS dates the quiet factsheet edit to that week: “Updated the ‘Maximum amount of grants’ table with the cap figures for 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026 and 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027.” The previous cap update on the same page was 16 May 2024 (“new Access to Work grant cap of £69,260 effective from 8 April 2024”).  
<https://www.disabilitynewsservice.com/labour-ministers-secretly-imposed-access-to-work-cut-last-year-dwp-update-quietly-reveals/>

DWP spokesperson, quoted in that piece and repeated 14 May 2026:

> “There are currently no plans to increase the upper cap limit in Access to Work but this will be kept under review. Only a very small number of Access to Work customers receive payments at the cap level.”

DNS’s own real-terms line was “roughly 3.5 per cent last year.” That matches one year of CPI (April 2024 → April 2025). It is now a low figure for a two-year freeze; use the two-year calculation below for a 2026/27 guide page.

### Real-terms cut — worked from ONS CPI, not from DNS

CPI all-items index, 2015 = 100, series D7BT (ONS, latest point July 2026):

| Month | CPI index | 12-month CPI rate | Change since April 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| April 2024 | 133.5 | 2.3% | — |
| April 2025 | 138.2 | 3.5% | **+3.5%** |
| April 2026 | 142.1 | 2.8% | **+6.4%** |
| July 2026 (latest in this pass) | 142.9 | 2.9% (July rate from Commons Library SN02792, 19 Aug 2026) | **+7.0%** |

Index source: ONS time series D7BT.  
<https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/timeseries/d7bt>  
April 2026 bulletin: <https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/consumerpriceinflation/april2026>

Worked pounds (April 2024 → April 2026, the clean two-year window that matches the freeze):

- Cash cap stays **£69,260**.
- 142.1 ÷ 133.5 = **1.0644** → CPI up **6.4%**.
- Cap needed to hold April 2024 purchasing power: 69,260 × 1.0644 ≈ **£73,720**.
- Cash shortfall versus an uprated cap: about **£4,460** a year.
- Purchasing power of the frozen £69,260 in April 2026 prices: 69,260 ÷ 1.0644 ≈ **£65,070**.

**Guide wording that stays accurate:** “The cap is still £69,260. Prices rose about 6.4% between April 2024 and April 2026, so the same money now buys less support.”

**Do not write:** “the cap was cut by £4,460.” That invents a cash cut DWP did not make.

**Flag — RNID typo.** RNID’s April 2026 AtW survey report prints the cap as “£69,620 for 2025-26.” Official figure is **£69,260**. Do not copy the RNID transposition into the guide.  
<https://rnid.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/RNID-Access-To-Work-Experiences-of-deaf-and-deafblind-people-and-people-with-hearing-loss-2026.pdf>

**London vs outside London, one line for the guide:** a registered interpreter’s mid-market full day in April 2026 is **£375 in London** and **£335–£365 in the other published English, Scottish and Welsh regions** (£275 in Northern Ireland). Half-day London £190; half-day elsewhere commonly £170–£185.

### B. How those fees have moved in the last 2–3 years

**Verified movement, not a reconstructed 2024 table.** This pass did not retrieve an archived copy of the May 2024 regional grid with every cell intact. What is published and checkable:

- NUBSLI issued updated fees on 20 May 2024 after a member survey.  
  <https://www.nubsli.com/nub-posts/nubsli-releases-updated-fees-guidance/>
- NUBSLI, 25 April 2025, “Updating NUBSLI’s fee guidance”: even after that year’s inflation adjustment, “interpreters are still charging on average **7.8% less** for a day’s work than they would be if fees had kept pace with inflation since 2015 — and interpreters who have kept their fees at the same level as the 2024 guidance are earning **10.9% less** for a day’s work.” Inflation “around 3.5%” at the time of that post.  
  <https://www.nubsli.com/nub-posts/updating-nubslis-fee-guidance/>
- The April 2026 table is a fresh full survey (March 2026), not just another inflation poll.

**Gap to log, not fill:** a cell-by-cell 2023 vs 2024 vs 2026 London/national comparison is not on the live NUBSLI page. Do not invent “London full day rose from £X in 2024 to £375.” Quote the April 2026 medians and the 2025 real-terms percentages NUBSLI itself published.

### C. What DWP advisers are told they may fund (not a market rate)

Access to Work staff guide — support-worker rate bands used by case managers. The same cash bands appear in a May 2024 extract circulating in the sector and on later copies of the guide. The GOV.UK staff-guide collection page was last updated **13 November 2025** in this pass (civil-servant eligibility edit). No published 2026 uprate of these bands was found.

| Support type | National hourly band | London hourly band |
|---|---|---|
| British Sign Language (NRCPD registered or trainee; NVQ Diploma in BSL or working towards accreditation) | £26.00 – £47.00 | £30.00 – £52.00 |
| Communication support (not a registered / trainee SLI) | £22.00 – £30.00 | £27.50 – £35.00 |
| Remote BSL VRS / VRI bundles, inclusive of VAT | 60 min/month £105; 125 min £202; 250 min £390; 525 min £780; 1,125 min £1,480 | Same bundles |

Staff guide landing page: <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-work-staff-guide>  
HTML: <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-work-staff-guide/access-to-work-staff-guide>

Customer factsheet (5 May 2026): if the interpreter works online, “Access to Work will not be able to pay for any travel time, if they are not travelling to support you.” Online BSL is payable.

**Worked comparison, labelled as arithmetic not official policy:**

- Top of the London staff-guide band × 7.5 hour day = £52 × 7.5 = **£390**. NUBSLI London RSLI full-day median = **£375**. The official band can cover a standard London day at the top of the range. It does not automatically cover travel, unsocial hours, a second interpreter for a long assignment, or an experienced specialist above the “standard” median.
- Frozen cap ÷ London RSLI full-day median: £69,260 ÷ £375 ≈ **185 full days** in a year, or about **3.5 days a week** if that were the only cost. Two interpreters for a conference-length day halves the days the cap will buy.
- Frozen cap ÷ top London hourly band: £69,260 ÷ £52 ≈ **1,332 hours** a year.

Those day-counts are illustrations for a guide, not DWP awards. Actual awards mix hours, travel, equipment and cost-share. DWP told DNS that only “a very small number” of customers sit at the cap.

## 52.4 Has DWP announced a change to the cap or to processing times since March 2026?

### Cap — no increase announced

| Date | What was said | Cap changed? |
|---|---|---|
| 12 March 2026 | PAC oral evidence on delays and the 37-week helpline message | No |
| 20 March 2026 | Timms written answer on BSL-interpreter customer counts | No |
| April 2026 | Government said it was “reviewing all aspects” of Access to Work (Commons Library SN06666) | Review language, not a new cap |
| 1 April / 5 May 2026 | Customer factsheet updated; 2026/27 row added at **£69,260** | Freeze extended, not lifted |
| 7 and 14 May 2026 | DWP spokesperson to DNS: “no plans to increase the upper cap limit… kept under review” | Explicit no |
| 19 May 2026 | DWP press notice on 480 extra staff. “Wider reforms to ensure Access to Work remains fair and sustainable are also being considered.” | Staffing, not cap |
| 26 June 2026 | Commons Library: cap “has not increased from 2024/25 to 2026/27” | Confirms freeze still in force |
| 12 June 2026 | PAC report still cites £69,260 as the maximum annual funding | No new figure |

**Finding for the guide:** as of 9 September 2026, no ministerial announcement raising the £69,260 cap has been found. The live official table runs the same cash cap through 31 March 2027.

Pathways to Work (March 2025 green paper) and the April 2026 “reviewing all aspects” line are about the future shape of the scheme, not a published new cap. Disability Rights UK said in March 2026 that evidence of widespread cuts to *packages* was “now overwhelming.” DWP’s position, recorded by the Commons Library and the NAO, is that policy has not changed and that some awards have fallen because guidance is being “more rigorously and consistently” applied. That is a package-level dispute, not a cap-table change. Keep the two issues apart on the guide page.

### Processing times — staffing plan announced; target unchanged

DWP press release, 19 May 2026, “Huge recruitment boost to tackle backlog in vital disability work scheme”:

- 480 additional case managers and caseworkers (described as a 72% increase on 658 people then working on the scheme).
- Aim: clear the inherited backlog by **September 2027**.
- Departmental claims in the same notice: “payment delays already eliminated” and “96 percent of urgent cases cleared within 28 days.”
- Context given by DWP: 48,270 applications awaiting a decision at the end of June 2024; “around 60,000 applicants are awaiting a decision” at the date of the notice.

<https://www.gov.uk/government/news/huge-recruitment-boost-to-tackle-backlog-in-vital-disability-work-scheme>

PAC (12 June 2026) records that announcement as following the 12 March evidence session, and adds the committee’s own caution: extra staff “may aid” clearance, but delays may also be IT, process and “lack of grip,” not headcount alone. PAC’s March 2026 backlog figure is about 66,000. DWP’s own clearance date remains September 2027 — eighteen months from the PAC report, not a new 25-day performance.

**Finding for the guide:** the 25-working-day *target* has not been replaced. The 37-week *helpline message* has not been withdrawn in any source found after March 2026. What changed after March 2026 is a recruitment plan and two departmental performance claims (payments; urgent cases) that have not yet been re-audited by the NAO.

## Stat block — copy-ready lines

Use these only after a human has opened the cited URL.

1. **Cap.** The most you can get from Access to Work in a year is **£69,260**. That limit has been frozen since **8 April 2024** and the official factsheet now prints the same figure for 2025/26 and 2026/27. Source: DWP customer factsheet, updated 5 May 2026.
2. **Real-terms cut.** Consumer prices rose **6.4%** between April 2024 and April 2026. The same £69,260 now buys less support. A cap that had kept pace with CPI would be about **£73,720**. Source: ONS CPI index D7BT.
3. **Interpreter cost.** In April 2026 the union mid-market full-day fee for a registered BSL interpreter was **£375 in London** and **£335–£365** in most other published UK regions. Source: NUBSLI fee guidance, survey March 2026, published April 2026. This is not a DWP tariff.

In June 2026 the Public Accounts Committee said the department’s figure had eased to **106 days**, while the helpline was still telling callers a decision could take **up to 37 weeks**. Sources: NAO HC 1644, 6 February 2026; PAC report, 12 June 2026.
5. **Since March 2026.** No announced rise in the cap. DWP said in May 2026 it had “no plans to increase the upper cap limit” and would recruit **480** extra staff, aiming to clear the backlog by **September 2027**. Sources: DNS 7 May 2026 (spokesperson quote); DWP press notice 19 May 2026.

- It does not write the claimant-facing scripts. That is prompt 53.
