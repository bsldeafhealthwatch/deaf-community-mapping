## Don’t list (print near both guides)

- Do not write “the cap was cut to £X.” Cash cap is still **£69,260**. The cut is real-terms.
- Do not write “DWP pays £375 a day.” That is NUBSLI mid-market guidance, not a tariff.
- Do not copy RNID’s printed **£69,620**. Official figure is **£69,260**.
- Do not treat the 25-working-day AtW target as a wait the person will get. Helpline still says **up to 37 weeks**.
- Do not treat “I need a BSL interpreter” on a *telephone* PIP invite as an in-person booking. Capita and Ingeus default that request to **SignVideo VRS**.
- Do not say lip-reading or written notes will do for a PIP assessment. BDA + Capita CIR-Deafness + CPIP/315/2018 say they will not.
- Do not tell people to bring family to interpret. BDA advises against it.
- Do not tell people which PIP descriptor letter they will score.
- Do not use Tomasz, Charlotte or Patrick by name until the publisher confirms.
- Do not present Rimmer (Jobcentre) or Alan Graham (NHS) as AtW/PIP quotes.
- Do not hold the page out as legal advice, welfare-rights representation, or a DWP form-filling service.
- Do not import AIS language onto DWP. Equality Act reasonable adjustment is the duty that applies.

## Locked stat block — copy only after a human opens the URL

Print this on the AtW page. Do not paraphrase the pounds.

1. **Cap.** The most you can get from Access to Work in a year is **£69,260**. Frozen since **8 April 2024**. Official factsheet lists the same figure for 2025/26 and 2026/27 (to 31 March 2027).  
   Source: DWP factsheet, updated 5 May 2026.  
   https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-work-factsheet/access-to-work-factsheet-for-customers

2. **Real-terms.** CPI rose **6.4%** April 2024 to April 2026 (ONS D7BT 133.5 → 142.1). A cap that had kept pace would be about **£73,720**.  
   https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/timeseries/d7bt

3. **Interpreter cost.** April 2026 union mid-market full day: **£375 London**, **£335–£365** most other GB regions. Not a DWP rate.  
   https://www.nubsli.com/guidance/interpreter-fees/

4. **Wait.** Target **25 working days**. NAO average **109 working days** in November 2025. PAC (12 June 2026) **106 days**, helpline **up to 37 weeks**.  
   https://www.nao.org.uk/reports/the-access-to-work-scheme/  
   https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm5902/cmselect/cmpubacc/92/report.html

5. **Since March 2026.** No cap rise. DWP May 2026: “no plans to increase the upper cap limit.” +480 staff, backlog aim **September 2027**.  
   https://www.gov.uk/government/news/huge-recruitment-boost-to-tackle-backlog-in-vital-disability-work-scheme

Context, not the block: 3,210 customers paid for a BSL Interpreter in 2024/25, average £18,200 (UIN 120203, 20 Mar 2026). Staff-guide bands still National £26–£47 / London £30–£52 an hour (page updated 13 Nov 2025; no 2026 uprate found).

## Scripts — published first, drafts second

### Use as-is (credit on the page)

**PIP request + no-show — BDA.**  
https://bda.org.uk/pip-guide/  
Claimant must ask. Refuse to proceed if no interpreter. Notes and lip-reading are not substitutes. Yellow badge only. Trainee alone is not suitable. Do not use family.

**PIP phone assessment — NDCS.**  
Request a face-to-face assessment *and/or* a BSL interpreter.  
https://www.ndcs.org.uk/advice-and-support/all-advice-and-support-topics/money-and-benefits/personal-independence-payment-pip

**PIP Q9 tick — Citizens Advice.**  
Tick yes if you need an interpreter or signer. Judged in your first language.  
https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/benefits/sick-or-disabled-people-and-carers/pip/help-with-your-claim/fill-in-form-pip/9-communicating-verbally/

**AtW workplace checklist — DWP Planner.** Link, do not clone.  
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-work-bsl-adjustments-planner

**After the event — Disability Justice** (public body did not provide BSL).  
https://www.disabilityjustice.org.uk/take-action/accessible-information-and-communication/

### Contractor default Claude must warn about

Asking for “a BSL interpreter” on a telephone PIP appointment currently triggers **VRS**:

- Capita: https://haas.capita.co.uk/en/benefit-types/pip/video-relay-service (page dated 4 Sep 2026)
- Ingeus: https://haas.ingeus.co.uk/assessments/personal-independence-payment/british-sign-language-relay-assessments/
- Maximus: contact at least **two working days** before; video or face-to-face BSL. https://haas.maximusuk.co.uk/assessment-process/before-your-assessment/
- DWP easy-read: BSL users “may be asked to have a video assessment using VRS.”

If the person wants in-person BSL they must ask for **both** a face-to-face assessment and an in-person yellow-badge interpreter.

Capita CIR-Deafness (assessor-facing, useful as the rule): if BSL/ISL or a lip speaker was requested, the assessment should not go ahead without it. Lip-reading is not an acceptable PIP communication method.  
https://haas.capita.co.uk/dam/documents/cir-ni/CIR-Deafness.pdf

### Site-drafted only — label as example wording

**AtW assessment request** (no charity published this sentence):

> I am a Deaf BSL user. British Sign Language is my first language. Please book a registered (NRCPD yellow-badge) in-person BSL/English interpreter for my Access to Work assessment on [date]. Video relay is not an adequate alternative for this appointment. Please confirm the interpreter’s name and registration in writing.

**PIP / AtW — VRS offered instead of booked in-person:**

> I booked an in-person registered BSL interpreter for a planned assessment. Video relay is not what I asked for and it is not an adequate replacement today. Please rearrange with an in-person yellow-badge interpreter and confirm the new date in writing. I am not refusing the assessment. I am refusing to go ahead without the adjustment I asked for.

**PIP Activity 7 starter** (do not claim a score):

> British Sign Language is my first language. I cannot follow or take part in a spoken English conversation without a registered BSL/English interpreter. That is true for simple one-sentence information and for longer or complicated information. Hearing aids / lip-reading / written notes do not give me access to spoken English.
