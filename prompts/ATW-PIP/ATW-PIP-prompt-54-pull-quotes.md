# Prompt 54 — Short, sourced pull-quotes for the AtW and PIP guide pages

**Master index prompt 54.** Last file in the AtW / PIP plain-language guide batch (prompts 52–54). Compiled 9 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps. Do not take quotes from forums, Facebook comments, or unverifiable blogs.

**What this prompt is for.** One- or two-sentence pull-quotes a public guide page can print. Prompts 20–33 covered process. Prompt 52 covered numbers. Prompt 53 covered scripts. This file only asks: what testimony is already on a named organisation’s page or report, who it is attributed to, and what that organisation says about reuse.

**Guide-writing rule.** Print the quote, then in the same block: named or anonymised; organisation; title; date; URL. Do not invent a name for an anonymised survey line. Do not put a named person on a public page without checking the publisher’s consent position. Fair dealing for quotation still exists under UK copyright law; it does not replace a charity’s own media-consent rule for a living named individual.

---

## Headline

Usable AtW pull-quotes sit in **RNID’s April 2026 survey report** and the matching RNID news page. One person on that news page is **named (Tomasz)**. The rest of the survey lines are anonymised by age, gender and BSL-user status.

Usable PIP / assessment pull-quotes sit in **Sense** (named Charlotte plus anonymised deafblind lines) and in an older **Citizens Advice Exeter** case study. **BDA, SignHealth and Disability Rights UK did not publish a short first-person PIP-assessment or AtW-award quote** that meets the brief in this pass. BDA’s PIP guide is advice, not testimony. SignHealth’s *Reform not cuts* quotes interpreters’ organisations, not a Deaf employee describing an award. DR UK’s nearest named DWP case is **Rimmer** (Jobcentre interpreter failures), which is the wrong benefit for these two guide pages.

Reuse is not a free-for-all. RNID’s website terms allow printing non-password pages for non-commercial teaching and private use; other copying needs prior written consent. Sense and the other charities do not publish a “you may reprint our case-study quotes” licence. Safe default for the hub: short quotation + full citation + “reproduced from [org]” + email the press desk before using a **named** living person.

## 54.1 Access to Work — impact of an award, delay or refusal on the job

### Best named quote (check consent before the guide uses the name)

**Tomasz, 40, London.** Coordinator for the Sign Language Interpreting Service at his local council. Published by RNID, 15 April 2026, on the news page that launched the survey report.

> “When I do not have access to an interpreter at work, I struggle significantly. I can’t make telephone calls without one, which means I have to delay my tasks and my workload builds up. It really affects my confidence.”

Second sentence on the same page, still Tomasz:

> “If further changes are made such as grants being cut further, I wouldn’t be able to do my job at all.”

- Attribution: **named individual**, with age, city and job.
- Organisation: RNID.
- Title: “New research highlights widespread failings of Access to Work.”
- Date: 15 April 2026.
- URL: <https://rnid.org.uk/2026/04/new-research-highlights-widespread-failings-of-access-to-work/>
- Reuse: RNID terms (below). Named case study — treat as needing RNID press confirmation before a public guide reprints the name. The quote itself is short enough for fair-dealing quotation with citation.

### Best anonymised quotes from the same research (safer for a guide page)

Source for all four: RNID and DeafATW, *Access to Work: Experiences of deaf and deafblind people and people with hearing loss*, April 2026. Survey December 2025–January 2026, n=358 (267 BSL users).  
PDF: <https://rnid.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/RNID-Access-To-Work-Experiences-of-deaf-and-deafblind-people-and-people-with-hearing-loss-2026.pdf>

**Award working — clinician, anonymised.**

> “I could not continue to practise as a clinician safely without the communication support provided via Access to Work.”

- Attribution: anonymised. The PDF does not attach an age/gender line to this sentence in the extract used here; print it as “RNID / DeafATW survey respondent, 2026.”

**Award working — dignity, anonymised.**

> “For me, it’s not just about communication — it’s about dignity, connection, and being given the same chance as everyone else.”

- Attribution: Female BSL user, aged 45–54, self-employed.

**Award cut — hours and errors, anonymised.**

> “They reduced my budget by 50% and then spread it out over two years. … I have reduced my training and development, I have made errors in my English writing/subtitle-making, I avoid meetings unless necessary, I email more which leads to confusion and miscommunication.”

- Attribution: Female BSL user, aged 25–34, employed full-time.

**Award squeezed — employer sees underperformance, anonymised.**

> “They seem to focus on what I can do without an interpreter and trying to reduce the hours of needing a BSL interpreter. I have to argue my case when I shouldn’t be arguing for what I need to enable to do my job effectively. They are basically undermining our job to a point where the employers might see us as underperforming.”

- Attribution: Male BSL user, aged 35–44, employed part-time. Also submitted by RNID as written evidence AWS0095 to the PAC AtW inquiry.

**Decisions follow the interpreter, not the skill, anonymised.**

> “Decisions about hours, roles, and professional opportunities are influenced by whether adequate communication support is available, rather than by my skills or willingness to take on new challenges.”

- Attribution: Male BSL user, aged 45–54, employed part-time.

### One detailed anonymised case (PAC evidence — use as a short box, not a pull-quote)

**DeafATW written evidence AWS0121**, 24 March 2026. Case study “AB”: Deaf BSL user, owner-director of a deaf-led business, AtW for 30+ years. Renewal submitted March 2025; decision after seven months; award expired in between; annual award cut from £69,260 to £39,107; weekly hours capped at 20 with no banking; hourly rate capped at £37.50 against a stated market rate of about £50.

AB is **anonymised initials**, not a public name. Do not invent a first name. The document is parliamentary written evidence, not a charity web case study.

<https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/163145/html/>

### Sense — named, but the job support is vision equipment not BSL hours

**Patrick**, Sense blog, 4 September 2023, “My fight for support to see at work.”

> “Today, it wouldn’t be possible for me to do my job if I didn’t have access to a digital magnifier.”

And: after “bad advice from my Access to Work team, I was left struggling to see for 18 months. … It meant that for those 18 months I could only do part of my job.”

- Attribution: **named** (first name). Sense published.
- URL: <https://www.sense.org.uk/blog/my-fight-for-support-to-see-at-work/>
- Use on a DeafBlind AtW line, not as the BSL-interpreter pull-quote. Different impairment, same scheme.

URL: <https://signhealth.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Reform-not-cuts-Access-To-Work-2025.pdf>

## 54.2 PIP — impact of an award, a refusal, or a communication failure at assessment

### Sense — strongest listed-org PIP / DWP-process quotes

**Sense, “Putting disabled people at the heart of the benefits system,”** 8 November 2024.  
<https://www.sense.org.uk/about-us/policy-public-affairs/putting-disabled-people-at-the-heart-of-the-benefits-system/>

**Named — application format, not the assessment room.**

Charlotte: the application process is “frustrating” and “tiring,” with DWP sending paper forms that her mother had to read out, “despite them knowing she was deafblind.”

- Attribution: **named** (first name). Sense published. Confirm consent with Sense before a public guide uses “Charlotte.”

**Anonymised — documents in the wrong format.**

> “Why should I answer the Department for Work and Pensions’ questions when they don’t even send me the documents in the right format?”

- Attribution: “one deafblind person we spoke to.”

**Anonymised — assessment felt like a trap.**

> “They asked the same question, but in a few different ways, as if they wanted to catch me out.”

- Attribution: “one deafblind person we support.”

**Aggregated figure to sit next to those lines, not as a quote:** 45% of disabled people with complex needs who had been assessed “did not get the right communication support.” That is Sense’s survey finding, not a sentence a person said.

### Citizens Advice — assessment communication failure (older, still published)

**Citizens Advice Exeter, *Appealing for Common Sense*, February 2018.** Case study 2: 23-year-old woman, profoundly deaf, little lip-reading, no spoken English, epilepsy, anxiety.

The assessor “stated that the applicant’s deafness was not an issue as she could use her phone for communication.” Tribunal allowed the appeal and preferred her account.

- Attribution: **anonymised** (“the applicant”). Not named.
- Organisation: Citizens Advice Exeter.
- Date: February 2018.
- URL: <https://asauk.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Appealing-for-Common-Sense-Citizens-Advice-Exeter-Report-February-2018.pdf>
- Flag: eight years old. Still a published CA case study that matches “communication failure at assessment.” Do not present it as 2026 fieldwork.

## 54.4 Reuse and citation — what each publisher actually says

Short quotation for criticism, review or news reporting can still fall under fair dealing in the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988. That is not a blank licence to drop a named case study onto a campaign site. Check the publisher before the page goes live.
