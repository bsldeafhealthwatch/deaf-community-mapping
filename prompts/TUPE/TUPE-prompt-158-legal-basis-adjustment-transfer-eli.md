# Prompt 158 — Does an agreed reasonable adjustment automatically transfer under TUPE, and must it appear in employee liability information?

## Headline

**Two duties run in parallel. They are not the same sentence.**

- **TUPE regulation 4** transfers the *contract* and “all the transferor’s rights, powers, duties and liabilities under or in connection with” that contract. The live GB text does not name disability, reasonable adjustments, workplace-adjustment passports or Access to Work. No Employment Tribunal or EAT decision found on this pass holds that an *informal* or *passport-only* adjustment is itself a regulation-4 contractual right. That silence is the gate finding.
- **Equality Act 2010 s.20 / s.39** is a statutory duty on whoever is the employer. From the completion of a relevant transfer the transferee *is* the employer of the transferring employee (regulation 4(1): the contract “shall have effect after the transfer as if originally made between the person so employed and the transferee”). ACAS’s live after-transfer page therefore says, in terms: “The new employer must make sure they have put in place any reasonable adjustments for disabled employees.” That is an Equality Act instruction sitting next to TUPE guidance. It is not a finding that the particular pre-transfer arrangement transferred as a term.
- **Employee liability information (regulation 11)** is a **closed list**: identity and age; ERA 1996 s.1 written particulars; two-year disciplinary and grievance records; actual or reasonably-anticipated court/tribunal claims; collective agreements that will have effect after the transfer. Disability status, agreed adjustments, passports and Access to Work awards are **not named**. They enter the statutory pack only if they already sit inside one of those five heads — most plausibly a written “other benefit” on the s.1 statement (a 2020 addition, unpublished as applied to BSL interpreter hours), a grievance or claim about the adjustment in the last two years, or a collective agreement that records it. Incomplete ELI is a **transferee-versus-transferor** tribunal claim under regulation 12 (starting point £500 per employee, just-and-equitable, three months from the transfer). It is not, by itself, a finding that the outgoing employer breached the Equality Act against the employee.

**No published grace period.** Neither TUPE nor the Equality Act gives the incoming employer a number of days to “get up to speed.” The practical gap is the Equality Act knowledge defence in Schedule 8 paragraph 20: the s.20 duty does not apply if the employer does not know, and could not reasonably be expected to know, that the person is disabled and likely to be placed at the relevant substantial disadvantage. A silent ELI pack can leave the incoming employer without *actual* knowledge. Whether that is also *constructive* knowledge — because a transferring disabled employee, a union, or the transferor’s HR file existed — is fact-specific and unpublished on these facts. Do not print “the new employer has 28 days” or “the new employer has no duty until told.”

**Closest named case is not the informal-adjustment question.** *NHS Direct NHS Trust v Gunn* UKEAT/0128/14/BA, Langstaff P, judgment 14 May 2015: a transferring employee is **not** an Equality Act “applicant” for the job they already hold; regulation 4 moves the existing contract automatically; the incoming employer then owes the Equality Act as employer. On the facts Ms Gunn had objected and received a *fresh* offer of a different site and hours, so s.39(1) (applicant) still applied to that offer. The case does not decide whether a passport or a standing interpreter booking is a contractual term. No Deaf / BSL / DeafBlind TUPE-adjustment judgment found on this pass.

**Civil Service staff-transfer language is not TUPE ELI.** GOV.UK staff-transfer guidance (employee page last updated 26 September 2022; exporting-manager page updated 18 March 2026) lists “any reasonable adjustments” among the things that “normally” move with a civil servant changing department, and tells the exporting manager to discuss hard-kit transfer and factor order-time into the start date. COSOP (Cabinet Office Statement of Practice on Staff Transfers in the Public Sector, page last updated 22 February 2024) is public-sector policy sitting *alongside* TUPE for outsourcing from the public sector. Neither instrument rewrites regulation 11. Do not paste a CS importing-manager sentence into a private-sector ELI cell.

**Northern Ireland is a separate row.** GB TUPE 2006 applies in NI for business transfers; service-provision change in NI is the Service Provision Change (Protection of Employment) Regulations (Northern Ireland) 2006 (NISR 2006/177). ELI in NI is still a closed list; the notification period remains **14 days**, not the GB 28-day period introduced by SI 2014/16. Do not paste 28 days into an NI cell.

## 158.1 Three machines, not one

The originating brief asked whether a reasonable adjustment “counts as a term, a benefit, or neither.” On the live texts the honest split is:

1. **If it is written into the contract or the s.1 statement** (including, from April 2020, as an “other benefit”), regulation 4 carries it as part of the contract pack, and regulation 11 carries the written particular into ELI. No source found that treats a BSL standing booking this way as a matter of course.
2. **If it is only on a passport or in an OH email**, regulation 4 does not, on any published holding found here, convert that document into a contractual right. The incoming employer still faces s.20 from day one *if* it has actual or constructive knowledge.
3. **Access to Work** is a DWP grant to the employee in respect of a named job and employer. It is not a contractual term of the old employment and it is not an ELI head. Whether the award survives the change of legal employer is prompt 160, not this gate.

ACAS is the only official voice that puts the two machines on the same page, and it keeps them in separate sentences: assume terms transfer; separately, put reasonable adjustments in place.

## 158.2 What *Gunn* does and does not do

*Gunn* is the named join between TUPE and the Equality Act. Langstaff P’s guidance sentence is that a transferring employee is not an applicant for the job they already have, because regulation 4 does not work by offer and acceptance. That is why an incoming employer cannot say “we have not yet offered you this job, so s.20 does not bite.” Once the transfer completes, they *are* the employer.

The case is easy to over-read. Ms Gunn had already objected, stayed with the outgoing employer, and was litigating a *pre-transfer offer of different work*. The EAT let the applicant claim proceed on that fresh offer. It did not decide whether her 8.5-hour pattern was a contractual term that would have bound NHS Direct had she transferred. It is not authority that a passport transfers, and it is not a Deaf/BSL authority.

## 158.3 ELI silence is a between-employers problem first

Regulation 12 exists so the incoming employer can recover from the outgoing employer when the statutory pack is late, wrong or missing. The £500 floor is per employee, just and equitable, mitigable. *ABM v Proway* shows the machinery working on *pay* particulars — the incoming contractor said it had not been told how “price work” was calculated. No equivalent published judgment was found in which the missing particular was a BSL booking, a captioning contract, a DeafBlind guide-communicator arrangement or a passport.

Data-protection overlay (ICO historic TUPE good-practice note, 21 May 2008, DPA 1998-era; later Bird & Bird / ICO correspondence restating GDPR compatibility): the transferor *must* disclose the statutory ELI list because the disclosure is required by law. That note does not enlarge the list to include health or adjustment data. Extra due-diligence beyond ELI is a commercial negotiation, typically covered by warranties and indemnities, not by regulation 11.

## 158.4 What is unpublished or contested

- Whether a passport, an OH recommendation, or a standing interpreter SLA is “under or in connection with” the contract for regulation 4(2)(a).
- Whether “any other benefits” in ERA s.1 now pulls a written adjustment into ELI.
- Whether an incoming employer who received a silent ELI pack but could have asked the employee or the union has constructive knowledge for Sch.8 para.20.
- Any quantified series of how often adjustment information is omitted from real ELI packs.
- Any named Deaf/BSL/DeafBlind transfer in which the incoming employer’s first meeting ran without the old booking.

Those absences are the input to 159 (was the consultation itself accessible), 160 (does AtW restart), and 161 (does any due-diligence template list the passport). They are not a licence to print a named employer’s pack as non-compliant.
