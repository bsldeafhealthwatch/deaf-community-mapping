# Prompt 160 — Does an Access to Work award survive a TUPE change of employer, or does the transfer open a gap?

**Master index prompt 160.** Load-bearing file in the TUPE batch (prompts 158–163). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Test the administrative join the originating brief flagged: an Access to Work award is built around a named employer and a named job; TUPE is built around business continuity. Ask whether the live staff guide and the employer factsheet actually close that join for interpreter hours, equipment and a support worker, and whether any documented case shows a booking or kit lapsing because of a transfer. Do not reprint the cap, the 3,210 BSL-interpreter customer count, or the staff-guide hourly bands as if they answered portability — those figures already live in prompts 20, 52–54 and 116. Do not print that DWP “must” keep the old award running without a named source that says so. Do not treat NAO/PAC processing times as TUPE-gap evidence unless the source ties the delay to a change of employer.

## Headline

**The staff guide and the employer factsheet do not say the same thing.** That mismatch is the finding.

The **employer factsheet** (updated 7 October 2025) is the public sentence a new HR team will actually read: “If an individual changes employer, they may be able to transfer equipment to their new employer, but they cannot automatically transfer awards for support workers or travel – they would need to contact the Access to Work team to discuss their new arrangements.” A BSL interpreter is a support worker on AtW’s own typology. On this page, interpreter hours do not travel automatically.

The **staff guide** is the instruction to the case manager, and it *does* name TUPE. **Live check 14 September 2026:** HTML fetched from the published URL; `TUPE` appears once; heading “New employer (new owner of business), same job” is still in the Change of circumstances chapter as paragraphs 39–44. Visible page date “Updated 18 June 2026”; JSON-LD `dateModified` 18 June 2026 10:50; `govuk:updated-at` meta 4 September 2026 10:37. The block was **not** removed. The TUPE hyperlink on the page still points at a dead Directgov URL. Do not re-cite the November 2025 landing date as current. Verbatim: “Under the TUPE regulations, the new owner of a business is obliged to take on the contractual obligations of their predecessor. This includes the cost sharing agreements with AtW.” If the customer contacts because a new employer has taken over the business, the case manager must update the employment details, contact the new employer to confirm they will carry forward the existing support arrangements, and make the new employer aware of ownership and maintenance obligations for any equipment. If the new employer wants to swap an external support worker for internal staff, the business case is reviewed; the change can be approved if it means a reduction or no change in AtW support; AtW will not pay for support that appears to have been withdrawn to secure funding from AtW.

**Cost-share “job movers” block, same guide:** a new employer who takes over the business **and the job remains the same** is a **repeat application**. A new employer who takes over the business **and changes the customer’s fundamental duties** is a **new application** with a new AtW start date. A move to a *different* business doing the same job is neither automatic nor a clean continuation: the case manager must identify transferable elements and negotiate with both employers.

**So the award is administratively tied to the named employer.** Even on the friendliest reading (TUPE, same job, customer phones AtW, new employer agrees to carry forward), someone has to make the call, the record has to be updated, and the new employer has to verify claims. The employee-facing page does not tell them that a same-job takeover is a “repeat application.” It tells them support-worker awards cannot transfer automatically. A transferring Deaf employee who follows the factsheet, or a new HR team that follows only the factsheet, is pointed at a fresh conversation with AtW — against a scheme whose processing times are already measured in months (52–54), not days.

**No documented case found** in which a standing interpreter booking, a captioning arrangement or an assistive-technology set-up lapsed, was delayed, or had to be renegotiated *specifically because of a TUPE transfer or a re-tendered contract*. BDF’s public line is the leaving-job sentence: “If Access to Work has funded a support worker, such as a sighted guide or sign language interpreter, the support will stop when the employee leaves their employer.” That is resignation / new-job, not TUPE same-job. RNID’s 2026 survey splits renewal / new job / change-of-circumstance / review; it does not isolate TUPE. The gap the originating brief expected is therefore **documented in the two official texts talking past each other**, not in a named fulfilment failure.

**Do not print “AtW continues automatically across TUPE.”** The staff guide’s carry-forward instruction is a case-manager action after the customer makes contact, not an automatic system event. Do not print “AtW always requires a brand-new application after TUPE.” Same-job takeover is written as a repeat application, not a new start date, *if* duties are unchanged.

## 160.1 Two official sentences (live check 14 September 2026)

Both halves of this pair were still on GOV.UK when the HTML was fetched on 14 September 2026. The staff-guide TUPE block was **not** withdrawn in the 18 June 2026 visible update or the 4 September 2026 meta stamp. What moved is the *landing date we had been citing* (13 November 2025 is stale). Re-fetch before any public sentence.

Do not over-read the pair as two DWP lawyers contradicting each other on the same object. They address different audiences and the staff guide itself splits two boxes: takeover-of-the-business / same job (TUPE heading, paras 39–44) versus new-employer-different-business / same job (paras 45–49, “some elements will not be transferable”). The public factsheet never mentions TUPE and treats every “changes employer” as: kit maybe; support-worker and travel awards not automatic; phone the team.

The load-bearing tension is not “DWP has no rule.” It is that the rule a new employer will read and the rule a case manager will read are different widths.

- Factsheet, written for employers: support-worker awards cannot transfer automatically; phone AtW.
- Staff guide, written for case managers: on a TUPE same-job takeover, update the record, call the new employer, carry the support and the cost-share agreement forward, treat it as a repeat application unless duties have fundamentally changed.

Neither text is an automatic system event. Both require the customer or the new employer to make contact. The staff-guide carry-forward is conditional on that contact *and* on the new employer confirming they will take the arrangement on. If the new employer’s HR system, invoice route or preferred interpreter agency is different, the “carry forward” is a negotiation, not a flick of a switch.

The staff-guide sentence that TUPE “obliges” the new owner to take on the predecessor’s cost-share agreement with AtW is **DWP’s operational reading**. Prompt 158 already holds that AtW is not an ELI head and that an informal adjustment is unpublished as a regulation-4 term. Do not upgrade the staff-guide sentence into a tribunal holding that the cost-share contract novates.

## 160.2 Interpreter hours are the support-worker product

On AtW’s own typology a BSL interpreter is a support worker. The factsheet’s “cannot automatically transfer awards for support workers” line therefore lands on interpreter hours. Kit has a separate, more portable track (discuss, take it if appropriate, AtW will pay reasonable transport, buy new if the old employer will not release it). Captioning purchased as a service sits closer to the support-worker track than to SAE. None of those tracks is written as “the booking diary at Sign Solutions / Convo / the in-house list survives the transfer date.”

A standing interpreter booking is usually a commercial contract between the *old employer* (or the employee) and an agency. TUPE does not novate that agency contract. Even if AtW funding is carried forward on the staff-guide model, the booking still has to be re-let onto the new employer’s purchase order. That re-letting step is unpublished as a measured delay. It is also the practical place a first post-transfer meeting can run without an interpreter.

## 160.3 What is unpublished or contested

- Any named employee whose interpreter hours stopped, thinned or waited on AtW because of a TUPE date.
- How often case managers actually run the “contact the new employer and carry forward” script, versus closing the old award and inviting a new application.
- Whether a service-provision change (the typical outsourced-department / lost-retender fact pattern in the originating brief) is treated by case managers as “new owner of a business, same job” or as “new employer, different business.” The staff guide uses “taken over the business.” SPC is the more common private-sector pattern. Unpublished which box it goes in.
- How long the carry-forward conversation takes against the 52–54 processing-time backdrop.
- Whether the new employer’s cost-share band (size threshold) is recalculated when the legal employer changes, even if the job does not.

Those absences are the input to 163. They are not a licence to print a national replacement of interpreter hours by a gap.
