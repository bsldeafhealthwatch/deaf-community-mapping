# Prompt 161 — Does a workplace adjustment passport go into employee liability information, or does anyone even list it on a TUPE due-diligence checklist?

**Master index prompt 161.** Fourth file in the TUPE batch (prompts 158–163). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Ask whether any published TUPE due-diligence template, HR practitioner guide or named employer process lists “existing workplace adjustments / adjustment passport” as an item to disclose and carry over when the *legal employer* changes. Do not collapse prompt 102 into this cell: 102 asked whether a passport travels across manager, role or contract-type change *inside one employer or a Civil Service move*. This file asks whether the document is treated as ELI or extra due diligence on a TUPE transfer. Keep COSOP / CS importing-manager guidance in its own row. Do not print that omitting a passport from ELI is unlawful without a named statute, regulation or judgment. Do not print a passport as a legal guarantee that the incoming employer must implement the old list without a fresh reasonableness assessment. Do not reprint Lisa’s 2016 Civil Service blog as a TUPE example.

**Index check.** Wrapper: `prompts/TUPE/TUPE-prompts-158-163.md`. Gate 158: ELI is a closed list; passports are not a named head; s.20 still bites from day one. 160: AtW is a separate administrative machine. 102 already holds the passport is a voluntary, worker-owned record whose published purpose is to stop re-explaining to a new manager *inside* the same employer.

## Headline

**A passport is not employee liability information.** Regulation 11’s closed list (158) still does not name disability, adjustments or passports. No official ACAS, GOV.UK, CIPD-public, NHS Employers or Civil Service TUPE checklist found on this pass adds “adjustment passport” to that statutory list. Omitting a passport from ELI is therefore not, on any named holding, a regulation 11 failure. Do not print it as unlawful.

**Extra due diligence is a different column, and one published handbook puts adjustments there.** Unite’s *Handling TUPE Transfers* (v3, learnwithunite.org) prints a two-column table: statutory ELI on the left, “additional due diligence information which may be sought/provided” on the right. The extra column includes “Details of any reasonable adjustments that have been accommodated.” That is the closest published template. It is a union training handbook, not a regulator product, and it lists the *fact of adjustments*, not the passport document. ACAS’s own TUPE transfer-plan checklists (1 July 2024) do not mention adjustments, disability or passports on the public landing page. CIPD HR-inform’s “Carrying out due diligence in a Tupe transfer” checklist exists behind a member wall; its public blurb names “complete employee records and monetary benefits,” not passports. Contents unpublished on this pass.

**Every live passport product still describes an intra-employer move.** ACAS (reasonable-adjustments record page): show the new *manager* what has been agreed. TUC/GMB model (2019, still circulated): move roles or line-manager change; confidential to the worker, manager and HR; not to be shared without written consent. BDF Tailored Adjustments Plan: “new role within the organisation or if their manager changes.” NHS Employers Health Passport (still pointed to on NHS Employers pages in February 2026 and the November 2025 adjustments booklet): “new line managers” and movement “through roles within the NHS” / “between NHS organisations.” Civil Service staff-transfer guidance (158) names “any reasonable adjustments” as something that normally moves *between departments* on the CSET form — COSOP / machinery-of-government excepted. None of those pages says “attach the passport to the ELI pack when the contractor loses the retender.”

**The confidentiality design cuts against automatic disclosure.** TUC model: the passport “should not be shared with anyone without the written consent of the worker.” A transferor who dropped every passport into the buyer’s data room without consent would be fighting the product’s own rule, and the data-protection overlay already logged against ELI in 158. The worker can *choose* to show the incoming manager the document after the transfer date. That is the 102 mechanism pointed at a new legal person. It is not a due-diligence default.

**No named case, FOI or practitioner commentary found** that a passport was treated as ELI, or that omitting one from extra DD was litigated. Harper James’s public TUPE checklist mentions reasonable adjustments only as a *location-change* question for transferring staff, not as a document to collect. ACAS after-transfer still says put adjustments in place, and does not mention the passport as the vehicle.

## 161.1 Two columns, only one of them is law

The Unite handbook is useful because it draws the line the originating brief needs. Statutory ELI is the left-hand column and it is short. Everything else a buyer might want — including “details of any reasonable adjustments that have been accommodated” — is the right-hand column, optional, negotiated, usually wrapped in warranties and indemnities. A passport is not even named on that right-hand list. The *fact* of adjustments is.

That is why 158’s “other benefits” construction and 160’s AtW carry-forward sit in different files. A written interpreter arrangement might, in theory, be an s.1 particular. A passport is a worker-owned notebook. Putting the notebook in the data room without consent fights the TUC product rule and does not complete the regulation 11 list.

## 161.2 Intra-employer products pointed at the wrong event

ACAS, BDF, TUC/GMB and NHS Employers have all spent years telling disabled staff that the point of a passport is not to re-explain the same list to the next manager. That is a real, documented friction (102: GMB “fight to keep existing adjustments when they get a new manager”; BDF 2023, 62% of disabled employees have no passport). It is not a TUPE due-diligence rule. The incoming legal employer is not “the next manager.” They are a new data controller, a new AtW named employer (160), and a new s.20 duty-holder from day one (158). The passport can help the employee start that conversation. Nothing found *requires* the outgoing employer to put it in the pack.

## 161.3 What is unpublished or contested

- The contents of CIPD HR-inform’s paid TUPE DD checklist and of the ACAS Word checklists behind the 1 July 2024 download buttons.
- How often extra-DD questionnaires in real transactions ask for adjustments or passports.
- Whether any NHS outsourcing pack (community services, facilities, 111) treats the Health Passport as transferable paper when staff leave the NHS for a contractor.
- Any named incoming employer that asked for passports and was refused on confidentiality grounds.
- Whether a transferor’s disclosure of a passport without consent has ever been an ICO or ET issue.

Those absences feed 163. They do not license a public sentence that “employers routinely omit passports from ELI unlawfully.”
