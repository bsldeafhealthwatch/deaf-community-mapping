# Prompt 163 — Lived experience and the case for change: Deaf, BSL-using or DeafBlind employees and a TUPE transfer

**Master index prompt 163.** Last file in the TUPE batch (prompts 158–163). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Collect documented first-person testimony of a TUPE transfer — the consultation, whether an existing adjustment survived, whether the new employer knew in advance, how any gap was resolved — and any campaign or union submission that names *disability-accommodation continuity in a transfer*, as distinct from generic “TUPE protects terms and conditions” copy or generic reasonable-adjustment copy that never mentions a transfer. Flag thin, unpublished, contested or single-source claims. Do not generalise a union outsourcing campaign that never names disability or BSL into a Deaf TUPE finding.

**Index check.** Wrapper: `prompts/TUPE/TUPE-prompts-158-163.md`. Legal and administrative cells are already filled: informal adjustments unpublished as a regulation-4 term (158); ACAS consultation join names documents not BSL (159); AtW factsheet and staff guide talk past each other (160); passport is not ELI (161); no asked jurisdiction publishes the join (162). Post-transfer grievance stays in 109–114.

## Headline

**UK in-post first-person of this object is not found.** No named Deaf BSL-using or DeafBlind employee has published an account of being consulted about a TUPE transfer, of an interpreter booking or kit surviving or failing on the transfer date, or of the incoming employer knowing — or not knowing — about the adjustment in advance. Limping Chicken, Disability News Service, RNID 2026, SignHealth, BDA, Sense and Deafblind UK were searched on this pass. The absence is the finding, not a failed search.

**No union or charity campaign found that names the join.** UNISON, GMB, Unite, PCS and Prospect all run TUPE and outsourcing work, and all run reasonable-adjustments / passport work. The two streams do not meet in a published submission that says “when the contractor changes, the interpreter arrangement / AtW award / passport must travel.” Unite’s *Handling TUPE Transfers* handbook (161) puts “details of any reasonable adjustments that have been accommodated” in an extra due-diligence column; that is the closest published ask, and it is a training table, not a campaign. TUC/GMB passport copy is still “new manager / move roles.” UNISON Disabled Members 2025 BSL motion is welfare-reform consultations (159). BDF redundancy-consultation guidance books a sign-language interpreter for a *redundancy* meeting (159) — adjacent, not TUPE.

**Scope’s leaving-job pages are the nearest public advice and they describe a different event.** “If your employer provides a support worker” — including a BSL interpreter — “in most cases this support will end with your employment. If you need support at your new workplace, you will have to apply for Access to Work again.” Equipment may move if the old employer agrees; otherwise a new AtW application. That is resignation / new-job language. It is consistent with the employer factsheet in 160. It is not a TUPE same-job account.

**One published judgment records that a hearing-impaired employee had previously transferred under TUPE, and then later brought a reasonable-adjustments claim against the incoming employer.** *Stinton v Cambria Automobiles (South East) Ltd* 3200016/2018: employed from 2004, TUPEd to the respondent in 2013, RA claim years later held out of time. The judgment is not testimony about the 2013 consultation or about whether an adjustment crossed the transfer date. Do not stretch it into a lived-experience cell.

**The case for change that *can* be written from this batch is administrative, not testimonial.** Three official texts already on disk do not line up: the employer factsheet says support-worker awards cannot transfer automatically (160); the staff guide tells the case manager to carry a same-job TUPE takeover forward as a repeat application (160); ACAS tells the new employer to put adjustments in place from day one and does not mention AtW or the passport as the vehicle (158, 161). A transferring Deaf employee who follows the factsheet, and a new HR team that follows only the factsheet, are pointed at a fresh AtW conversation against a scheme whose waits are already measured in months. That mismatch does not need a first-person story in order to be true. It also does not become a national “interpreter hours vanish on TUPE day” finding. No source counted that.

## 163.1 Why the testimony cell is empty

TUPE is common. Deaf BSL-using employees exist. The join is rare as a published story because the people who write first-person Deaf workplace pieces (Limping Chicken, RNID survey open-text, union disabled-members conferences) organise around AtW waits, passport fights with a new manager, and inaccessible public consultations. Those are documented in 52–54, 102 and 159. A change of the *legal employer* while the job stays the same is a lawyer’s event. It does not currently have a charity or union collecting box.

That is not a reason to invent a composite “Deaf worker whose interpreter vanished on TUPE day.” It is a reason to keep the administrative mismatch in 160 as the load-bearing finding of the batch, and to treat 163 as a confirmed absence.

## 163.2 What a later researcher should not recycle

- Generic UNISON/GMB “protect TUPE rights on outsourcing” campaign copy.
- Generic “employers must make reasonable adjustments” campaign copy.
- Scope / BDF leaving-job or redundancy-consultation pages, unless clearly labelled as adjacent.
- *Stinton* as if it decided adjustment continuity.
- Any banned name from the list at the top of this file.
- International successor-employer material from 162.

## 163.3 What remains unpublished

- Any FOI or subject-access trail in which a transferring employee asked for the passport or AtW award to be listed on the ELI pack.
- Any NUBSLI / ASLI note on bookings that had to be re-let because the purchase-order name changed.
- Any Deafblind UK or Sense in-work (not into-work) account of a communicator-guide or RIDB arrangement across a transfer.
- Whether extra-DD questionnaires in real NHS or local-government outsourcings ask the Unite question.

Those are collection tasks, not findings.
