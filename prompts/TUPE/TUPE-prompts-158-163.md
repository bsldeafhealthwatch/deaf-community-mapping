Anchor: a Deaf BSL-using employee has an agreed reasonable adjustment — a standing interpreter booking for team meetings, or a captioning/notetaking arrangement for a helpdesk role — with their current employer. Their employer outsources the department (or the incumbent contractor loses a re-tendered contract to a new provider) and the employee is told they are "TUPE'd across" to a new employer with effect from a stated date. Nobody has confirmed in writing whether the interpreter booking, the assistive technology, or the workstation set-up transfers with them, whether the new employer even knows the adjustment exists, or what happens if the new employer's HR system, IT helpdesk, or Access to Work in-work support arrangement is different from the old one. The employee finds out whether the adjustment survived only when they turn up to the first post-transfer meeting.

Proposed domain: stub under the existing Welfare & Employment cluster, alongside 92–157 — a transition-event layer that intersects outsourced services, OH, passports, AtW and grievance rather than a new numbered Domain.

## Notes for this batch

- **Gate prompt: 158.** Whether a reasonable adjustment counts as something that automatically transfers under TUPE (as opposed to a discretionary practice, a non-contractual benefit, or a statutory duty that simply bites the new employer from day one) governs how 159–161 are read. If the legal commentary is silent or contested on informal/passport-based adjustments as distinct from a written contractual term, that silence is itself the headline finding, not a failed search. "The Equality Act duty exists against whoever is now the employer" and "TUPE regulation 4 transferred this particular arrangement as a contractual right" are different claims and must not be blurred.
- **Load-bearing prompt: 160.** Echoes the funding-gap pattern already logged in the outsourced-services review (124): an Access to Work award and an adjustment passport are both built around the assumption of a stable employer, and TUPE is a mechanism built around business continuity — neither was designed with the other in mind. If no documented case or DWP guidance closes this gap, log the gap itself.
- **Still-alive scepticism** does not apply cleanly here, because this batch is a legal mechanism and consultation process rather than an organisation catalogue. Any named workplace-adjustment-passport scheme (NHS Employers Health Passport, Civil Service Workplace Adjustment Passport, Royal Mail Care and Responsibility Passport, TUC product) must be checked as still current before being cited. COSOP and CS staff-transfer pages are not TUPE unless the source applies them to a TUPE/COSOP transfer.
- **Hard-don't.** Do not print that a specific employer's TUPE due-diligence process, ELI disclosure, or consultation format was unlawful or non-compliant without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/EAT judgment. "TUPE requires X" and "in practice this is commonly omitted" are different claims and must not be blurred. Do not print a missed SLA, a lapsed interpreter booking, or a silent ELI pack as automatically an Equality Act breach. Do not print Northern Ireland regulations as the GB rule. Do not treat a Civil Service inter-departmental move as a TUPE finding.
- **Held-back leg.** This batch does not include a dedicated post-transfer grievance/tribunal-remedy prompt — that ground is already covered generically by prompts 109–114 (Grievance & Escalation) and should be read alongside this batch rather than duplicated here. Prompt 81-style lived-experience hold-back does not apply; 163 is in the batch.
- **Banned names** unless a source actually describes a TUPE transfer of that person: Bussey, Clinton, Graham, Rimmer, Clarke, Borys, Murphy, Bentley, Davies, Fletcher, Iqbal, Khan, Lisa (2016 CS passport blog).
- **Dossier rule.** Stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; no DEI or anti-DEI banner.
