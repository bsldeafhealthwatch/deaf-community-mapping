# Prompt 162 — International comparative practice: does any asked jurisdiction join transfer-of-undertakings rules to continuity of a disability accommodation?

## Headline

**No asked jurisdiction publishes the join as a product.** Transfer-of-undertakings rules move the contract (or, outside the EU, sometimes the industrial instrument and some service). Disability-accommodation duties sit on whoever is the employer *now*. Regulators have not issued a third text that says “put the existing accommodation list in the transfer due-diligence pack” or “the predecessor’s interpreter arrangement survives the closing date without a fresh assessment.”

**European Union.** Directive 2001/23/EC (Acquired Rights Directive) transfers “the transferor’s rights and obligations arising from a contract of employment or from an employment relationship.” Disability accommodation is not named. Directive 2000/78/EC Article 5 is a separate reasonable-accommodation duty on the employer of the day. Member-state transpositions follow that split. Ireland S.I. 131/2003 copies the ARD structure and has no UK-style service-provision-change limb. France Code du travail L.1224-1 transfers contracts by operation of law. Germany BGB § 613a steps the new owner into all rights and duties from the employment relationship, including (on German labour commentary) binding works customs — an established workplace practice *might* be argued as a *betriebliche Übung*; that construction is unpublished as a disability-accommodation holding and is not a due-diligence template. No member-state labour inspectorate or equality body found on this pass that publishes “accommodation continuity across Betriebsübergang / transfert d’entreprise” as its own page.

**United States.** There is no TUPE. Asset buyers do not automatically inherit the workforce. Federal common-law successor liability (*EEOC v. MacMillan Bloedel Containers*, 6th Cir. 1974, and progeny) can make a buyer who continues the same business liable for the *predecessor’s discrimination claims* if there is substantial continuity, notice of the claim, and the predecessor cannot provide relief. That is a claims-inheritance doctrine, not an accommodation-file doctrine. The ADA reasonable-accommodation duty attaches to the employer of a current employee; an asset buyer who does not hire the person is not that employer. EEOC guidance lists interpreters and reassignment as accommodations. It does not tell buyers to carry the predecessor’s accommodation list into diligence as a named item. Do not recode successor-claim liability as accommodation continuity.

**Australia.** Fair Work Act 2009 transfer-of-business rules move transferable industrial instruments and some National Employment Standards service. They do not name disability adjustments. The Disability Discrimination Act / AHRC reasonable-adjustments duty sits on the new employer independently. The Employment Assistance Fund is a grant analogous to Access to Work; JobAccess reasonable-adjustments guidance (updated 30 June 2025) does not describe EAF portability across a transfer of business.

**Canada.** No national transfer-of-undertakings statute. The Canadian Human Rights Act and provincial codes impose a duty to accommodate to the point of undue hardship on the current employer. Successor questions in the case law are about inheriting *claims* and about when a site-owner or contractor is close enough to “employment” to owe the duty (*Schrenk*; *MacKay v. Civeo* 2024 BCHRT 271). They are not about moving an accommodation file.

**Closest transferable machines, and why they stay in this file.** Germany’s “all rights and duties from the employment relationship” plus *betriebliche Übung* is the nearest *doctrinal* hook. US successor liability is the nearest *claims* hook. Australia’s EAF is the nearest *grant* hook. None of them is a published standard that an incoming employer must implement the old accommodation list on day one without a fresh assessment. That is the same split the UK already has between regulation 4, section 20 and Access to Work (158 / 160). International practice does not close the UK gap. It repeats it.

## 162.1 Why the two-machine pattern repeats

Every asked system that has a transfer rule wrote it to stop the new owner tearing up the *contract*. Every asked system that has an accommodation duty wrote it as a fact-specific obligation on the employer who is asking the person to work *today*. The second duty does not need the first in order to bite, and the first does not list the second as a scheduled particular. That is why the UK’s regulation 4 / section 20 / AtW split (158, 160) is not an island. International practice reproduces it.

The practical consequences travel only as *context*:

- A German incoming owner who steps into “all rights and duties” is closer to a contractual-continuity story than an Australian incoming owner who takes the award but not a named adjustment.
- A US asset buyer who does not hire the Deaf employee never becomes the ADA employer; a US stock-sale buyer already was.
- A Canadian contractor on a client site may owe the duty because of nexus, not because a file transferred.

None of those sentences belongs in a UK cell.
