# Prompt 159 — Does the TUPE information-and-consultation duty itself have to be accessible to a Deaf or BSL-using employee?

**Master index prompt 159.** Second file in the TUPE batch (prompts 158–163). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Test whether the *consultation process itself* is an Equality Act reasonable-adjustment event, separately from whatever adjustment is being discussed as a transferred term (that is 158). Confirm who the statute actually addresses — appropriate representatives first, individuals only in defined cases — and do not collapse a union-rep briefing into an individual-access finding. Do not print a named employer’s briefing format as unlawful without a named statute, regulation or judgment. Do not treat English captions, a written pack, or “the union will explain it” as discharging a documented BSL-interpreter request for the meeting.

Gate 158 already holds that informal/passport adjustments are unpublished as a regulation-4 term and that s.20 bites the incoming employer from day one. This file is about the *pre-transfer conversation*, not the transferred term. Distinct from 110 (Acas/HMCTS accessibility of early conciliation and the hearing), 151 (statutory-leave request meetings), BDF redundancy-consultation guidance (adjacent, labelled). 163 owns first-person; this file only records whether a published case or union report of *this object* exists.

## Headline

**ACAS is the only official voice that joins TUPE consultation to the Equality Act, and the join is thin.** The live how-to page (23 July 2025) says employers “must make sure all employees can access the information. This includes: disabled employees who might need reasonable adjustments – for example documents in an accessible format.” That is the published sentence. It names accessible *documents*. It does not name a BSL interpreter, live captions, a DeafBlind manual interpreter, or a plain-English/BSL summary of the regulation 13 pack. It does not say that a hearing-only briefing is a regulation 13 failure.

**Regulation 13 is a representatives duty first.** Information must go to the appropriate representatives (recognised independent union, or elected/appointed employee representatives) “long enough before” the transfer to allow consultation. Consultation about “measures” is with those representatives, with a view to seeking agreement. Individual employees are the statutory addressee only where there are no appropriate representatives *and* the employer is using the direct-consult route: originally micro-businesses with fewer than 10 employees (regulation 13A, transfers on or after 31 July 2014); extended from **1 July 2024** to employers with fewer than 50 employees, or any size of employer transferring fewer than 10 employees (GOV.UK *Consulting and informing*; LexisNexis 30 July 2024). Where a union is recognised, a one-to-one accessible briefing of every Deaf member is **not** what regulation 13 requires. Whether the union then owes its own members an accessible explanation is a different duty (union as service provider / association), not this cell.

**No named case, complaint or union report found of a Deaf, BSL-using or DeafBlind employee being TUPE-consulted in a format they could not access.** UNISON’s live TUPE page (captured 5 September 2026) restates the representatives duty and does not mention BSL, Deaf employees or accessible packs. UNISON Disabled Members Conference 2025 carried a motion on BSL in *welfare-reform* consultations — wrong object. GMB/Unite/PCS/Prospect published TUPE guides on this pass do not supply a named inaccessible-briefing account. JustAnswer threads about Unite and BSL concern union casework and tribunal representation, not a TUPE consultation meeting; they stay out. Business Disability Forum tells employers to book a sign-language interpreter for *redundancy* consultation (page last updated 8 July 2026) — transferable practice, not a TUPE product.

**Two remedies, do not blur them.** A regulation 13/14 failure is a protective award of up to **13 weeks’ uncapped gross pay** per affected employee (regulation 15; ACAS). That award punishes failure to inform/consult representatives (or, in the direct-consult cases, the individuals). It is not drafted as a format-of-the-meeting award. An inaccessible meeting that puts a disabled employee at a substantial disadvantage is an Equality Act s.20 question, judged on knowledge and reasonableness, with the usual Sch.8 para.20 defence (158). No judgment found that treats a missing BSL interpreter at a TUPE briefing as either a regulation 13 failure or an automatic s.20 breach.

**Expected thinness is confirmed.** TUPE consultation accessibility for disabled employees is not a published research category. That absence is the finding.

## 159.1 Representatives first, individuals only in the defined gaps

The originating brief’s picture — a Deaf employee in a room being told they are “TUPE’d across” — is the lived moment. It is not the statutory addressee in a recognised-union workplace. Regulation 13 is built for the union or the elected rep. The individual becomes the addressee when there is no recognised union and no existing reps *and* the employer is small enough, or the transfer is small enough, to use the direct-consult route (13A from 2014; <50 / <10-transferring from 1 July 2024).

That split matters for later files. A beautifully accessible union-rep briefing that is never passed on in BSL is a *union-to-member* problem, not a regulation 13 finding against the employer. A direct-consult employer who runs a voice-only all-hands for nine transferring staff is the fact pattern where s.20 and regulation 13 sit on the same meeting. No published example of either pattern was found.

## 159.2 What ACAS actually joined

The 23 July 2025 how-to page is the only official join. The example is documents in an accessible format. That is real, and it is narrower than the originating brief. It is also aimed at “keeping employees informed” through intranet, email, noticeboards and video calls — the broadcast layer — not at the regulation 13 written pack that goes to representatives.

Do not write “ACAS requires a BSL interpreter at every TUPE consultation.” It does not.

## 159.3 What is unpublished or contested

- Any ET or EAT holding that a hearing-only or English-only TUPE briefing was a regulation 13 failure, an s.20 failure, or both.
- Any UNISON / GMB / Unite / PCS / Prospect published case study of a Deaf or DeafBlind member and a TUPE consultation format.
- Whether regulation 13(8) “facilities as may be appropriate” can be read to include an interpreter for a Deaf representative. Unpublished construction; do not print it as the rule.
- Whether a union’s failure to relay the pack in BSL is a Part 3 services issue against the union. Adjacent, not this cell.
- Quantified data on how often TUPE packs are produced in BSL or Easy Read.

Those absences feed 163 (lived experience) and should not be filled from redundancy-consultation guidance or from tribunal-hearing interpreter rules (110).
