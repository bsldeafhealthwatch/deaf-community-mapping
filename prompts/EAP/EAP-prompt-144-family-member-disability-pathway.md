# Prompt 144 — Does any UK EAP publish a pathway for an employee calling about someone else’s disability?

**Master index prompt 144.** Family-member-disability file in the Employee Assistance Programme batch (prompts 141–149). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** The caller is a **hearing employee** (or any employee who is not themselves the disabled person) using their employer’s EAP because a child, partner or dependant is Deaf, DeafBlind, disabled or neurodivergent. That is a different object from 72–77 (CODA / Deaf-of-Deaf) and 66–71 (ageing Deaf people themselves). Do not treat “we cover family issues” or “caring responsibilities” as a named Deaf / DeafBlind / ND pathway. Test whether any provider names that situation, trains counsellors on it, or publishes a referral route to NDCS, Sense, NAS or equivalent.

**Index check.** Wrapper: `prompts/EAP/EAP-prompts-141-149.md`. 141 = confidentiality of the *employee’s* call. 142 = BSL channel for the employee. 143 = session format for a neurodivergent *employee*. This file is the third-party subject.

## Headline

UK EAP scopes routinely name **family, relationships, parenting, childcare, eldercare and “caring responsibilities.”** None of the retrieved mainstream pages names support for an employee whose *family member* is Deaf, DeafBlind, BSL-using or neurodivergent, as a distinct pathway. No published counsellor-training standard or standing referral protocol to NDCS, Sense, NAS, RNID, BDA or SignHealth was found inside an EAP product.

What exists instead:

- **Generic family cover**, contract-dependent. Health Assured buyer packs list a “Family advice line” on childcare / eldercare. Spectrum.Life university/staff pages list child mental-health assessments and “support with finding and funding adult care.” Bupa EAP lists “family, marriage and children.” Vivup sells a separate **Family Care** benefit (childcare / eldercare / pet care matching) — a booking product, not a disability-specialist counselling track.
- **Carers as a workforce category, not a clinical specialism.** Carers UK’s 2025 workplace-health report: 78% of surveyed employers offer an EAP or wellbeing scheme; only about three in ten promote that support *specifically to carers* or mention caring in the comms. Carers UK’s own rights factsheet (2026/27) points to Carer’s Leave, time off for dependants and Equality Act association discrimination — not to a named EAP pathway.
- **Volume without specificity.** HR Magazine (3 December 2025) reports ~623,000 EAP contacts in 2024/25; third-most-common reason was parents worried about children’s mental health. That is generic child-MH demand. It is not a Deaf-child or disabled-dependant series.
- **Tax structure cuts the other way.** EAPA UK / HMRC note (March 2023): EAP services to family members and dependants are **not** tax-exempt under EIM 21845. EAPA’s recommendation was not to put family cover in the core offering. Providers still advertise household access on many contracts; the tax line is why “family included” is a commercial add-on, not a statutory standard.

NDCS, Sense, SignHealth, RNID and BDA were not found to publish an account of a parent or partner using (or failing to use) a mainstream employer EAP about the family member’s deafness or disability. NDCS’s own *staff* EAP appears in an Employee Benefits cancer-support case study — the wrong caller. SignHealth’s BSL EAP covers “family and relationships” for **Deaf employees**, not for hearing parents of Deaf children.

No first-person, named case, or charity survey was found for this exact object. Absence is the finding.

**Hard-don't.** Do not treat “family issues” or “caring responsibilities” as a Deaf/ND pathway. Do not paste CODA 72–77 or Deaf-older 66–71 here. Do not print that generic family cover is unlawful or inadequate as a legal conclusion.

## 144.1 Why this is a different situation from 141–143

141–143 assume the *caller* is the person with the access need. Here the caller can use the phone. The gap, if there is one, is content: does the counsellor know what NDCS, a BSL household, or a Sense communicator-guide actually is, or do they run a generic parenting / stress session? No public source answers that. A hearing parent who can use the voice helpline still meets a product built for “family issues,” not for “my child is Deaf and the CAMHS pathway will not book a BSL clinician.”

Household access (partner and older children can call *for themselves*) is also the wrong object. The prompt is the employee calling *about* the other person.

## 144.2 What is unpublished or contested

Unpublished:

- Any EAP service schedule that names Deaf, DeafBlind, BSL or a specific neurodivergence of a dependant.
- Any counsellor CPD module or referral SOP naming NDCS / Sense / NAS.
- Any first-person of a parent or partner using an employer EAP about the family member’s deafness or disability, including a “it was just generic counselling” account.

Contested / thin:

- How often “family advice line” staff actually signpost to a specialist charity. Unpublished.
- HR Magazine’s third-place “worried about my child” bucket — not disability-split.

Do not run this silence forward as “EAPs abandon working carers of Deaf children.” Carers UK’s finding is the tighter one that *can* be printed: most employers have an EAP; most do not aim it at carers.
