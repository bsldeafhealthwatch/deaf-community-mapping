## Headline

An EAP is **not** just another outsourced phone line. UK industry practice, set out in EAPA UK’s Standards of Professional Practice (dated December 2022; PDF issued January 2023 — no later full replacement found this pass), treats confidentiality as a “central tenet.” Self-referral is the ordinary route and is designed to bypass line management and HR. Purchaser reports must protect “individual confidentiality and anonymity.” Named disclosure to the employer needs the service user’s consent (the management-referral track). That is **industry standard and contract practice**, not a statute that “bars” every provider from telling every employer. Exceptions in the same standard are threat to life or others, child protection, and legal compulsion. BACP’s Ethical Framework binds the counsellor separately. UK GDPR / Data Protection Act 2018 bind the provider as controller of health data.

The Equality Act **does reach** an employer-arranged EAP. Section 39(2)(b) covers access to “any other benefit, facility or service.” Section 39(5) applies the s.20 duty to the employer. Nothing in the Act says the duty stops because the benefit is voluntary or confidential. ACAS lists EAPs as a workplace support resource and, on a different page, lists a sign-language interpreter as an example auxiliary aid — it does **not** join those two sentences. No published ACAS, EHRC Employment Code, EAPA or CIPD text found this pass says “signing an EAP contract discharges the reasonable-adjustment duty” or “an inaccessible EAP is outside Part 5 because it is confidential.”

The structural bind is therefore **real as a design collision, unpublished as a named legal problem.** Arranging a BSL interpreter or a non-voice route normally needs the employer (or AtW via the employer) to know and act. The EAP’s selling point is that the employer does *not* know who called. Schedule 8 paragraph 20 limits the employer’s Part 5 duty where A does not know and could not reasonably be expected to know of the disability and the likely disadvantage. A worker who never tells HR they cannot use the helpline may leave the employer without the knowledge the Part 5 duty is built on. The contractor can still sit under Part 3 via s.31(5) (closed-group staff service; employees treated as a section of the public). Schedule 3 paragraph 20’s insurance / personal-pension carve-out does **not** name counselling or EAPs — do not stretch it.

**No named tribunal, EHRC investigation or ICO decision notice** was found that tests EAP accessibility for a Deaf, BSL-using or DeafBlind employee, or that tests whether an employer discharged s.20 by buying an EAP without checking channels. The 12 July 2024 BBC File on 4 report on Health Assured (corporate clients listening to helpline calls; provider disputed a confidentiality breach; EAPA opened an investigation) is evidence that the industry claim is **contested in practice**. It is not a finding that any named contract is unlawful, and it is not a Deaf/BSL case.

**Hard-don't for anything public.** Do not print that a named EAP provider’s practice is unlawful, discriminatory or in breach of the Equality Act. Do not print that providers are “contractually barred” as if that were a statute. Do not print that contracting out the EAP discharges the employer’s duty. Do not treat the Health Assured broadcast as an ICO or court finding.

## 141.1 Industry structure — self-referral, two tracks, what the employer sees

EAPA UK is a membership body. Its Standards of Professional Practice are the document UK buyers are pointed to. They are not regulations and they do not bind a non-member. The December 2022 / January 2023 PDF remains the file on the EAPA site this pass; later EAPA reports (*Holding It Together*, March 2023; *Securing the Future of the EAP*, March 2024) discuss market quality and digital delivery but do not replace section 7.

Section 7 is the load-bearing confidentiality text: statement to the purchaser; statement to every service user before help starts (read out or typed, then agreed, on a phone or online session); personal confidentiality contracts for EAP staff; security of records; named exceptions. Section 3 is the reporting text: the purchaser gets management information, anonymised. Section 21 is the management-referral text: a different door, with specified, consented feedback to the referrer.

That two-track design is why an EAP is not an IT helpdesk with a missing VRS clause. On a helpdesk, the worker *wants* the employer to know there is a ticket. On an EAP, the worker is sold the opposite. Provider marketing (Health Assured; Life & Progress; others in the same mould) repeats the line that the employer cannot see who called. Treat that as commercial description of the product, not as a court-tested absolute.

## 141.2 Equality Act — the duty reaches the benefit; knowledge and reasonableness stay live

s.39(2)(b) is the employment-side “benefit, facility or service” limb. An employer-paid EAP is inside those words on any ordinary reading. s.39(5) then applies s.20. The Employment Code’s benefits chapter (EHRC Employment Code, file dated 2021 on the Commission site, still the live employment code this pass) discusses pay, pensions, insurance and childcare exceptions; it does not contain an EAP worked example. Absence of an example is not an exemption.

Two limits stay in play and must not be collapsed:

1. **Knowledge (Sch. 8 para 20).** Part 5 is not anticipatory in the Part 3 sense. If the only person who knows the helpline is unusable is the worker who was promised confidentiality, the employer may never acquire the knowledge the duty needs. Whether “we employ BSL users and bought a voice-only EAP” is enough for “could reasonably be expected to know” is untested.
2. **Reasonableness.** Cost, practicability, effectiveness, and the extent of the employer’s control over the contractor are factors. Prompt 121 already recorded that contractor limits are a reasonableness *factor*, not a switch-off. Same here.

s.31(5) can put a second duty on the provider (Part 3, anticipatory, closed staff group). That is the cleaner statutory hook for “the helpline itself has no non-voice route,” because Part 3 does not wait for the employer to be told. It is also untested on an EAP. Schedule 3 paragraph 20 does not rescue the provider: EAPs are not insurance or personal pensions.

## 141.3 What is unpublished or contested

Unpublished, and the point of this gate:

- Any ACAS, EHRC, EAPA, CIPD or ICO document that treats EAP *channel* accessibility as a reasonable-adjustment question.
- Any document that tells a Deaf employee how to get a BSL route *without* telling HR they are using the EAP.
- Any ET or ICO decision on these facts.
- Any census of UK EAP contracts that records whether accessibility is a specification line.

Contested:

- Whether confidentiality is in fact held in every live contract. The July 2024 Health Assured broadcast is the named public contest. Use it only as evidence that the tenet is disputed in the market, not as a finding against that provider and not as a Deaf-access case.
- Whether “digital / virtual” in EAPA section 8 already includes an accessible route. The standard does not say so. Channel facts move to 142.

Do not fill those gaps with 121’s helpdesk reasoning pasted across. The helpdesk worker can escalate through HR. The EAP worker is told not to.
