Checked first: the master index and existing batches. Prompts 121–126 (outsourced service channels) cover the same phone-first-outsourced structural pattern in general (IT helpdesk, payroll, facilities, whistleblowing, pensions) but explicitly did not include the EAP. EAPs raise problems those other channels do not: the service is by design confidential and self-referred (no line manager or HR involvement, which is usually how a Deaf employee's other adjustments get arranged, per 97–102's SLA-processing findings); it is a mental-health/counselling service (raising a distinct confidentiality tension from the "interpreter as a new party" problem already flagged in 127–133 for financial information barriers, but now for therapeutic disclosure rather than commercial confidentiality); and it can be the point of contact for support about a family member's disability, not the employee's own — a population and use-case no other batch has addressed at all. This is new ground, not a duplicate of 121–126 or any adjustment-pipeline batch.

Distinct from: 20–26 / 52–54 (AtW/PIP, not an employer-contracted counselling line); 85–91 (neurodivergence pathways, not EAP delivery format); 92–96 (Occupational Health recommendations, not a confidential self-referral helpline); 97–102 (HR/IT ticket SLAs — the usual route for arranging an interpreter, which this batch tests as mutually exclusive with EAP confidentiality); 109–114 (grievance/ET once an adjustment has already failed); 115–120 (DeafBlind in-post workplace adjustments); 121–126 (outsourced *operational* channels — EAP was carved out); 127–133 (Chinese Walls / commercial confidentiality, not therapeutic disclosure); 134–140 (devices/AI at work). Family-member disability support through an EAP is not CODA 72–77 (children of Deaf adults) and not Deaf-older 66–71 (ageing Deaf people themselves).

Anchor: three linked situations every prompt in this batch must stay honest to — (a) a Deaf BSL-using employee wants to self-refer to their EAP for a personal issue and finds the only access route is a telephone helpline, with no advertised video-relay, text, or BSL-interpreted route, and self-referral is specifically designed to bypass HR/line-manager involvement, which is also the usual route through which an interpreter would normally be arranged — creating a structural bind where using the confidential route and getting an accessible route may be mutually exclusive; (b) a neurodivergent employee needing EAP counselling finds standard talking-therapy-style delivery (unstructured phone conversation, no advance agenda, sensory demands of a live call) is not adapted to their needs, distinct from a communication-access problem; and (c) a hearing employee whose child or partner is Deaf, DeafBlind, or has additional needs contacts the EAP for their own stress/coping support around caring responsibilities, and the EAP counsellor has no training or referral pathway specific to the caregiving-for-a-disabled-family-member context, as distinct from generic "caring responsibilities" advice.

Proposed domain: stub under the existing Welfare & Employment cluster — a specific, high-sensitivity outsourced service channel (a sub-case of the 121–126 pattern, requiring its own batch given the confidentiality/self-referral and family-member dimensions), not a new numbered Domain.

### Notes for this batch

- **Gate prompt: 141.** The self-referral/confidentiality structure is what makes EAPs institutionally different from every other outsourced channel this project has covered (97–102, 121–126) — a missed SLA can be escalated through HR, but a confidential self-referred service is deliberately designed so HR does not know it is being used, which may be a structural reason no accessible route gets arranged at all, since nobody with the authority to fix it necessarily knows there is a problem. This tension should govern how 142–145 are read.
- **Prompt 145 tests the premise directly rather than assuming it.** Graeme's own framing ("not fit for purpose with multiple issues") is exactly right as a hypothesis to test, but this batch must not print it as a confirmed finding unless a documented case or provider gap actually supports it; if nothing is found, log the absence of any coordinated-multi-need EAP model as the finding.
- **Prompt 144 is a genuinely novel cross-cut.** Family-member disability support through an EAP has not been touched by any other batch (66–71 covers ageing Deaf people themselves, not hearing family members supporting them; CODA 72–77 covers children of Deaf adults specifically, not the reverse — a hearing employee with a Deaf child).
- **Prompt 148 is distinct from 143 and 145.** 143 asks whether delivery format suits neurodivergent users and 145 asks whether multiple needs are coordinated at all; 148 asks whether the counsellor answering the phone has any actual training, specialism, or case-matching for a complex/Deaf/BSL/neurodivergent profile in the first place, as opposed to a generalist being assigned regardless. This is a counsellor-competency question, not an access-channel or coordination question, and should not be collapsed into either.
- **Prompt 149 is distinct from 147.** 147 asks for testimony from people who tried to use an EAP and found it inaccessible; 149 asks the prior question: whether Deaf employees simply don't attempt it at all (for reasons beyond pure access — hassle, distrust, stigma, not knowing a route exists), and whether the employer's own reason for offering an EAP is better understood as legal/duty-of-care risk management than genuine wellbeing provision. This prompt must not assert employer motive as fact without a named source, and must treat "no Deaf-specific uptake data exists" as a legitimate, useful finding rather than a failed search — the absence of disaggregated uptake data is itself evidence of how invisible this population is to EAP providers' own monitoring.
- **Hard-don't for this batch:** do not print any claim that a named EAP provider's practice is unlawful, discriminatory, or in breach of the Equality Act without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/court judgment. Do not assert that EAPs are "not fit for purpose" as a general finding — the batch may confirm this is undocumented rather than false, which is a different and more useful finding for the dossier's evidence hierarchy. Do not print that a named counsellor or provider is unqualified or negligent without a named BACP/UKCP, tribunal, or equivalent finding. Do not infer that a named employer offers an EAP solely to limit legal liability.
- **Held-back legs:** none — nine prompts, because the topic genuinely splits into the structural self-referral/confidentiality tension (gate), Deaf/BSL access specifically, neurodivergent delivery-format fit specifically, family-member-disability support specifically, the multiple-compounding-needs test, international comparison, lived experience, counsellor competency/specialism specifically, and uptake/avoidance/employer-motive specifically — collapsing any of these would lose which population or which specific failure mode a finding belongs to.
- **Relationship to 121–126 (outsourced services) and the rest of the Welfare & Employment cluster:** this batch is a sub-case of 121–126's general pattern (outsourced, phone-first, no accessibility clause) but is not a duplicate — 121–126 explicitly did not cover EAPs, and the confidentiality/self-referral structure, family-member-support angle, counsellor-competency question, and uptake/avoidance question here are unique to this channel.
- **Do not conflate with Graeme's personal OH / HR thread** — this batch produces general, citable evidence for the dossier, not advice on an individual EAP call.
- **England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland** are separate systems. Do not paste a Scottish workplace-wellbeing or public-service interpreting default into an England employment cell.
