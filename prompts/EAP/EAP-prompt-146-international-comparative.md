# Prompt 146 — International comparative: published EAP access standards and family-disability categories

**Master index prompt 146.** International-only file in the Employee Assistance Programme batch (prompts 141–149). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. **None of this fills a UK data cell.** Do not paste ADA, EEOC, Australian EAF or EU sentences into 141–145 or 147–149.

**What this prompt is for.** Three asks, all outside the UK: (1) any jurisdiction or named EAP that publishes accessible delivery (video relay, sign-language-interpreted, text) *as a matter of course*, not case-by-case; (2) any jurisdiction that names support for an employee caring for a disabled or Deaf family member as a *distinct EAP category*; (3) what does not transfer.

**Index check.** Wrapper: `prompts/EAP/EAP-prompts-141-149.md`. UK channel = 142. UK family pathway = 144. This file is context only.

---

## Headline

No asked jurisdiction was found that publishes a **sign-language video-relay EAP as a standing, as-of-right product standard.** What exists is a short stack of different legal objects:

- **Canada (closest published EAP-level door).** Health Canada’s EAP, used by 90+ federal organisations, publishes a dedicated TTY/digital number (`1-800-567-5803`) on the programme page and the contact page, plus “digital services if you’re deaf or hard of hearing” and sessions by video, phone or email. The Treasury Board *Directive on Employee Assistance Programs* requires access for persons employed **and their dependants**, and official-language delivery in English and French. It does **not** name ASL or LSQ video relay as the EAP door. TTY + email + video is still the strongest *published EAP-page* non-voice route in the asked set.
- **United States (employer-duty, not EAP-product standard).** EEOC *Enforcement Guidance on Reasonable Accommodation* lists employee assistance programmes among the “benefits and privileges of employment” that must be accessible under the ADA, absent undue hardship. EEOC *Hearing Disabilities in the Workplace* lists interpreters, VRS, VRI, captioned/TTY phones as workplace accommodations generally. FCC VRS is a free telecoms service the employee can use to call any number, including an EAP. That is **infrastructure plus an employer duty**. It is not an EAPA-US rule that every EAP contract includes a VRS queue.
- **Australia.** Named EAP providers publish phone / video / in-person and, in one case (Australian EAP), clinicians who can support existing neurodivergent diagnoses and CALD languages. AccessEAP publishes **dedicated lines** for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander employees and for LGBTQIA+ employees — identity-matching, not Auslan. No Auslan-as-standard EAP page found. The National Relay Service exists as national telecoms infrastructure; it was not found written into an EAP product standard this pass. EAF is workplace-adjustment funding — do not transfer.
- **Ireland.** One university EAP (UCD / Spectrum.Life) lists “British Sign Language” among 34 counselling languages. That is a language-matching bullet on one contract, not a national ISL EAP standard. Ireland’s ISL Act and Work and Access hour caps are employment-interpreting law, not EAP specifications.
- **EU / Netherlands.** EUD’s June 2025 European Accessibility Act report asks for 24/7 national VRS — customer-service and telecoms, not EAP. Dutch UWV funds interpreter hours for work, education and private life via Tolkcontact — a social-insurance product, not an EAP.

**Family-member disability as a distinct EAP category:** not found. Canada and most market EAPs include *dependants* as eligible callers and sometimes publish generic “caregiving” content. None of the retrieved pages names “my child is Deaf / my partner is DeafBlind / my dependant is autistic” as its own service line.

International EAPA *Standards and Professional Guidelines* (2010 edition still the public text) define core EAP technology. They do not specify sign-language or relay delivery. A 1996 EAPA International Guidelines document is cited in that history; the 2010 file retrieved does not carry a VRS clause.

**Hard-don't.** International only. Do not paste into UK cells.

## 146.1 What does not transfer

| Object | Why it stays out of UK cells |
|---|---|
| ADA “EAP is a benefit/privilege” | Different statute. UK EA s.39 reaches a voluntary EAP as a benefit (141) without needing the US sentence. |
| FCC VRS as a free, 24/7, any-number service | England has no equivalent national non-emergency VRS (78–80). An employee cannot assume they can VRS-call a UK EAP. |
| EEOC interpreter / VRI list | Employer-accommodation examples, not an EAP call-centre spec. |
| Australian EAF | Post-interview / workplace adjustment grant. Wrong product. |
| UWV / Tolkcontact hours | Social-insurance interpreter budget, not a contracted EAP. |
| EAA 24/7 VRS recommendation | EUD policy ask aimed at EAA services. Not commenced as an EAP rule. |
| Canada TTY number | Transfers only as *comparative shape* (a published second number on the EAP page). Canada’s official-languages and federal-directive machinery do not exist in the UK EAP market. |
| AccessEAP dedicated identity lines | Shows a market will publish a *separate number* for a named population. Those lines are cultural/identity, not sign language. |

## 146.2 What is unpublished or contested

Unpublished:

- Any national EAP association standard (US EAPA, EASNA, EAPA Australia) that mandates VRS or a sign-language counsellor queue as default.
- Any US/CA/AU/EU utilisation figure for Deaf employees using an EAP via relay.
- Any named family-disability EAP service category.

Contested / easy to over-read:

- “ADA covers EAPs” is true as an employer duty and easy to mis-file as “US EAPs all have VRS.” They do not, on the pages retrieved.
- Canada’s TTY line is real and published; treating it as ASL/LSQ VRS would be a category error.
- UCD’s “British Sign Language” bullet is one Irish university contract using a UK-market provider. Do not promote it into an Ireland-wide standard.
