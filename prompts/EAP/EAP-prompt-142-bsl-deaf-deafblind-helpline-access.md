# Prompt 142 — Do named UK EAP providers publish a non-voice access route, and is there a documented Deaf/BSL helpline failure?

**Master index prompt 142.** Channel-census file in the Employee Assistance Programme batch (prompts 141–149). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Record what named UK EAP providers *publish* about non-voice routes (video relay, textphone / Relay UK, live chat, BSL-interpreted or BSL-fluent video counselling). Separate a buyer-brochure line from a standing employee-facing self-referral channel. Record any documented case or complaint where a Deaf or BSL-using employee could not use an EAP because the only advertised door was a voice telephone. Do not treat “we support all employees” or “accessible” as a BSL route. Do not print that a named provider is non-compliant.

## Headline

The published UK shape is **phone-first mainstream EAP + one specialist BSL product that is not a self-referral helpline.**

Mainstream providers (Health Assured, Vivup, Spectrum.Life / AXA-branded EAP, Bupa EAP, Validium, Workplace Options, CiC) advertise a 24/7 **telephone** helpline as the core door. Several also advertise live chat, email, SMS/WhatsApp, or video counselling. That is not the same as a published BSL video-relay or BSL-fluent counselling route that an employee can use without telling HR.

Two narrower published routes exist:

1. **Text relay, not BSL.** Validium’s employer Q&A tells staff with a “hearing impediment” to use Next Generation Text / Relay UK (`18001` + the helpline number). Health Assured buyer PDFs list Relay UK alongside Braille, Easy Read and Minicom. Relay UK is English text-to-voice. It is not BSL.
2. **BSL as a bought extra or a specialist contract, not as the default helpline.** Health Assured sales packs list “Video counselling with BSL counsellors,” in one pack marked “subject to request.” That is a buyer-facing catalogue line, not a verified standing feature of every Health Assured contract, and not an employee-facing self-referral VRS number on the public site this pass. SignHealth sells a **BSL Employee Assistance Programme** (page dated 28 August 2025; offer PDF same day) delivered by Deaf or BSL-fluent therapists. The employer *emails a referral*. Assessment is 90 minutes in BSL, then a course of sessions (offer PDF: 10 sessions; 2025/26 fees assessment £162.75 / session £110.25). SignHealth’s own PDF calls this “the UK’s only BSL Employee Assistance Programme” and says “Most traditional Employee Assistance Programmes (EAPs) are not designed with British Sign Language (BSL) users in mind.” Royal Mail is the named employer partner (Fiona McAslan quote on the live page).

AXA Health and Bupa publish Relay UK and Convo BSL *for their health-insurance / customer-service phone lines*. Those pages are not, on the text retrieved this pass, the EAP counselling product. Do not paste them into an EAP-helpline cell.

**No documented named case** was found of a Deaf or BSL-using employee who tried a mainstream EAP helpline, found only a voice telephone, and whose outcome (went without, used email, escalated to HR) is on the public record. BDF, RNID, BDA and SignHealth do not publish an EAP-helpline failure series. Locked Out (27 November 2025) lists “private counselling or psychological therapy” among situations that exclude BSL users — adjacent, not an EAP named-case. DeafBlind-specific EAP channel: **no reliable figure found.**

The bind from 141 therefore still stands on the channel side: the only fully BSL-native product found is reached by *employer referral*, which is the opposite of confidential self-referral.

**Hard-don't.** Do not print that Health Assured, Vivup, Spectrum.Life, Bupa, AXA, Validium or Workplace Options is non-compliant. Do not treat a sales-PDF bullet as a live employee channel. Do not treat SignHealth’s “only BSL EAP” claim as independently audited — it is the provider’s own marketing, and no rival BSL-native 24/7 helpline was found this pass.

## 142.1 What “a route” means on these pages

A live-chat widget, an email address, or a booked Zoom session in spoken English is a non-voice door. It is not a BSL door. Relay UK is a non-voice door for people who can work in written English. BSL users who do not work in written English are not served by that door. Prompt 141 already recorded that EAPA’s own standard names “telephone, digital, virtual, face to face” and does not name VRS or BSL. This pass matches that silence on the public mainstream pages.

Health Assured is the only mainstream name whose *buyer* material this pass lists BSL counsellors and Relay UK in the same slide. That is why it is in the table. It is also why the cell is marked catalogue-not-channel: the public consumer site was not found to expose a BSL self-referral path, and one pack says “subject to request.”

## 142.2 SignHealth is a different object

SignHealth’s product is BSL-first therapy sold to employers for Deaf staff. The access model on the live page is “Email us to refer a deaf colleague.” That is management / HR involvement. Under 141, that is the track that *can* carry consented feedback to a referrer — and the track a worker uses when they do not want HR to know they need counselling. The specialist product therefore solves the language problem by re-opening the confidentiality bind. Do not file it as evidence that the mainstream 24/7 helpline is accessible.

Royal Mail is the only named purchaser on the public page. Do not generalise from one partnership.

## 142.3 What is unpublished or contested

Unpublished:

- Any employee-facing “call this VRS number / scan this QR to reach our EAP in BSL” page from Health Assured, Vivup, Spectrum.Life, Bupa EAP, AXA EAP, Validium or CiC.
- Any FOI or contract schedule that shows how often BSL video counselling is actually included in a UK EAP call-off.
- Any named Deaf/BSL first-person of a failed helpline attempt (outcome included). That absence is itself the finding; do not fill it from Locked Out’s private-therapy bullet or from SignHealth’s marketing sentence.
- Any DeafBlind EAP channel.

Contested:

- SignHealth’s “UK’s only BSL EAP.” No rival product was found; that is not the same as an audit.
- Whether Health Assured’s BSL-counsellor bullet is live on a given employer’s contract. Unpublished unless the employer publishes it.
- Whether AXA/Bupa Convo covers the EAP number. The retrieved pages do not say so.

Do not run 142’s silence forward as “EAPs are not fit for purpose.” 145 tests that premise. 147 is lived experience of people who *tried*. 149 is people who never called.
