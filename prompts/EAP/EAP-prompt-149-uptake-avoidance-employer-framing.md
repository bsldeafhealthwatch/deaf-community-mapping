# Prompt 149 — Uptake, avoidance, and how employers themselves describe why they buy an EAP

**Master index prompt 149.** Final file in the Employee Assistance Programme batch (prompts 141–149). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. **Do not print that a named employer offers an EAP solely or primarily to limit legal liability without a named source saying so.** Absence of a Deaf-specific uptake split is a finding, not a failed search. People who *tried* the service sit in 147. This file is people who never start, plus the employer-side framing.

**What this prompt is for.** Four asks: (1) how CIPD / ACAS / case law *themselves* describe the purpose of an EAP; (2) published utilisation benchmarks and whether any split by disability, deafness or communication need exists; (3) documented reasons a Deaf / BSL / DeafBlind employee did not use an available EAP, other than the channel already covered in 141–142; (4) unpublished or contested.

## Headline

**No Deaf-, BSL- or DeafBlind-specific EAP uptake figure exists in official or industry statistics.** That absence is the finding. Industry benchmarks for the *whole* workforce sit in a contested band (EAPA’s own 2022 figure “topped 12%”; historical EAPA ROI-tool average “around 10%”; vendor and trade-press reprints of 3–5%). None of those series is cut by disability or communication need. Providers cannot currently say whether Deaf employees use the product they sell.

Employer-side framing that *is* published, without inferring motive:

- **Hatton v Sutherland [2002] EWCA Civ 76 / [2002] IRLR 263.** Hale LJ’s propositions on psychiatric-injury claims include: an employer who offers “a confidential advice service, with referral to appropriate counselling or treatment services, is unlikely to be found in breach of duty.” Later commentary (and later first-instance applications) treat that as *reducing but not eliminating* risk. The sentence is in the judgment. It is not a CIPD brochure saying “buy an EAP to win a tribunal.”
- **ACAS.** Duty of care = do all that is reasonably possible for health, safety and wellbeing. An EAP is listed as an *example of a resource* managers should know about and tell workers exists. ACAS does not say the EAP discharges the duty.
- **CIPD Health and wellbeing at work 2025** (Simplyhealth). 41% of surveyed employers offer an EAP as a mental-health step; EAP is one of the two most-used methods for reducing workplace stress (with flexible working); 70% of public-sector respondents offer one. CIPD also records that offering counselling / an EAP is not itself a statutory requirement.
- **EAPA 2024 *Securing the future*.** Employers “will only believe they are receiving value for money if utilisation levels are above 10%.” Higher use is also framed inside the same report as a sign that more staff are reaching crisis. That is a value-for-money / demand-pressure frame, not a liability frame.

**Avoidance testimony specific to Deaf / BSL / DeafBlind employees who never called:** none found (consistent with 147’s empty tried-it cell). Structural reasons that *are* documented and do not need a first-person: 141’s self-referral / confidentiality bind (arranging BSL often needs HR); 142’s voice-first door; BDF 2023: “less than half of disabled employees said their employer’s employee assistance programme was accessible and inclusive to them.” That last figure is *perception of inclusiveness* among disabled employees generally, not a Deaf headcount and not a reason-for-not-calling survey.

SignHealth’s “leaving many without the help they need” remains a product claim. Do not recode it as utilisation data.

**Hard-don't.** No inferred employer motive. No “Deaf people don’t use EAPs” as a rate. No collapse into 147.

## 149.1 Two frames that are published — keep them separate

| Frame | What the source actually says | What not to print |
|---|---|---|
| Wellbeing / stress-management offer | CIPD 2025: EAP is a common mental-health step and a top method for reducing stress. ACAS: tell staff the resource exists. | “The EAP is the wellbeing intervention.” |
| Psychiatric-injury risk | Hatton: offering a confidential advice + counselling service makes a breach finding *unlikely*. Commentary: not an absolute defence. | “Employers buy EAPs to limit tribunal exposure” as an unsourced motive. |

Both sentences can be true in the same organisation. This file does not choose which one an unnamed HR director “really” holds.

## 149.2 Why the missing split matters

EAPA, CIPD and provider dashboards can say how many people called. They cannot, on anything retrieved, say how many of those people were Deaf, BSL-using or DeafBlind — or how many of the eligible Deaf headcount never called. Combined with 141 (the access route often needs HR, which is the thing the EAP is designed to bypass) and BDF’s “less than half of disabled employees” inclusiveness figure, the monitoring gap is the evidence that this population is invisible to the product’s own scorecard. That is the prompt’s intended finding.
