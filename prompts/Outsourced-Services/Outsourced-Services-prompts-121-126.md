# Prompts 121–126 — Outsourced / offshored internal service-channel accessibility

Added: 10 September 2026. Compiled as findings: 11 September 2026. Stub under the Welfare & Employment cluster. Not a new Domain.

Checked first: the master index and batches 78–80 (VRS/Telecoms — general access, not internal corporate contracts), 92–96 (OH), 97–102 (HR/IT *adjustment-ticket* SLAs), 103–108 (recruitment), 109–114 (grievance/ET), 115–120 (DeafBlind in-post adjustments). None of those batches ask whether an ordinary, disability-neutral internal channel — IT helpdesk, payroll/HR shared-service line, facilities helpdesk, whistleblowing/ethics hotline, pension/benefits administrator helpline — was ever built so a Deaf or DeafBlind employee could reach it once the channel itself had been outsourced or offshored.

Anchor: a Deaf BSL-using employee in London calls their employer's IT helpdesk for a routine password reset. The call is routed to an outsourced support desk with no video-relay capability, no advertised live-chat fallback, and a script that assumes a hearing telephone caller. When the employee asks about a BSL interpreter or video relay, the agent has no process and no budget authority to bring in “a third party.” The managed-service contract was priced without an accessibility line item.

Gate: prompt 121. Load-bearing: prompt 124 (the funding gap). Hard-don't: do not print that a named employer, outsourcing provider or procurement contract is unlawful, discriminatory or in breach of the Equality Act without a named statute, regulation or tribunal/court judgment.

Headline across the batch: the reasonable-adjustment duty owed by the *employer* under Part 5 is not discharged by outsourcing the channel. The *contractor* may separately owe a Part 3 services duty because Equality Act s.31(5) treats employees as a section of the public when the employer has arranged a closed-group service. Employer-arranged insurance and personal-pension services are carved out of Part 3 by Schedule 3 paragraph 20. No named UK tribunal or EHRC finding was found that applies those sections to an outsourced IT helpdesk, payroll line, facilities desk, whistleblowing hotline or pension helpline used by a Deaf, BSL-using or DeafBlind employee. Access to Work can fund the employee’s own VRS / interpreter minutes; it is not designed to retrofit a contractor’s service infrastructure. That funding-and-procurement gap is the finding, not a failed search.
