## Headline

The employer’s **Part 5** reasonable-adjustment duty is **not discharged** by outsourcing the channel. Equality Act 2010 s.20 / s.21 / s.39 and Schedule 8 sit with the employer in respect of its own provisions, criteria and practices — including the practice of routing staff through a phone-first contractor. The contractor’s commercial limitations are not, on the face of the Act, a defence that turns the employer’s duty off. Whether any particular step (standing VRS on the desk; a written fallback; a booked interpreter) is *reasonable* remains fact-specific. That last sentence is why this batch must not print “unlawful” without a named judgment on these facts.

A **second** duty-holder can exist. Equality Act s.31(5) says that where an employer arranges for another person to provide a service **only** to the employer’s employees, the employer is **not** the service-provider, but the employees **are** a section of the public. The contractor then sits under Part 3 (s.29 + s.20 via Schedule 2). That Part 3 duty is anticipatory as against disabled people generally. The 2026 Services Code (laid 21 May 2026; the GOV.UK page recorded commencement on 5 August 2026) restates the split at paras 11.69–11.72: employer conduct stays in Part 5; contractor discrimination against the closed staff group is Part 3.

**Carve-out that matters for pensions.** Schedule 3 paragraph 20 disapplies s.29 to “relevant financial services” provided in pursuance of employer arrangements — insurance and services relating to membership of or benefits under a **personal pension scheme**. An outsourced pension-administrator helpline arranged as a consequence of employment therefore does **not** pick up the Part 3 services duty via s.31(5). The employer’s Part 5 duty is untouched by that carve-out. Occupational-scheme administration is not named in paragraph 20; do not stretch the carve-out past the words.

**Procurement is not a substitute duty.** For public-sector employers, Public Contracts Regulations 2015 regulation 42(8) required technical specifications for anything intended for use by natural persons — “whether the general public **or staff of the contracting authority**” — to take account of accessibility criteria for disabled persons or design for all users, “except in duly justified cases.” That rule applied to legacy PCR procurements. The Procurement Act 2023 (in force for new covered procurements from 24 February 2025) **does not re-enact** regulation 42(8) as a free-standing “must include accessibility” clause. Cabinet Office technical-specifications guidance (HTML, updated 13 July 2026) says specifications “will need to be drawn up to take account of the needs of people with disabilities,” pointing to the Public Sector Equality Duty (Equality Act s.149) rather than to a Procurement Act clock. How commonly an accessibility line item is written into a managed-service helpdesk contract: **no reliable figure found**.

**No named case on this exact cell.** No published employment-tribunal, county-court, EHRC investigation or enforcement notice was found on this pass that concerns a Deaf, BSL-using or DeafBlind *employee* and an outsourced or offshored internal service channel (IT helpdesk, payroll shared-service, facilities desk, whistleblowing hotline, pension helpline). Adjacent named cases stay in their own batches (Rimmer = JCP; Iqbal = Home Office office-hours BSL + OH; Bentley = ET hearing admin). Absence of a judgment is not a finding that the duty does not apply.

**Hard-don't for anything public.** Do not print that a named employer, outsourcing provider or procurement contract is in breach. Do not treat PCR 2015 regulation 42(8) as if it still governs every live public contract. Do not treat Schedule 3 paragraph 20 as wiping the employer’s Part 5 duty off a pension helpline.

## 121.1 Two statutes, two duty-holders, one employee

A password-reset call is not a “reasonable adjustment request” in the 97–102 sense. It is use of an ordinary internal service. The legal map still splits:

1. **Employer — Part 5.** The PCP is “staff contact IT / payroll / facilities / the speak-up line by voice telephone to the contracted desk.” If that PCP puts a Deaf BSL-user at a substantial disadvantage compared with non-disabled colleagues, s.20 is in play. The employer can change the PCP (open a written / VRS / ticket-without-voice route), provide an auxiliary aid, or require the contractor to do so. The contract is the employer’s document.
2. **Contractor — Part 3, if s.31(5) bites.** A closed staff-only helpdesk arranged by the employer is a service to a section of the public. The contractor’s own phone-only PCP can generate an anticipatory services duty. Employees do not lose that claim merely because they are employees of someone else.
3. **Pensions / insurance exception.** If the channel is an employer-arranged personal-pension or insurance service, paragraph 20 takes Part 3 off the table. Part 5 remains.

s.111 (instructing, causing or inducing) and ss.109–110 (employees and agents) sit behind both. They are hooks, not findings.

## 121.2 Procurement required accessibility in the spec — then the wording moved

PCR 2015 regulation 42(8) is the cleanest UK sentence this batch has: staff-use procurements must take accessibility into account unless the authority can duly justify not doing so. It is a specification rule, not a BSL-VRS product mandate, and it does not bind private employers.

From 24 February 2025 new covered procurements run under the Procurement Act 2023. Section 56 tells authorities how to write specifications (functional requirements, standards, no unnecessary brand-lock). It does not repeat regulation 42(8). The Cabinet Office’s own guidance puts disability accessibility back onto the Equality Act and the PSED “across the whole commercial lifecycle.” That is a relocation, not a repeal of the Equality Act. It is also thinner as a *procurement* hook: there is no longer a one-sentence “shall take into account accessibility criteria… except in duly justified cases” inside the procurement statute itself.

How often a public or private managed-service contract for an IT / HR / facilities desk actually contains a BSL, Relay UK, live-chat or VRS line item: **no reliable figure found**. EHRC 2022 and practitioner blogs (e.g. Make Things Accessible, 25 November 2025) treat the omission as a known commercial risk. That is commentary.
