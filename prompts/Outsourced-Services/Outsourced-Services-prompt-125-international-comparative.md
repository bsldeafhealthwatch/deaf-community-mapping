# Prompt 125 — International comparative practice on outsourced internal-service accessibility

**Master index prompt 125.** Fifth file in the Outsourced / offshored internal service-channel batch (prompts 121–126). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** International context only. Identify any jurisdiction with a published procurement or accessibility standard that specifically requires outsourced / offshored *internal* corporate service contracts (IT support, HR shared services) to provide a non-voice-only channel, and any documented practice of building interpreter / video-relay cost into the service-desk contract as standard. None of it fills a UK data cell.

**Index check.** Distinct from prompt 80 (universal / portable VRS models) and prompt 118 (DeafBlind workplace CG models). This file is internal corporate service *contracts*.

## Headline

**International — do not fill a UK cell.**

The closest *binding* text located is **US Revised Section 508**, Chapter 6. Standard **603.3** requires ICT support services — “including, but not limited to, help desks, call centers, training services, and automated self-service technical support” — to “accommodate the communication needs of individuals with disabilities.” Support services must be provided directly or by referral. The duty attaches to federal agencies and, through FAR / agency clauses, to contractors delivering ICT on their behalf. It is an ICT-accessibility standard, not a private-employer employment statute, and it does not name ASL-VRS as the mandated channel.

**Canada.** Accessibility Standards Canada publishes procurement guides for accessible goods and services that explicitly ask whether the service will need “accessibility accommodation for end users (e.g., ASL interpretation, captioning, plain language formats)” and require a written justification if accessibility is omitted from a Government of Canada procurement (Directive on the Management of Procurement clauses 4.2.7.1 / 4.2.7.1.1 as cited in the ASC guide). That is federal procurement practice, not a private helpdesk statute.

**European Union (context for member states, not UK law).** Directive 2014/24/EU Article 42(1) — the parent of UK PCR 2015 regulation 42(8) — requires technical specifications for procurement intended for use by natural persons, including **staff of the contracting authority**, to take account of accessibility criteria or design for all, except in duly justified cases. CEN/CLC/ETSI TR 101 551 (2025/2026 revisions) walks contracting authorities through specifying ICT accessibility against the European Accessibility Act and the Web Accessibility Directive. The EAA itself is a products-and-services accessibility statute with 2025 application dates in the Union; it is not a UK duty.

**Australia.** Disability Discrimination Act reasonable-adjustment and government procurement guides exist (Queensland *Accessibility in procurement* guide is one published example). The Employment Assistance Fund can fund workplace interpreters and some equipment. Neither was found this pass as a national rule that a private employer’s outsourced IT desk must include a standing VRS line item.

**Documented practice of building interpreter / VRS cost into the service-desk *contract as standard*, rather than as an individual accommodation:** **not found** as a named national default in the USA, Canada, Australia or an EU member state on this pass. Section 508 603.3 is the nearest *standard*; how often federal helpdesk contracts price ASL-VRS as a standing item is unpublished here.

**Hard-don't.** Do not paste Section 508 603.3, ASC procurement checklists, Article 42(1) or EAF dollar caps into a UK cell. PCR 2015 regulation 42(8) was the UK’s own Article 42 descendant and is already logged in prompt 121 as a *legacy* rule.

## 125.1 What transfers as *context*, not as a cell

The useful comparison is structural. Several public-procurement systems tell the *buying* authority to write accessibility into specifications for things staff will use, and the United States goes one step further by naming help desks and call centres as ICT support services that must accommodate communication needs. The UK had the specification sentence (PCR 2015 regulation 42(8)); the Procurement Act 2023 moved the work onto the Equality Act / PSED (prompt 121). Nobody in the comparison set was found to have made “VRS minutes inside the managed-service price” the default commercial term for a private-sector staff desk.

## 125.2 What must not be pasted

- Section 508 603.3 as if it bound a UK LLP’s Indian helpdesk.
- Canadian justification-if-omitted rules as if they sat in the Procurement Act.
- EAA 2025 dates as UK commencement.
- Any EAF or ADA cost cap.
