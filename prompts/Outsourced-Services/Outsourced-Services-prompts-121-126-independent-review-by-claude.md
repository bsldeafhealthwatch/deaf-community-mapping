> Outsourcing the helpdesk does not switch off the employer's Equality Act duty. ## Don't list

- Do not print that a named employer, outsourcer or procurement contract is unlawful or in breach without a named statute, regulation or judgment on these exact facts — none exists in this batch.
- Do not print "the duty is discharged because we outsourced IT." Neither s.20 nor s.39 says that.
- Do not print "the contractor has no Equality Act duty" — s.31(5) can attach a Part 3 duty to a closed staff-only service; this is fact-specific, not automatic either way.
- Do not stretch Sch. 3 para 20 (pension/insurance carve-out) to cover occupational-scheme administration — the statute names personal pension schemes only.
- Do not treat PCR 2015 reg. 42(8) as governing every live 2026 public contract — new covered procurements since 24 Feb 2025 sit under the Procurement Act 2023, which did not copy the sentence across.
- Do not print "most UK staff helpdesks are voice-only" as a measured rate — there is no census, only absence of a published one.
- Do not treat Sign Solutions' or InterpretersLive!'s marketing copy as an official process or a documented coverage rate.
- Do not file customer-facing VRS deployments (Barnet, Three, Virgin Media, Fidelity) as evidence about staff-only desks — different object.
- Do not print Safecall's written-portal recommendation as "the UK standard" for reconciling VRS with whistleblower anonymity — it is one vendor's advice, and the anonymity problem itself remains unaddressed in any UK source found.
- Do not print "Protect offers BSL VRS" — Protect's own site shows phone plus a web form; the BSL sentence comes from a third-party (LawWorks) directory entry, not a Protect-published product page.
- Do not print AtW as a fund capable of varying a managed-service contract or funding a contractor-side VRS seat — no official text describes it that way.
- Do not present the AtW VRS product as available to civil servants — the introduction to the staff guide was edited 13 November 2025 to record that civil servants can no longer apply (see spot-check note: this is distinct from, and does not contradict, the underlying 1 April 2022 transfer of responsibility to individual departments — the November 2025 edit is a wording change to the introduction, not a new policy date).
- Do not reprint the £69,260 cap, BSL bands, or VRS bundle prices as discoveries of this batch — they were already verified in 20/52–54/116.
- Do not import Section 508 §603.3, Canadian federal procurement rules, EU Directive 2014/24 Art. 42(1), or the European Accessibility Act as if they were UK duties — international context only.
- Do not reuse Rimmer, Iqbal, Bentley, or other named individuals' testimony from neighbouring batches as if it were first-person evidence about an outsourced internal channel — none of the six prompts located any such account.

## Spot-check note (independent verification, this pass)

Two claims re-verified live against primary sources on 14 September 2026:

**Equality Act 2010 s.31(5).** Fetched legislation.gov.uk directly. The subsection reads exactly as the findings file states: "Where an employer arranges for another person to provide a service only to the employer's employees — (a) the employer is not to be regarded as the service-provider, but (b) the employees are to be regarded as a section of the public." Holds verbatim. This is the load-bearing gate for the entire batch's Part 3/Part 5 split and it is solid.

**AtW staff guide — civil-servant exclusion, dated 13 November 2025.** This needed a second look. The guide's own eligibility text states the underlying policy change took effect from **1 April 2022** ("all government departments are responsible for arranging workplace assessments and adjustments for their employees known as civil servants"), which reads as a much older change than "13 November 2025" suggested on first read. Fetching the publication's own update-history list resolved this: there is a distinct history entry dated 13 November 2025 reading "Introduction edited to show civil servants can no longer apply for Access to Work support" — separate from an earlier 22 August 2025 entry adding a section on "Identifying Civil Servants and Public Servants." So the findings file's claim holds verbatim as stated (an edit to the introduction's wording, logged 13 Nov 2025), but if this is ever used publicly, do not present 13 November 2025 as the date civil servants lost AtW eligibility — the underlying transfer of responsibility happened in April 2022; November 2025 is only when the guide's introduction was reworded to state it plainly. Recommend patching prompt 124 with this distinction so it isn't mis-cited later as "the 2025 exclusion."
