Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Test whether the same phone-first shape documented as a gap for IT desks in prompt 122 recurs across payroll / HR shared-service lines, facilities helpdesks, whistleblowing / ethics hotlines and pension / benefits administrator helplines. ## Headline

Across the four service types, **no UK provider or employer was found this pass that publishes a named, Deaf-specific route into the outsourced channel itself** — as opposed to a general employer “we will make reasonable adjustments” policy that may not bind the administrator.

**Whistleblowing / ethics.** This is the one cell with any published disability language, and it does **not** reconcile anonymity with video relay. Safecall’s 28 April 2026 accessibility blog tells organisations that for reporters who are deaf or hard of hearing they should “ensure that an accessible written channel — such as the online portal or a text-based relay service — is clearly communicated as an alternative.” It does not discuss BSL, a named VRS product, or the fact that a video interpreter sees and hears the report. Protect (the UK whistleblowing charity, not a commercial hotline) runs a phone advice line (020 3117 2520) plus an online contact form; a LawWorks directory entry (page dated 17 April 2026) states Protect “also provide translation services, including for BSL” if asked. That is the charity advice line, not an employer ethics hotline. GOV.UK’s 6 April 2026 employer whistleblowing guidance lists anonymity options as something a policy should mention; it does not mention BSL or VRS.

**Payroll / HR shared service and facilities helpdesks.** No named published BSL / VRS / Relay UK staff-channel protocol found this pass. CIPD public materials located here are general HR practice, not a shared-service accessibility specification.

**Pension / benefits administrator helplines.** Schedule 3 paragraph 20 is the legal fact that belongs in this cell: s.29 does not apply to employer-arranged personal-pension and insurance services. No named UK administrator accessibility statement for *Deaf employees using the helpline* was found. Do not treat a scheme’s public-facing WCAG statement as a staff-helpline VRS product.

**Hard-don't.** Do not write that written/text is “the standard workaround” for VRS-anonymity. Do not treat Protect’s BSL-on-request sentence as a commercial hotline specification. Do not apply Part 3 to an employer-arranged personal-pension helpline.

## 123.1 Four channels, one published sentence

The only UK sentence located that addresses deafness on an *outsourced ethics line* is Safecall’s: offer a written portal or text relay. That sentence treats deafness as a hearing-loss / telephony problem. It does not treat BSL as a language, and it does not notice the anonymity problem that video relay creates (a named interpreter is now a witness to the disclosure). Leaving that tension unresolved is the correct finding.

Protect can arrange BSL for *its own* advice calls if asked, according to a third-party directory. That does not bind Capita, Safecall, Navex or an in-house speak-up line.

## 123.2 Pensions are a different legal object

Prompt 121 already logged Schedule 3 paragraph 20. Repeating it here is deliberate: a Deaf employee ringing an employer-arranged personal-pension helpline does not pick up a Part 3 services claim against the administrator via s.31(5). Any claim about that call sits in Part 5 against the employer, or nowhere. Occupational-scheme administration is not named in paragraph 20; do not invent the extension.
