# Prompt 122 — What is the documented shape of Deaf / BSL access to a workplace IT helpdesk?

**Master index prompt 122.** Second file in the Outsourced / offshored internal service-channel batch (prompts 121–126). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Document whether any UK employer or IT-outsourcing provider has published a named process for a Deaf or BSL-using *employee* to reach IT support other than by voice telephone, and whether any published case records a lockout / password-reset failure because the only live channel was voice. This is expected to be thin. Do not treat customer-facing VRS (banks, councils, NHS 111) as evidence about an employer’s *staff* desk.

**Index check.** Read through prompt 121: the employer’s Part 5 duty is not switched off by the outsourcing contract. This file is practice, not a breach finding.

---

## Headline

Workplace IT-helpdesk accessibility for a Deaf *employee* is **almost unpublished**. ITIL and related service-desk standards do not contain a BSL, Relay UK or video-relay requirement. AbilityNet and Business Disability Forum publish extensively on accessible *products*, inclusive *procurement of technology*, and customer-facing channels; they do not publish a measured UK rate of staff-desk voice-only operation, and they do not publish a named employer protocol for a Deaf employee locked out of the very portal that would let them raise a ticket.

What exists on the supply side is a **commercial product**, not a documented employer standard. Sign Solutions states on its “Recruiting Deaf employees” page that Access to Work “will usually fund” in-person and video-remote interpreters, and that the firm “can also support organisations in making their employee helplines accessible to their Deaf employees by adding a video relay service link on their intranet pages.” InterpretersLive! is the named on-demand product (public hours commonly advertised 08:00–20:00, seven days). That is a vendor offer. It is not evidence of how many UK staff desks have bought it.

Public-sector *portals* are a different object. The 2018 Public Sector Bodies Accessibility Regulations cover intranets. A WCAG-conformant ticket form still fails the employee who cannot log in because the password-reset step is a phone call to an offshore desk.

**Named published case of a Deaf employee unable to clear an IT lockout because the live-support channel was voice-only: not found.** The lockout-blocks-the-ticket scenario is a coherent operational risk. It is not a documented judgment or charity case study on this pass.

**Hard-don't.** Do not print “most UK helpdesks are voice-only” as a measured rate. Do not treat Sign Solutions’ sales page as an official process. Do not file customer-facing VRS (Barnet, Three, Virgin Media, Fidelity — all on the same Sign Solutions clients page) as staff-desk evidence.

## 122.1 Two different objects

A WCAG-conformant staff portal and a reachable live desk are not the same thing. The 2018 regulations, where they apply, discipline the first. The second is an Equality Act / contract-design question (prompt 121) with almost no published operational literature.

The commercially available workaround is the employee-side VRS minute (Access to Work remote-BSL bundles already logged in prompt 116) or an employer-bought InterpretersLive! / SignVideo / Convo button on the intranet. Whether the *outsourced agent* on the other end will accept a relay call, has a process to pause the script, and has authority to complete a password reset via a third party, is unpublished.

## 122.2 Why the lockout scenario matters even with no case

If the only way to raise a ticket is to be logged in, and the only way to get back in is to speak to a voice desk, a Deaf employee is in a closed loop. That loop is the operational content of the batch anchor. It remains a documented-risk statement, not a documented-incident statement, until a named source appears.
