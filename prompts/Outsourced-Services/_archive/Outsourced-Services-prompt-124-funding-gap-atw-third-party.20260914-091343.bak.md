# Prompt 124 — Can Access to Work or anyone else’s budget pay for access to an outsourced internal channel?

**Master index prompt 124.** Load-bearing file in the Outsourced / offshored internal service-channel batch (prompts 121–126). Compiled 11 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment; do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. An unsourced AI claim is the lowest tier. Gaps are logged as gaps, not filled guesses.

**What this prompt is for.** Test the batch premise: that “no budget for a third party” is a structural procurement gap Access to Work was never designed to plug. Separate (a) AtW funding the *employee’s* interpreter or VRS minutes from (b) anyone funding a retrofit of the *contractor’s* service. Log the gap itself as the finding if no documented route closes it.

**Index check.** Cap, BSL bands and VRS bundles are already verified in prompts 20, 52–54 and 116. Do not reopen the freeze debate. Civil Service exclusion from AtW (staff-guide update 13 November 2025) belongs in this cell because many of the largest UK outsourced desks sit behind public employers.

---

## Headline

Access to Work **can** fund support the *employee* uses to make a workplace telephone or video call — the staff guide’s own example is a deaf customer using remote BSL interpreting “for phone calls,” with VRS defined as the customer, the person they are communicating with and the interpreter in three different places. Published remote-BSL bundles (inc. VAT) run 60 minutes **£105** to 1,125 minutes **£1,480**. Cost share does **not** apply to support workers on the live employer factsheet (updated 7 October 2025). That is funding for the individual, inside one cap of **£69,260** (frozen to 31 March 2027).

Access to Work is **not** described, in any official page opened this pass, as a grant that pays to vary a managed-service contract, stand up a VRS seat on an offshore desk, train the contractor’s agents, or create a budget line the contractor can draw on when a Deaf employee rings. Sign Solutions’ employer page says AtW “will usually fund” interpreters and that the firm can add a VRS link to employee helplines / intranet pages — vendor language, not DWP language.

**Civil servants cannot apply.** The staff-guide HTML was edited on 13 November 2025 so the introduction shows civil servants can no longer apply for Access to Work support. For that workforce the “AtW will pay the VRS minutes” route is closed; any channel access sits on the employer / contractor side only.

**Named case in which an employer or contractor cited “no budget for a third party” as the reason for refusing an accessible internal channel: not found.** RNID/DeafATW 2026 and AWS0095 / AWS0121 are about AtW delays, cap pressure and interpreter pay — not about an outsourced helpdesk budget line. Sense ATW0075 (2014) is a DeafBlind cost/rarity cluster already logged in prompt 116, not a helpdesk-contract case.

The **gap is the finding.** There is a product that can sit on the employee (AtW VRS minutes, where the employee is eligible and the award includes them). There is a product that can sit on the employer intranet (a bought VRS button — Sign Solutions, SignVideo, Convo). There is no official product that sits on the *contractor’s* side of an already-priced managed-service desk. When the contract was scoped without that line item, nobody in the live call has a budget code. That is a procurement-design fact, not a documented breach.

**Hard-don't.** Do not print the gap as unlawful. Do not print AtW as a contractor-infrastructure fund. Do not treat 2014 Sense evidence or 2026 AtW-backlog evidence as helpdesk-budget evidence.

## 124.1 Three wallets, one empty

1. **Employee wallet — AtW.** Minutes and hours for *that person*. Works if the employee has an award, the award lists VRS or BSL, the cap has headroom, and the person is not a civil servant. Does not change the contractor’s script.
2. **Employer wallet — reasonable adjustment / contract change.** The employer can buy a VRS button, open a written desk, or vary the managed-service contract so the supplier must accept relay and complete the transaction. Cost lands on the employer unless the original tender already priced it.
3. **Contractor wallet — not priced.** If the statement of work has no accessibility line, the agent has no code to book an interpreter and no authority to spend. That is the “no budget for a third party” objection. It is a description of how the contract was written, not a published confession by a named firm.

Wallet 3 is the structural gap. Wallets 1 and 2 can paper over individual calls. They do not convert a phone-first offshore desk into an accessible channel for the next Deaf employee.

## 124.2 Civil Service is a closed door on wallet 1

The 13 November 2025 staff-guide edit matters for this batch more than for general AtW work. A large share of the UK’s outsourced payroll, IT and facilities desks sit behind government and NHS employers. Where the worker is a civil servant, AtW is not available as the standing interpreter for those desks. The channel problem becomes a pure employer/contractor specification problem — which is prompt 121 plus this gap, with wallet 1 removed.
