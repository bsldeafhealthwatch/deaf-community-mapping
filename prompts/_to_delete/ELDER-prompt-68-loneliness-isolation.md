## Prompt text

As a researcher specializing in ageing, disability, and social isolation, using Age UK research and loneliness campaign publications, Independent Age, the Campaign to End Loneliness, SignHealth and RNID research on Deaf isolation, ONS or DHSC data on loneliness by disability status, and any peer-reviewed research on Deaf ageing, identify what is documented specifically about loneliness and social isolation among older BSL-using Deaf and DeafBlind people in the UK — distinct from general older-age loneliness research and distinct from working-age Deaf isolation research. Cover: (1) any survey or study that measures loneliness prevalence or severity among older Deaf/DeafBlind people specifically, rather than folding them into a general "hearing loss" or "disabled elderly" category, (2) the specific mechanisms driving isolation for this group — loss of a Deaf social network as peers die or move into hearing-staffed care, inability to use mainstream older-people's day centres or lunch clubs run entirely in spoken English, transport and travel barriers to reach BSL-using social groups that may be borough- or region-wide rather than local, digital exclusion from video-call socialising if a person is also losing vision (DeafBlind), (3) any documented Deaf social clubs, Deaf churches, or Deaf senior groups that closed, shrank, or lost funding in the last ten years, and what (if anything) replaced them, (4) any charity, NHS, or local-authority programme specifically designed to reduce isolation for older Deaf or DeafBlind people, with evidence of whether it worked. For each source, give title, author/organisation, date, and URL. Flag clearly where evidence is thin or where researchers have simply merged Deaf elders into a general "hard of hearing older adults" statistic that obscures the BSL-specific picture.

> Format your answer as a table with these exact columns: **Label | Value | Source name | Source URL | Publication date | Confidence (Verified/Contested/No reliable figure found)**.
