# Prompt 165 — Household-Leave addendum: does the statutory toolkit track a disabled dependant's actual developmental continuity past 16/18, or only their chronological age?

Added: 14 September 2026. Covers a gap the original 150–157 batch did not test.

Checked first: prompt 150 (gate) confirmed unpaid parental leave, including its disabled-child whole-week exception (MAPLE 1999 Sch. 2 para. 7), is "exercisable before the child's 18th birthday" — an explicit chronological-age cutoff. Prompt 150 also confirmed carer's leave and s.57A turn on "dependant" status (a class, not an age band) rather than a stated age limit, but the batch never confirmed that reading against the statute text itself, and never asked what happens for a young person who is a legal adult (16+, or 18+) but remains, in practice, dependent on parental support because of a disability that produces developmental delay or later maturation — a common pattern given many EHCPs run to age 25. This is not a duplicate of 150 (which fixed the *rights and their units*, not the *dependant's age boundary*) or of 156/157 (lived experience / workplace-caused consequence, which did not raise this angle). Distinct from 66–71 (Deaf/DeafBlind older people — the employee as the person ageing) and 72–77 (CODA — a hearing or Deaf-of-Deaf child of Deaf adults, not a disabled child of any employee). Addendum to the Household-Leave batch (150–157), not a new numbered batch or Domain.

Anchor: a Deaf or BSL-using employee whose child has an EHCP running to age 25 and a disability producing significant developmental delay — the child turns 16, then 18, and is chronologically an adult, but the employee's caring role, appointment attendance, and decision-making involvement continue largely unchanged. Does any of the four statutory rights, or Article 8, recognise that continuity, or does the toolkit's protection narrow or vanish at the same chronological point the child's actual need does not?

## Notes for this addendum

- Gate sub-question: confirm directly against ERA 1996 s.57A and the Carer's Leave Regulations 2024 (SI 2024/251) whether "dependant" (in each right separately — they are not defined identically) has any age ceiling at all, and if none is stated, say so as a finding, not an assumption carried over from parental leave's 18-year cutoff.
- Still-alive scepticism: EHCP-to-25 continuation is itself conditional (annual review, local authority discretion to end early) — do not treat "runs to 25" as automatic or uniform across councils.
- Hard-don't: do not print that any employer's treatment of an over-16/18 disabled dependant as "no longer a dependant" is unlawful, or that it is lawful, without a named statute, regulation, or judgment on point. Do not import SEND/EHCP transition-to-adulthood commentary as if it were employment-law authority — it usually addresses the young person's own rights (education, care, benefits), not the parent's employment-leave position.
- Held-back leg: this addendum does not reopen 150's core "no stacking recognised" finding — that stands regardless of the dependant's age. It also does not test Scotland/Wales/NI variation; if England-only sources are thin, say so rather than substituting another jurisdiction's rule.
