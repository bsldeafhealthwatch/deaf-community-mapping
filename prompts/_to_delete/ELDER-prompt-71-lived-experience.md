## Prompt text

As a researcher documenting health and social-care inequality for older Deaf and DeafBlind people, using Deaf and DeafBlind advocacy organisation reports (British Deaf Association, SignHealth, RNID, Deafblind UK, Sense, Age UK), CQC and local government/social-care ombudsman investigations, academic qualitative research, and first-person or advocacy-published accounts, identify documented cases and testimony of older Deaf or DeafBlind people (or their families) experiencing harm, distress, isolation, or loss of dignity in retirement, care home, or later-life settings due to communication access failures. Include: (1) any formal complaints, safeguarding referrals, or ombudsman findings where lack of BSL/tactile access contributed to harm, neglect, or prolonged isolation for an older person, (2) advocacy organisations' own calls for reform specifically on ageing and later-life care (as distinct from working-age or end-of-life-specific asks already covered in this project's other research), (3) any research or testimony on what it feels like to lose access to a Deaf peer network in old age — bereavement of Deaf friends, moving into a hearing-run care setting, the fear of "going back into the hearing world" after a lifetime signing, (4) examples — anywhere in the world — of what meaningful improvement has looked like for older Deaf/DeafBlind people specifically, so this isn't only a catalogue of failure. For each source, give title, organisation/author, date, and URL. Where testimony is anonymised or aggregated, note that clearly rather than presenting it as a named case.
