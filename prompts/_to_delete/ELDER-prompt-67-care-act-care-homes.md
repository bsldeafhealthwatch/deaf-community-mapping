## Prompt text

As a UK social-care policy researcher, using the Care Act 2014 statutory guidance, CQC inspection reports and thematic reviews, ADASS (Association of Directors of Adult Social Services) publications, SignHealth's residential care work (including SignHealth Longley Road), and any local-authority safeguarding or ombudsman findings, identify the specific barriers BSL-using Deaf and DeafBlind older people face when undergoing a Care Act needs assessment, choosing a care home or extra-care housing placement, and living day-to-day inside CQC-regulated residential and nursing care. Cover: (1) whether local authorities' Care Act needs assessments for older Deaf/DeafBlind adults document BSL or tactile communication needs as a matter of course, or only when a family member pushes for it, (2) how many CQC-registered care homes in England (and specifically within the South West London ICB footprint — Croydon, Kingston, Merton, Richmond, Sutton, Wandsworth) are BSL-specialist or Deaf-led versus mainstream homes with no BSL-fluent staff, (3) documented cases of an older Deaf or DeafBlind resident isolated inside a mainstream care home because no staff member or fellow resident could sign with them, (4) any CQC inspection report, safeguarding review, or ombudsman finding that names communication isolation as a factor in a care home's rating or in a specific resident's harm. For each source, give title, publishing body, date, and direct URL.
