## Prompt text

As a researcher on disability and transport access, using Department for Transport accessibility guidance, Transport for London / local transport authority accessibility policy for the SWL boroughs, Disabled Persons Railcard and bus pass scheme documentation, and Deaf and DeafBlind advocacy research on travel, identify what is documented about the specific travel and mobility barriers facing older BSL-using Deaf and DeafBlind people trying to reach medical appointments, social groups, places of worship, or family — as distinct from general disabled-passenger accessibility research, which is dominated by wheelchair/mobility-impairment findings rather than communication-access findings. Cover: (1) whether BSL or visual-information provision (departure boards, platform announcements, disruption alerts) at stations and on buses in the SWL area is documented anywhere as adequate or inadequate for Deaf passengers, (2) DeafBlind-specific travel barriers — guiding, tactile wayfinding, communicating with drivers or station staff — and whether any communicator-guide or travel-buddy scheme exists and is funded in South West London or comparable areas, (3) any research connecting transport/travel barriers directly to the isolation findings in prompt 68 — i.e., older Deaf people who want to attend a Deaf club or BSL church but cannot physically get there, (4) any local-authority or charity-run transport scheme (dial-a-ride, community transport) that has been adapted or is documented as failing to adapt for Deaf/DeafBlind users specifically. For each source, give title, publishing body, date, and URL.
