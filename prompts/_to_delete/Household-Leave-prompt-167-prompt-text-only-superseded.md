# Prompt 167 — Household-Leave addendum: emergency time off for a parent on DLA/PIP, statutory cover (or its absence) at end of life, and the employee's own PIP support needs during the same crisis

Added: 14 September 2026. Covers a gap neither the original 150–157 batch, nor addenda 165 or 166, tested.

Checked first: prompt 150 (gate) and addendum 165 already establish that "dependant" for carer's leave (ERA 1996 s.80J) and time off for dependants (s.57A) includes a parent, with no age qualifier and — on the face of the text — no requirement that the dependant be in receipt of any particular disability benefit. Prompt 150 also already found that the four statutory rights do not recognise "stacking" — each is a separate machine, assessed per right, not aggregated across however many family members are disabled. Addendum 166 tested DWP benefit-eligibility rules for Carer's Allowance between two partners, not an employee's own employment-leave rights when the dependant is a parent. The end-of-life/palliative-care brief (`deaf-deafblind-eol-palliative-care-brief.md`) researched the *patient's* NHS, hospice and DWP Special Rules access — it did not touch the *employee's* workplace leave position at all. This prompt sits in the gap between those files: an employee (any contract length, including full-time 35 hours) who is themselves in receipt of PIP and relies on support from their spouse, whose parent is separately in receipt of DLA or PIP, who receives emergency/crisis calls about that parent, in a case where the parent is at the end of life — and where every person in the household (employee, spouse, parent) is Deaf.

Anchor: an employee on a standard 35-hour contract who is themselves a PIP claimant relying on support from their husband or wife to manage day-to-day life. That employee's parent separately receives DLA or PIP for a long-term condition. The parent's condition deteriorates and enters a terminal/end-of-life phase. The employee starts receiving unpredictable emergency calls (a fall, a hospital admission, a crisis deterioration) and needs to leave work at short notice, repeatedly, over a period of weeks — and because the employee's own PIP-related support needs do not pause during a family emergency, the spouse's presence (interpreting, mobility support, managing the employee's own needs while also trying to help with the parent) becomes part of how the employee actually copes with each crisis. All three — employee, spouse, parent — are Deaf, so hospital and DWP contact during every one of these emergencies also has to clear BSL access (already researched generally in the AIS and EOL/palliative-care files, not re-opened here except where it bears directly on the employee's own leave position). Five sub-questions: (1) does the parent's DLA/PIP award have any bearing on the employee's own eligibility for s.57A or carer's leave, or can an employer lawfully ask for it as evidence before granting leave; (2) if the parent dies, is there any statutory right to bereavement leave for the death of a parent in Great Britain, as distinct from time off to attend a dying parent beforehand; (3) as crisis episodes repeat over an extended terminal decline, does s.57A's undefined "reasonable" amount of time off have any practical ceiling, and could an employer argue that once decline is expected, further episodes are no longer "unexpected" and fall outside s.57A; (4) does the parent's own DWP Special Rules for End of Life (SREL) status, if awarded, have any bearing on the employee's own employment-leave rights; (5) does the employee's own PIP award, or their reliance on their spouse's support to attend these same emergencies, have any bearing on the employee's own s.57A/carer's-leave eligibility or on the "reasonable" amount of time needed, and — separately — if the spouse is also employed, does the spouse hold any independent statutory right to their own time off to support the employee (as the spouse's dependant) during the same emergency, alongside whatever the employee is separately taking for the parent.

## Notes for this addendum

- Gate sub-question: check ERA 1996 s.57A(1)-(2) and ACAS's time-off-for-dependants guidance directly for any evidence requirement tied to the dependant's own benefit status, rather than assuming "no bearing" from the absence of an age qualifier alone (that absence was about age, not evidence). Check the same question for the *employee's own* PIP award — does the statute or guidance treat a PIP-claiming employee any differently when they are the one requesting time off, as opposed to the one being cared for.
- Still-alive scepticism: "bereavement leave" is used loosely in employer handbooks and in the press to mean anything from a paid discretionary day to the statutory Parental Bereavement Leave right — do not let general "bereavement leave" commentary stand in for the specific, narrower statutory right, which (unless this prompt finds otherwise) covers only the death of a child under 18 or a stillbirth.
- Hard-don't: do not print that there is, or is not, a statutory right to bereavement leave for a parent's death without a named Act, regulation, or ACAS Code citation. Do not print that an employer's request for benefit evidence, or their treatment of repeated crisis leave as "no longer unexpected," is lawful or unlawful without a named statute, regulation, or tribunal/EAT judgment. Do not import general end-of-life or bereavement charity guidance as if it stated employment-law entitlement — trace it to a right or say it is discretionary/good-practice only. Do not treat "the household is a multi-disabled Deaf family" as itself creating any enhanced or stacked right — prompt 150's no-stacking finding stands here too; this prompt only asks whether the *employee's own* PIP status and the *spouse's own independent* dependant right change anything, not whether disability density in the household does.
- Held-back leg: this prompt does not reopen the EOL/palliative-care brief's NHS/hospice/DWP findings about BSL access at hospital or DWP contact points, and does not reopen 150–152's general stacking findings — it stays narrowly on three linked employment-leave questions (the parent's benefit status, the employee's own PIP status, and the spouse's independent dependant right), touching SREL and BSL access only to the extent either has a direct, documented bearing on the employee-side leave position.
