# Prompt 57 — Complaints and escalation for HMRC communication-access failures

**Master index prompt 57.** Third file in the HMRC and BSL/Deaf access batch (prompts 55–61). Compiled 9 September 2026. Read with 55 (channels) and 56 (Extra Support routing). Dossier rule: stay unbiased; an absence from the public record is a finding, not a hole to fill.

**What this prompt is for.** Mirror the named-case method that made the EOL brief usable (Alan Graham, Mrs W, Samantha). If no HMRC Deaf/BSL equivalent exists, write that down. Do not recycle an NHS PHSO case as an HMRC one.

## Headline

No published Adjudicator’s Office or PHSO finding was found that **names a Deaf, DeafBlind or BSL-using complainant and an HMRC communication-access failure**. That is the parallel-to-Alan-Graham result: the public record does not currently give this project a named HMRC BSL case.

The nearest official cases sit next door, not on the point:

- **Elliott** (PHSO *Spotlight on accessible communication*, August 2026): self-employed man with **dyslexia and ADHD**, not Deaf/BSL. HMRC already knew he needed accessible contact (audio documents). It failed to return calls for more than a year on his SEISS grant claim. Adjudicator first; then PHSO. Grants approved after the Adjudicator pushed the case to HMRC’s discretionary board. PHSO required an apology, **£1,500** for distress, and a service-improvement undertaking for people with communication requirements.
- **Samantha** in the same PHSO spotlight is the NHS GP wrong-vaccine BSL case already on the project’s AIS list. Not HMRC.
- **Melanie Morris** (Limping Chicken, 29 September 2014): published extracts of a Deaf mother’s letter about stopped child tax credits and HMRC refusing a third-party phone call. A contemporaneous complaint letter, not an Adjudicator or PHSO finding.

HMRC publishes complaint **volumes and uphold rates by tier**, not a “communication access” or “reasonable adjustments” category. Escalation is **service complaint** (Tier 1 → Tier 2 → Adjudicator → PHSO via MP), which is a different track from a **tax-decision / penalty appeal** (usually 30 days, then statutory review, then First-tier Tribunal). Mixing the two delays the tax appeal. No published tribunal or court case was found that waived a self-employed tax penalty specifically because HMRC missed BSL provision.

## 57.2 HMRC published complaints data — no communication-access category

What is published:

- **Volumes and uphold rates by tier**, monthly, in HMRC performance updates. Example, April–August 2025 year-to-date: Tier 1 receipts 34,521 (fully upheld 26.5%, partially 19.2%, not upheld 54.3%); Tier 2 receipts 1,948 (fully 14.8%, partially 37.7%, not 47.6%). No breakdown by subject.  
  https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/hmrc-performance-update-august-2025/performance-report-2025-to-2026-august-2025-and-quarter-1
- **Adjudicator intake.** 2,216 HMRC/VOA complaints received 2024–25 (2,239 the year before). Full investigations up almost 40% (787 → 1,097). Premature complaints down 24%. Four-month service standard met in 76% of 2024–25 cases; **53%** in April–December 2025.  
  https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-adjudicators-office-annual-report-2025/the-adjudicators-office-annual-report-2025  
  https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/adjudicators-office-performance-against-our-service-standard/adjudicators-office-performance-against-our-service-standard
- HMRC response 26 March 2026: average complaint resolution time **36 days → 27 days**. Still no category split.
- Secondary write-up of FOI redress data (Contentious Tax Group / taxinvestigation.co, Feb 2026): ~94,000 formal complaints a year by 2024/25; redress spend about £1.9m. Still no access category. Treat as secondary.

What is **not** published: a table headed communication access, reasonable adjustments, BSL, Deaf, or extra support. Charter reporting in the 2024–25 annual report scores “being responsive” low (3.0/10) and talks about delay and first-contact resolution. That is not a BSL series.

**Finding for (2):** HMRC measures complaints as a service-quality volume, not as an Equality Act access series. A “communication-access failure rate” cannot be read off the published stats.

## 57.3 Escalation ladder — HMRC is not NHS

NHS uses PALS / local complaint / PHSO (health). HMRC uses a four-rung **service-complaint** ladder, and a separate **tax-appeal** ladder. A BSL-access failure that also produced a late-filing penalty needs both tracks, started in parallel.

### Service complaint (how HMRC treated you)

| Stage | Who | Published target / window | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| First point of contact / informal | Helpline supervisor, Extra Support, webchat | None published | LITRG: try a manager first on tax credits. Not a required step |
| **Tier 1** (first review) | HMRC complaints caseworker | **No target printed** on the live `gov.uk/complain-about-hmrc` page (public-updated 10 Oct 2024; HTML touched 9 Sep 2026). Older practitioner memory of “15 working days then 6 weeks” is **not on the current page**. HMRC now quotes an *average* resolution of 27 days (Mar 2026 response), which is an outturn not a customer right | Online (Government Gateway), phone or post. Agents cannot use the individual online form. Tell them if you need extra support |
| **Tier 2** (second review) | Different HMRC handler | No published target | Final HMRC view. Do not keep writing inside HMRC after this |
| **Adjudicator’s Office** | Independent of HMRC; investigates HMRC and VOA | Accepts normally **within 6 months** of the Tier 2 letter (exceptional later). Service standard: resolve **within 4 months** of receipt. Met 76% in 2024–25; **53%** Apr–Dec 2025 | Online form or post. “If you need any help or a reasonable adjustment when dealing with us, please let us know.” Phone 0300 057 1111, Mon–Fri 09:00–17:00 |
| **PHSO** | Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman | Must go **through an MP**. PHSO will accept BSL for *its own* process (DeafATW guidance; PHSO access pages). That is PHSO’s access, not HMRC’s | Last rung for service. Cannot reopen the tax figure |

### Tax decision / penalty (whether the penalty stands)

Separate from the service complaint. Usual window **30 days from the date on the penalty or decision letter**, not from the day it was understood. Late appeal needs a reason. Then HMRC view, optional statutory review, then First-tier Tribunal (Tax Chamber). Reasonable excuse is argued on this track.  
https://www.gov.uk/tax-appeals/penalty  
https://www.gov.uk/tax-appeals/print

LITRG: you cannot appeal staff behaviour through the tribunal; that is the complaints track. Disability *can* be a reasonable excuse for a missed filing or payment, but that is a merits argument, not an automatic waiver.  
https://www.litrg.org.uk/tax-nic/getting-help-hub/getting-tax-help-if-you-have-additional-needs

Charter: if the complaint is that HMRC missed a Charter standard (“being aware of your personal situation”), name the Charter in the complaint.  
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/hmrc-charter/the-hmrc-charter

## 57.4 Self-employed penalty overturned because of a communication-access failure

**No published case found** in which a self-employed or freelance person’s tax penalty was waived or overturned *specifically because* HMRC missed BSL provision or left no accessible route to lodge a reasonable excuse in time.

Closest published items, and why they do not fill the gap:

- **Elliott / SEISS grants.** Self-employed. Communication-access failure (unreturned calls despite a known accessible-format need). Outcome was grant approval plus £1,500 distress, not a late-filing or late-payment penalty waiver, and not BSL.
- **LITRG Joyce example** (prompt 56). Extra Support cancelled late-filing penalties for a self-employed cleaner with a reasonable excuse (family illness). Not a communication-access case and not Deaf.
- **Reasonable excuse manuals** (CH160900, CH160300). Communication difficulty is a *referral trigger* to Extra Support, not a listed example of reasonable excuse. Disability can support a reasonable-excuse argument if it actually prevented compliance and the person remedied the failure once the excuse ended. That is general law, not a BSL precedent.
- **Equality Act cases against HMRC** found in this pass are employment-tribunal staff cases (Linsley parking; Bentley homeworking), not taxpayer BSL-access service cases. *Everill Hyre v Border Force* [2026] UKFTT is an import-duty hearing-aid relief case, not a BSL contact failure.

Melanie Morris 2014 is the only published first-person Deaf/HMRC service letter found. It is about tax-credit stoppage and a refused third-party call, not a documented penalty waiver.

## How to use this in a guide page

- Quote **Elliott** only with the disability named (dyslexia/ADHD) and the remedy (£1,500 + grant approval). Do not caption it as a Deaf case.
- Quote **Melanie Morris 2014** only as a dated first-person letter, with the Limping Chicken URL, not as current official practice.
- Tell a self-employed reader to run **two clocks**: 30 days on the penalty letter, and the service-complaint ladder for the access failure. One letter can do both if it is explicit.
- Do not invent an HMRC-BSL ombudsman citation. The absence is the citable fact for this batch.
