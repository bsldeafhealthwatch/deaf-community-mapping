## Headline

**No BSL crypto-tax product was found.** Not on GOV.UK, not on HMRC’s YouTube channel, not on RAD’s HMRC topic list, not from LITRG, TaxAid, BDA, RNID or SignHealth. HMRC’s own crypto video (*Cryptoassets and paying tax*, 24 September 2024) is spoken English. The “Pay tax on cryptoassets” page gained a **Welsh** translation on 29 April 2026. There is no matching BSL version of that page, of the disclosure service, or of the CARF user-information guide.

That gap sits next to a regime that is already live and about to get a data feed:

- Individuals already pay **Capital Gains Tax** on disposals and **Income Tax / NIC** on tokens received as employment, mining, staking or airdrops.
- The dedicated **Cryptoasset Disclosure Service** is the route for unpaid tax from earlier years. After you submit, HMRC writes with a payment reference; pay in **30 days**. If the letter does not arrive in **15 working days**, the published contact is a **telephone** helpline: **03000 55 22 94**. No SignVideo host, no textphone and no Relay UK example are printed on that page.
- **CARF** (Cryptoasset Reporting Framework) commenced in the UK on **1 January 2026**. UK reporting cryptoasset service providers must collect user identity (name, date of birth, address, NI / UTR) and transaction data. First report to HMRC: **1 January–31 May 2027**, covering calendar 2026. Users who give inaccurate details, or none, to a UK provider face a penalty of **up to £300**. Undeclared tax HMRC later finds can carry a penalty of **up to 100%** of the tax plus interest; offshore matters can be higher.
- From **6 April 2027**, eligible stablecoins are due to be treated more like money (CGT exemption on disposal; interest-like returns as savings income). That change is a policy paper dated 13 July 2026, not yet a BSL explainer.

A BSL-first person with historic crypto gains therefore faces the same Extra Support / SignVideo / RAD doors as any other tax problem (prompts 55–56), plus a disclosure helpline that is published as voice-only, plus CARF data that will start landing on HMRC’s desk in 2027. There is no crypto-specific accessible channel.

## 61.1 What the law and the live pages actually say

### Everyday tax on crypto (individuals)

HMRC Cryptoassets Manual CRYPTO20050 (updated 28 November 2025): most individuals hold tokens as an investment and pay **CGT on disposal**. Income Tax and NIC apply to tokens received from employment, mining, transaction confirmation or airdrops. Trading as a business is “likely to be unusual”; if it is a trade, Income Tax takes priority.  
https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/cryptoassets-manual/crypto20050

Public guides:

- Check if you need to pay tax when you **sell**: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-pay-tax-when-you-sell-cryptoassets  
  Report on Self Assessment (cryptoasset section from 2024–25 onwards) or the CGT real-time service. Older unpaid years: Cryptoasset Disclosure Service.
- Check if you need to pay tax when you **receive** tokens (employment, mining, staking, DeFi): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-pay-tax-when-you-receive-cryptoassets  
  Updated 28 April 2025.

HMRC YouTube, 24 September 2024, *Cryptoassets and paying tax* (English, 3:31). Tags include the disclosure service. No BSL track.  
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJtKRHGV4iE

Stablecoins policy, 13 July 2026: eligible stablecoins more like money from **6 April 2027** (individuals/trustees) / 1 April 2027 (companies). CGT exemption on eligible-stablecoin disposals; interest-like returns as savings income.  
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tax-treatment-of-stablecoins/taxation-of-stablecoins

Loans and liquidity pools policy, same day: certain movements treated as no gain / no loss from 6 April 2027. HMRC’s own impact line: about **700,000** individuals in those arrangements.  
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cryptoasset-loans-and-liquidity-pools/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-loans-and-liquidity-pools

None of those pages, the manual, or the policy papers is published in BSL. The stablecoin draft legislation bundle includes an “accessible version” HTML of the draft clauses — that is a document-accessibility step, not a BSL product.

### Cryptoasset Disclosure Service

https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tell-hmrc-about-unpaid-tax-on-cryptoassets  
Published 29 November 2023.

Covers exchange tokens (e.g. bitcoin), NFTs and utility tokens. Current-year figures still go on the Self Assessment return. The disclosure is for unpaid tax you work out yourself, plus interest and a self-assessed penalty. HMRC may reject the penalty if it thinks the percentage is too low.

After submit:

- HMRC writes with a 14-character payment reference starting with X.
- If no confirmation in **15 working days**, contact the helpline.
- Pay the full amount within **30 days** of the disclosure.  
  https://www.gov.uk/guidance/pay-tax-on-cryptoassets (Welsh added 29 April 2026)

Helpline printed on both pages: **03000 55 22 94**, Monday–Thursday 09:00–17:00, Friday 09:00–16:30. No Relay UK example, no textphone, no SignVideo URL on those two pages.

The wider “How to make a voluntary disclosure” guide (updated 8 April 2026) added cryptoassets as a disclosure route on 6 August 2024 and added a pointer to extra support on the same date. Extra support here is the general product in prompts 55–56, not a crypto BSL desk.  
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/hmrc-your-guide-to-making-a-disclosure

Contractual Disclosure Facility is the fraud-admission product. Do not send a BSL user there for an ordinary missed CGT return.

### CARF — data collection already running

UK commencement: **1 January 2026**. SI 2025/744.  
https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/744  
HMRC manual IEIM8000050: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/international-exchange-of-information/ieim8000050

What a **user** must give every cryptoasset service provider they use (including non-UK providers): full name, date of birth, address and country of residence, tax identification number (NI or UTR if UK).  
https://www.gov.uk/guidance/information-youll-need-to-give-to-uk-cryptoasset-service-providers  
Updated 1 January 2026. Same update added the user-facing penalty lines below.

What a **UK reporting provider** must do: collect user and transaction data; first XML report to HMRC **1 January–31 May 2027** for calendar 2026; register with an online service that was still “not live yet” on the 1 January 2026 guidance.  
https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-youll-need-to-report-cryptoasset-data-to-hmrc  
https://www.gov.uk/guidance/reporting-cryptoasset-user-and-transaction-data (file size limit added 3 June 2026)

Practical meaning for a Deaf taxpayer: from 2027 HMRC will have exchange-reported identity and transaction summaries to match against Self Assessment. “HMRC does not know” is a shrinking defence. The user-information page is written in GOV.UK English. It is not BSL.

### Penalties (user-facing figures HMRC has printed)

On the CARF user-information page (1 January 2026 update):

- Inaccurate details, or no details, to a **UK** service provider: penalty **up to £300**. Non-UK providers: “the penalty could be higher.”
- Tax HMRC later finds unpaid: penalty **up to 100% of the tax due plus interest**. “For offshore matters or offshore transfers, the penalty could be much higher.”

On the disclosure page:

- Lookback: up to **20 years** if the failure was deliberate.
- Daily interest from the due date.
- Penalty percentage taken from the inaccuracies / failure-to-notify factsheets, then reduced for disclosure quality. Unprompted and prompted ranges differ. Offshore categories go above 100%.

HMRC does not publish a “crypto penalty” table separate from the general Schedule 24 / failure-to-notify ranges. Do not invent one.

CARF **provider** penalties (SI 2025/744) are a different stack: failure to report up to £5,000 then £600/day; inaccurate reports up to £100 per affected user. Those hit exchanges, not the individual BSL user, except as a reason the exchange will chase identity data harder.

## 61.3 What a guide page can tell a BSL-first reader without inventing a product

1. Crypto disposals and crypto income are ordinary tax. They go on Self Assessment (crypto section from 2024–25) or, for older unpaid years, the Cryptoasset Disclosure Service.
2. There is no BSL version of those pages. Use RAD for letter-translation and Extra Support / SignVideo for the conversation (prompts 55–56). RAD has not advertised crypto as a listed topic; ask anyway, then Extra Support.
3. The disclosure follow-up helpline is published as **03000 55 22 94**. Prefix **18001** for Relay UK (the general HMRC method, not printed on that page). Prefer SignVideo Extra Support and say it is about a crypto disclosure reference that has not arrived.
4. Pay within 30 days of the disclosure. That is a second clock, same shape as prompt 57.
5. From 2027, exchanges will report users to HMRC. Give the exchange your real NI / UTR. The printed penalty for giving a UK exchange nothing, or the wrong thing, is up to £300 — separate from the tax penalty.
6. Do not use Trusted Helper to file crypto CGT (prompt 59). Do not assume Access to Work will fund the disclosure call (prompt 58).
7. Do not walk a reader into the Contractual Disclosure Facility unless the facts are fraud. That is a different product.
