# Prompt 58 — Self-employed / freelance Deaf BSL users: Making Tax Digital and Access to Work

**Master index prompt 58.** Fourth file in the HMRC and BSL/Deaf access batch (prompts 55–61). Compiled 9 September 2026. Read with 52–54 (AtW cap and staff-guide rates) and 55–57 (HMRC channels, Extra Support, complaints). Dossier rule: stay unbiased; do not treat a commercial AtW-bookkeeping offer as official DWP policy.

**What this prompt is for.** Separate three things a freelance BSL user will mix up: (a) the legal MTD timetable; (b) HMRC’s extra-support / exemption doors; (c) whether Access to Work will pay for an interpreter at the HMRC end. They are different departments and different products.

---

## Headline

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax is **already live**. Sole traders and landlords with qualifying (gross self-employment + property) income over **£50,000 in 2024–25** have been required to keep digital records and send quarterly updates from **6 April 2026**. Next cohorts: over £30,000 in 2025–26 → start **6 April 2027**; over £20,000 in 2026–27 → start **6 April 2028**. HMRC said on 12 August 2026 that more than 436,000 people had sent the first 2026–27 quarterly update. Late-update **penalty points are switched off for the first 12 months** of the April 2026 cohort; the January 2028 tax-return deadline still carries penalty points.

HMRC does **not** publish a BSL-supported route for the quarterly update itself. Updates go through third-party compatible software. Extra Support, SignVideo and RAD can help a person understand a letter or book a one-off appointment. That is not a BSL button inside QuickBooks or FreeAgent.

A digital-exclusion exemption exists where age, health or disability **stops the person using a computer, tablet or smartphone**. Being a BSL-first user who *can* use a laptop is not the example HMRC gives. Applications go by **phone or letter** to Self Assessment general enquiries — the same voice-shaped door documented in prompts 55–56.

Access to Work **will** fund a BSL interpreter or CSW for a self-employed person’s *work*. The published staff guide and factsheet do **not** say that an Extra Support meeting, a penalty appeal or an MTD quarterly submission is “work”. They also do not say it is out of scope. That silence is the finding. Commercial bookkeepers advertise AtW-funded accountancy admin; that is a market claim, not a DWP line. RAD’s published offer is to book RAD, not to claim AtW for the HMRC call.

No published named case was found of a Deaf freelancer missing an MTD update or a Self Assessment deadline because the channel was not BSL-accessible, with a documented penalty outcome.

## 58.1 MTD for Income Tax — current timetable and BSL on the quarterly update

### Who and when (live GOV.UK, last updated 26 March 2026)

Qualifying income = gross income from self-employment and/or property before expenses. PAYE, dividends, pensions and partnership profit shares do **not** count.

| Qualifying income in this tax year | Must start MTD from |
|---|---|
| More than £50,000 in 2024–25 | **6 April 2026** (2026–27 tax year) |
| More than £30,000 in 2025–26 | **6 April 2027** |
| More than £20,000 in 2026–27 | **6 April 2028** (legislated reduction confirmed 24 March 2026) |

https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-and-when-you-need-to-use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax

Still file the pre-MTD year in the old way: 2025–26 return due **31 January 2027**. First MTD-year return (2026–27) due **31 January 2028**.

Quarterly update windows (standard):

| Period | Deadline |
|---|---|
| 6 Apr – 5 Jul | 7 August |
| 6 Apr – 5 Oct | 7 November |
| 6 Apr – 5 Jan | 7 February |
| 6 Apr – 5 Apr | 7 May |

Calendar-year accounting (31 March year-end) uses 1 Apr / 30 Jun etc., same deadlines.  
https://www.gov.uk/guidance/use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax/send-quarterly-updates

HMRC 5 February 2026: first-year April 2026 joiners **will not receive penalty points for late quarterly updates for the first 12 months**. Penalty points still apply to the tax-return deadline.  
https://www.gov.uk/government/news/act-now-864000-sole-traders-and-landlords-face-new-tax-rules-in-two-months

HMRC 12 August 2026: 436,000+ first quarterly updates sent; HMRC began auto-signing up remaining £50k-cohort customers from September 2026.  
https://www.gov.uk/government/news/436000-sole-traders-and-landlords-make-their-tax-digital

### Is there a BSL route for the quarterly update?

No published HMRC BSL product sits inside the update. The obligation is: use **compatible third-party software** to keep digital records and send totals. HMRC’s own MTD help page points at a digital assistant, software-vendor support, Extra Support, and (for the earlier testing cohort) a dedicated phone team whose number arrived in a welcome letter. None of those pages name SignVideo, InterpretersLive or a BSL quarterly-update service.  
https://www.gov.uk/guidance/use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax/help-and-support

The MTD-for-VAT accessibility statement (prompt 56) still says HMRC “can provide a British Sign Language (BSL) interpreter, or you can arrange a visit from an HMRC advisor to help you complete the service.” That is a VAT-service statement pointing at extra support, not an ITSA quarterly-update product.  
https://www.tax.service.gov.uk/accessibility-statement/vat-through-software

RAD listed a “Making Tax Digital Workshop | London” for 3 September 2026 on its HMRC Service Support page. That is a workshop, not a standing BSL filing channel.  
https://royaldeaf.org.uk/partner/hmrc-service-support/

### Exemption — disability is in the law; Deaf is not an automatic tick

Digital exclusion, in HMRC’s words: it is not reasonable for you to use compatible software to keep digital records or send updates / the return. Examples HMRC will consider: age, health condition or disability that **stops you using a computer, tablet or smartphone**; no internet at home or a suitable alternative; religious incompatibility. HMRC **will not** accept, on their own: previously filed on paper; unfamiliar with software; few records; extra time or cost.  
https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-you-can-get-an-exemption-from-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax

Apply by **phone or letter** to Self Assessment general enquiries. Title the letter “Making Tax Digital for Income Tax — digitally excluded application.” Friend, family or agent can apply; the test is still the taxpayer’s circumstances. HMRC aims to reply in **28 days**. Tell them any additional needs so they can “provide the right support.”  
https://www.gov.uk/guidance/apply-for-an-exemption-from-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax-if-you-are-digitally-excluded

LITRG, updated 6 September 2026, restates the statutory test (“not reasonably practicable … including age, disability, or location”) and warns that “I’m old / I’m Deaf” without explaining *how* that stops software use will not be enough.  
https://www.litrg.org.uk/tax-nic/making-tax-digital-income-tax/making-tax-digital-exemptions

A BSL-first freelancer who already uses video, email and a laptop is on the wrong side of HMRC’s published examples. They may still need Extra Support, RAD or an agent. That is support to comply, not an exemption from complying.

Blind Person’s Allowance recipients can be **automatically** exempt if that was on the 2024–25 return. There is no equivalent automatic tick for Deaf / BSL.

## 58.2 Does Access to Work fund a BSL interpreter for HMRC contact?

### What AtW will fund for a self-employed person

AtW is a DWP grant to help a disabled person start work, stay in work or move into self-employment. Self-employed eligibility (factsheet): operating a business on your own account, UTR, and — on the published factsheet — annual turnover of at least **£6,500**, with accounts or a bank-standard business plan (UTR + CV accepted for entertainers).  
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-work-factsheet/access-to-work-factsheet-for-customers

Support-worker list in the staff guide includes **British Sign Language interpreter**, lipspeaker, note-taker, palantypist, and a Communication Support Worker. Remote BSL VRS bundles are priced in the guide (60 minutes £105 inc VAT, up to 1,125 minutes £1,480). F2F BSL adviser bands remain National £26–£47 / London £30–£52 on the page last updated 18 June 2026 (prompt 52). Ceiling cap **£69,260** frozen from 8 April 2024 through 2026/27.  
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-work-staff-guide/access-to-work-staff-guide

Disability Arts Online’s self-employed guide (current on this pass) tells artists they must have filed, or be ready to file, a Self Assessment return to prove self-employment, and that AtW can fund a sign-language interpreter or CSW as a support worker for **work tasks**. It does not mention HMRC appointments.  
https://disabilityarts.online/resources/guides-and-toolkits/access-to-work/atw/self-employed/

RNID 2026 AtW survey includes self-employed BSL users describing interpreters for clinical practice, proofreading and “dignity at work.” None of the published quotes is an HMRC call.  
https://rnid.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/RNID-Access-To-Work-Experiences-of-deaf-and-deafblind-people-and-people-with-hearing-loss-2026.pdf

### What the staff guide does *not* say

A full-text search of published AtW customer and staff material in this pass did not return a sentence that either:

- includes Extra Support meetings, tax-return appointments, penalty appeals or MTD quarterly filings in the list of fundable work tasks, or
- excludes HMRC / tax-compliance contact as “outside work.”

“Outside of working hours” in the staff guide is a tightly capped coping-strategy product (max 14 days in 12 months), not a tax-compliance heading.

So: AtW will fund BSL for the self-employed person’s *job*. Whether “talking to HMRC about my own tax” is part of that job is **unpublished**. A decision-maker could reasonably treat quarterly record-keeping as running the business, or treat an HMRC extra-support call as a personal statutory obligation HMRC itself must adjust for. Neither reading is printed.

### Adjacent market claim, not DWP policy

Dakin Accountancy (and similar firms) advertise AtW-funded bookkeeping / accountancy / admin for self-employed disabled clients, framed as ongoing support-worker hours where disability makes those tasks harder. That is a commercial packaging of AtW, useful as a signal that some assessors have accepted accountancy-admin hours, not as a staff-guide rule that HMRC BSL will be paid.  
https://dakinaccountancy.co.uk/atw-faqs/

LITRG’s AtW page is about the **tax treatment** of the grant (income and matching expense in the accounts), not about using AtW to speak to HMRC.  
https://www.litrg.org.uk/working/access-work-scheme

Practical split, until DWP or HMRC prints one:

- Interpreter so the freelancer can do client work → AtW, if awarded.
- Interpreter so the freelancer can use HMRC’s own service → HMRC’s Equality Act / extra-support / SignVideo / RAD duty. Claiming AtW for that call is an untested extra, not a published product.

## 58.4 What RAD, TaxAid and LITRG actually tell people to do

Published, not inferred:

| Body | What they publish for a Deaf / extra-needs self-employed person | What they do **not** publish |
|---|---|---|
| **RAD HMRC Advice Team** | Book a remote (Calendly) or London face-to-face appointment. They translate HMRC letters into BSL, explain PAYE / P60 / Self Assessment vocabulary, screen-share form filling. Listed topics include Self Assessment. MTD workshop advertised September 2026. Funded by HMRC VCS grant; they then speak to Extra Support | Do not tell the reader to claim AtW for the HMRC appointment. Do not claim to file the MTD quarterly update for the client |
| **LITRG** | Use Extra Support for straightforward returns (they will not complete an online return — prompt 56). Apply for digital-exclusion exemption by phone or letter if software is not reasonably practicable; checklist PDF. Disability can be a reasonable excuse for a missed filing, not an automatic waiver. AtW grant is generally matched income/expense in the accounts | No BSL-specific MTD walkthrough. InterpretersLive still named on the additional-needs page (stale — prompt 55) |
| **TaxAid** | Named by LITRG as the handover where Extra Support cannot take a complex case. Low-income tax charity | No published Deaf/MTD protocol found this pass |
| **HMRC extra support / SignVideo** | SignVideo Extra Support host; webchat; visit form; phone-first default (prompts 55–56) | No MTD-quarterly BSL product |
| **AtW** | Interpreter / CSW / VRS for work; self-employed eligible | Silent on HMRC contact |

RAD: https://royaldeaf.org.uk/partner/hmrc-service-support/  
LITRG MTD exemptions: https://www.litrg.org.uk/tax-nic/making-tax-digital-income-tax/making-tax-digital-exemptions  
LITRG exemption checklist: https://www.litrg.org.uk/sites/default/files/AST11700_0.pdf  
LITRG Extra Support: https://www.litrg.org.uk/tax-nic/getting-help-hub/hmrcs-extra-support-team

Working order a guide page can print without inventing policy:

1. Check whether MTD actually applies (qualifying-income tool). Below threshold, ordinary Self Assessment still applies.
2. If software is not reasonably practicable *because the person cannot use a device*, apply for digital exclusion — by letter if the phone is unusable, and name extra-support needs on the form.
3. If software *is* usable but tax English is not: RAD appointment + Extra Support / SignVideo for the HMRC conversation; agent if the figures are not straightforward. EST will not complete an online return.
4. Run the penalty clock separately (prompt 57): 30 days on any penalty letter; first-year quarterly-update points are off for the April 2026 cohort only.
5. AtW: keep it for client-facing and day-to-day business interpreting unless and until a written AtW decision names HMRC contact. Do not tell the reader AtW “covers tax-compliance interpreting.” It is unpublished.

## What prompt 59 should not assume

Do not write “AtW covers tax-compliance interpreting.” Write: AtW covers work interpreting; HMRC contact is unpublished; HMRC’s own adjustment duty is the default door.

Do not write “Deaf people are digitally excluded from MTD.” Write: exclusion is “cannot reasonably use a device”; BSL-first plus a laptop is not HMRC’s example.

Do not invent a 2026 quarterly-update BSL penalty case. Points are off for year one of the first cohort.
