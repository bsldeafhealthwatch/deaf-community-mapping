# Prompt 60 — Scripts and pull-quotes for HMRC BSL / Deaf access

**Master index prompt 60.** Sixth file in the HMRC and BSL/Deaf access batch (prompts 55–61). Compiled 9 September 2026. Read with 55–59 before pasting any line into a public guide. Dossier rule: stay unbiased. Same writing rule as prompts 53–54: published sentences first, in quotation marks, with the organisation and URL. Anything this project adds is labelled **adaptation, not a published script**.

**What this prompt is for.** Copy-ready wording for a Know-Your-Rights tax page. It is not new research. If 55–59 marked a channel stale or a product silent, the script does not pretend otherwise.

---

## Headline

There is **no national “say this to HMRC” card** from RAD, LITRG, TaxAid or BDA. RAD publishes a booking page and a BSL film on how to use Calendly. LITRG publishes routing facts, not a spoken script. HMRC publishes the Charter, the extra-support guide, and the complaints ladder.

What a guide page can print today:

- **Open a contact:** SignVideo Extra Support host, Relay UK prefix, named textphones, RAD Calendly, EST webchat. InterpretersLive is not the live HMRC product (prompt 55).
- **Ask for Extra Support:** there is no public EST number. The person either uses the SignVideo host labelled Extra Support, uses webchat, or reaches a helpline adviser and asks to be transferred. LITRG is the only source for “warm handover” and “48-hour call-back.”
- **Complain:** Tier 1 then Tier 2 then Adjudicator then PHSO via MP. Name the Charter standard. Run the 30-day penalty-appeal clock in a separate sentence.
- **File / MTD:** Trusted Helper will not file Self Assessment. Digital-exclusion applications go by phone or letter. First-year April 2026 quarterly-update penalty points are off; the January 2028 return is not.
- **Pull-quotes:** thin. Use the Charter, LITRG, RAD’s own offer, and (with a date warning) Melanie Morris 2014. Do not caption Elliott as Deaf.

## 60.1 What to say — scripts

### A. Opening a BSL contact with HMRC

**Published facts the script must not contradict**

- Current VRS hosts: Extra Support `https://hmrcextrasupport.signvideo.net/` and Debt Management `https://hmrcdebtmanagement.signvideo.net/` (prompt 55).
- Relay UK: `18001` then the helpline number.
- Income Tax / Self Assessment **complaints** textphone: 0300 200 3319. That number is not printed on every general-enquiry page.
- RAD remote booking: Calendly from `https://royaldeaf.org.uk/partner/hmrc-service-support/`. RAD’s June 2025 film says the service is “national across the UK, and it’s remote only.” The partnership page still also offers London face-to-face by email. Flag the split.

**Adaptation, not a published script — SignVideo Extra Support**

> My name is [name]. My National Insurance number is [NI]. British Sign Language is my first language. I cannot use the telephone. I need Extra Support, and I need you to keep BSL as my contact method on this record. Please do not ask me to phone a helpline.

If the call lands on a general adviser rather than Extra Support (prompt 56: HMRC has not published the skill group behind the SignVideo button):

> Please transfer me to the Extra Support Team. LITRG says that should be a warm handover so I do not explain this again. If no Extra Support adviser is free, please take my details and have Extra Support contact me on this SignVideo service within 48 hours. Do not book a voice call-back.

**Adaptation — Relay UK / textphone**

> I am using Relay UK / textphone because I am Deaf and British Sign Language is my first language. Please speak in short sentences and wait for the relay. I need Extra Support. My National Insurance number is [NI].

**Published RAD lines the page can quote, then link the button**

RAD partnership page:

> “Are you deaf? Struggling to understand letters from HMRC or need assistance with various HMRC-related matters? RAD is here to help!”

> “We offer translation of HMRC letters into British Sign Language (BSL) and provide support for a wide range of topics, including: Self-Assessment, Tax-Child Benefit, Marriage Allowance, Pay As You Earn (PAYE).”

RAD how-to page / YouTube, 16 June 2025 / 25 June 2025:

> “This Service is national across the UK, it’s remote only.”

> “We have partnered with HMRC to provide free support for people who are deaf, deafblind or hard of hearing.”

https://royaldeaf.org.uk/partner/hmrc-service-support/  
https://royaldeaf.org.uk/how-to-book-an-appointment-to-get-help-with-hmrc/

**Do not put in a script:** “Use InterpretersLive.” That listing is stale on HMRC’s own page (prompt 55). LITRG still names it; a guide footnote can say so.

### B. Extra Support webchat (written English)

EST webchat is an eligibility screen then an adviser if one is free. Hours on the contact page: Monday–Friday 08:00–19:30, Saturday 08:00–16:00. No phone number.  
https://www.gov.uk/find-hmrc-contacts/extra-support-team

**Adaptation**

> I am Deaf. British Sign Language is my first language. I cannot use the telephone. National Insurance: [NI]. I need Extra Support. Please do not send me a voice call-back. Contact me by [SignVideo Extra Support / Relay UK / email / RAD appointment]. I also need you to record BSL as my communication need on this record so the next adviser does not ask me to phone.

If the screen says they cannot help:

> Please tell me which team can help, and how I contact that team in BSL. Extra Support is the route HMRC publishes for sensory disability.

### C. Penalty letter — two clocks (prompt 57 + 58)

HMRC’s penalty-appeal window is usually **30 days from the date printed on the letter**, not from the day it was understood. The service complaint is a different track. Mixing them delays the tax appeal.

**Adaptation — first paragraph of a letter or SignVideo opening**

> I disagree with penalty [reference] dated [date]. I am appealing within 30 days. I also make a service complaint about how HMRC contacted me.

> British Sign Language is my first language. I cannot use the telephone. I did not have an accessible way to [file / pay / explain] by the deadline. That is my reasonable excuse. I am now [filing / paying / sending the information] without delay.

> Please send Extra Support or use SignVideo Extra Support. Do not phone me. Please confirm in writing that BSL is recorded on my record.

> This letter is both an appeal against the penalty and a Tier 1 complaint that HMRC did not meet the Charter standard “Being aware of your personal situation.”

Point the reader at https://www.gov.uk/tax-appeals/penalty and https://www.gov.uk/complain-about-hmrc. Do not tell them Access to Work will pay for this call (prompt 58: unpublished).

### D. Making Tax Digital / digital exclusion

Official letter heading from HMRC / LITRG checklist:

> Making Tax Digital for Income Tax — digitally excluded application

https://www.gov.uk/guidance/apply-for-an-exemption-from-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax-if-you-are-digitally-excluded  
LITRG checklist: https://www.litrg.org.uk/sites/default/files/AST11700_0.pdf

**Adaptation — only use if the person cannot reasonably use a computer, tablet or smartphone.** Being BSL-first with a working laptop is not HMRC’s example (prompt 58).

> I am applying for exemption from Making Tax Digital for Income Tax on digital-exclusion grounds. National Insurance: [NI]. Unique Taxpayer Reference: [UTR].

> I currently send my tax return by [paper / agent / Extra Support / RAD help]. I cannot reasonably use compatible software to keep digital records or send quarterly updates because [spell out the device / access barrier — not only “I am Deaf”].

> Additional needs: British Sign Language is my first language. Do not contact me by telephone. Use SignVideo Extra Support or write. A friend / RAD / agent [is / is not] helping me apply.

If software *is* usable and the problem is tax English:

> I am not applying for exemption. I can use a laptop. I need Extra Support and RAD so I can understand the letters and the software in BSL. Please do not treat this as a digital-exclusion claim.

First-year April 2026 cohort: no penalty points for late quarterly updates for 12 months. Still file the 2026–27 return by 31 January 2028.

### E. Trusted Helper — what not to say

Official line:

> “Your trusted helper cannot use this service to help you with your Self Assessment.”

https://www.gov.uk/appoint-tax-agent/friend-relative-manage-your-tax-online

**Adaptation — PAYE / Personal Tax Account only**

> Please register as my Trusted Helper for my Personal Tax Account. I will accept within 28 days. I know this lasts 90 days and does not let you file my Self Assessment.

**Same-room call (only if one person can use a phone)**

> We are in the same room. I am Deaf and use BSL. [Helper name] is speaking because I cannot use the telephone. My National Insurance number is [NI]. I consent to them helping me with my Personal Tax Account. Please do not take this as consent for a voice call-back later.

Two-Deaf households: do not offer this fallback as if it works. Use RAD or an agent.

### F. Tier 1 complaint (service, not the tax figure)

https://www.gov.uk/complain-about-hmrc  
Charter: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/hmrc-charter/the-hmrc-charter

**Adaptation**

> This is a Tier 1 complaint about HMRC’s service, not an appeal against the tax calculation.

> British Sign Language is my first language. I asked to use SignVideo Extra Support / RAD / Relay UK. What happened was: [date, channel, what they asked you to do instead].

> The Charter standard I say HMRC missed is “Being aware of your personal situation”: “We’ll be mindful of your wider personal situation, and will give you extra support if you need it.” Also “Recognising that someone can represent you.”

> I want: an apology; BSL recorded on my record; Extra Support as my route; [penalty reviewed / Time to Pay / letter in a format I can use]; confirmation in writing, not a phone call.

> I need extra support with this complaint because I am Deaf. Do not phone me.

If Tier 1 fails: ask for Tier 2. After Tier 2: Adjudicator within six months. After Adjudicator: MP → PHSO. PHSO will take BSL for *its* process; that is not HMRC’s channel.

## 60.2 Pull-quotes a guide page can print

Reuse rule as prompt 54: short quotation + full citation. Email the press desk before reprinting a **named living person**. Fair dealing is not a charity media-consent waiver.

### Official standards (safest)

**HMRC Charter — Being aware of your personal situation**

> “We’ll listen to your worries and answer any questions clearly and concisely. We’ll be mindful of your wider personal situation, and will give you extra support if you need it.”

- HMRC Charter, GOV.UK. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/hmrc-charter/the-hmrc-charter

**HMRC Charter — Recognising that someone can represent you**

> “We’ll respect your wish to have someone else deal with us on your behalf, such as an accountant, friend or a relative. We’ll only deal with them if you have authorised them to represent you.”

- Same page.

**HMRC extra-support landing line**

> “If you need extra support, you can get a phone or video appointment with the extra support team. Ask your adviser when you call an HMRC helpline or use the extra support team webchat service.”

- https://www.gov.uk/get-help-hmrc-extra-support

**HMRC BSL line (current supplier)**

> “You can contact HMRC using a BSL video interpreter from the SignVideo service.”

- Extra-support guide, cannot-use-telephone chapter. https://www.gov.uk/get-help-hmrc-extra-support/cannot-use-telephone-different-way-to-contact

**Trusted Helper limit**

> “Your trusted helper cannot use this service to help you with your Self Assessment.”

- https://www.gov.uk/appoint-tax-agent/friend-relative-manage-your-tax-online

### Adviser routing (cite as LITRG, not as HMRC)

> “There is not a unique phone number for the team. … If so, this should be a warm handover to the extra support team so that you do not have to explain your circumstances again. We understand from HMRC that if a member of the extra support team is not available, the helpline adviser will take your details and arrange for an extra support team adviser to call you back within 48 hours.”

- LITRG, HMRC’s extra support team, updated 29 July 2026.  
  https://www.litrg.org.uk/tax-nic/getting-help-hub/hmrcs-extra-support-team

> “The service can only be used for help with your personal tax account. It cannot be used to help you with your self assessment tax return, for example.”

- LITRG, Online tax accounts, updated 9 September 2026.  
  https://www.litrg.org.uk/tax-nic/how-tax-collected/online-tax-accounts

### RAD (organisational, not a named client)

> “We offer translation of HMRC letters into British Sign Language (BSL).”

> “This Service is national across the UK, it’s remote only.”

- RAD HMRC Service Support / how-to-book page, 2025. URLs above.

Charity Today, 23 September 2024, quoting RAD Operations Manager Sarah Watson on what the team sees. Check RAD press consent before using the name on a public hub. Safer as an organisational paraphrase: most contacts are vocabulary, letter translation, and form-filling, and the team works with Extra Support.

https://www.charitytoday.co.uk/hmrc-celebrates-royal-association-for-deaf-peoples-support-for-deaf-taxpayers/

### First-person — dated, not current official practice

**Melanie Morris, published extracts, Limping Chicken, 29 September 2014.**

> “On the 28th July, you sent me an eight page letter to tell me that my child tax credit had stopped. … It took me until the 12th September to find one hearing person that understood sign language to help me phone you to find out why my payments stopped.”

- Named, 12 years old, a complaint letter not an Adjudicator finding. Use only with the date and URL, as evidence that third-party phone access has been a documented problem, not as a description of 2026 channels.  
  https://limpingchicken.com/2014/09/29/hmrc-tax-deaf-mother/

### Do not use as a Deaf HMRC quote

**Elliott** (PHSO accessible-communication spotlight, August 2026) is self-employed, dyslexia and ADHD, SEISS grants, £1,500 distress payment. Communication-access + HMRC + self-employed — **not BSL**. If a page uses it, the caption must name the disability correctly (prompt 57).

**Samantha** in the same PHSO report is NHS, not HMRC.

## 60.3 Don’t-list (same job as prompt 49, HMRC-shaped)

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| Call Extra Support on [number] | There is no public EST number. Use SignVideo Extra Support, webchat, or ask a helpline adviser to transfer |
| Use InterpretersLive for HMRC | SignVideo Extra Support / Debt Management are the live GOV.UK hosts. InterpretersLive is a stale third-party listing |
| Register a Trusted Helper to file your tax return | Trusted Helper cannot do Self Assessment. Use an agent, RAD + Extra Support, or file yourself with BSL support |
| Access to Work will pay for your HMRC interpreter | Unpublished. AtW pays for work interpreting. HMRC’s own adjustment duty is the default door |
| Deaf people are exempt from Making Tax Digital | Exemption is “cannot reasonably use a device.” BSL-first plus a laptop is not HMRC’s example |
| HMRC must give you a face-to-face BSL appointment | Extra Support *can* offer video, face-to-face or a visit. It is not a hard F2F-BSL default |
| There is an HMRC Deaf ombudsman case like Alan Graham | There isn’t. Elliott is the closest official case and is not Deaf |
| Family can interpret your Debt Management call as of right | DMBM510500: professional interpreter first; family only if the taxpayer asks, they are 16+, capacity not in doubt |
