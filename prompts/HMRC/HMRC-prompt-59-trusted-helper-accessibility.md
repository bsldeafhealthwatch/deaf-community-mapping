# Prompt 59 — Trusted Helper and other “someone else talks to HMRC” routes

**Master index prompt 59.** Fifth file in the HMRC and BSL/Deaf access batch (prompts 55–61). Compiled 9 September 2026. Read with 55 (channels), 56 (Extra Support), 57 (complaints) and 58 (self-employed / MTD). Dossier rule: do not collapse four different legal products into one “get a helper” sentence.

**What this prompt is for.** Trusted Helper is the product HMRC’s extra-help principles page names next to tax charities. A BSL-first reader who cannot use a phone will be pointed at it. The question is whether the product is actually usable for that reader, and what to use instead when it is not.

---

## Headline

**Trusted Helper is a 90-day Personal Tax Account product.** A friend or relative registers online, the taxpayer accepts within **28 days**, the helper can then see and update the Personal Tax Account for **90 days**, and can be helper for up to **five** people. The taxpayer stays legally responsible. Official GOV.UK pages state, in those words, that a trusted helper **cannot** use the service to help with Self Assessment.

That makes Trusted Helper the wrong tool for the freelance / MTD problem in prompt 58. Quarterly updates and the tax return sit on Self Assessment / MTD software, not on the Personal Tax Account helper permission.

Setting the helper up is digital-first (Government Gateway + identity proof). If the taxpayer cannot go online, the fallback is a **same-room phone call**: the helper and the taxpayer must be physically together, with the National Insurance or tax reference number, so HMRC can confirm identity and consent. There is no published BSL or SignVideo path for that consent call, and no published BSL version of the Trusted Helper registration journey.

HMRC also operates three other “someone else” routes that get mixed up with Trusted Helper: a one-off **intermediary** (friend / relative / voluntary organisation, including RAD), a **tax agent** (64-8 or digital handshake, can file Self Assessment and MTD updates), and a **Lasting Power of Attorney** (property and financial affairs). Debt Management’s published interpreter rule (DMBM510500, prompt 56) still prefers a professional interpreter and will take a family member only when the taxpayer asks and capacity is not in doubt.

No published Adjudicator, PHSO or HMRC case was found of a Deaf or BSL-using taxpayer being unable to appoint a Trusted Helper. Melanie Morris 2014 (prompt 57) is the dated letter about a refused third-party tax-credits call — intermediary/phone practice, not the Trusted Helper product.

## 59.2 Accessibility of Trusted Helper itself

### Digital set-up

- Helper needs a Government Gateway sign-in and may have to prove identity with photo ID.
- Taxpayer must then accept the request **online within 28 days**. LITRG adds that acceptance can be authorised **over the telephone by calling HMRC if necessary**.
- After acceptance, online help lasts **90 days**. Then the whole registration runs again.
- Blocking a helper inside the Personal Tax Account is **permanent** — that person cannot be re-used.

A BSL-first taxpayer who does not already have a working Personal Tax Account and a Gateway login cannot complete the accept step without either going online or making the same-room phone call. There is no published SignVideo / InterpretersLive journey labelled “accept a Trusted Helper.”

### Same-room phone fallback

Live helper page, public-updated 4 September 2015, HTML touched 9 September 2026:

> “If they cannot go online, you may be able to call HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) on their behalf. They must be in the same room when you call.”

Also: National Insurance or tax reference number required; helper must be over 16 if interpreting for a non-English speaker; “Read guidance on HMRC services if you or the person you’re helping has a disability.”

That last link is the extra-support guide (prompts 55–56), not a BSL annex to Trusted Helper.

The same-room rule is a voice-shaped identity check. It assumes one of the two people can use a telephone. If the taxpayer is BSL-first and the helper is also Deaf, the published fallback does not work. If the helper is hearing, the taxpayer must be physically present while the helper speaks — the 2014 Melanie Morris letter is the public example of HMRC refusing a third-party call when the Deaf taxpayer was not on the line in a way HMRC accepted.

### What a helper still cannot fix

Even after a successful 90-day set-up:

- no Self Assessment filing
- no MTD quarterly update
- no taking legal responsibility for errors (LITRG: helper mistakes are treated as the taxpayer’s)
- no substitute for Extra Support, RAD or an agent on a complex return

So Trusted Helper is useful for a PAYE employee who needs a family member to look at a tax code or claim a refund. It is not the access product for a self-employed BSL user under MTD.

## 59.3 Intermediary, RAD, and Debt Management family interpreters

**Intermediary** is the extra-support answer to “I need someone to talk to HMRC for me.” Friend, relative or voluntary organisation. They can talk and help with forms. They do not get Personal Tax Account access. Set-up is a letter, or a short-term call with the person present.  
https://www.gov.uk/get-help-hmrc-extra-support/if-you-need-someone-to-talk-to-hmrc-for-you  
https://www.gov.uk/appoint-tax-agent (section 4)

**RAD** is a funded voluntary organisation on the HMRC VCS list (prompt 55). Practically they act as an intermediary-plus: BSL letter translation, screen-share form filling, then they speak to Extra Support. They are not a Trusted Helper and they do not take Self Assessment legal responsibility. Book RAD first if the need is “understand this letter / this form in BSL.”

**Debt Management DMBM510500** (updated 7 September 2026, prompt 56): prefer a professional interpreter; a family member or friend only if the taxpayer asks, they are 16+, and capacity is not in doubt. That is a debt-manual rule, not the Trusted Helper spec, and it still assumes a voice call.

**Agent** is the product that actually files. A friend or relative *can* be an agent if they meet HMRC agent standards and take a 64-8 or digital handshake. That is a different commitment from a 90-day PTA helper.

## How to use this on a guide page

Do not write “get a Trusted Helper” as the default BSL answer. Write the product that matches the job:

- Look at a tax code or PTA refund → Trusted Helper, if both people can complete Gateway + 28-day accept, or can make the same-room call.
- Understand a letter or sit with Extra Support in BSL → RAD, then Extra Support / SignVideo.
- File Self Assessment or send an MTD quarterly update → tax agent (or the taxpayer plus Extra Support / RAD for a straightforward year). Trusted Helper will not do this.
- Person has lost, or may lose, capacity → LPA (property and financial affairs), then tell HMRC. Helper / intermediary / ordinary agent fall away.

Same-room phone consent is a structural barrier for two-Deaf households. Name it. Do not invent a SignVideo Trusted Helper flow that HMRC has not published.

## What prompt 60 should not assume

Do not put “register a Trusted Helper” in a self-employed BSL script as the way to file. Put RAD + Extra Support + agent. Trusted Helper belongs in a PAYE / Personal Tax Account script only, with the 28-day, 90-day and same-room limits printed in the same paragraph.
