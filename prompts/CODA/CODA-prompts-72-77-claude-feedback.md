## Don’t list (print near any CODA / family page)

- Do not print a UK “number of CODAs.” None exists. Do not derive one from Census 2021 BSL-main-language **22,000**.
- Do not print BBC “tens of thousands” as a statistic.
- Do not print NDCS “more than 90% of deaf children are born to hearing parents” as a **UK measured percentage**. The NDCS footnote is Mitchell & Karchmer **2004, United States**. Direction is the consensus; the exact % is not a CRIDE or Census table.
- Do not treat “90% of children of Deaf parents are hearing” (Schein rule of thumb / CODA UK copy) as a Census result.
- Do not use **CODA** when the sentence is about a Deaf child of Deaf parents. They are not interchangeable.
- Do not use **CODA** when the sentence is about hearing parents of a Deaf child (BDA *BSL in Our Hands*, most NDCS caseload).
- Do not cite Co-Dependents Anonymous UK (`codauk.org`) as the CODA organisation.
- Do not write that using a child to interpret for a Deaf parent is **“illegal”** or **“against safeguarding law”** in England. Prompt 49 discipline. England: “inappropriate” (DHSC migrant health guide) + local CP “never use a child as an interpreter” + Equality Act s.20. Scotland NHS: never under 16. US ADA 28 CFR § 35.160(c) is the binding comparator — do not import it as UK law.
- Do not write “AIS bans family interpreters.” 2025 implementation guidance says family/carers should **generally not** be relied on for BSL; professional support **must be offered**; recorded explicit preference can still be used, especially where there are complex / non-standard communication needs. That is softer than Scotland’s under-16 ban.
- Do not write that hearing CODA children are automatically **young carers** under Children and Families Act 2014 s.96. The definition could stretch; no national series names the role. Napier & Ackroyd 2022 even records a parent refusing a school young-carer referral (“you’re not a carer”).
- Do not write that SEND / EHCP / Teacher of the Deaf practice treats Deaf-of-Deaf as a separate pathway. CRIDE does not collect parental hearing status.
- Do not present US KODA camps, Preston 1995, or the 2021 film *CODA* as descriptions of the NHS or English SEND.
- Do not invent a UK Deaf-of-Deaf childhood testimony chapter from Gallaudet ethnography.
- Do not hold the page out as a children’s service, a safeguarding referral route, or legal advice.
- Do not wear a DEI or anti-DEI banner. Follow money and fulfilment. Camp places and interpreter bookings are the fulfilment facts.

## Addendum to prompt 74 (found after the batch was written — use this, do not pretend 74 already had it)

AIS **2025 implementation guidance** (NHS England long-read) has a section **“Using family members, friends or carers as interpreters.”** It:

- says professional registered support **must be offered** for BSL / deafblind manual / other systems;
- says family or carers should **generally not** be relied on (accuracy, quality, safety, privacy, safeguarding);
- then allows recorded, reviewed preference — more readily where communication is highly personalised or the person has complex / additional impairments.

Older 2017-era AIS implementation text was already in that shape (professional MUST be used for sensory loss alone unless explicit preference is documented). The 2025 refresh did **not** add an under-18 ban.

**CQC Dental mythbuster 20** (page updated 25 June 2026): family/friend interpreting is “not without risk”; if the patient wants it, take informed consent in their own language **independently of** the family member. Equality Act s.20(7): the dentist cannot make the Deaf patient pay for the interpreter.

**Napier & Ackroyd 2022** sample size to print if cited: **12** young hearing people aged **16–25** in the UK healthcare paper (the 2021 book’s UK child arm remains 17 children aged 5–15 + 10 Deaf parents). One interviewee was put in touch with a young-carers association by school; the Deaf parents said the child was not a carer.

**Same BBC package as Bussey** also names **Ella Depledge**, 21, who decided to refuse interpreting for her Deaf parents. Usable as a second named UK voice; still not a population.

**Victoria, Australia** (state interpreting guidelines): “It is not acceptable to ask children under 18 years of age to interpret.” That is the closest *written* cousin to the ADA rule in the Anglophone set after the US regulation — state policy, not verified here as a Commonwealth Act. Do not upgrade it without the primary instrument.

### 2. Law and guidance — name the stack, do not invent a ban

| Lever | Who | What it can do | What it cannot do |
|---|---|---|---|
| AIS 2025 implementation guidance — family section + “must offer professional BSL” | NHS England / providers | The live English health/social-care instruction | Does not create an under-18 criminal offence; preference can still be recorded |
| DHSC migrant health guide + trust policies (“inappropriate to use children”) | DHSC / trusts | Professional-standards hook for a complaint | Not “illegal” |
| NHSScotland national interpreting policy (never under 16) + separate interpreter in CP | Scottish Government / NHS boards | The tightest UK written rule | Not English law; not a CODA Act |
| Equality Act s.20 on the **service**, not the child | Provider | Reasonable adjustment = book the interpreter | Does not automatically classify the child as a young carer |
| Children and Families Act 2014 s.96 | Local authority | Young-carer assessment *if* the role is recognised as care | No official example names BSL brokering |
| Working Together / local CP procedures | Safeguarding partners | “Never use a child as an interpreter” in CP work; BSL named as a method needing a professional | National WT text does not contain a CODA paragraph |
| US ADA 28 CFR § 35.160(c) | Not a UK lever | Comparative model only | Do not write “we should have the ADA” as a legal conclusion on an England page |

Public page: report the stack as a fact. Do not write “non-compliant with the Act” as a volunteer legal conclusion (DAGM 42).

## Page structure if Claude drafts anything

One optional public “families / CODA” page, same skeleton as the later-life / AtW guides:

1. **What this is / is not.** Hearing children of Deaf parents, and separately Deaf children of Deaf parents. Not NDCS. Not a headcount. England badge; Scotland has a tighter NHS under-16 rule.
2. **Two groups, two doors.** Hearing child → CODA UK & Ireland camp. Deaf child → NDCS. Do not send a Deaf-of-Deaf child to a hearing-CODA camp page as if that were their service.
3. **The interpreting rule, in four lines.** Service must offer a professional. Child should not be used. England “inappropriate” + CP “never.” Scotland NHS “never under 16.” Not “illegal.”
4. **What we cannot count.** Hearing CODA population. Deaf-of-Deaf population. Young-carer identifications. Then the FOI list in one sentence.
5. **Named testimony, labelled UK.** Bussey 2021. Casson-Webb 2018. Ella Depledge refusal, same BBC package, if used. Pointer to Domain 16 for the dying-parent hospital facts.
6. **Disclaimer.** Not legal advice, not a children’s service, not a safeguarding referral form. If a child is at risk, the ordinary local-authority / police route — with a professional interpreter for the Deaf parent, not the child.
