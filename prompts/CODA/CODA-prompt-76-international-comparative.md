# Prompt 76 — International comparative practice: child-interpreting prevention and CODA support

**Master index prompt 76.** Fifth file in the CODA batch (prompts 72–77). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. The project standard is **what is actually delivered**, not what a strategy document aspires to.

**Split reminder.** Prevention of child interpreting = hearing CODA issue. Deaf-of-Deaf support is a different column. Do not score a country as “good on CODA” only because it has a strong Deaf-child BSL policy.

---

## Headline

The only comparator on this pass with a **binding, specific legal rule** that covered entities must not rely on a minor child as a sign-language interpreter (except a true emergency when no qualified interpreter is available) is the **United States ADA** effective-communication regulation, 28 CFR § 35.160(c). Adult companions can be used only if the disabled person requests it, the adult agrees, and reliance is appropriate. **That adult exception “does not apply to minor children.”**

No equivalent subsection was found in the Equality Act 2010, the BSL Act 2022, or the British Sign Language (Scotland) Act 2015. UK practice is guidance + reasonable-adjustment duty + local safeguarding procedures (prompt 74). Scotland’s NHS interpreting policy (“never under 16”) is the tightest **written service rule** inside the UK, but it is national NHS policy, not an Act of the Scottish Parliament creating an individual right to sue.

Funded **children’s** CODA programmes that are actually delivered:

- **USA:** a mature KODA camp circuit (Camp Mark 7, KODA Midwest, KODA West, KODA Nation / Ohio, and others) plus CODA International. Charity / fee / grant funded, not a federal statutory service.
- **UK & Ireland:** CODA Camp (14th year in 2026, 144 spaces, sold out) — charity CIC, not government-funded as a national programme (prompt 73).
- **Nordic / German-speaking:** CODA Norge, CODA d.a.ch, Austrian KODA Sommercamp appear on international KODA directories as live camp brands. Treat as voluntary/charity unless a statutory funding line is produced.

No country on this pass was found to run a **government-funded national CODA education service** analogous to a SEND service. Prevention of child interpreting, where it is real, is an **access-to-professional-interpreters** rule, not a children’s programme.

## 76.1 United States — legal restriction (delivered as regulation)

**What it is.** ADA Title II (public bodies) and Title III (public accommodations) effective-communication rules. DOJ’s current “ADA Requirements: Effective Communication” page states:

- Covered entities cannot require a person to bring their own interpreter.
- They may rely on an accompanying **adult** only if the individual requests it, the adult agrees, and reliance is appropriate — and **not** when impartiality is in doubt.
- They may rely on an accompanying **minor child** only in an emergency involving imminent threat to safety or welfare, and only when a qualified interpreter is not available.
- The non-emergency adult exception **does not apply to minor children.**

California All-County Information Notice I-39-24 (27 September 2024) restates the same federal rule for child-welfare departments and adds the operational line that it is inappropriate for a hearing child to interpret for a DHH parent in conversations about abuse or domestic violence.

**Legal duty or voluntary?** Binding federal regulation for covered entities. Enforceable by DOJ complaint / private ADA litigation. That is a different legal species from UK guidance.

**Funded CODA programme?** CODA International (1983, Millie Brother) and the KODA camp circuit are the long-running national/regional infrastructure. They are membership and philanthropy, not a federal budget line. Camp Mark 7 (Old Forge, NY), KODA Midwest (Wisconsin), KODA West (California) and KODA Nation / Ohio are the named multi-year camps. KODA Midwest published 2025 kamper-grant awards (board budgeted $7,000, awarded $5,990).

**Who it is for.** Hearing KODA/CODA identity. Deaf-of-Deaf children are not the camp brand.

**Aspiration vs delivery.** The ADA child-interpreter ban is delivered law. Whether every hospital complies is a separate fulfilment question (US media cases exist; they are not UK evidence — see prompt 77).

**Sources**

- ADA Requirements: Effective Communication — ADA.gov. “Use of Accompanying Adults or Children as Interpreters.” <https://www.ada.gov/resources/effective-communication>
- CDSS ACIN I-39-24, 27 September 2024. Restates 28 CFR § 35.160(c); hearing child must not interpret abuse/DV conversations. <https://cdss.ca.gov/Portals/9/Additional-Resources/Letters-and-Notices/ACINs/2024/I-39_24.pdf>
- KODA organizations directory — KODAheart. Lists US camps and CODA UK & Ireland. <http://kodaheart.com/resources/koda-organizations/>
- CODA Midwest blog, 2 June 2025 kamper grants. <https://www.codamidwest.org/blog.html>

## 76.2 Scotland — tighter NHS rule, BSL Act plans, no CODA statute

**Child-interpreting prevention.** NHSScotland Interpreting, Communication Support and Translation National Policy: never use a child under 16 as an interpreter. BSL primary-care companion: always provide a face-to-face interpreter for children; if child-protection issues, book a **separate** BSL interpreter for the Deaf parent so the child’s voice is heard. That is **delivered written policy** for NHS Scotland. It is not an individually enforceable “CODA right” in an Act.

**BSL infrastructure.** British Sign Language (Scotland) Act 2015 requires listed authorities to publish BSL plans. National Plan 2023–2029 (published 6 November 2023) has a Children, Young People and their Families priority: GIRFEC; BSL pathway through education for deaf and deafblind children; BSL-using parents to be fully involved in their child’s education. Contact Scotland BSL is the national VRS for residents (already mapped in this project). None of that is a children’s CODA programme.

**CODA support.** No Scottish Government-funded KODA camp found. CODA UK & Ireland covers Scotland as part of UK & Ireland; children from Scotland have attended the English camp historically.

**Aspiration vs delivery.** The 2015 Act and 2023–2029 plan are real. They target BSL users and Deaf children, not hearing CODA welfare. Do not cite Scotland as a “CODA model.” Cite it as the UK’s tightest **health interpreting** rule plus a Deaf-child BSL education ambition.

**Sources**

- NHSScotland national interpreting policy. <https://publichealthscotland.scot/media/3059/interpreting-communication-support-and-translation-national-policy.pdf>
- BSL National Plan 2023–2029 — Scottish Government, 6 November 2023. <https://www.gov.scot/publications/bsl-national-plan-2023-2029/>
- Children, Young People and their Families chapter. <https://www.gov.scot/publications/bsl-national-plan-2023-2029/pages/6/>

## 76.3 England and Wales — duty without the ADA sentence

**Child-interpreting prevention.** Equality Act 2010 s.20 reasonable adjustment; AIS for NHS and publicly funded adult social care (prompt 34); DHSC migrant health guide (“inappropriate to use children”); local safeguarding procedures (“a child should never be used as an interpreter”). BSL Act 2022 is a ministerial reporting duty on BSL communications products, not an interpreting-guarantee for families.

**CODA support.** CODA UK & Ireland CIC camp — delivered, small, not a DfE or NHS programme.

**Aspiration vs delivery.** “Deaf people should have interpreters so children are not used” is the advocacy ask. The legal hook is the Equality Act + professional guidance, not an ADA-style prohibition. Printing “England has banned child interpreting” would be false.

## 76.4 Australia — research site, not a found statutory child ban

Napier’s 2021 monograph used **Australia** for the one-to-one interview arm (11 heritage signers aged 13–55+). That is research evidence, not an Australian legal model.

No Australian Commonwealth statute equivalent to 28 CFR § 35.160(c) was verified on this pass. NDIS and state interpreting services exist for Deaf adults; treating them as a child-interpreting ban would need the primary instrument. Flag as **not confirmed as a legal model** rather than asserting either presence or absence beyond “not verified here.”

## 76.5 Nordic and German-speaking charity camps

International KODA directories list:

- **KODA Norge / CODA Norge** — Norway, varying locations.
- **CODA Camp d.a.ch** — Germany (and the DACH region).
- **KODA Sommercamp** — Austria.

These are the European cousins of CODA UK & Ireland: voluntary, camp-shaped, hearing-CODA identity. No statutory “children must never interpret” Act was retrieved for Norway, Germany or Austria on this pass. Do not upgrade a camp listing into a legal duty.

## 76.6 What is often cited as a model but is not a delivered CODA right

| Citation that appears in advocacy | What it actually is |
|---|---|
| “The ADA bans using children as interpreters” | True for covered US entities, with a narrow emergency exception. Not UK law. |
| “Scotland is best on BSL” | True-ish for listed-authority BSL plans and NHS interpreting policy. Not a CODA children’s service. |
| “UNCRPD means children cannot interpret” | UNCRPD Articles 7, 21, 25 support accessible information and the child’s best interests. They do not, by themselves, create a UK criminal offence or a funded KODA service. |
| “NDCS / BDA family BSL classes” | Support for families of **Deaf children**, mostly hearing parents. Not hearing-CODA prevention. |

## How to write this in public copy

If the dossier wants a “legal model,” name the **ADA child-interpreter rule** and say England does not have it. If it wants a “delivered children’s peer space,” name **CODA Camp UK & Ireland** and the US KODA camps, and say they are charity-run. Do not call Scotland’s BSL plan a CODA policy.
