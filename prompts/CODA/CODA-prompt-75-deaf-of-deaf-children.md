# Prompt 75 — Deaf-of-Deaf children: early language access, education, and identity provision

**Master index prompt 75.** Fourth file in the CODA batch (prompts 72–77). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment.

**Split reminder.** This file is only about **Deaf children born to Deaf parent(s)**. Hearing CODA children are prompts 72–74 and 76–77. Do not recycle hearing-CODA camp copy as Deaf-of-Deaf provision.

**International-literature flag.** “Deaf-of-Deaf children are native signers and culture-carriers” is a staple of **US** Deaf Studies (Gallaudet Annual Survey lineage; Mitchell & Karchmer; NAD language-acquisition letters). UK policy documents reviewed on this pass do **not** operationalise parental hearing status as an EHCP or Teacher of the Deaf caseload field. Do not present the US framing as established English SEND practice.

---

## Headline

UK early-years and school systems treat “deaf child” as **one category**. Parental hearing status is not a CRIDE field, not a School Census field, and not a factor named in the SEND Code of Practice or in published Local Offer templates reviewed on this pass.

The sector **does** repeat the demographic fact that most Deaf children have hearing parents and therefore arrive without a home sign language. That sentence is used to justify family BSL tuition and Teacher of the Deaf early-years work — BDA *BSL in Our Hands* (from 18 March 2024), NDCS family-support copy, BATOD “90–95% born to hearing parents” in the 2025 positive-language publication. It is **not** used to create a second pathway for the minority who already have native BSL at home.

No UK Deaf school, resource provision or national organisation was found that runs a named programme *only* for Deaf-of-Deaf children. Deaf-of-Deaf pupils sit inside the ordinary deaf-education offer — and, where the family is already signing, that offer can look like a poor fit because the typical ToD early-years script is written for hearing parents new to deafness.

There is **no official UK count** of Deaf-of-Deaf children (prompt 72). Any “~5–10%” line is still the US Mitchell & Karchmer / Schein orbit.

## 75.1 Does UK early-years and school guidance recognise native-BSL access in Deaf-of-Deaf homes?

**Partially as a contrast sentence; not as a separate developmental pathway.**

What UK documents actually do:

- NDCS statistics and family-facing pages: “More than 90% of deaf children are born to hearing parents, who may be completely new to deafness.” Purpose of the sentence: explain why hearing families need information. The inverse group is implied, not specified.
- BDA *BSL in Our Hands*: same 90% line; ask is **free BSL tuition for families of newly identified deaf children** — i.e. the hearing-parent majority.
- BATOD / Deaf teachers “Positive use of language” (updated 2025): “As 90–95% of deaf children are born to hearing parents with no prior experience of deafness…” Used to argue against “hearing impairment” terminology, not to specify Deaf-of-Deaf pedagogy.
- CRIDE communication breakdown (2023 UK-wide figures still quoted on NDCS stats): **89% spoken language only; 2% BSL; 6% sign + spoken; 4% other.** That is the child’s language use as reported by services, **not** parental hearing status. A Deaf-of-Deaf child who uses BSL at home and speech at school is invisible inside that four-way split.

What they do **not** do:

- State that a Deaf-of-Deaf child typically has full native BSL from birth.
- Set different language-development milestones for that group.
- Warn services against applying a language-deprivation template to a child who already has a complete first language at home.

Language deprivation as a concept is live in international Deaf Studies and in UK advocacy about **Deaf children in hearing families** who are not given accessible language early. Applying that literature wholesale to Deaf-of-Deaf children would be a category error.

**Sources**

- NDCS Deaf statistics. Last reviewed May 2025. <https://www.ndcs.org.uk/about-us/news-and-media/deaf-statistics>
- BDA BSL in Our Hands. <https://bda---bsl-in-our-hands.webflow.io/>
- BATOD and Deaf teachers, Positive use of language, 2025 update. <https://www.batod.org.uk/information/batod-and-deaf-teachers-publication-positive-use-of-language/>
- CRIDE communication / placement figures as quoted by NDCS (CRIDE 2023 UK-wide for the language split). Same NDCS stats page.

## 75.2 SEND, EHCP, Teacher of the Deaf caseload — is parental Deaf status a factor?

**Not in the published national instruments.**

| Instrument | Parental hearing status named? |
|---|---|
| SEND Code of Practice 2015 (still the England code) | No. “Hearing impairment” / sensory needs. Parents are partners; their own deafness is not an assessment variable. |
| EHCP statutory contents | Needs, provision, placement. No field for “parent is Deaf.” |
| School Census SEN | Primary/secondary need = hearing impairment. No parent field. |
| CRIDE service survey | Headcount, communication, ToD posts, resource provisions, referrals from NHSP. No parental-hearing question in published reports or in the 2026 England questionnaire example seen on this pass. |
| BATOD role-of-QToD series (mainstream 2025 revision; early years 2025 revision) | ToD supports child *and* family. Early-years documents assume families may be new to deafness. No Deaf-of-Deaf caseload protocol. |
| Local Offer (Children and Families Act 2014 ss.30–37) | Must describe provision for deaf children locally. Published Local Offers describe ToD, radio aids, resource provisions. Parental Deaf status as a placement factor was **not found** in national guidance. |

A ToD *in practice* will know if the parents sign. That informal knowledge is not the same as a national assessment distinction.

**Sources**

- NDCS, additional support / EHCP (England). ToD must be consulted on a statutory assessment. <https://www.ndcs.org.uk/advice-and-support/all-advice-and-support-topics/education-and-learning/education-and-learning-england/additional-support-england>
- BATOD, Models of specialist services; Articulating the specialism series. <https://www.batod.org.uk/information/models-of-specialist-services-for-deaf-children-and-young-people/>
- CRIDE 2025 England and UK-wide reports. <https://www.batod.org.uk/information/cride-reports/>

## 75.3 Identity, belonging, and named UK provision for this group

**International academic framing exists. UK-specific published research on Deaf-of-Deaf *children* as a named identity group is thin.**

What is commonly said in Deaf Studies (mostly US, some UK academic):

- Deaf-of-Deaf children are often treated as core culture-carriers of the signing community.
- Outcomes literature (US Annual Survey lineage) associates having at least one Deaf parent with much higher rates of signing at home (Mitchell & Karchmer 2005: 93.3% signing at home if ≥1 Deaf parent).
- Some hearing-CODA spaces have historically been uneasy about Deaf-of-Deaf adults joining as “CODA” (Handspeak / community commentary). That tension is community politics, not UK education policy.

What this pass found in the UK:

- No named “Deaf-of-Deaf club,” school stream or NDCS workstream.
- Deaf schools and resource provisions serve Deaf pupils regardless of parental hearing status. CRIDE 2023 placement mix (NDCS stats): 78% mainstream; 6% mainstream with resource provision; 2% special schools for deaf children; 13% other special schools.
- Identity research on UK Deaf children is dominated by the majority experience (hearing family, spoken-language school). Flag that skew rather than filling it with Gallaudet ethnography presented as English practice.
- Scotland’s BSL National Plan 2023–2029 has a “Children, Young People and their Families” priority: GIRFEC; pathway for deaf and deafblind children to use BSL through education; “Parents who use BSL will have the same opportunities as other parents to be fully involved in their child’s education.” That is about **BSL-using parents’ access to school**, not a Deaf-of-Deaf pedagogy stream.

Paddy Ladd’s *Deafhood* (UK academic, c. 2003) is the named British theoretical frame for Deaf cultural identity. It is not a children’s service specification.

**Sources**

- Mitchell & Karchmer (2005), Parental Hearing Status and Signing among Deaf and Hard of Hearing Students. US Annual Survey 2001–02. <https://www.researchgate.net/publication/234673909_Parental_Hearing_Status_and_Signing_among_Deaf_and_Hard_of_Hearing_Students>
- BSL National Plan 2023–2029, Children, Young People and their Families — Scottish Government, 6 November 2023. <https://www.gov.scot/publications/bsl-national-plan-2023-2029/pages/6/>

## 75.4 Do mainstream placement decisions reference parental Deaf status?

**No published national rule found.** Placement turns on the child’s needs, parental preference, and what the local authority will name in an EHCP. “Parent is Deaf / already signs BSL” is not a listed decision factor in the Code or in NDCS placement advice.

A hearing-parent family fighting for a signing environment and a Deaf-parent family who already sign at home may want the same school for different reasons. The system does not code that difference.

## How to write this in public copy

Deaf-of-Deaf children are a real group with a different home-language starting point. UK services do not measure them and do not run a separate offer. The dominant UK sentence is still about hearing parents of Deaf children. Do not import US “culture-carrier” language as if it were in the SEND Code.
