# Prompt 73 — Existing UK support, advocacy, and community organisations for CODA

**Master index prompt 73.** Second file in the CODA batch (prompts 72–77). Compiled 10 September 2026. Dossier rule: stay unbiased; follow money and fulfilment. Gaps are logged as gaps. Volunteer-run groups can look live on a homepage and be dormant in practice — check a last-12-month event, post or filing.

**What this prompt is for.** Test whether meaningful UK-specific support already exists for children growing up with Deaf parents, as opposed to only CODA International (US) and adult-reflection networks. Distinguish hearing-CODA provision from Deaf-of-Deaf child provision. Distinguish children currently growing up from adults looking back.

**Split reminder (prompt 72).** Hearing CODA ≠ Deaf-of-Deaf. NDCS family services are built around **Deaf children** (most of whom have hearing parents). That is not CODA support.

---

## Headline

The UK **does** have a live, named national CODA organisation and a live children’s camp. That is not a gap.

**CODA UK & Ireland CIC** (08284195) is Active at Companies House, filed a confirmation statement on **7 November 2025**, and is running **CODA Camp 2026** (14th year) at PGL Hailsham, **Friday 31 July – Monday 3 August 2026**. The camp is **sold out** with a waitlist; **144** camper spaces; ages **7–17**; deposit **£85**; volunteer call for **30 July – 3 August 2026** addressed to “Hearing CODAs.” All children and adult volunteers at camp are themselves CODAs. That is delivered provision, not a dormant Facebook group.

What it is **not**:

- It is not a statutory service, helpline or young-carer pathway.
- The camp page and Instagram save-the-date (27 August 2025) address **hearing** Children of Deaf Adults. Deaf-of-Deaf children are not a separately run stream.
- NDCS, BDA and local Deaf children’s societies support **Deaf children and their families**. That includes Deaf parents of Deaf children as a subset of “parents of deaf children,” but it is not a CODA programme and does not target hearing siblings as its primary user.

No second national UK children’s CODA organisation was found. No local-authority or NHS-commissioned “CODA support service” was found. Adult weekenders and conferences exist alongside the children’s camp — do not treat the adult programme as the only UK provision.

**Who it is for.** “If either one (or both) of your parents are Deaf, then you’re a CODA. Regardless of whether you sign or not.” Activities “for CODAs of all ages”: children’s camp, over-18 weekenders, workshops, social media.

**Hearing vs Deaf-of-Deaf.** The 2026 volunteer call is explicitly “Calling all Hearing CODAs.” The camp is “for any children with either 1 or 2 deaf parents, aged from 7–17” and “we welcome back hearing Children of Deaf Adults.” The organisation does not run a named Deaf-of-Deaf children’s programme. In practice the live product is hearing-CODA peer space.

**Children vs adults.** Both. Flagship for *current* children is CODA Camp (14th year in 2026; the organisation’s own history page said the camp was in its 8th year as of 2020, which is consistent). Adults have weekenders and the October 2024 CODA Croí Festival at University College Cork (CODA UK & Ireland with UCC) — adult/reflection and research-facing, not a children’s service.

**History (so the “new trend” claim is rejected).** CODA International 1983 (Millie Brother, US). Term brought back to the UK after WFD Finland 1987. 1990 BDA centenary conference: Deaf parents and CODAs set up a London grouping and an HMFD (“Hearing Me, Mother Father Deaf”) newsletter. CODA-Europe hosted in Ireland 2004. CIC from 2012.

## 73.2 What is *not* a UK children’s CODA service

**CODA International.** US non-profit founded 1983. Parent of the acronym and of the international conference circuit. Not a UK children’s service. UK people attend; that does not make it UK provision.

**CoDA UK (codauk.org).** Co-Dependents Anonymous. Different organisation, different acronym collision. Do not cite.

**NDCS.** National charity for **deaf children** and their families. Helpline (including SignVideo), local groups, Family Fund partnership for BSL courses and assistive kit for deaf children aged 0–11 / primary (announced 3 February 2026), My NDCS app. Deaf parents of a Deaf child can use these services as parents of a deaf child. Hearing CODA children are not the named user. NDCS does not publish a CODA workstream.

**BDA.** Deaf-led. *BSL in Our Hands* (from 18 March 2024) campaigns for free BSL tuition for families of **newly identified deaf children** — that is the hearing-parent / Deaf-child majority, not hearing CODA.

**Local Deaf children’s societies / NDCS groups.** Peer space for families of Deaf children. May include Deaf-parented households. Not CODA-specific.

**Deaf schools and resource provisions.** For Deaf pupils. A Deaf-of-Deaf child may be on that caseload; a hearing CODA is not.

**No UK university centre** was found that runs an ongoing children’s CODA support programme (as opposed to research: Napier / Heriot-Watt is research, see prompt 74).

## How to write this in public copy

Say there **is** a UK children’s CODA camp and a live CIC. Do not write “the UK has no CODA support.” Do not write that NDCS “covers CODA.” Do not treat Camp 2026 as a national mental-health or safeguarding service — it is a peer weekend run by a small CIC.
