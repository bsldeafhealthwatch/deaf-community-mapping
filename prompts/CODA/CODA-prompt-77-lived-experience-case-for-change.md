## Headline

UK published CODA testimony exists. It is **short, first-person, and clustered around hospital interpreting failures** — not a large interview corpus.

Named UK accounts on this pass:

- **Francesca Bussey** (London): hearing child of two Deaf parents; in 2019 interpreted the news that her father was dying after a month in hospital with **two hours** of booked BSL. BBC World Service, 28 December 2021; BSL-signed companion film.
- **Amanda Casson-Webb**: interpreted in an ambulance at age seven when her father was dying; in 2018 interpreted through a 12-day admission for her Deaf mother after the hospital provided “just under two hours” of professional interpreting. *The Limping Chicken*, 27 November 2018.
- **Pearl Clinton** (petition / BBC same package): collected further family stories that “it is still happening.” Adult-child interpreting for a dying parent, not a primary-school child — still a CODA role.
- **College of Policing CODA officer** (named on the College site as a reflective first-person piece, 25 May 2023): grew up as de-facto interpreter; later police officer; flags vicarious trauma and the risk of a child interpreting in domestic-abuse / evidence settings.

UK **Deaf-of-Deaf childhood** first-person material published as that named identity is **thin**. Do not fill the gap with Gallaudet ethnography.

UK organisational **policy asks** that are actually on the record are mostly about **professional interpreters for Deaf adults** (so the child is not used), not about a national KODA service or a young-carer classification. CODA UK & Ireland’s public mission is peer space, camp, heritage — not a published legislative demand on this pass.

The bulk of book-length CODA memoir remains **American** (Preston 1995; the 2021 film *CODA*). Label it as such.

## 77.1 Hearing CODA children used to interpret — UK attributable accounts

### Francesca Bussey — UK, named, hospital, death notification

BBC World Service / BBC News, 28 December 2021, Humera Iqbal and David Reid:

- Grew up hearing with two Deaf parents in the 1980s; signing from seven months.
- Father in hospital a month in 2019. She “repeatedly asked nurses to book an interpreter”; he received **two hours** of BSL.
- Doctors came. “There was no time lag. I was told it. I interpreted it. I had to tell him he was dying.”
- BBC frames her as one of “tens of thousands” who routinely interpret for Deaf parents — that denominator is **journalistic colour, not a sourced UK population figure** (see prompt 72). Do not recycle “tens of thousands” as a statistic.

Companion BSL-signed film: *Stories from children of deaf adults*, BBC World Service, December 2021.

**Documented consequence:** child-then-adult required to deliver a death diagnosis; missed professional interpreter despite requests; last month of life described as frightening and unexplained for the Deaf parent.

This is the same Francesca Bussey already cited in the project’s EOL file. The CODA-specific fact is that the interpreter who was used was **his daughter**.

### Amanda Casson-Webb — UK, named, ambulance + hospital

*The Limping Chicken*, 27 November 2018:

- Age seven: interpreted for her father in an ambulance; he died later that year.
- 2018: mother admitted via ED. Daughter told staff immediately that a BSL interpreter was required. **Twelve hours** later, still none; told nothing could be done until after the weekend because it was Friday evening.
- Twelve-day admission, emergency surgery. Professional interpreting: **just under two hours**. Remainder by the daughter (“BSL literate daughter” in the discharge notes) and a family friend who is an interpreter.

**Documented consequence:** childhood ambulance interpreting; adult-child covering an entire admission; hospital noted the daughter as the communication support.

### College of Policing CODA officer — UK, named-as-author reflective piece

25 May 2023, College of Policing *Going Equipped*:

- “I am a child of Deaf adults (CODA) and I assumed the de-facto interpreter role as a child, conveying personal and complex information.”
- Describes professionals relying on the child as language broker; vicarious trauma; risk that a child who has witnessed domestic abuse is then used to manage communication; CODAs “cannot be expected to be impartial.”

**Documented consequence:** professional analysis of evidential and welfare harm, grounded in the author’s own childhood and later policing work. Not a case-file with a published outcome.

### Napier UK child sample — attributable as a study, anonymised as persons

Napier 2021 UK arm: 17 young hearing heritage signers aged 5–15 plus 10 Deaf parents. Published themes include SLB as gift, shame-resilience, and cooperation — **and** brokering in healthcare. Napier & Ackroyd 2022 is the healthcare-specific UK interview paper. Use as research testimony, not as named individuals.

Pearl Clinton’s petition stories, as quoted by the BBC package, are second-hand (“I’ve heard so many stories… It is still happening”). Usable as advocacy context, not as separately verified incidents.

## 77.2 Deaf-of-Deaf childhood — UK vs international

**UK-sourced, attributable, published as Deaf-of-Deaf childhood:** not found as a comparable set of named first-person features on this pass. UK Deaf autobiography more often describes growing up Deaf with **hearing** parents (the majority experience) or adult Deaf-community belonging.

**Do not substitute:**

- US / international Deaf-of-Deaf ethnography (Gallaudet family studies; “Deaf family” narratives) as if it were an English school-system account.
- Hearing-CODA pride narratives (“we are natives of Deaf culture”) as if they were Deaf-of-Deaf testimony. They can overlap in family culture; they are not the same childhood.

If a later pass finds a named UK Deaf-of-Deaf memoir or NDCS / BDA interview, add it here. Until then the honest line is: **the published UK first-person stack on this topic is hearing-CODA + hospital interpreting, not Deaf-of-Deaf schooldays.**

International (for contrast only, labelled):

- Paul Preston, *Mother Father Deaf* (Harvard, 1995) — 150 adult US CODAs.
- 2021 feature film *CODA* (US/French production; Oscar 2022) — hearing child, Deaf parents and Deaf sibling. Cultural reference, not evidence of UK practice.
- US local-news cases of hospitals using CODA children for diagnoses (e.g. UAB / Daily Moth 2023) — ADA-regime illustrations, not NHS.

## 77.3 UK bodies that have asked for a specific change

| Body | What it actually asked | Date | Is it a CODA ask? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual CODAs in the BBC / Limping Chicken accounts | Book the professional interpreter so the child or adult child is not the interpreter in hospital | 2018, 2021 | Yes — mechanism is Deaf-adult access, effect is CODA protection |
| Pearl Clinton petition (as reported by BBC) | Interpreters in hospital; stories still happening | 2021 package | Deaf-adult access; CODA role is the failure mode |
| SignHealth / RNID *Still Ignored* and BSL Advisory Board *Locked Out* | AIS fulfilment, professional BSL in health and social care | 2024–2025 | Deaf-adult access. Family-as-interpreter is a side finding, not a children’s-welfare chapter |
| CODA UK & Ireland CIC | Public mission: peer support, camp, heritage, youth groups. No legislative demand document found on this pass | Live 2026 | Organisation exists; a drafted “ban child interpreting / fund a national CODA service / classify as young carers” manifesto was **not found** |
| BDA *BSL in Our Hands* | Free BSL tuition for families of newly identified Deaf children | From 18 Mar 2024 | Hearing parents of Deaf children — not hearing CODA |
| NDCS | Family support and ToD capacity for Deaf children | Ongoing | Deaf-child charity, not a CODA campaign |
| College of Policing CODA author | Do not rely on the child; impartial professional interpreters; think about evidence contamination in DA | 25 May 2023 | Yes — practice ask inside policing / safeguarding |

**No UK charity was found on this pass that has published a numbered demand for:** (a) a statutory ban matching the ADA child-interpreter rule; (b) automatic young-carer identification of hearing CODA children; or (c) a government-funded national CODA service.

The live, sourced ask that *does* protect hearing CODA children is the same ask this project already carries for Deaf adults: **the service books a professional BSL interpreter so the family is not the interpreter.**

## How to write this in public copy

Use Bussey and Casson-Webb by name. They have published the accounts. Say the UK stack is thin and hospital-weighted. Do not promote the BBC “tens of thousands” line to a statistic. Do not invent a Deaf-of-Deaf childhood chapter from US sources. The policy change that is actually being asked for in the UK material is **professional interpreters so children are not used** — not a new CODA statute.
