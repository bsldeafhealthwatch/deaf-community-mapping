RNID
Manage Closely
National Founded 1911 18m+ deaf & HoH in UK rnid.org.uk
Organisation
The UK's largest charity for deaf people and those with hearing loss, supporting over 18 million people. Operates across research, policy, campaigns, and community services. Led the landmark campaign that secured official recognition of BSL as a language of England, Scotland and Wales under the BSL Act 2022. A primary architect of national NHS accessibility policy for deaf people.
Still Ignored — April 2025 (co-authored with SignHealth)
The most significant deaf healthcare access report published in the UK. Based on responses from over 1,100 deaf and hard of hearing people. Core findings reframed as a legal and patient safety issue, not just a quality concern.
70% never asked about comms needs
67% of BSL users denied interpretation
56% don't fully understand appointments
Fewer than 1 in 5 BSL users trust the NHS
1 in 10 avoid A&E or ambulance
1 in 4 avoid seeking help for new health concerns
RNID Director of Health Crystal Rolfe: "Imagine not being able to understand a cancer diagnosis, or having to rely on a family member to tell you that you're seriously ill or even dying." RNID and SignHealth are now calling on DHSC to mandate AIS training across the NHS. NHS England's National Patient Safety and Healthcare Inequalities teams are expected to publish a Patient Safety Healthcare Inequalities Reduction Framework citing the AIS.
Engagement Actions
Co-develop AIS compliance tools Policy endorsement route BSL Act compliance guidance Campaign partnership cite in any briefing documents monitor DHSC AIS mandate progress
SignHealth
Manage Closely
National Deaf health specialist signhealth.org.uk
Organisation
The UK's only charity dedicated specifically to Deaf health. Provides direct services in British Sign Language: psychological therapy, domestic abuse support, social care, and children & families services across England. Directly works with NHS commissioners to improve AIS compliance. Philippa Wynne, Director of Engagement: "The NHS is not working for deaf people. Every day the health of deaf people is put at risk."
Key Data & Policy Work
Co-authored Still Ignored with RNID (April 2025). Worked directly with Healthwatch South West London on the AIS review across all SWL ICB GP practices — the work that led to the DA Languages BSL interpreter rollout in October 2024. Commissioned by National Deaf CAMHS.
NHS failures cost £80–100m/yr (est.)
56% don't understand their appointment
88% say inaccessible comms affects mental health
82% say it impacts physical health
SignHealth notes the 2014 Sick Of It report estimated deaf healthcare failures cost the NHS £30m per year — updated to £80–100m accounting for inflation, social care, and mental health costs. Only 56% of deaf people say they leave an NHS appointment having understood what they were told.

Primary national clinical partner AIS compliance route into NHS BSL-accessible services model SWL ICB commissioning gateway user research access deaf awareness training resources

Organisation
South West London & St George's Mental Health NHS Trust delivers the National Deaf Mental Health Service — the UK's leading NHS body for Deaf mental health, based at Springfield University Hospital in Tooting. This is both the most powerful local NHS deaf specialist body and one of the most important nationally. Located approximately 15–20 minutes from Sutton, it is the closest major NHS deaf services facility to the SWL ICB southern boroughs.
Services
Bluebell Ward (Morrison Building, entrance 3) — national inpatient deaf mental health unit, telephone answered 24 hours. Deaf Adult Community Team (Newton Building, entrance 7) — outpatient and community mental health for Deaf adults, BSL-fluent clinical staff. National Deaf CAMHS also based here. SWLSTG recently opened the Trinity and Shaftesbury Buildings (Springfield Village), providing upgraded inpatient wards, outpatient services, and teaching facilities. Also operates from Tolworth Hospital in Kingston.

National Clinical academic network bsmhd.org.uk
Organisation & Relevance
The only UK charity solely dedicated to the intersection of deafness and mental health. An academic-clinical membership network connecting NHS clinicians, psychologists, psychiatrists, social workers, and academics who work with Deaf patients. Holds significant clinical authority nationally.

Springfield connection: Staff from the SWLSTG National Deaf Services team (Springfield University Hospital, Tooting) actively participate in BSMHD conferences and publish within its network. This makes BSMHD the direct bridge between national clinical standards for Deaf mental health and the SWL ICB's local NHS provision. An endorsement or citation from BSMHD carries weight with NHS commissioners and clinical governance teams.

Royal Association for Deaf People (RAD)
Keep Satisfied
National · SWL presence Founded 1841 Royal Patron: HM King Charles III (2025) royaldeaf.org.uk
Organisation
Founded in 1841, RAD is one of the oldest deaf charities in the UK, with Royal patronage since Queen Victoria in 1876 — HM King Charles III became Royal Patron in 2025. RAD is a Deaf-led organisation providing services in BSL: Information Advice & Guidance, advocacy, youth and transition services, employment, social care, interpreting, community engagement, and consultancy. NHS-listed as an active service provider (November 2025). Cited by Healthwatch Sutton as a provider of Deaf awareness training resources for SWL GP practices.
SWL Local Presence
Croydon — embedded social care: RAD caseworker is formally embedded within Croydon Council's Sensory Impairment Service, providing caseworker support following social care assessments for profoundly deaf Croydon residents. This service operates at the direct intersection of deaf community need, NHS primary care, and social care commissioning.

Lambeth — fortnightly IAG via DASL: A RAD-trained adviser delivers an advice session every other Wednesday (10am–3:30pm) from the DASL (Disability Advice Service Lambeth) office. Covers welfare benefits, debt, employment, correspondence, and health system navigation.

Wandsworth — Family Information Service: Listed on Wandsworth Council's FIS for families with deaf children. Relevant to health and SEND pathways across Wandsworth and neighbouring boroughs.

GP deaf awareness training: Cited by Healthwatch Sutton as a resource for GP practice Deaf awareness training across the SWL ICB.

Healthwatch South West London
Keep Satisfied
SWL ICB · 6 boroughs Croydon · Kingston · Merton · Richmond · Sutton · Wandsworth swlhealthwatch.org.uk
Organisation & Role
The coordinated patient voice network for all six South West London ICB boroughs. Holds formal accountability powers over NHS commissioners and is the primary route for patient experience into SWL ICB decision-making. The Healthwatch organisations in Sutton, Merton, Wandsworth, Richmond, Kingston and Croydon operate jointly on cross-borough NHS issues — deaf healthcare access being a recent example.
DA Languages BSL Rollout — October 2024
The most significant recent deaf healthcare access improvement in SWL. Following the Healthwatch SWL AIS review, the SWL ICB commissioned a new BSL interpreter provider for all SWL GP practices. DA Languages replaced Language Line on 1 October 2024, introducing:
  • On-demand video BSL interpretation — a new feature in GP services
  • In-person BSL interpretation available
  • Translation for over 500 spoken languages
  • Training materials for all GP staff across the six boroughs
Prior to October 2024, patients reported Language Line took too long to book, was unreliable, and raised privacy concerns. Healthwatch SWL is now running a follow-up feedback survey (patients, carers, and GP staff) with responses open until 12 June 2025. Contact: iyinoluwa@swlhealthwatch.org.uk · alyssa@swlhealthwatch.org.uk.

Respond to DA Languages feedback survey by 12 June 2025 ICB commissioning influence via Healthwatch Monitor survey results publication AIS monitoring partner patient voice amplifier for any SWL deaf health work

RAD — Croydon Caseworker Service
Keep Informed
SWL ICB · Croydon Embedded in Croydon Council Sensory Impairment Service
Organisation & Relevance
RAD is formally embedded within Croydon Council's Sensory Impairment Service, providing a dedicated caseworker for profoundly deaf Croydon residents following social care assessments. This is a frontline service operating at the direct intersection of NHS primary care, social care commissioning, and the Deaf community.

The caseworker role means RAD Croydon has direct knowledge of what health needs profoundly deaf Croydon residents are presenting with, what NHS services they are or are not accessing, and where AIS failures are causing real harm. This makes RAD Croydon a critical intelligence source for understanding the local gap between NHS obligations and actual delivery. It also sits alongside the Croydon Hearing Resource Centre (same Sensory Impairment Service), making Croydon's DDPO/council/RAD ecosystem the most developed in SWL.

Croydon Hearing Resource Centre
Keep Informed
SWL ICB · Croydon Croydon Council Sensory Impairment Service RAD caseworker partnership
Organisation & Relevance
Operated by Croydon Council's Sensory Impairment Service in formal partnership with RAD, the Croydon Hearing Resource Centre is a specialist assessment and support service for deaf and hard of hearing residents. It provides: hearing aid maintenance and support, specialist equipment assessment, home alert devices for safety and independence, and liaison with NHS audiology services.

Healthcare access dimension: The Centre operates at the community-level delivery end of the AIS — it is where residents actually receive the practical equipment and support that the Accessible Information Standard is supposed to guarantee them. Croydon Council explicitly notes it provides equipment to alert deaf residents to sounds in their home environment for safety and independence. This is also the most concrete existing example in SWL of a council-NHS-charity partnership delivering on AIS obligations in practice.

AIS community-level delivery model Sensory equipment assessment Home alert devices pathway Council–NHS–charity partnership model Hearing aid & audiology liaison

Organisation
One of the oldest deaf support organisations in the UK, established in 1971. Offices in Bromley, Ealing, Whitechapel, Birmingham, Bath, Hampshire and Surrey. Delivers Information, Advice & Guidance; advocacy; employment and training; lipreading rehabilitation; DeafBlind support; equipment assessment; and personal budgets. Supported by deaf ambassadors Rose Ayling-Ellis (EastEnders) and Gok Wan.

Bromley Chain succession (March 2024): When Bromley Chain (charity 281051) was formally dissolved by the Charity Commission in March 2024, its assets transferred to Breakthrough / deafPLUS. deafPLUS is now the primary formal Deaf support organisation in Bromley borough, combining its own longstanding services with what Bromley Chain previously provided.
Bromley Office — Confirmed Active January 2025
The Bromley office (NHS-listed, Bromley Council directory, Bromley Simply Connect — all updated 2025) provides:
  • IAG service: Mon–Fri 9:30am–4pm, by appointment (face-to-face and digital)
  • Hard of Hearing social group: 2nd and 4th Mondays, with guest speakers
  • BSL practice group: all levels including hearing BSL learners
  • Tinnitus support group
  • Hearing Equipment Demonstration Service: residents try and compare assistive hearing devices in person — this is direct AIS community-level delivery
Text/SMS only: 07732 449349. The Surrey office covers the Sutton–West Sussex corridor.

British Deaf Association

National Founded 1890 BSL-led · cultural advocacy bda.org.uk
Organisation & Relevance
BSL-led national campaigning organisation founded in 1890. Primary focus is language rights and cultural recognition for Deaf BSL users — the BSL Act 2022 campaign, BSL Charter compliance for public bodies, and political advocacy for the Deaf community. Covers all South London boroughs. Active across national policy, with particular strength in matters of BSL as an official language.

For healthcare access work specifically: BDA is less directly engaged than RNID or SignHealth in NHS AIS compliance. However, it is highly relevant if any work involves: BSL Charter sign-off from public sector bodies (NHS trusts, ICBs), culturally framing healthcare access as a language rights issue rather than a disability accommodation issue, or connecting with the broader Deaf cultural community where BSL identity — not hearing loss — is the primary frame of reference.

The UK's leading charity supporting people with combined sight and hearing loss — a distinct and often severely marginalised population within the broader deaf healthcare space. Peterborough-based.

For SWL healthcare access work: Monitor only unless the work specifically addresses dual-sensory impairment — for example, if NHS accessibility tools are being designed to serve people with both hearing and sight impairment simultaneously, or if the SWL ICB is considering commissioning for this population specifically. Oak Lodge School
Monitor
SWL ICB · Wandsworth Nightingale Lane, SW12 Ofsted Good 2025
Organisation & Relevance
Maintained special school for Deaf students aged 10–19 in Wandsworth, rated Good by Ofsted (2025). Hosts the Deaf First advice service. Also runs an Adult Deaf Lifelong Learning programme, drawing students from 25 London boroughs — making it one of the broadest community hubs for Deaf adults in South West London.

For healthcare access: Not a health delivery organisation. However, it is the only institution in SWL providing structured ongoing education for Deaf adults, which includes health literacy components. The Adult Lifelong Learning programme reaches Deaf adults who may otherwise have limited contact with formal NHS communications. The school's broader network through 25 boroughs provides reach into communities that formal health communications often miss.
