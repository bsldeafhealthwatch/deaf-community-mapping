SocialSportYouthSE London
UK's largest Deaf club network — 14 clubs across London, charity income £407,994 (2024 accounts). Entirely community-focused with no healthcare delivery. South London presence is strongest in SE boroughs: Eltham lunch club (SE9), SE6 playscheme, BR1 badminton, Beckenham lunch club. The Parent & Children Group welcomes hearing CODAs and hearing parents of deaf children.
Engagement Approach
The Monday South London Badminton Club (BR1) and Monday Eltham Lunch Club (SE9) are your two most practical in-person entry points. Remark!'s charity team is based at 18 Leather Lane, EC1N — contactable via remarkcommunity.org. The lunch club advocacy element (making phone calls, writing letters for members) shows they already work on everyday independence tasks, making doorbell alerts a natural fit to raise informally.
Deaf Unity
Engage Now
✓ Confirmed active — BSL Café at Royal Festival Hall listed on live Deaf Unity website as ongoing monthly event (third Friday of each month). Inclusion London DDPO directory entry updated May 2025. Active events calendar. Registered charity in good standing.
EmploymentBSL
Deaf-led London charity focused on education-to-employment transitions. Monthly community BSL café at the Royal Festival Hall Southbank is a publicly accessible, longstanding Deaf community gathering point — free to attend, no appointment needed. Join their Facebook group for dates. They also run a West London community project (Heathrow Community Trust funded) and employment empowerment workshops.
Engagement Approach
The Royal Festival Hall BSL café is the single most accessible entry point into the South London Deaf community for an outsider. Attend as a community member, not a project promoter. Facebook group for event updates. Deaf Unity can also be approached via deafunity.org for awareness training partnerships — their employment toolkit angle (working from home, receiving deliveries safely) aligns directly with the doorbell project.
Deaf DMCS — Deaf Multilingual Community Support
Engage Now
✓ Confirmed active — Inclusion London DDPO directory listing confirmed updated May 2025. IAG officer provides support twice weekly. Website deaf-dmcs.org is live.
CommunityMultilingual
Full name is Deaf Multilingual Community Support (DMCS) — an important correction from the original map. They serve multilingual Deaf communities (BSL plus other sign languages), covering all South London boroughs. Their IAG (Information, Advice and Guidance) service includes help with housing, benefits, education, Home Office immigration issues, and translating documents into BSL. They have in-house interpreters. Note: they do accompany people to medical appointments, which puts them at the edge of the "no healthcare involvement" boundary, but their primary purpose is community support and information access.
Engagement Approach
DMCS is particularly important for reaching South London's multilingual Deaf community — people whose first language may be Arabic Sign Language, Kenyan Sign Language or another national sign language, who are often the most isolated from mainstream Deaf services. A doorbell alert that only reaches the BSL-first community misses a significant proportion of London's Deaf population. Contact via deaf-dmcs.org to understand community distribution.

BME Deaf WomenEmpowerment
National organisation run and controlled by Deaf women from Black and Ethnic Minority groups. Covers all South London boroughs. Empowers BME Deaf women to have choice and control without compromising identity.
Engagement Approach
Critical for inclusive co-design. Reach via dewa.org.uk. Treat as co-designers, not just end users — BME Deaf women's insight on home safety, privacy expectations, and cultural dimensions of having a visible external alert system on your door is essential to getting the product right.
Deafinitely Theatre
Keep in View
✓ Very active — 2025 season formally announced April 2025: UK tour of The Vagina Monologues (BSL) plus premiere of Barrier(s) by deaf writer Eloise Pennycott. Companies House status: Active. National Lottery funded. Paula Garfield awarded MBE 2024. 2025 accounts due Dec 2025.
ArtsBSL
The UK's only professional Deaf-led bilingual theatre company. Founded 2002, currently in its strongest position — expanding from London into national touring. Deafinitely Youth Theatre alumni include Rose (EastEnders) and William Grint (RSC). Paula Garfield directed BSL Shakespeare at the Globe. Arts Council England and National Lottery supported.
Engagement Approach
High cultural authority in the Deaf community — a connection signals genuine engagement with Deaf identity rather than medical/welfare framing. Consider approaching their Deaf Awareness training arm for consultation on how to frame accessibility tools as empowerment. Contact via deafinitelytheatre.co.uk.
British Deaf Association
Keep in View
✓ Confirmed active — Inclusion London DDPO updated May 2025. National campaigning organisation, founded 1890. bda.org.uk active.
BSL RightsAdvocacy
Oldest national Deaf-led organisation in the UK. All board trustees are Deaf, 80% of staff are Deaf. Campaigns for BSL language rights and equal access. The BSL Charter is their public commitment tool — organisations sign it to formally recognise BSL users' needs.
Engagement Approach
Keep informed of the doorbell alert project's existence. If Sutton Council or a housing provider adopts the alert system as policy, the BDA's BSL Charter is a useful public commitment framework to cite. Contact via bda.org.uk.
Bright DeafBlind
Keep in View
✓ Confirmed active — brightdeafblind.com website live · Inclusion London DDPO directory May 2025.
Dual-sensory
Provides deafblind-friendly social activities and workshops for profoundly deaf, visually impaired BSL users across London. Their membership is among the most isolated in the Deaf community. Standard visual flashing doorbells may be insufficient for deafblind users — vibration or tactile alerts would be needed.
Engagement Approach
Consult early on dual-sensory design requirements. If the doorbell alert system can incorporate vibration components, Bright DeafBlind moves from "keep in view" to a high-priority partner. Contact via brightdeafblind.com.

Bromley Chain / Bromley Deaf Social Club — DISSOLVED
Closed · Succeeded by deafPLUS Bromley
✗ Formally dissolved — but positively succeeded. Bromley Chain (charity 281051) was formally dissolved by the Charity Commission on 19 March 2024. Its funds were transferred to Breakthrough (Deaf-Hearing Integration) — charity 1073468 — which operates as deafPLUS. This is not a dead end: deafPLUS Bromley is confirmed active and has actually expanded what Bromley Chain offered, including a physical office with professional IAG support. The Bromley Chain social club activities may have merged into deafPLUS's Bromley social groups.
DissolvedSEL · Bromley
Bromley Chain was a parent charity founded by parents of deaf children. It ran Deaf Access, Bromley Deaf Social Club, Bromley Deaf Children's Society, and administered the Phyllis Kither Bequest grants fund. All of these activities were absorbed into deafPLUS when the funds transferred in March 2024. Contact deafPLUS Bromley to understand what continues.
What This Means
The dissolution of Bromley Chain is not a loss — it's a consolidation into a stronger organisation. deafPLUS Bromley (see panel) is now the relevant Bromley contact. The Phyllis Kither Bequest small grants fund may also still be available via deafPLUS for community projects — potentially relevant for a doorbell alert pilot.
deafPLUS — Bromley ★
Bromley Chain Successor · Confirmed Active
★ Confirmed active as of January 2025. NHS service directory listing updated 14 January 2025. Bromley Council local organisations directory lists deafPLUS. Bromley Simply Connect directory confirms current services including BSL practice group (all levels, hearing welcome), Hard of Hearing social group (2nd and 4th Monday), tinnitus support group, and hearing equipment demonstration service. Office open Mon–Fri 9:30am–4pm by appointment. Text/SMS: 07732 449349. Formerly known as Breakthrough (Deaf-Hearing Integration), which received Bromley Chain's funds on dissolution in March 2024.
Social GroupsIAGSEL · Bromley
deafPLUS was established in 1971 as Breakthrough (Deaf-Hearing Integration) — one of the oldest deaf support organisations in the UK. It has offices in Bromley, Ealing, Whitechapel, Birmingham, Bath, Hampshire and Surrey. The Bromley office provides face-to-face information, advice and guidance; a BSL practice social group (all levels including hearing BSL learners); Hard of Hearing social group with cake and guest speakers; tinnitus support; lipreading classes; and hearing equipment demos. The Bromley Chain fund transfer in 2024 strengthened their Bromley presence. Supported by Rose Ayling-Ellis (EastEnders deaf actress) and Gok Wan.
Engagement Approach
This is the highest-priority Bromley contact — replacing the dissolved Bromley Chain entirely. The Hard of Hearing social group (2nd and 4th Monday) and the BSL practice group are both in-person community touchpoints ideal for introducing a doorbell alert prototype. The equipment demonstration service is directly relevant — deafPLUS Bromley already helps people assess assistive technology. Text 07732 449349 or email via deafplus.org. Ask specifically about the Bromley social groups schedule and whether they'd be interested in co-presenting an assistive technology session.
